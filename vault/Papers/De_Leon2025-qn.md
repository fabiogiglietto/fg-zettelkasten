# The political weaponization of online content: From direct to networked exposure

> de León, E., Kristensen, J. B., Makhortykh, M., Mayerhöffer, E., & Adam, S. (2025). The political weaponization of online content: From direct to networked exposure. *SocArXiv*. https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/grne7_v1
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/grne7_v1)

## Summary

This conceptual paper introduces the notion of **networked exposure** as a corrective to the dominant framing in research on online political information encounters. The authors argue that existing work has focused too narrowly on *direct* exposure — people's encounters with overtly problematic content such as misinformation, conspiracy material, and hyperpartisan outlets. In doing so, it has overlooked how seemingly ordinary content, including traditional news stories and Wikipedia articles, is circulated and politically weaponized within extremist and conspiracy-aligned networks. The central claim is that ostensibly neutral content acquires political meaning through the ideologically charged networks in which it travels, and that capturing this process requires an analytical lens distinct from direct-exposure measurement.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **networked exposure** as a concept complementing the established focus on direct exposure.
- Draws attention to the **political weaponization of ordinary content** (mainstream news, reference material) inside extremist and conspiracy-aligned networks.
- Offers a reframing for how online political information exposure should be conceptualized, studied, and measured.

## Methods

The paper is primarily conceptual and theoretical, developing the networked-exposure framework. The available abstract does not detail specific empirical procedures or data.

## Findings

- No specific empirical findings are reported in the available material; the contribution is conceptual.
- The core argument is that distinguishing *direct* from *networked* exposure reveals a category of exposure that prior research systematically missed.
- Content's political valence is treated as emergent from circulation context rather than intrinsic to the content itself.

## Connections

This paper sits at the intersection of conspiracy-media and information-disorder scholarship, reframing how exposure is theorized rather than catalguing harmful content directly. Its concern with how content circulates and gains meaning through networks resonates with work on conspiracy and extremist media ecosystems such as [[Grusauskaite2026-po]], [[Tuters2026-vo]], and [[Marwick2026-qd]], and its critique of exposure measurement connects to empirical studies of political information diets like [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] and [[Budak2024-ef]].
