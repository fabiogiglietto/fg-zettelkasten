# The political weaponization of online content: From direct to networked exposure

> de León, E., Kristensen, J. B., Makhortykh, M., Mayerhöffer, E., & Adam, S. (2025). The political weaponization of online content: From direct to networked exposure. *SocArXiv*. https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/grne7_v1
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.31235/osf.io/grne7_v1)

## Summary

This paper introduces the concept of **networked exposure** to broaden how we understand audiences' encounters with political information online. Its central argument is that prior exposure research has been too narrowly fixated on *direct* encounters with obviously problematic material — misinformation, conspiracy content, hyperpartisan outlets — and has thereby missed a distinct pattern: people also encounter seemingly ordinary content, such as mainstream news stories or Wikipedia articles, that has been circulated and *weaponized* within extremist and conspiracy-aligned networks. The paper positions networked exposure as a corrective and complement to the direct-exposure paradigm, showing how ostensibly neutral content acquires political meaning through the ideological context of its circulation.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **networked exposure** as an analytically distinct concept alongside direct exposure.
- Foregrounds the **political weaponization of ordinary content** (traditional news, Wikipedia) inside extremist and conspiracy networks.
- Offers a **reframing of how exposure is studied and measured**, arguing the field must account for circulation context, not just content type.
