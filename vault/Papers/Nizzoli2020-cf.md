# Coordinated Behavior on Social Media in 2019 UK General Election

> Nizzoli, L., Tardelli, S., Avvenuti, M., Cresci, S., & Tesconi, M. (2020). Coordinated Behavior on Social Media in 2019 UK General Election. *Proc. AAAI Intl. Conference on Web and Social Media (ICWSM) 2021*.
>
> [View paper](http://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08370v2)

## Summary

This paper proposes a network-based computational framework for detecting and characterizing coordinated behavior on social media, motivated by the role such behavior plays in disinformation and influence operations. The authors argue that coordinated online activity is a core mechanism amplifying the spread of disinformation, and that existing detection approaches either depend on labor-intensive manual investigation or rely on oversimplifying assumptions to remain computationally tractable. Their framework is presented as a more faithful alternative that can surface and analyze coordination at scale, demonstrated through a case study of Twitter activity around the 2019 UK General Election.

## Key Contributions

- A network-based framework for uncovering and studying coordinated behavior on social media.
- An extension of prior detection systems that avoids the bold assumptions and oversimplifications of earlier computational methods.
- An empirical case study grounded in the 2019 UK General Election.

## Methods

The core method is a network-based analytical framework that models coordination among accounts and detects clusters exhibiting suspiciously similar behavior. The authors apply this framework to Twitter data collected during the 2019 UK General Election, positioning it as an advance beyond both manual investigation and simplified computational models of coordinated activity.

## Findings

- The framework is applied to the 2019 UK General Election to surface coordinated behaviors; full empirical results are not specified in the available abstract.
- The case study demonstrates the framework's capacity to detect and characterize coordination in a real electoral context.

## Connections

This work sits at the intersection of coordinated inauthentic behavior detection and election-focused social media research, closely related to other network- and similarity-based detection efforts such as [[Luceri2025-tr]], [[Minici2024-tf]], and [[Mannocci2025-ig]], and to the broader coordinated-link-sharing detection tradition exemplified by [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] and [[Giglietto2020-6278a4aa]]. Its focus on coordination as a disinformation-amplification mechanism connects it to foundational work on organized influence operations like [[Starbird2019-qv]] and to electoral disinformation studies including [[Keller2019-nk]].
