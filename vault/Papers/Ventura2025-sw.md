# Misinformation beyond traditional feeds: Evidence from a WhatsApp deactivation experiment in Brazil

> Ventura, T., Majumdar, R., Nagler, J., & Tucker, J. A. (2025). Misinformation beyond traditional feeds: Evidence from a WhatsApp deactivation experiment in Brazil. *J. Polit.*. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4457400
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.4457400)

## Summary

This paper uses a field experiment in Brazil to estimate the causal effects of deactivating WhatsApp on users' political knowledge, beliefs, and exposure to misinformation during an electoral period. The authors argue that encrypted messaging platforms occupy a distinct position in political information ecosystems compared with feed-based social media like Facebook and Twitter, and that any adequate understanding of misinformation must extend beyond public-facing feeds to private messaging. By inducing a set of participants to stop using WhatsApp and comparing them to a control group, the study offers experimental leverage on how a messaging platform shapes informational and attitudinal outcomes in a key non-U.S. context where WhatsApp is central to political communication.

## Key Contributions

- Provides experimental causal evidence on the political effects of a major encrypted messaging platform, extending a literature dominated by studies of Facebook and Twitter.
- Expands the empirical scope of misinformation research to private, non-feed-based platforms.
- Supplies evidence from Brazil, a Global South context where WhatsApp plays an outsized role in political communication, contributing to comparative debates about platform effects.

## Methods

- Field experiment using a WhatsApp **deactivation design**, in which treated participants were induced to stop using the platform for a defined period.
- Conducted in Brazil, around an electoral period, comparing deactivated (treated) users against a control group.
- Outcomes measured include political knowledge, political beliefs, and exposure to misinformation.

## Findings

- The study reports causal effects of WhatsApp deactivation on users' information environments and political attitudes in Brazil (precise magnitudes not available from the abstract alone).
- The results support the claim that messaging-based platforms operate differently from feed-based social media in shaping political information exposure.

## Connections

This deactivation design is methodologically aligned with the social media abstention and deactivation literature exemplified by [[Allcott2017-yz]], [[Allcott2019-gn]], and [[Allcott2025-jb]], while extending that paradigm from feed-based platforms to encrypted messaging. It also connects to broader debates about the actual scale and effects of misinformation exposure found in [[Guess2020-rr]], [[Allen2020-nj]], and [[Budak2024-ef]], and to work by the same research group on Brazilian political information environments such as [[Ventura2026-yc]].
