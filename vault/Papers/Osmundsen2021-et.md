# Partisan polarization is the primary psychological motivation behind political fake news sharing on twitter

> OSMUNDSEN, M., BOR, A., VAHLSTRUP, P. B., BECHMANN, A., & PETERSEN, M. B. (2021). Partisan polarization is the primary psychological motivation behind political fake news sharing on twitter. *American Political Science Review*, *115*, 999–1015. https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003055421000290
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0003055421000290)

## Summary

This paper asks *why* citizens share political fake news on Twitter, pitting three competing psychological accounts against one another: that sharers are ignorant and cognitively inattentive, that they are driven by chaotic or status-quo-disrupting impulses, or that they are motivated by partisan animosity in a polarized environment. Linking psychological measures to observed sharing behavior, the authors conclude that **partisan polarization is the primary driver**. The core argument is that misinformation research has overemphasized cognitive deficits — laziness, ignorance, lack of attention — when the real engine is motivated partisan reasoning and the desire to attack political opponents. The paper thus repositions fake news sharing as a problem of political and motivational psychology rather than one of information literacy alone.

## Key Contributions

- Claims to be the first study to *directly* test competing psychological motivations behind political fake news sharing.
- Shifts the explanatory frame away from cognitive/informational deficits toward partisan and motivational psychology.
- Adjudicates between three rival hypotheses (ignorance, disruption/chaos-seeking, partisan attack) using actual sharing behavior rather than self-report of intentions alone.
- Connects individual-level psychology to broader democratic concerns about misinformation in polarized Western societies.

## Methods

The study empirically tests competing psychological hypotheses — ignorance/inattention, disruptive/chaos-seeking motives, and partisan attack motives — against observed behavior. Psychological measures are linked to real fake news sharing activity on Twitter, allowing the authors to compare the explanatory power of each account. (Specific datasets, sample sizes, and analytic techniques are not recoverable from the abstract alone.)

## Findings

- Partisan polarization emerged as the strongest predictor of political fake news sharing.
- The motivation to attack partisan opponents outperformed both the ignorance/laziness account and the general disruption/chaos account.
- The evidence weighs against the view that fake news sharers are primarily cognitively lazy or uninformed.

## Connections

This paper directly engages the "cognitive deficit vs. motivated reasoning" debate about misinformation, standing in tension with the inattention-based account associated with [[Pennycook2021-jq]] and complementing accounts of affective polarization such as [[Iyengar2019-jj]]. Its behavioral analysis of who shares false content on Twitter connects to work on the diffusion and correlates of fake news sharing, including [[Vosoughi2018-at]], [[Mosleh2024-op]], and [[Lazer2018-mm]], and its emphasis on partisan identity as the engine of sharing resonates with research on partisan echo chambers and network structure like [[Barbera2015-fw]] and [[Bakshy2015-rn]].
