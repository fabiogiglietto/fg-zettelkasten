# Does the method affect the outcome? How measures of partisan slant of media outlets and affective polarization drive results about polarization in the United States

> Schemer, C., Langmann, K., Hasell, A., & Weeks, B. (2026). Does the method affect the outcome? How measures of partisan slant of media outlets and affective polarization drive results about polarization in the United States. *Political Communication*, 1–25. https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2699105
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2699105)

## Summary

This paper interrogates whether the well-documented inconsistencies in the partisan-media-and-polarization literature stem from genuine substantive differences or from methodological choices in how researchers operationalize their key variables. Using three-wave panel data from the 2020 U.S. presidential election, the authors deploy specification curve analysis (SCA) across 504 combinations of independent-variable (media partisanship) and dependent-variable (affective/belief polarization) operationalizations. Their central argument is that measurement decisions substantially inflate or deflate the estimated strength of the relationship between partisan media diets and polarization—though not its direction—meaning that seemingly conflicting findings across studies can largely be attributed to "researcher degrees of freedom." The paper thus reframes a substantive controversy as, in significant part, a measurement problem.

## Key Contributions

- Systematic empirical demonstration that measurement choices, not just data or context, drive divergent conclusions in the partisan-media–polarization literature.
- Distinguishes the practical consequences of three scoring paradigms: analytic content-based, holistic content-based, and audience-based media partisanship scores (MPS).
- Surfaces partisan asymmetries that bipolar/difference scores conceal, recommending disaggregated component reporting.
- Offers best-practice recommendations: align measurement with the research question, run sensitivity analyses, and report both bipolar and component-based results.
- Connects methodological choices to the broader normative debate over how serious a social problem partisan media and polarization actually are.

## Methods

The authors fielded an original three-wave YouGov panel survey (Sept–Oct 2020; wave 1 n = 1,800 quota-matched, wave 2 n = 1,401). Media use was captured via the list-frequency technique across 63 U.S. news outlets. They assembled **17 published MPS** spanning analytic, holistic, and audience-based approaches, combined into 42 IV specifications (weighted/unweighted, continuous/categorical). Twelve DV specifications covered nine affective polarization measures (feeling thermometers and trust toward Trump, Biden, and their supporters, as single and difference scores) plus three belief-polarization (misperception) measures. SCA (R package `specr`) ran all 504 IV × DV combinations with variance decomposition, controlling for partisanship, political interest, knowledge, demographics, and local news use. Robustness checks excluded Independents and used lagged-dependent-variable regressions on wave 2 outcomes.

## Findings

- Most of the 504 specifications yielded positive, significant relationships: more conservative diets predicted greater Trump favorability/trust and Trump-favoring misperceptions.
- Coefficient magnitudes varied widely (mean b = .33); the largest significant coefficient (.47) was nearly seven times the smallest (.07)—but no signs flipped.
- Audience-based MPS produced the largest estimates (M = .35); analytic content-based scores the smallest; holistic in between.
- MPS choice accounted for 56% of coefficient variance; DV choice for 42%.
- Trust and misperception measures produced stronger coefficients than feeling thermometers.
- Disaggregation revealed asymmetry: conservative diets showed stronger absolute associations (M = .24) than liberal diets (M = -.19), with centrist diets near zero.
- Longitudinal (lagged) analyses yielded consistently small coefficients with far less variation.

## Connections

This paper sits at the intersection of the partisan-news/polarization literature and the methodological-validity strand, applying the specification-curve paradigm to a substantive dispute about measurement. Its concern with how measurement and modeling choices shape polarization conclusions resonates with other work probing the robustness and interpretation of polarization effects, such as [[Stagnaro2025-pz]], [[Ventura2026-yc]], and [[Lyons2026-ca]], while its foundational reliance on audience-behavior data recalls exposure-based studies like [[Bakshy2015-rn]]. None of the listed papers directly replicate its multiverse SCA approach to partisan-slant scoring, so the methodological contribution stands largely on its own within this set.
