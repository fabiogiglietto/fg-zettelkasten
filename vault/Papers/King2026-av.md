# AI article reviews dominate human peer reviews at their best: Evidence and policy choices

> King, G., & Worthington, S. (2026). AI article reviews dominate human peer reviews at their best: Evidence and policy choices. *Gary King*.
>
> [View paper](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=AI%20article%20reviews%20dominate%20human%20peer%20reviews%20at%20their%20best%3A%20Evidence%20and%20policy%20choices)

## Summary

This paper presents empirical evidence that AI-generated reviews of academic articles now outperform reviews written by well-compensated human experts. Crucially, the human benchmark was not an average or weak comparison group but a set of reviews that journal editors and authors had independently judged to be of unusually high quality. Across every quality measure the authors constructed, AI reviews matched or surpassed this demanding standard. Having established this capability, the paper turns to journal policy, weighing the concerning downsides of AI in peer review against potential gains in publication speed, review quality, and a less adversarial experience for the scholars involved.

## Key Contributions

- Empirical evidence that AI article reviews surpass high-quality human expert reviews on every constructed measure.
- A particularly demanding benchmark: human reviews pre-vetted as unusually good by editors and authors, rather than a weak comparison set.
- A policy framework for journals to reason through the risks and opportunities of adopting AI in peer review.
- Identification of concrete potential benefits — faster publication, higher review quality, and reduced adversarial interactions.

## Methods

The authors compare AI-generated reviews against human expert reviews using multiple constructed measures of review quality. The human comparison set was deliberately chosen to be strong: reviews that editors and authors had already flagged as being of unusually high quality, raising the bar for any claim of AI superiority. The empirical assessment is paired with a policy analysis that discusses the downsides and benefits of AI reviewing for the various stakeholders in the publication process (editors, reviewers, authors, and readers).

## Findings

- AI reviews outperformed high-quality human expert reviews on every evaluation measure used.
- The result holds even against the demanding benchmark of unusually well-regarded human reviews.
- Alongside concerning downsides, there are plausible paths to improve the publication process through AI assistance (specifics not detailed in the abstract).
