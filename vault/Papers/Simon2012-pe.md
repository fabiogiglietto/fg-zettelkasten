# Generative AI and News Report 2026: From standalone tools to information infrastructure

> Simon, F., Nielsen, R. K., & Fletcher, R. (2012). Generative AI and News Report 2026: From standalone tools to information infrastructure.
>
> [View paper](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Generative%20AI%20and%20News%20Report%202026%3A%20From%20standalone%20tools%20to%20information%20infrastructure)

## Summary

This Reuters Institute report presents the third annual wave of a nine-country survey (Argentina, Australia, Denmark, Finland, France, Japan, Norway, UK, USA) tracking how the public uses and perceives generative AI in everyday life and in news. Its central argument is that generative AI is shifting from a collection of standalone tools toward embedded **information infrastructure** — an information intermediary that increasingly mediates how people access and interpret information, often incidentally. Weekly use has reached roughly half the population in these high-income democracies within four years of ChatGPT's launch, yet public attitudes toward AI in journalism remain cautious and largely unchanged since 2024. The report documents a persistent "comfort gap" around AI-made news and a striking optimism divide between intense users and never-users.

## Key Contributions

- Delivers a third annual wave of comparative, cross-national survey data, enabling 2024–2026 trend analysis on AI adoption and attitudes.
- Introduces and documents the concept of **incidental AI exposure** — encountering AI in services used for other purposes — distinct from intentional use.
- Reframes generative AI as an emerging **information intermediary** and **information infrastructure** rather than a set of standalone tools.
- Applies the SAIL4ALL AI literacy scale and the ability-benevolence-integrity (ABI) trust framework to a large public sample, surfacing a user/non-user optimism gap and a cross-sector "honesty problem."
- Supplies evidence on the persistent comfort gap regarding AI in news, relevant to journalists, technologists, and scholars.

## Methods

Online survey fielded by YouGov for the Reuters Institute (14 August–28 September 2026), ~2,000 respondents per country, using nationally representative quota sampling (age, gender, region, education, political leaning) with weighting; non-probability sampling means ±2pp differences are treated as non-meaningful. Trend analysis is restricted to the six countries present in all three waves. AI literacy was measured with the SAIL4ALL scale (52 true/false items across five dimensions) via a matrix sampling design, and trust perceptions were analysed through the ABI framework. The authors used large language models (ChatGPT 6, Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.1) to assist analysis and drafting, with human review.

## Findings

- Weekly use of standalone generative AI rose from 34% (2025) to 51% (2026) in the six core countries; daily use nearly doubled year-on-year (7% → 16% → 28%).
- ChatGPT leads (weekly 22% → 34%), with Gemini, Copilot, and Claude growing; weekly users access ~two systems on average.
- Exposure is increasingly incidental: 57% saw AI summaries in search, 25% on devices, 21% on social/messaging apps; 49% encountered an AI feature on a news site/app.
- Use skews younger, higher-income, more educated, and higher-skilled; 18% have paid AI access — identical to the share with paid news access.
- Using AI for information rose from 11% (2024) to 38% (2026); AI news use grew from 3% to 9% in core countries, remaining niche but growing.
- Among users, trust in AI output is high, but convenience (64%) and interactivity (56%) drive use far more than perceived reliability (21%).
- Higher AI literacy correlates with greater awareness but, controlling for awareness, slightly lower use and trust — driven by knowledge of AI's limitations.
- The public is optimistic about AI use by scientists, healthcare, and AI companies, but pessimistic about governments, social media, politicians, and news media; pessimism is driven by perceived dishonesty.
- Comfort with AI-made news is low and stable: only 12% comfortable with fully AI-made news (19% with human oversight) vs 67% for human-made; people expect AI to make news cheaper but less transparent and less trustworthy.

## Connections

This report sits within the **generative-ai-imaginaries** strand, drawing explicitly on sociotechnical imaginaries and trust theory, and complements algorithmic-folk-theory work on how publics make sense of opaque systems like [[Bucher2017-qh]] and [[Cotter2019-js]]. Its documentation of cautious public attitudes toward AI in journalism connects to news-sector studies of generative AI such as [[Moran2025-qn]] and [[Gottfried2026-ww]]. As a large-scale survey of public perceptions, it offers empirical grounding for the labor-impacts and public-attitude questions raised across the broader register.
