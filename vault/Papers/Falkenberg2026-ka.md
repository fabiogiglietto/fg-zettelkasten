# Toward an integrated and globally equitable understanding of political polarization

> Falkenberg, M., Cinelli, M., Galeazzi, A., Bail, C. A., Benito, R. M., Bruns, A., Gruzd, A., Lazer, D., Lee, J. K., McCoy, J., Nagayoshi, K., Rand, D. G., Scala, A., Siegel, A. Toward an integrated and globally equitable understanding of political polarization. *Nature communications*.
>
> [View paper](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Toward%20an%20integrated%20and%20globally%20equitable%20understanding%20of%20political%20polarization)

## Summary

This perspective article argues that research on political polarization is both **fragmented across disciplines** and **geographically skewed** toward a small set of Western democracies, especially the United States. Written by a large, multinational and interdisciplinary group of scholars spanning computational social science, political science, communication, network science, and psychology, the paper calls for an integrated and globally equitable research agenda. The authors contend that without frameworks and methods capable of capturing polarization across diverse political, cultural, and media contexts, the field risks mischaracterizing the phenomenon and overgeneralizing from atypical cases.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes an agenda for **integrating disparate strands** of polarization research now siloed by discipline.
- Advocates for **global equity** in the geographic and cultural scope of polarization studies, pushing beyond a US-centric evidence base.
- Assembles a broad international, interdisciplinary authorship to frame future directions for the field.

## Methods

- A perspective/synthesis article rather than an empirical study.
- Draws on cross-disciplinary expertise from a large multinational authorship.
- Reviews and synthesizes existing literature to diagnose gaps and propose directions.

## Findings

- The field's evidence base is disproportionately concentrated on a limited set of countries, leaving major gaps in understanding polarization elsewhere.
- Disciplinary silos hinder a coherent, comparable account of polarization dynamics.
- A globally equitable and integrated approach is needed to characterize polarization accurately across different societies.

## Connections

As a programmatic call for broader and more integrated study, this paper sits above much of the empirical work under this topic that examines polarization within specific national or platform contexts. It relates conceptually to work analyzing cross-platform and cross-national polarization dynamics such as [[Bouchaud2026-np]] and [[Eady2025-vm]], and to the affordance- and platform-focused studies (e.g., [[Bakshy2015-rn]]) whose findings the authors would argue need to be tested against more diverse settings.
