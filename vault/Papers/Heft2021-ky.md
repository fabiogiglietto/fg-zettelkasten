# Toward a transnational information ecology on the right? Hyperlink networking among right-wing digital news sites in Europe and the United States

> Heft, A., Knüpfer, C., Reinhardt, S., & Mayerhöffer, E. (2021). Toward a transnational information ecology on the right? Hyperlink networking among right-wing digital news sites in Europe and the United States. *The International Journal of Press/Politics*, *26*, 484–504. https://doi.org/10.1177/1940161220963670
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/1940161220963670)

## Summary

This paper asks whether right-wing digital news sites in Europe and the United States constitute a genuinely transnational information ecology rather than isolated national media environments. Using hyperlink network analysis, the authors map how these sites reference one another both within and across national borders and identify third-party websites that serve as shared reference points binding the ecology together. The work situates itself within research on alternative digital news infrastructures and the broader transnationalization of the political right, arguing that hyperlinking is not merely a technical practice but an infrastructural mechanism through which shared epistemologies and cross-border information flows are constructed.

## Key Contributions

- Empirically maps the transnational structure of right-wing alternative news infrastructures across multiple countries.
- Advances understanding of how shared epistemologies and information flows emerge across distinct national right-wing media environments.
- Extends comparative research on alternative/right-wing media into a cross-national, network-analytic framework.
- Frames hyperlinking as infrastructure for circulation and epistemic alignment, not just as a measure of connectivity.

## Methods

- Hyperlink network analysis of a set of right-wing digital news sites.
- Comparative design spanning several European countries and the United States.
- Mapping of intra-national and cross-border interconnections, plus identification of externally shared transnational reference sites acting as common nodes.

## Findings

- Right-wing digital news sites are interconnected at both national and transnational levels.
- Certain external sites function as commonly shared reference points that link otherwise nationally distinct right-wing news ecologies.
- Hyperlinking supports the circulation of information and the establishment of shared epistemologies among right-wing audiences across borders.
