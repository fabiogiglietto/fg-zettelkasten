# Political Astroturfing on Twitter: How to Coordinate a Disinformation Campaign

> Keller, F. B., Schoch, D., Stier, S., & Yang, J. (2020). Political Astroturfing on Twitter: How to Coordinate a Disinformation Campaign. *Political Communication*, 1–25. https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2019.1661888
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2019.1661888)

## Summary

This paper offers an empirically grounded study of *political astroturfing* — centrally coordinated disinformation campaigns in which paid operatives masquerade as independent, grassroots citizens. Its central methodological advance is exploiting a rare "ground truth" case: the South Korean National Information Service (NIS) operation on Twitter, for which the identities of the campaign's agents were later confirmed. Using these verified identities as a benchmark, the authors show that coordinated accounts leave detectable behavioral signatures that distinguish them from genuine users, even when individual accounts try to appear authentic. The paper thereby addresses a persistent challenge in the study of covert influence operations — the near-absence of reliable validation data.

## Key Contributions

- Provides one of the few empirically validated studies of political astroturfing built on a rare verified-ground-truth dataset.
- Advances behavioral methods for detecting and characterizing centrally coordinated disinformation campaigns.
- Offers a conceptual definition of astroturfing that distinguishes it from other forms of online disinformation, foregrounding the combination of central coordination with a pretense of grassroots independence.

## Methods

A case study of the NIS disinformation campaign on Twitter, using the confirmed identities of campaign agents and instigators as a validation benchmark. The authors analyze Twitter activity data to surface patterns of coordination — traces left by centralized direction that persist despite efforts to mimic ordinary users — and compare astroturfing accounts against genuine ones.

## Findings

- Coordinated astroturfing accounts exhibit distinguishing behavioral patterns relative to ordinary users, making them detectable through activity signatures.
- Verified agent identities make it possible to assess the true scope and coordination structure of the campaign — information that is otherwise inaccessible in covert operations.
- Such campaigns have the potential to influence electoral outcomes and other political behavior.

## Connections

Its conceptual framing of coordinated influence operations resonates with research on state-linked and networked disinformation including [[Starbird2019-qv]] and [[Bruns2019-nr]], while the broader concern with disinformation's political reach links it to [[Vosoughi2018-at]] and [[Lazer2018-mm]].
