# The disinformation order: Disruptive communication and the decline of democratic institutions

> Bennett, W. L., & Livingston, S. (2018). The disinformation order: Disruptive communication and the decline of democratic institutions. *European Journal of Communication*, *33*, 122–139. https://doi.org/10.1177/0267323118760317
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/0267323118760317)

## Summary

Bennett and Livingston advance a structural argument about disinformation in contemporary democracies: rather than treating the spread of false information as an isolated communication pathology, they frame it as a symptom of deeper legitimacy crises within democratic systems. They introduce the concept of a "disinformation order" — a self-reinforcing dynamic in which disruptive communication both reflects and accelerates the decline of confidence in core democratic institutions. The paper foregrounds the role of radical right movements and parties, which mobilize supporters against centrist parties and the mainstream press by circulating false information through social media and websites that mimic journalistic formats.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes disinformation as an *outcome* of systemic democratic legitimacy crises rather than a standalone media or platform problem.
- Introduces the concept of a "disinformation order" to name the linkage between disruptive communication and institutional decline.
- Bridges political communication scholarship with democratic theory and research on the rise of the radical right.

## Methods

Conceptual and theoretical analysis. The paper builds an argument linking disinformation to institutional decline and legitimacy erosion; it does not report a specific empirical study but synthesizes across cases of disinformation in democratic nations.

## Findings

- Disinformation frequently mimics journalistic formats to borrow credibility and extend reach.
- Radical right actors are prominent producers and amplifiers of disinformation targeting mainstream parties and the press.
- Flows of disinformation correlate with weakening institutional legitimacy and eroding citizen trust.
- Declining confidence in democratic institutions functions as a *driver* of disruptive communication, not merely a consequence of it.

## Connections

This paper is a foundational statement of the institutional-decline framing of disinformation and connects closely to the propaganda-and-network account in [[Benkler2018-lw]] and to work on the mainstream press's entanglement with disruptive information flows, such as [[Tsfati2020-uo]]. Its emphasis on radical-right mobilization and partisan animosity links it to research on affective polarization like [[Iyengar2019-jj]] and to the hybrid-media-system perspective in [[Chadwick2015-ii]].
