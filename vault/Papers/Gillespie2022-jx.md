# Do not recommend? Reduction as a form of content moderation

> Gillespie, T. (2022). Do not recommend? Reduction as a form of content moderation. *Social Media + Society*, *8*, 205630512211175. https://doi.org/10.1177/20563051221117552
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/20563051221117552)

## Summary

Gillespie argues that scholarship and public debate on content moderation have been unduly fixated on **removal** — deleting posts and suspending accounts — while a quieter but increasingly pervasive practice has gone underexamined: **reduction**, the deliberate suppression of content's visibility in feeds, search, and recommendations. Platforms use machine learning classifiers to identify "borderline" material judged too misleading, risky, or offensive to promote, yet not clearly rule-violating enough to remove. This content is left in place but algorithmically demoted. The paper positions reduction as a distinct mode of platform governance that raises its own questions about transparency, accountability, and user awareness, since it operates below the threshold of visible enforcement.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes the content moderation debate to encompass **reduction** alongside removal and suspension.
- Introduces and foregrounds "reduction" (demotion, de-amplification) as an analytical category for studying platform governance.
- Highlights the distinctive accountability and visibility problems posed by moderation that acts through ranking rather than deletion.
- Draws attention to the role of ML classifiers in sorting content into remove / reduce / leave-alone tiers.

## Methods

Conceptual and analytical essay grounded in critical platform governance scholarship. Gillespie examines how platforms operationalize degrees of harm through machine learning classifiers, and how those classifications feed into recommendation and ranking systems that determine whether content is removed, reduced, or left untouched. ## Findings

- Platforms increasingly rely on demotion and de-amplification as an alternative to outright removal.
- ML classifiers sort content into categories that map onto differing moderation responses.
- Reduction occupies a middle ground for content deemed problematic but not clearly violating removal-warranting rules.
- Because reduction is largely invisible to users, it evades the transparency and appeal norms that have developed around removal.
