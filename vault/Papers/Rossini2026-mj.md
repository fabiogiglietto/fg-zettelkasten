# Disrupting democracy: how informational pathways and misinformation shape trust in election results

> Rossini, P., Mont’Alverne, C., & Kalogeropoulos, A. (2026). Disrupting democracy: how informational pathways and misinformation shape trust in election results. *Journal of Elections Public Opinion and Parties*, 1–23. https://doi.org/10.1080/17457289.2026.2701896
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/17457289.2026.2701896)

## Summary

This paper investigates how different informational pathways and belief in electoral misinformation shaped Brazilians' trust in the results of the 2022 Presidential Election — a contest in which incumbent Jair Bolsonaro waged a sustained disinformation campaign against the voting system. Using a three-wave panel survey conducted around the two voting rounds and results certification, the authors find that mainstream news use bolsters electoral trust over time (even among Bolsonaro voters), while belief in electoral misinformation and voting for Bolsonaro predict lower trust. The central argument reframes disinformation as a *strategic elite practice* embedded in evolving media systems rather than isolated false content, and positions professional journalism as a source of democratic resilience against elite-driven erosion of electoral legitimacy.

## Key Contributions

- First over-time (panel) evidence on how informational pathways and misinformation beliefs shape electoral trust in a major Global South, multi-party democracy, extending a largely US-centric literature.
- Demonstrates the stabilizing role of mainstream journalism as a counterweight to elite disinformation — even among electoral losers.
- Disaggregates messaging-app use into news consumption versus discussion across strong-, weak-, and no-tie networks, revealing heterogeneous effects on trust.
- Reframes disinformation as a strategic elite practice embedded in evolving media systems, informing theories of democratic backsliding and resilience.

## Methods

An original three-wave panel survey of Brazilian internet users (Wave 1 = 1,600; Wave 2 = 1,328; Wave 3 = 1,034), fielded in October, November, and December 2022 around the two voting rounds and certification. Participants were recruited via opt-in panels (Ipec Inteligência) with quotas for age, gender, social class, and region. The dependent variable was a single-item 4-point measure of trust in election results (adapted from the World Values Survey). Predictors included mainstream news use, social media news use, WhatsApp news use, WhatsApp/Telegram group talk across strong-, weak-, and no-tie networks, and an electoral "misinformed score" (0–8) drawn from fact-checked statements (measured in Wave 2). The authors used random effects within-between models to separate within-person change from between-person differences, with a Bolsonaro-voter subset analysis and Bonferroni correction.

## Findings

- Belief in electoral misinformation was negatively and significantly associated with electoral trust (between-person, β = −.157).
- Increases in mainstream news use predicted increases in trust over time (within-person, β = .069).
- Social media news use had null effects; messaging news use showed no over-time effect but a negative between-person association (β = −.129).
- Voting for Bolsonaro strongly predicted lower trust (β = −.946); right-wing ideology remained negatively associated even controlling for vote choice.
- WhatsApp group discussion effects depended on network composition: weak-tie weakly positive, no-tie (stranger) negative, strong-tie non-significant in the full sample.
- Among Bolsonaro voters, mainstream news still raised trust, but messaging news use and no-tie group talk lowered trust over time, while strong-tie group talk raised it (β = .165).
