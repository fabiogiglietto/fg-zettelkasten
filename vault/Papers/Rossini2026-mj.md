# Disrupting democracy: how informational pathways and misinformation shape trust in election results

> Rossini, P., Mont'Alverne, C., & Kalogeropoulos, A. (2026). Disrupting democracy: how informational pathways and misinformation shape trust in election results. *Journal of Elections, Public Opinion and Parties*, 1–23. https://doi.org/10.1080/17457289.2026.2701896
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/17457289.2026.2701896)

## Summary

This paper investigates how different informational pathways and belief in electoral misinformation shaped Brazilian citizens' trust in the results of the 2022 Presidential Election — a contest defined by incumbent Jair Bolsonaro's systematic disinformation campaign against the voting system. Using a three-wave panel survey spanning both voting rounds and results certification, the authors argue that mainstream news consumption acts as a stabilizing force for electoral trust (even among Bolsonaro voters), while belief in electoral misinformation and having voted for Bolsonaro predict markedly lower trust. The central reframing is that disinformation during democratic backsliding should be understood as a strategic elite practice embedded in evolving, fragmented media systems rather than as discrete false claims. The study deliberately moves beyond the US-centric literature by examining a Global South, multi-party, weak-partisanship context.

## Key Contributions

- First over-time (panel) evidence on how informational pathways and misinformation beliefs shape electoral trust in a major Global South, multi-party democracy.
- Demonstrates professional/mainstream journalism as a source of democratic resilience, bolstering trust even among electoral losers.
- Disaggregates messaging-app use into news consumption versus group discussion across strong-, weak-, and no-tie networks, revealing heterogeneous effects.
- Reframes disinformation as an elite strategic practice embedded in media systems, contributing to theories of democratic backsliding and resilience.

## Methods

An original three-wave panel survey of Brazilian internet users (W1 = 1,600; W2 = 1,328; W3 = 1,034) was fielded in October, November, and December 2022, recruited via opt-in panels with quotas for age, gender, social class, and region. The dependent variable was a single-item 4-point measure of trust in the election results (adapted from the World Values Survey). Predictors included scales for mainstream news, social media news, and WhatsApp news use; WhatsApp/Telegram group discussion with strong-, weak-, and no-tie networks; and an electoral "misinformed score" (0–8) based on fact-checked statements (measured in Wave 2). The analysis used random effects within-between models to separate within-person change from between-person differences, with a Bolsonaro-voter subset analysis and Bonferroni correction.

## Findings

- Belief in electoral misinformation was negatively associated with electoral trust (between-person β = −.157).
- Increases in mainstream news use predicted over-time increases in trust (within-person β = .069).
- Social media news use had null effects; messaging news use had no over-time effect but a negative between-person association (β = −.129).
- Voting for Bolsonaro strongly predicted lower trust (β = −.946); right-wing ideology remained negatively associated even controlling for vote.
- WhatsApp group discussion effects depended on network composition: weak-tie weakly positive, no-tie (stranger) negative, strong-tie non-significant in the full sample.
- Among Bolsonaro voters: mainstream news still raised trust, messaging news use lowered trust over time (β = −.069), no-tie talk lowered trust, and strong-tie talk raised trust (β = .165).
