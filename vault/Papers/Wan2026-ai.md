# How does political efficacy condition clicks on politics? Understanding information-selection behavior in algorithmic feeds over time

> Wan, J., Araujo, T., Helberger, N., & de Vreese, C. (2026). How does political efficacy condition clicks on politics? Understanding information-selection behavior in algorithmic feeds over time. *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication*, *31*. https://doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmag022
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcmc/zmag022)

## Summary

This paper examines how **political efficacy** — a citizen's sense that they understand and can influence politics — shapes the way individuals engage with political content inside algorithmically curated news feeds. Combining survey measures with digital trace data from the Google Discover usage histories of 186 Dutch users, the authors move beyond static snapshots of algorithmic exposure to distinguish *within-person* change over time from *between-person* differences. The central argument is that information selection in algorithmic feeds is not purely a product of the algorithm; user-level psychological dispositions like political efficacy help condition which political content people click on and how diverse their exposure becomes.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces **political efficacy** as an explanatory factor for information-selection behavior in algorithmic feeds, importing a construct from political communication scholarship into algorithmic-exposure research.
- Integrates self-report survey data with behavioral trace data from a real, fully algorithmic feed (Google Discover), rather than relying on lab settings or platform-supplied logs.
- Analytically separates *within-person* temporal dynamics from *between-person* differences in political information selection.

## Methods

- Combined survey data with digital trace data (usage histories) from **186 Dutch individuals**.
- Studied **Google Discover**, a fully algorithmically curated feed, as the empirical site.
- Modeled within-person changes in selection behavior over time alongside between-person differences conditioned by political efficacy.
- Measured multiple dimensions of engagement: the proportion of political content selected and the diversity of exposure.

## Findings

- Political efficacy conditions **between-person differences** in how much political information users select from algorithmic feeds.
- Information-selection behavior varies both **within individuals over time** and **between individuals**.
- Reported outcomes concern the proportion of political information selected and exposure diversity; the available abstract is truncated and does not detail effect directions or magnitudes.

## Connections

This work sits at the intersection of algorithmic curation and selective exposure, complementing large-scale platform studies of feed-driven exposure such as [[Guess2023-ai]], [[Guess2023-ur]], [[Gonzalez-Bailon2023-uy]], and [[Nyhan2023-gb]], but distinguishes itself by centering a user-level disposition rather than the algorithm alone. Its concern with the diversity of political exposure links it to classic filter-bubble and cross-cutting-exposure debates in [[Bakshy2015-rn]], [[Flaxman2016-lm]], and [[Messing2014-jc]]. The framing overlaps with de Vreese's own broader agenda on algorithmic news in [[de-Vreese2026-zx]].
