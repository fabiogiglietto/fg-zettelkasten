# Three Consequences of Big Data on the Practices and Scholarships of Political Communication

> Giglietto, F. (2020). Three Consequences of Big Data on the Practices and Scholarships of Political Communication. *COMUNICAZIONE POLITICA*. https://doi.org/10.3270/96423
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.3270/96423)

## Summary

This conceptual essay argues that big data and pervasive connected technologies have reshaped political communication along three interlocking vectors: the blurring of political organizational boundaries through networked publics, the rise of micro-targeted political advertising, and new dynamics of strategic attention and amplification within a hybrid media system. Giglietto contends that each vector generates distinct methodological and conceptual difficulties for researchers — particularly around measuring exposure, accessing platform data, and distinguishing official from unofficial political actors — and that these vectors interact to produce a state of "permanent potential influence" that eludes existing regulatory and analytical frameworks.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes an integrative three-vector framework (networked publics, micro-targeting, attention/amplification) for analyzing how big data restructures political communication.
- Diagnoses specific methodological problems for the field: obsolete source lists, weakened exposure concepts, and inadequate platform APIs (notably Facebook's Ad Library).
- Bridges contemporary platform phenomena with classical communication theory (Lazarsfeld, Granovetter, Meyrowitz).
- Frames a policy tension between platform transparency initiatives and the persistence of unregulated fringe amplification.

## Methods

Conceptual/theoretical essay synthesizing prior literature. Uses illustrative cases (Cambridge Analytica, Facebook Ad Library, Twitter's political ad ban, Occupy, Indignados, Five Star Movement, Podemos) and a critical review of methodological shortcomings in existing political communication research.

## Findings

- Networked collaboration yields skewed contribution distributions — small dedicated cores plus many occasional participants — enabling loose coalitions to rival formal organizations.
- Unofficially affiliated fringe groups can support mainstream candidates while granting plausible deniability, undermining analyses tied to official channels.
- Facebook's Ad Library, despite its scale (5M+ ads, ~$900M in U.S. spend from May 2018), lacks the targeting granularity needed to assess exposure.
- Social media's flattened display format weakens outlet brand cues, making self-reported media exposure unreliable.
- Engagement metrics (likes, shares, retweets) are only weak proxies for actual attention or exposure.
- Micro-targeting enables contradictory messaging to narrow audiences, though evidence of strong persuasive effects remains thin.
