# From the Wild West to the Walled Garden

> Giglietto, F., & Puschmann, C. (2026). From the Wild West to the Walled Garden. *M/C Journal*. https://doi.org/10.5204/mcj.3257
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.5204/mcj.3257)

## Summary

This article offers a historical-analytical account of how academic access to Twitter/X data has evolved over roughly two decades, situating that trajectory within broader cross-platform trends. Giglietto and Puschmann periodize this history into three phases: an open "Wild West" API era (2006–2020), a brief "Golden Age" anchored by the Academic API (2020–2023), and the post-Musk commercial paywall era (2023–present). Against this backdrop, they examine how the EU's Digital Services Act—particularly Article 40—attempts to reframe data access as a regulatory right rather than discretionary platform philanthropy. Their central argument is that progress toward research transparency is non-linear and fragile, contingent on a contested negotiation between platforms, regulators, and researchers, and that independent platform research should be treated as a public good sustained by institutional infrastructure rather than corporate generosity.

## Key Contributions

- A synthesized two-decade periodization of Twitter/X data access useful for platform governance and digital methods scholarship.
- Generalization of the Twitter/X case into a structural cross-platform pattern (Meta's CrowdTangle closure, Reddit's API pricing, TikTok's restrictions).
- An early empirical assessment of DSA Article 40 implementation challenges, drawing on EDMO findings and enforcement actions.
- Concrete forward-looking proposals: academic consortia for collective bargaining, public investment in data intermediaries/trusts, multi-method resilience, and extension of DSA-like frameworks beyond the EU.
- Reframing of platform research access as a public good requiring durable institutional support.

## Methods

The paper uses a historical-analytical review of social media data-access policies, organized as a case study of Twitter/X with comparative reference to Meta, Reddit, TikTok, Yahoo Webscope, AOL, and Microsoft Research. The authors structure the history into three periods and analyze regulatory frameworks—especially DSA Article 40—drawing on EDMO reports and early enforcement proceedings.

## Findings

- Early "data philanthropy" (Facebook's emotional-contagion and 61-million-person experiments, Twitter Data Grants, Yahoo Webscope) was opaque and relationship-based, exemplifying "independence by permission."
- The 2014 Data Grants and 2020 Academic API briefly offered sanctioned, merit-based access hailed as best practice.
- Musk's 2023 closure of the Academic API and disbanding of policy teams returned access to a financially determined regime.
- DSA implementation is highly uneven: response times from 2 to 100+ days, boilerplate denials, conflation of access tracks, and whitelisting-for-scraping offered in lieu of structured data.
- The European Commission has opened proceedings against X, TikTok, and Meta; preliminary 2025 findings found TikTok and Meta in breach of Article 40.12.
- The DSA's EU-only reach creates a two-tier global research system, pushing researchers toward limited alternatives like data donation, browser-based collection, and scraping—deepening a "data abyss" for Global South and early-career scholars.
