# Selective Exposure in the Age of Social Media: Endorsements Trump Partisan Source Affiliation When Selecting News Online

> Messing, S., & Westwood, S. J. (2014). Selective Exposure in the Age of Social Media: Endorsements Trump Partisan Source Affiliation When Selecting News Online. *Communic. Res.*, *41*, 1042–1063.
>
> [View paper](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=Selective%20Exposure%20in%20the%20Age%20of%20Social%20Media%3A%20Endorsements%20Trump%20Partisan%20Source%20Affiliation%20When%20Selecting%20News%20Online)

## Summary

This paper challenges the conventional wisdom that the internet inevitably deepens media fragmentation and partisan selective exposure. Messing and Westwood argue that social media fundamentally alters how people encounter news: rather than confronting bare partisan source labels, readers meet stories embedded in a social context, recommended by a politically heterogeneous network of contacts. In this environment, social endorsement cues become more salient than the partisan identity of the outlet. Extending existing models of news selectivity, the authors show that social recommendations "trump" partisan source affiliation when people choose what to read online — suggesting that social media dynamics may actually moderate, rather than amplify, selective exposure.

## Key Contributions

- Reframes debates about internet-driven polarization by centering social media's distinctive influence on news selection.
- Extends theoretical models of news selectivity to incorporate social endorsement cues alongside partisan source cues.
- Provides evidence that social recommendation mechanisms may counteract partisan selective exposure rather than reinforce it.

## Methods

The paper builds on and extends established theoretical models of news selectivity and selective exposure, then empirically investigates the competing weight of social endorsements versus partisan source affiliation in online news selection. The design pits social recommendation cues against partisan source labels to observe which more strongly governs readers' choices.

## Findings

- Endorsements from other users outweigh ("trump") the partisan affiliation of the source in driving news selection online.
- The influence of a source's partisan label diminishes when social recommendations accompany a story.
- The social context of consumption emphasizes social value over partisan identity, with potential to mitigate selective exposure.

## Connections

This paper sits alongside [[Bakshy2015-rn]], which similarly examines how social media networks and algorithmic exposure shape ideological diversity in news, and both push back against strong echo-chamber narratives echoed in work like [[Flaxman2016-lm]] and [[Barbera2015-je]]. Its optimistic framing about social cues moderating partisanship contrasts with accounts of affective polarization and hostile partisan sorting in [[Iyengar2019-jj]] and [[Bail2018-fk]], and it complements broader assessments of social media's political effects such as [[Gonzalez-Bailon2023-uy]] and [[Nyhan2023-gb]].
