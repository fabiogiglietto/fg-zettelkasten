# Facebook reactions in the context of politics and social issues: a systematic literature review

> Anwar, S., & Giglietto, F. (2024). Facebook reactions in the context of politics and social issues: a systematic literature review. *Frontiers in Sociology*. https://doi.org/10.3389/fsoc.2024.1379265
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.3389/fsoc.2024.1379265)

## Summary

This systematic literature review synthesizes 64 peer-reviewed studies (2016–2023) on Facebook's Reactions feature (Love, Haha, Wow, Sad, Angry), introduced in 2016 as an expansion of the Like button. Anwar and Giglietto organize the fragmented, interdisciplinary literature into three thematic clusters — the introduction of Reactions, politics and far-right groups, and other social issues (psychological, healthcare, business, miscellaneous) — and conduct a meta-analysis of patterns across them. Their central argument is that Reactions function as paralinguistic digital affordances that provide low-cost but nuanced affective signals, and that these signals reveal a systematic divergence between lifestyle content (which draws predominantly positive reactions) and sociopolitical content (which draws a broader, more negative emotional spectrum, with anger consistently amplifying engagement — especially for populist and far-right actors).

## Key Contributions

- First comprehensive systematic synthesis of Facebook Reactions research spanning 2016–2023 across political, social, health, and business domains.
- A thematic taxonomy that organizes an otherwise fragmented literature into coherent categories.
- Identification of cross-study regularities, especially the role of negative emotion — particularly anger — in driving engagement with political and populist content.
- Diagnosis of methodological gaps: absence of unified data sources, limited cross-cultural work, and underexploration of event-based and tabloid content.
- A forward-looking research agenda proposing LLM-based categorization of reaction patterns and applications in misinformation, fact-checking, and digital activism studies.

## Methods

The authors follow Briner and Denyer's (2012) five-step systematic review protocol: formulating research questions, locating studies, selecting and evaluating them, analyzing findings, and reporting. Database searches in Scopus, Google Scholar, and other engines were conducted monthly from July–September 2023 using combinations of "Facebook" and "Reaction" with Boolean operators, restricted to peer-reviewed journal articles and conference proceedings published 2016–2023. The resulting 64 articles were thematically coded via Armstrong's (2010) comparative naturalistic method and grouped into three main categories with sub-topics covering political news, populism, healthcare, business, and emotion detection.

## Findings

- Distribution of the corpus: 6 articles on the introduction of Reactions, 19 on politics and the far right, and 39 on other social issues.
- Negative emotional appeals — particularly anger — generate significantly more engagement and sharing than positive content in political campaigns (e.g., Mexico, Brexit, EU Parliament).
- Populist and far-right leaders elicit disproportionately more Angry and Haha reactions than mainstream politicians, with anger-triggering content boosting shares.
- Hyperpartisan and junk news show a bivalent affective logic: Love and Anger rarely co-occur, while Anger tends to co-occur with Sad and Wow.
- Reactions have been used computationally to predict emotion classes, controversy (via entropy of reaction distributions), and sentiment, though reaction-to-emotion mappings remain ambiguous — Haha and Wow can carry negative or ironic meaning in political contexts.
- Reactions serve as useful sentiment labels for low-resource languages (e.g., Sinhala, Bangla).
- Cultural context shapes reaction patterns, illustrated by comparisons of China-critical, China-supporting, and neutral media audiences in Hong Kong.
- Lifestyle and entertainment posts attract predominantly positive reactions; sociopolitical posts attract a wider, more negative spectrum.
