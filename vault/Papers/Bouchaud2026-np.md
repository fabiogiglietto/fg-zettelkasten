# Community Notes undermoderate polarizing content by design creating risks in electoral processes

> Bouchaud, P., & Ramaciotti, P. (2026). Community Notes undermoderate polarizing content by design creating risks in electoral processes. *Science Advances*, *12*. https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee6932
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aee6932)

## Summary

This paper conducts a large-scale empirical audit of X's Community Notes, the crowdsourced moderation system that relies on a bridging-based algorithm privileging notes that attract cross-partisan agreement. Drawing on the full corpus of ~1.9 million notes and ~135 million ratings through March 2025, cross-referenced with ideological scaling data across 13 countries, the authors argue that the system's core design principle — requiring cross-ideological consensus to surface a note as helpful — structurally undermoderates precisely the polarizing content most consequential for democratic discourse. They frame this not as an implementation gap but as a built-in feature of bridging moderation, with escalating risks as the system is deployed globally in electoral contexts.

## Key Contributions

- First large-scale, cross-national empirical audit of Community Notes spanning 13 countries with differing polarization structures.
- Identifies a structural — not incidental — limitation of bridging-based moderation algorithms in handling polarizing and electorally sensitive material.
- Contributes evidence to platform governance and electoral integrity debates as bridging systems scale beyond the US context.

## Methods

- Analysis of the complete corpus of ~1.9M Community Notes and ~135M ratings through March 2025.
- Cross-referencing note authors and raters with ideological scaling datasets covering 13 countries.
- Comparative evaluation of the bridging algorithm's surfacing behavior across national contexts with varying polarization profiles.

## Findings

- Notes addressing polarizing content are systematically less likely to be surfaced as "helpful" because they fail to attract cross-partisan agreement.
- Moderation coverage and effectiveness vary substantially across the 13 countries, tracking each country's polarization structure.
- The undermoderation of polarizing material follows directly from the algorithm's design premise rather than from data sparsity or rater behavior alone.
