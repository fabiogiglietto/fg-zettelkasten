# Exposure to untrustworthy websites in the 2016 US election

> Guess, A. M., Nyhan, B., & Reifler, J. (2020). Exposure to untrustworthy websites in the 2016 US election. *Nature Human Behaviour*, *4*, 472–480. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-0833-x
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-0833-x)

## Summary

This paper provides an empirical account of who actually consumed political misinformation online during the 2016 US presidential campaign, moving debates over "fake news" and echo chambers from speculation toward direct behavioral measurement. By linking survey responses to individual-level web-traffic data, the authors characterize the volume and partisan slant of exposure to untrustworthy websites and examine the roles of social media referrals and fact checking. The central finding is that exposure was unevenly distributed across the electorate and concentrated among Donald Trump's supporters.

## Key Contributions

- Replaces commentary-driven assumptions about echo chambers with empirical measurement of actual online misinformation consumption.
- Combines behavioral web-traffic data with survey data to identify *who* consumes fake news at the individual level.
- Advances understanding of how social media and fact checking shape exposure to factually dubious content.

## Methods

- Analysis pairing survey data with individual-level web-browsing data collected during the 2016 campaign.
- Operationalizes "fake news" as exposure to websites that publish factually dubious content.
- Examines social media referrals and fact-checking behavior as drivers of exposure patterns.

## Findings

- Trump supporters were disproportionately exposed to untrustworthy websites during the campaign.
- Exposure to political misinformation was concentrated among specific segments of the electorate rather than evenly spread.
- The study also assesses the effects of social media and fact checking on exposure and downstream behavior (though these details are truncated in the available abstract).

## Connections

This work is a foundational behavioral-data study of fake-news consumption that sits alongside related audience and exposure measurements such as [[Grinberg2019-ua]], [[Guess2019-ym]], and [[Allcott2017-yz]], and connects to broader debates about selective exposure and echo chambers explored in [[Flaxman2016-lm]] and [[Del-Vicario2016-uj]]. It also complements later work by the same authors on misinformation exposure and correction, including [[Guess2021-ym]], [[Guess2023-ai]], and [[Nyhan2023-gb]], and relates to assessments of the overall prevalence and impact of misinformation such as [[Allen2020-nj]] and [[Budak2024-ef]].
