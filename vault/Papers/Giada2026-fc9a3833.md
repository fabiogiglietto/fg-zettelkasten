# Exploring the Zombie Internet: Anatomy of Three Deceptive Information Operations on Facebook

> Giada, M., Fabio, G., Anwesha, C., Massimo, T., & Samuel, O. (2026). Exploring the Zombie Internet: Anatomy of Three Deceptive Information Operations on Facebook. *MEDIASCAPES JOURNAL*.
>
> [View paper](https://ora.uniurb.it/handle/11576/2779031)

## Summary

This paper introduces "Deceptive Information Operations" (DIOs) as a new analytical framework for understanding online manipulation on Facebook, arguing that existing approaches—false-news taxonomies, deception theory, strategic information operations, and Meta's own Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour (CIB) category—each capture only one facet of contemporary manipulative activity and are individually inadequate. Through three empirically distinct case studies (a pro-Putin propaganda network, a coordinated gambling promotion campaign, and a cluster of poorly moderated groups distributing adult content and fraudulent links), the authors show that despite differing actors, harms, and motives, these operations share a common structural logic built on deceptive content, exploitation of platform affordances, and coordinated participatory behaviour. The paper's broader claim is that platforms like Facebook constitute a "Zombie Internet"—an engagement-optimized ecosystem where the line between authentic and automated, genuine and manipulated activity dissolves—and that governance frameworks fixated on content falsity or narrow coordination-plus-inauthenticity definitions systematically fail to address this condition.

## Key Contributions

- Proposes DIOs as a unifying analytical framework synthesising deception theory, strategic information operations, and coordinated inauthentic behaviour research.
- Demonstrates empirically that structurally similar operational logics underlie disparate cases pursuing epistemic, economic, and social-normative harms, making motive analytically secondary to structure.
- Identifies specific gaps in platform enforcement (Meta's CIB/CSH categories) through direct evidence of failed moderation and unresponsive reporting.
- Provides a reusable codebook and an automated, adaptive detection workflow (built on CooRTweet) for monitoring coordinated deceptive activity at scale via the Meta Content Library.
- Empirically operationalises the journalistic "Zombie Internet" concept as a systemic condition of engagement-driven platform design.

## Methods

The authors used purposive case selection to draw three cases from 17 networks flagged by the vera.ai monitoring system, chosen for diversity in actor type, coordination mechanism, and harm category. Detection relied on an automated workflow (VeraAI Alert System using CooRTweet) seeded from fact-checker-flagged accounts and a large corpus of Meta-flagged false pages. Data were gathered via the Meta Content Library—one of the first large-scale academic uses of this tool—covering account metadata and top posts per network over an eleven-month window. Coding followed a constructivist grounded theory approach, combining theoretically motivated dimensions (identity management, coordination, content manipulation) with inductively derived categories, supported by cross-coding for reliability.

## Findings

- The pro-Putin network (27 groups) used synchronized renaming, AI-generated heroic imagery, and rapid coordinated sharing (e.g., a meme reaching 1.1M views across six groups within six minutes) blended with benign content to mask intent.
- The gambling network (260 groups) showed heavy automation and generative AI use, anomalous engagement metrics indicating synthetic activity, and brandjacking of gambling platform names, with most top posts later removed.
- The unmoderated groups network (222 groups) disguised adult content and scam links through repeated renaming, fake activity-status links, and unrelated distractor content, including a viral Coca-Cola hoax reaching 3.5 million views.
- Across cases, all three networks concealed intent behind benign facades and exploited engagement-driven ranking, though they differed in the nature of user engagement (genuine, synthetic, or inadvertent amplification).
- Facebook's enforcement mechanisms failed consistently to detect or act on these operations despite direct researcher reporting, exposing structural blind spots in current platform governance.
