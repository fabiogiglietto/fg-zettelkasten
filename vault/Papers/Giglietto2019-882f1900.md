# Diverging patterns of interaction around news on social media: insularity and partisanship during the 2018 Italian election campaign

> Giglietto, F., Valeriani, A., Righetti, N., & Marino, G. (2019). Diverging patterns of interaction around news on social media: insularity and partisanship during the 2018 Italian election campaign. *Information, Communication & Society*. https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2019.1629692
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2019.1629692)

## Summary

This paper investigates how partisan communities interact with political news on social media during the 2018 Italian general election campaign, offering a non-US, multiparty vantage point on debates over selective exposure and echo chambers. Combining Twitter data (to estimate the partisan attention and "insularity" of news sources) with Facebook engagement metrics (comments, shares, reactions) across 84,815 political news stories, the authors show that sources favored by supporters of populist parties—the Five Star Movement (M5S) and the League—are more insular, and that insularity shapes *how* audiences engage: insular sources are amplified via shares, while cross-partisan sources become arenas of comment-based contestation. M5S emerges as uniquely effective at strategically shaping the social media attention economy, a capacity the authors attribute to the fusion of populist ideology with long-term investment in digital infrastructure (the "anti-elitist cyber party").

## Key Contributions

- Extends the Media Partisanship Attention Score (MPAS) from a two-party (US) design into a Multi-Party MPAS (MP-MPAS) suited to multiparty systems.
- Introduces a novel "insularity" index for news sources, combining maximum partisan attention with the Gini coefficient of a source's partisan-attention distribution.
- Demonstrates a triangulation strategy using Twitter for partisan attention and Facebook for engagement, working within contemporary data-access constraints.
- Provides empirical evidence from a polarized-pluralist multiparty context, addressing the US-centric bias of the echo-chamber literature.
- Nuances the echo-chamber thesis: insular sources drive amplification while cross-partisan sources host contestation.
- Identifies a distinctive "cyber party" effect for M5S linking populism to attention-economy tactics.

## Methods

The authors estimated user partisanship from ~216,765 retweets of official party/leader Twitter accounts (January 2018), then followed the top 5,000 partisan contributors via DMI-TCAT, collecting ~4.4 million tweets and ~130 million resolved URLs (Feb–Mar 2018). Sources were scored with MP-MPAS and ranked by the insularity index, classifying 634 sources into four tiers. A separate Facebook engagement dataset of 84,815 stories from 4,113 domains (Sep 2017–Mar 2018) was built via Huginn (harvesting from Google News, GDELT, Twitter) and the Facebook Graph API polled every two hours for a week per story. Manual sentiment coding (toward M5S, PD, League, Forza Italia) was applied to the top decile of highly engaged stories by three coders (Krippendorff's alpha 0.72–0.84). Statistical tests included Kruskal-Wallis, Dunn's post-hoc with Bonferroni correction, and Spearman correlation.

## Findings

- Median insularity: M5S = 0.73, League = 0.67, LeU = 0.63, PD = 0.63 (system mean 0.65); M5S significantly higher than all others (p < .001).
- Of 634 adjudicated sources, 33.9% attributed to the League, 27.9% to M5S, 19.1% cross-partisan, 9.5% to PD, 7.7% to LeU; Forza Italia and FdI received almost none.
- Insularity correlates negatively with the comments/shares ratio (Spearman rho = −.31, p < .001): more insular → more shares per comment; less insular → more comments per share.
- For M5S, positive stories received more shares per comment and negative stories more comments per share (p < .001).
- For PD, Forza Italia, and the League, the opposite pattern held.
- Cross-partisan, low-insularity sources functioned as arenas for comment-based contestation rather than amplification.
