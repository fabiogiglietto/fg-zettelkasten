# From the fringes into mainstream politics: intermediary networks and movement-party coordination of a global anti-immigration campaign in Germany

> Klinger, U., Bennett, W. L., Knüpfer, C. B., Martini, F., & Zhang, X. (2023). From the fringes into mainstream politics: intermediary networks and movement-party coordination of a global anti-immigration campaign in Germany. *Information, Communication & Society*, *26*, 1890–1907. https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118x.2022.2050415
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118x.2022.2050415)

## Summary

This paper traces how extremist political framing migrates from fringe movements into mainstream party politics through aligned intermediary networks that obscure the content's radical origins. Focusing on the German anti-immigration campaign "Migrationspakt Stoppen" opposing the 2018 UN Global Compact for Migration, the authors show how a campaign originated by the small extremist Identitarian Movement spread through low-level AfD activists, superspreader accounts, and far-right media sites before the AfD party leadership adopted it as official policy — eventually claiming sole ownership. The central argument is that such intermediary networks act as "source-laundering" mechanisms, allowing a radical-right party to take up ideas it could not openly endorse, and pushing extremist frames into mainstream media and public discourse without any evidence of formal coordination.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces an empirical framework for tracing how extremist ideas move into mainstream politics via aligned intermediary networks, extending the concept of "information laundering" to movement-party dynamics.
- Demonstrates a method combining multi-platform data collection with dynamic network and time-series analysis to detect network "handoffs" absent formal coordination.
- Advances theory on networked framing, networked agenda-setting, network alignment, and superspreaders in hybrid media systems.
- Offers a generalizable case relevant to other movement-party interactions (e.g., Trump/MAGA/QAnon, Le Pen and the Gilets Jaunes, AfD and PEGIDA).
- Highlights the regulatory and democratic challenges posed by dispersed, networked spread of extremist content.

## Methods

The authors set up a multi-platform monitoring system of the German far-right media ecology (11 July–15 December 2018). They conducted dynamic network analysis (Python, Gephi, ForceAtlas2) of retweet networks with nodes sized by indegree and colored by actor type, plus time-series analysis of tweet/retweet volumes. Data spanned YouTube (1,606 videos, 681 channels via `tuber`), Twitter (506,079 tweets from 43,579 accounts via Crimson Hexagon), 21 far-right news sites (Media Cloud RNIS collections), six mainstream German outlets, and the AfD's own channels. Accounts were manually and semi-automatically coded into actor types (mass media, far-right news, Identitarian, low- and high-level AfD, top-100 superspreaders), and bot detection via Botometer found automation to be minimal (5.9% of accounts, 3.4% of tweets).

## Findings

- Before the Identitarian campaign launch (16 September 2018), activity around the Global Compact was only sporadic.
- After Martin Sellner's launch video, the @migrationspakt account became the most-retweeted in the network within a day, and intermediary activity spiked immediately.
- Superspreaders were just 0.27% of network nodes but accounted for ~21% of all interactions, both amplifying and bridging separate networks.
- Far-right news sites played a dual role — early intermediaries bridging extremists and party, later amplifiers of the party campaign.
- The AfD launched a mirror-image official campaign in early October and a parliamentary petition on 1 November (100,000+ signatures), then falsely claimed sole originator status.
- The overall network reached 23% of its final size during the October AfD launch and nearly doubled after the November petition.
- Mainstream media covered the issue after the petition but largely without the "stoppen" frame, focusing instead on CDU-CSU immigration conflict.
