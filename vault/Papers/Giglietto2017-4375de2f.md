# A Hashtag Worth a Thousand Words: Discursive Strategies Around #JeNeSuisPasCharlie After the 2015 Charlie Hebdo Shooting

> Giglietto, F., & Lee, Y. (2017). A Hashtag Worth a Thousand Words: Discursive Strategies Around #JeNeSuisPasCharlie After the 2015 Charlie Hebdo Shooting. *Social Media + Society*. https://doi.org/10.1177/2056305116686992
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/2056305116686992)

## Summary

This paper investigates how Twitter users deployed the counter-hashtag #JeNeSuisPasCharlie ("I am not Charlie") in the aftermath of the January 2015 Charlie Hebdo shooting in Paris. Against the backdrop of the overwhelming solidarity expressed through #JeSuisCharlie, the authors treat the dissenting hashtag as a case study in how minority voices carve out space for disagreement during a moment of near-unanimous public mourning. The central argument is that #JeNeSuisPasCharlie served as a site of nuance and dissent, where users articulated reservations about the dominant narrative without endorsing the violence they were responding to.

## Key Contributions

- Offers an empirical case study of counter-hashtag practices and dissenting expression during a high-salience, emotionally charged event.
- Illuminates how solidarity hashtags coexist with — and actively provoke — oppositional discursive formations.
- Contributes to debates about free speech, identity, and online public expression in the specific context of the Charlie Hebdo attack.

## Methods

The authors collect Twitter posts containing #JeNeSuisPasCharlie in the period following the shooting and conduct a discursive, qualitative analysis of tweet content. The focus is on identifying the rhetorical strategies through which users signalled dissent while distancing themselves from the perpetrators.

## Findings

- The counter-hashtag functioned as a vehicle for dissent and nuance rather than as endorsement of the attack.
- Users adopting the hashtag deployed distinct discursive strategies to voice disagreement with the dominant #JeSuisCharlie framing while explicitly condemning or avoiding association with the violence.
