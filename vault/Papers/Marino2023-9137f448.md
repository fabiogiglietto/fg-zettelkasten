# The power of Alternative Influence Networks (AIN) for spreading Covid-19 problematic information on Facebook during a year of pandemic

> Marino, F. G. G. (2023). The power of Alternative Influence Networks (AIN) for spreading Covid-19 problematic information on Facebook during a year of pandemic. https://doi.org/10.1445/106772
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1445/106772)

## Summary

This paper maps the Italian Alternative Influence Network (AIN) on Facebook during the first year of the Covid-19 pandemic, asking how alternative micro-celebrities and legacy media jointly amplified problematic pandemic information. Working from a dataset of 175,228 posts by previously identified covid-skeptic accounts, the authors extract mentioned public figures, cluster them, and analyze engagement signatures and content. They argue that an "Intellectual Dark Web" (IDW) subcluster produces disproportionately shareable content, and that legacy journalists and right-leaning newspapers — not fringe skeptic pages — are the primary amplifiers. A central mechanism is *remediation*: AIN actors recycle expert statements from TV talk shows and newspapers to lend credibility to covid-skeptic and conspiracy narratives while sidestepping fact-checking.

## Key Contributions

- Empirical mapping of the Italian AIN during Covid-19, identifying a distinct IDW subnetwork.
- Analytical demonstration that a comments/shares ratio can distinguish hyperpartisan amplification (share-heavy) from contested debate (comment-heavy).
- Extension of remediation theory to alternative media, showing how AIN actors repurpose legacy-media expert content to complicate fact-checking.
- Contribution to inter-media agenda-setting research by documenting the co-production of visibility across AIN, alternative outlets, and legacy media.
- A replicable mixed-methods workflow combining coordinated-behavior detection, named-entity extraction, clustering, engagement metrics, and stratified content analysis.

## Methods

The authors collected 175,228 Facebook posts (March 2020–March 2021) via CrowdTangle from covid-skeptic Italian accounts previously identified through coordinated link sharing analysis. Capitalized proper nouns were extracted across message, title, description, and image-text fields; the top 2,000 terms were reduced to 254 manually classified last names across five non-mutually-exclusive categories (Alternative Influencers, Doctors/Scientists/Experts, Journalists, Other Public Figures, Politicians). After filtering to 79 non-institutional figures, k-means clustering produced five groups. Engagement statistics and a comments/shares ratio (−1 to 1) were computed per cluster. Finally, deductive content analysis was applied to a stratified random sample of 150 posts drawn from the top 10% most-engaging content, stratified by cluster and ratio category.

## Findings

- Five clusters emerged: Politicians, Other Public Figures, Journalists, Doctors/Scientists/Experts, and IDW Influencers.
- IDW Influencers had the lowest comments/shares ratio (−0.28), signaling share-driven amplification; Doctors/Scientists/Experts also skewed toward shares (−0.21).
- Journalists produced the most contested content (ratio 0.16); Politicians were most prolific (9,879 posts) with moderate engagement.
- Despite low volume, IDW Influencers (1,649 posts) and Other Public Figures (1,958 posts) achieved the highest mean interactions per post (1,030 and 983).
- Top amplifiers of the most-engaging posts were mainstream-affiliated journalists (Nicola Porro, Gianluigi Paragone) and right-wing newspapers (La Verità, Libero) rather than fringe skeptic pages.
- Content themes varied by cluster: immigration and anti-government framings (Politicians), judicial scandals (Other Public Figures), covid-skeptic quotations of figures like Zangrillo or Tarro (Doctors/Scientists), and conspiracy narratives about microchips and global tyranny (IDW), often sourced from TV talk show clips.

## Connections

This paper builds directly on the authors' prior work on coordinated link sharing behavior and engagement metrics, connecting to [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] and [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]], from which the comments/shares ratio approach is drawn. Its focus on alternative influencers and hybrid amplification of Covid-19 misinformation resonates with [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]] on the Italian pandemic information environment, and its account of legacy media's role in propagating problematic content connects to broader work on mainstream amplification such as [[Budak2024-ef]] and [[Tornberg2025-ir]]. The remediation-based argument about how alternative actors exploit expert TV appearances also relates thematically to studies of alternative and hyperpartisan ecosystems like [[Frischlich2025-vn]] and [[Rieder2026-pp]].
