# The power of Alternative Influence Networks (AIN) for spreading Covid-19 problematic information on Facebook during a year of pandemic

> Marino, F. G. G. (2023). The power of Alternative Influence Networks (AIN) for spreading Covid-19 problematic information on Facebook during a year of pandemic. https://doi.org/10.1445/106772
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1445/106772)

## Summary

This paper maps how Alternative Influence Networks (AIN) amplified problematic Covid-19 information on Italian Facebook during the first pandemic year (March 2020–March 2021). Working from a corpus of 175,228 posts drawn from previously identified covid-skeptic accounts, the authors extract and classify the public figures mentioned, cluster them, and analyze engagement patterns and content. They argue that a distinct "Intellectual Dark Web" (IDW) subset of influencers produces highly shareable content, that legacy media outlets and journalists remain central conduits for these voices, and that **remediation** — recycling expert statements originally aired on TV and in newspapers — is a key mechanism for laundering and amplifying covid-skeptic and conspiracy narratives while evading fact-checking.

## Key Contributions

- Empirical mapping of the Italian AIN during Covid-19, identifying an IDW subnetwork with distinct engagement signatures.
- Demonstrates the analytical value of a **comments/shares ratio** metric for distinguishing hyperpartisan amplification (share-heavy) from contested debate (comment-heavy).
- Extends remediation theory to alternative media, showing how AIN actors leverage legacy-media-produced expert statements to amplify problematic information.
- Contributes to inter-media agenda-setting literature by documenting how AIN, alternative outlets, and legacy media co-produce news visibility in a hybrid system.
- Offers a replicable mixed-methods workflow: coordinated-behavior detection → named-entity extraction → clustering → engagement metrics → stratified content analysis.

## Methods

The authors collected 175,228 Facebook posts via CrowdTangle from a prior list of covid-skeptic Italian accounts (identified through coordinated link sharing analysis). They extracted capitalized proper nouns across message, title, description, and image-text fields, took the top 2,000 terms, and manually classified 254 last names into five non-mutually-exclusive categories (Alternative Influencers, Doctors/Scientists/Experts, Journalists, Other Public Figures, Politicians). After filtering out figures in apical public roles, 79 figures were clustered via k-means into five clusters. Engagement statistics and a comments/shares ratio (range −1 to 1) were computed per cluster, followed by deductive content analysis on a stratified random sample of 150 posts drawn from the top 10% most-engaging content.

## Findings

- Five clusters emerged: Politicians, Other Public Figures, Journalists, Doctors/Scientists/Experts, and IDW Influencers.
- The IDW Influencer cluster had the lowest comments/shares ratio (−0.28), signaling share-driven amplification; Doctors/Scientists/Experts also skewed toward shares (−0.21).
- Journalists' posts had the highest ratio (0.16), indicating contested/commented content; Politicians were the most prolific (9,879 posts) with moderate engagement.
- Despite low post volume, IDW Influencers (1,649 posts) and Other Public Figures (1,958 posts) achieved high mean interactions (1,030 and 983 respectively).
- Top amplifying pages were dominated by mainstream-affiliated journalists (Nicola Porro, Gianluigi Paragone) and right-wing newspapers (La Verità, Libero), with covid-skeptic micro-celebrities appearing further down.
- Recurring content themes: immigration and anti-government framings (political clusters), judicial scandals (Other Public Figures), covid-skepticism quoting figures like Zangrillo or Tarro (science cluster), and conspiracy narratives (microchips, global tyranny) often sourced from TV talk show clips (IDW cluster).
