# Fake news as a rhetorical weapon: Strategic delegitimization and selective amplification in Italian newspapers

> Mosca, L., & Paxton, F. (2026). Fake news as a rhetorical weapon: Strategic delegitimization and selective amplification in Italian newspapers. *European Journal of Communication*. https://doi.org/10.1177/02673231261458935
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1177/02673231261458935)

## Summary

This article reconceptualizes the phrase "fake news" not as a neutral descriptor of misinformation but as a rhetorical weapon wielded in political conflict. Mosca and Paxton propose the **Strategic Delegitimization and Selective Amplification (SDSA) model**, which captures a dual dynamic: political actors deploy fake news accusations to delegitimize opponents as part of negative campaigning, while media outlets unevenly amplify these accusations according to their political alignment or strategic interest. The study situates this argument empirically in the Italian newspaper context, positioning itself against dominant approaches that frame fake news primarily as an epistemic or information-quality problem.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces the **SDSA model** as a new conceptual tool for analyzing fake news accusations.
- Shifts the scholarly framing of "fake news" from a misinformation problem toward a strategic communicative practice.
- Bridges research on negative campaigning, political delegitimization, and media amplification.
- Applies the framework to the Italian media–political system as an empirical case.
