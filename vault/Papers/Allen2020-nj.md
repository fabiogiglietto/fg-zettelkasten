# Evaluating the fake news problem at the scale of the information ecosystem

> Allen, J., Howland, B., Mobius, M. M., Rothschild, D. M., & Watts, D. (2020). Evaluating the fake news problem at the scale of the information ecosystem. *Sci. Adv.*, *6*, eaay3539. https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3502581
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.3502581)

## Summary

This paper pushes back against the popular alarmist narrative that fake news is pervasive across the web and social media with catastrophic consequences for democracy. The authors argue that this conventional wisdom is an artifact of studying social media or individual platforms in isolation. When fake news consumption is instead measured against the *entire* information ecosystem — everything Americans watch, read, and click across their devices — it turns out to constitute only a tiny sliver of total media diet. The central methodological argument is that the magnitude of the problem cannot be properly assessed without situating it at ecosystem scale.

## Key Contributions

- Provides an ecosystem-scale, multimode measurement of fake news consumption rather than a platform-specific snapshot.
- Introduces a nationally representative, cross-device dataset (mobile, desktop, television) covering all content categories.
- Offers empirical grounding to recalibrate both public and scholarly understanding of how large the fake news problem actually is.

## Methods

- Analysis of a unique multimode dataset spanning mobile, desktop, and television consumption.
- Nationally representative sample of U.S. media consumption.
- Coverage of *all* media content categories, enabling comparison of fake news against the full media diet rather than measuring it against social media alone.

## Findings

- Fake news makes up only a small fraction of total media consumption when assessed at the scale of the whole information ecosystem.
- Claims of the pervasiveness of fake news are not supported once the full breadth of media consumption is accounted for (precise quantitative figures are not available from the abstract).

## Connections

This is a cornerstone of the "recalibration" literature that argues misinformation exposure is heavily concentrated and small in aggregate; it aligns closely with [[Allen2021-ai]] on the same ecosystem-scale framing and with [[Grinberg2019-ua]] and [[Guess2019-ym]] on the concentration of fake news exposure among small subsets of users. It also speaks to work weighing the real-world effects and prevalence of misinformation such as [[Budak2024-ef]], [[Guess2020-rr]], and [[Tsfati2020-uo]], and to studies like [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] that examine platform-level exposure dynamics.
