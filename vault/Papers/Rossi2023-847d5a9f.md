# Cracking Open the European Newsfeed

> Rossi, L., Giglietto, F., & Marino, G. (2023). Cracking Open the European Newsfeed. *Journal of Quantitative Description: Digital Media*. https://doi.org/10.51685/jqd.2023.020
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.51685/jqd.2023.020)

## Summary

This paper extends Guess et al.'s (2021) US-focused study of Facebook news quality to three major European countries — Germany, France, and Italy — over the 2019–2022 period. Drawing on Meta's URL Shares Dataset combined with NewsGuard domain trustworthiness ratings, the authors document a general decline in URL sharing on Facebook, a rising share of untrustworthy URLs (especially in electoral years in Germany and Italy), but relatively stable proportions of *Views* from untrustworthy sources. They further confirm in a European context the pattern that older users dominate both exposure to and sharing of untrustworthy content. Alongside the empirical results, the paper offers a methodological critique of the URL Shares Dataset and NewsGuard as tools for longitudinal, cross-national platform research.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic quantitative description of trustworthy vs. untrustworthy news circulation on Facebook in France, Germany, and Italy using Meta's privacy-protected URL Shares Dataset.
- Temporal and geographic extension of Guess et al. (2021) through 2022 and into EU comparative territory.
- Methodological critique of the URL Shares Dataset — especially its 100-share inclusion threshold — and of using NewsGuard ratings longitudinally, with explicit stability checks.
- Empirical documentation of age-skewed engagement with untrustworthy news in Europe, mirroring US findings.
- Framing of results as directly relevant to platform transparency obligations under the EU Digital Services Act.

## Methods

The authors replicate and adapt Guess et al.'s (2021) analytical pipeline (via Aslett's 2022 open code) to Meta's URL Shares Dataset v10.1, filtering by `top_country` for DE, FR, IT, and the US. Domains are classified as Trustworthy (≥60) or Untrustworthy (<60) using an October 2022 NewsGuard snapshot of 8,506 rated domains, applied retrospectively; a stability check finds only ~1–3% of domains change category across 2019–2022. NewsGuard coverage is benchmarked against the Lasser-Rupp list, Media Bias/Fact Check, and Grinberg et al. (2019). Confidence intervals account for the dataset's differential-privacy noise (σ=14 shares, σ=2,228 views). Views and Shares are disaggregated across six age cohorts.

## Findings

- France consistently has the highest URL volume, then Italy; Germany's is roughly half or less, consistent with much lower Facebook news use in Germany (17% vs 45% in Italy per Reuters 2022).
- Untrustworthy URL shares were similar (~13–14%) across the three countries in 2019, diverging sharply by 2022 (France 11%, Germany ~21%, Italy 22%), with peaks aligning with national election years.
- Absolute Views and Shares of untrustworthy URLs peaked in 2020 (notably in France and Italy) before declining alongside overall URL volume.
- The percentage of *Views* from untrustworthy sources remained stable or slightly declined despite rising percentages of untrustworthy URLs, suggesting platform-level dampening of exposure.
- Sharing to untrustworthy domains rose sharply in Germany (22% in 2021) and Italy (26% in 2022).
- Sharing of untrustworthy content skews older than exposure: 65+ dominates in France (28%), 55–64 in Germany (36%), 45–54 in Italy (33%); users under 34 play a marginal role.
- US data show a much tighter correlation between NewsGuard's share of untrustworthy domains and their URL-level prevalence than European data do, undermining direct US–EU comparability.

## Connections

This paper sits squarely in the lineage of large-scale URL-level Facebook studies of news quality and belongs alongside other work built on Meta's URL Shares Dataset and its methodological aftermath, such as [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] and the reassessment in [[Bak-Coleman2025-pm]]. Its age-cohort findings on older users' outsized role in untrustworthy sharing extend a well-established US pattern into Europe, connecting to broader misinformation-prevalence and exposure debates in [[Budak2024-ef]] and [[Allen2025-ot]]. On the platform-governance side, its framing as a stress test of privacy-protected research datasets under the DSA connects to work on data access and platform research infrastructures such as [[Rieder2025-ju]], [[Rieder2026-pp]], and [[Bruns2026-yv]].
