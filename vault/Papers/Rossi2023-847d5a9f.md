# Cracking Open the European Newsfeed

> Rossi, L., Giglietto, F., & Marino, G. (2023). Cracking Open the European Newsfeed. *Journal of Quantitative Description: Digital Media*. https://doi.org/10.51685/jqd.2023.020
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.51685/jqd.2023.020)

## Summary

This paper extends Guess et al.'s (2021) US-based study of news quality on Facebook to the European context, analyzing the prevalence, exposure, and sharing of trustworthy versus untrustworthy URLs in Germany, France, and Italy from 2019 to 2022. Drawing on Meta's privacy-protected URL Shares Dataset combined with NewsGuard domain trustworthiness ratings, the authors document a steady decline in total URLs shared, a rising proportion of untrustworthy URLs that tracks national electoral cycles, but relatively stable proportions of *Views* from untrustworthy sources — suggesting platform-level dampening of exposure. The study also confirms, in a European setting, that older age cohorts dominate both exposure to and sharing of untrustworthy content, while younger users play a marginal role. Methodologically, the paper is as much a critique of its own data infrastructure as an empirical description, cautioning against naive US–EU comparisons given NewsGuard's US-skewed coverage and the dataset's 100-share threshold.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic quantitative description of trustworthy vs. untrustworthy news circulation on Facebook across three major European countries using Meta's URL Shares Dataset.
- Temporal (through 2022) and geographic (to the EU) extension of Guess et al. (2021), enabling cross-national comparison *within* Europe.
- A methodological critique of the URL Shares Dataset (notably the 100-share threshold) and of using NewsGuard ratings longitudinally, including stability checks of ratings over 2019–2022.
- Documents age-based patterns of misinformation engagement in European contexts, extending US findings about older users' outsized role.
- Highlights implications for upcoming EU Digital Services Act transparency obligations and platform research access.

## Methods

The study replicates and extends Guess et al. (2021) using adapted public code (Aslett, 2022) applied to Meta's URL Shares Dataset v10.1 (Jan 2017 – Oct 2022), filtered by `top_country` for Germany, France, Italy, and the US. Domains were classified using an October 2022 NewsGuard snapshot (8,506 rated domains; Trustworthy ≥60, Untrustworthy <60), applied retrospectively. The authors assessed NewsGuard's representativeness against alternative lists (Lasser-Rupp, Media Bias/Fact Check, Grinberg et al. 2019) and checked rating stability over time (finding only ~1–3% of domains changed category). Confidence intervals were computed around Views and Shares to account for differential-privacy noise, and engagement was disaggregated across six age cohorts.

## Findings

- France consistently has the highest Facebook URL volume, followed by Italy; Germany's volume is roughly half or less, consistent with lower Facebook news adoption there.
- Untrustworthy URL shares were similar (~13–14%) across all three countries in 2019 but diverged sharply by 2022 (France 11%, Germany ~21%, Italy 22%), with peaks in national election years.
- Absolute Views and Shares of untrustworthy URLs peaked in 2020 before declining, mirroring the overall decline in URL volume.
- The *percentage* of Views from untrustworthy sources stayed stable or slightly declined despite the rising percentage of untrustworthy URLs — suggesting platform-level dampening of exposure.
- Sharing of untrustworthy content skews older than exposure: 65+ dominates sharing in France (28%), 55–64 in Germany (36%), and 45–54 in Italy (33%).
- US data show a stronger correlation between NewsGuard's share of untrustworthy domains and untrustworthy URLs than European data, motivating the EU-only comparative focus.

## Connections

This paper is an explicit European extension of [[Guess2021-ym]], and shares that work's finding on older users' outsized role in sharing untrustworthy content with the broader exposure-and-prevalence literature such as [[Grinberg2019-ua]] (whose domain list it uses as a benchmark) and [[Gonzalez-Bailon2023-uy]], which similarly uses privacy-protected Meta data to show that platform exposure to untrustworthy content is more concentrated and limited than headline fears suggest. Its emphasis on how a small share of circulation reaches only a modest share of audiences resonates with scale-of-misinformation arguments in [[Allen2020-nj]], while the authors' own prior Italian election work ([[Giglietto2019-882f1900]], [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]], [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]]) grounds the national electoral-cycle observations.
