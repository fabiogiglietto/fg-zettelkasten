# Cracking Open the European Newsfeed

> Rossi, L., Giglietto, F., & Marino, G. (2023). Cracking Open the European Newsfeed. *Journal of Quantitative Description: Digital Media*. https://doi.org/10.51685/jqd.2023.020
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.51685/jqd.2023.020)

## Summary

This paper replicates and extends Guess et al.'s (2021) US analysis of news quality on Facebook to three major European countries — Germany, France, and Italy — covering 2019–2022. Using Meta's URL Shares Dataset combined with NewsGuard domain trustworthiness ratings, the authors document declining overall URL volumes, a rising share of *untrustworthy* URLs in Germany and Italy (peaking in national election years), but a notably stable proportion of *Views* from untrustworthy sources. They confirm in a European setting that older users disproportionately drive both exposure to and sharing of untrustworthy content, while users under 34 play a marginal role. The paper doubles as a methodological critique of the URL Shares Dataset and its 100-share threshold, arguing that direct US–EU comparisons are unreliable.

## Key Contributions

- First systematic quantitative description of trustworthy vs. untrustworthy URL circulation on Facebook across Germany, France, and Italy using Meta's privacy-protected URL Shares Dataset.
- Temporal and geographic extension of Guess et al. (2021) through 2022 and into three European media systems, enabling intra-European comparison.
- Methodological audit of the URL Shares Dataset (100-share threshold, differential privacy noise) and of NewsGuard's longitudinal stability and source coverage.
- Empirical confirmation, in European contexts, of the age-skew in misinformation sharing previously observed in the US.
- Framing of these findings as a stress test for forthcoming EU Digital Services Act platform research-access obligations.

## Methods

The authors adapt the public Guess et al./Aslett (2022) replication code to URL Shares Dataset v10.1 (Jan 2017–Oct 2022), filtering by `top_country` for DE, FR, IT, and the US. Domains are classified as Trustworthy (NewsGuard ≥60) or Untrustworthy (<60) using an October 2022 snapshot of 8,506 rated domains, applied retrospectively after verifying that only ~1–3% of domains changed category across 2019–2022. NewsGuard coverage is benchmarked against the Lasser-Rupp list, Media Bias/Fact Check, and Grinberg et al. (2019). Confidence intervals account for differential privacy noise (σ=14 for Shares, σ=2,228 for Views), and Views/Shares are disaggregated across six age cohorts.

## Findings

- France has the highest URL volume on Facebook, followed by Italy; Germany is roughly half of France's volume, consistent with lower Facebook news adoption there.
- Untrustworthy URL shares converged around 13–14% across the three countries in 2019 but diverged sharply by 2022: France 11%, Germany ~21%, Italy 22%, with peaks aligning with national election years.
- Absolute Views and Shares of untrustworthy URLs peaked in 2020 and then declined alongside overall URL volume.
- The *percentage* of Views from untrustworthy sources remained stable or slightly declined even as the share of untrustworthy URLs rose, suggesting platform-level dampening of exposure relative to availability.
- Sharing skews older than exposure: 65+ dominates in France (28%), 55–64 in Germany (36%), 45–54 in Italy (33%); users under 34 contribute marginally.
- US data show a much stronger correlation between NewsGuard's untrustworthy-domain share and dataset URL composition than European data, undermining direct US–EU comparison.

## Connections

This paper sits squarely in the lineage of large-scale URL-Shares-Dataset analyses of news quality on Facebook, most directly extending [[Bakshy2015-rn]] and the broader debate over platform-mediated exposure to misinformation that runs through [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]], [[Budak2024-ef]], and [[Allen2025-ot]]. Its finding that older users disproportionately share untrustworthy content echoes a recurring empirical pattern visible in work such as [[Mosleh2024-op]] and [[Freelon2024-sc]]. The methodological reflections on industry data access and the DSA connect it to platform-governance discussions including [[Rieder2025-ju]], [[Rieder2026-pp]], and [[Ohme2026-nv]], while its substantive focus on European information environments resonates with [[Humprecht2025-ml]] and [[Cazzamatta2026-lo]].
