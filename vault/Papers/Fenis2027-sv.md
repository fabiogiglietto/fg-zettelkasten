# From polls to persistence: Examining coordinated political discourse outside the Philippine election period on Facebook

> Fenis, M. R. B., Agonos, M. J. M., & Figueroa, P. D. (2027). From polls to persistence: Examining coordinated political discourse outside the Philippine election period on Facebook. *Asian Politics & Policy*, *19*. https://doi.org/10.1111/aspp.70113
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/aspp.70113)

## Summary

This paper examines how coordinated political behavior on Facebook persists in the Philippines *outside* formal election periods, focusing on the first two years of the Marcos Jr. administration (June 2022–May 2024). Combining social network analysis with qualitative content analysis of cross-posted material, the authors argue that coordination is not an episodic campaign tactic but an ongoing, structural, and cumulative infrastructural process. They frame coordinated behavior as a **socio-cultural assemblage** that binds together platform affordances, coordinated actors, algorithmic systems, and partisan political culture to manufacture polarization and sustain the Philippines' dynastic, clientelistic, and personality-driven politics in digitally persistent form. Crucially, they contend that Facebook does not autonomously produce polarization; polarization emerges *relationally* through the interaction of these elements.

## Key Contributions

- Extends influence-operations research on Philippine elections into the under-studied non-election period, reframing coordinated behavior as a permanent structural feature rather than a campaign-bound event.
- Integrates affordance theory (Kakavand's six affordances) with Deleuze and Guattari's assemblage theory to theorize platforms as *relational actors* rather than deterministic or neutral forces.
- Operationalizes cross-posting as an empirical marker of sustained coordinated activity and manufactured engagement.
- Challenges technological-determinist accounts of platform power by situating polarization in relational dynamics among actors, affordances, and political culture.
- Connects digital coordination mechanisms to the perpetuation of dynastic, clientelistic, personality-driven politics across electoral cycles.

## Methods

A case study within a larger project on influence operations in Southeast Asia. The authors used Google Trends to identify three macro-discourses (elite political contest, foreign policy, the 2022 elections) that informed personality-centered keyword strings (e.g., "uniteam [bongbong marcos]", "martial law [bongbong marcos]"). Social network analysis of publicly available Facebook pages identified coordination not through fixed cross-post thresholds but via combined behavioral metrics — content duplication (identical links, text, media), statistically improbable temporal synchronization, and structural network ties indicating centralized orchestration. A three-person coding team then conducted qualitative content analysis of post types, elements, engagement metrics, and manifested affordances. The theoretical lens combines affordance theory with assemblage theory (via Hopkins's integration of the two).

## Findings

- SNA identified **14 communities** of interconnected pages/profiles, comprising 1387 posts containing 356 unique messages; coordinated clusters ranged from two to 30 pages.
- Posts appeared with an average delay of only **61.71 seconds** between accounts — far faster than organic behavior — indicating orchestrated amplification.
- Despite high coordination, **engagement remained low**: scalability was weaponized to project structural presence and an illusion of consensus rather than to achieve virality — signaling to political patrons that campaign infrastructure remains active.
- Connectivity hardened factional partisanship: distinct opposition-aligned clusters (around Robredo and Pangilinan, sustained via the Angat Buhay Foundation) versus fluid pro-administration coalitions linking dynastic figures.
- Persistence turned Facebook into an uncensored digital archive (posts from 2021 remained accessible), though re-contextualization mostly occurred within ~5 days of original publication rather than through resharing legacy content.
- Replicability appeared as standardized duplication (identical captions, hashtags, formatting); nearly **half of high-centrality pages were deleted, made unavailable, or rebranded** between 2022 and 2023 to evade scrutiny while preserving follower bases.
- Searchability was exploited through recognizable hashtags (#PBBM, #BBM, #UniTeam) and charged labels ("dilawan", "oligarkiya", "komunista"), including "oppositional searchability" where critics appropriated pro-administration tags.
- Identifiability varied: state/government media showed high institutional identifiability, while advocacy pages minimized it with generic avatars and journalism-mimicking names (e.g., "The Good News PH", "CAVITE CONNECT") to cultivate "mimicked legitimacy."
- The interplay of persistence and replicability can generate an **illusory truth effect**, raising perceived credibility of repeated claims regardless of accuracy.

## Connections

This paper sits squarely within the coordinated inauthentic behavior literature that operationalizes cross-posting and temporal synchronization as coordination markers, extending methods associated with Giglietto and colleagues' work on coordinated link sharing (e.g. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]]). Its focus on Philippine disinformation and manufactured engagement resonates with other Philippines-centered work in this register, such as [[Gaw2025-ru]] and [[Pante2025-pq]], while its relational, assemblage-based challenge to technological determinism speaks to broader debates on platform affordances and polarization taken up in [[Klinger2023-qu]]. The emphasis on repetition producing illusory credibility also links to misinformation-credibility studies like [[Mosleh2024-op]].
