# Conversational AI shifts beliefs and policy support among skeptics across contested societal issues

> Kotz, J., Tiede, K. E., Meyer, J., Sterba, M., Breunig, C., & Gaissmaier, W. (2026). Conversational AI shifts beliefs and policy support among skeptics across contested societal issues. https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/7szrn_v1
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/7szrn_v1)

## Summary

This large-scale preregistered experiment (N=6,558 U.S. participants) tests whether interactive, evidence-based dialogues with GPT-5 can shift beliefs and policy support across three structurally distinct contested issues: climate change, vaccination, and economic inequality. Compared to a neutral control conversation, brief three-round AI dialogues shifted beliefs in all three domains and increased support for politically contested policies including carbon and estate taxes. The headline finding inverts a common assumption in persuasion research: effects were strongest among initially skeptical participants — the hardest-to-reach group — and were amplified by trust in science rather than driven by partisan identity. The authors argue conversational AI could serve as a scalable, generalizable tool for evidence-based public communication, while flagging associated governance concerns.

## Key Contributions

- Demonstrates the **domain generality** of AI-based persuasion across three structurally distinct contested issues, moving beyond single-domain studies.
- Extends AI persuasion from belief change to **specific contested policy instruments** (carbon tax, mandatory vaccination, estate tax), showing belief change alone does not reliably translate into policy support.
- Provides systematic evidence on **heterogeneity**, finding effects strongest among skeptics and high-trust-in-science individuals — contrasting prior work showing static AI messages work best on sympathetic audiences.
- Characterizes the **persuasive strategies** conversational AI adopts and how it adapts them to participants' baseline attitudes.
- Offers a low-cost communication tool (requiring only a brief system prompt) while raising ethical concerns about persuasion at scale.

## Methods

Preregistered online experiment via Qualtrics with 6,558 U.S. participants (mean age 58.6; deliberate conservative oversample, ~57.6% Republican). Ten randomly assigned conditions crossed three topics (climate, vaccination, inequality) with three intervention types (belief-targeted, policy-targeted, combined), plus a neutral control discussing cats versus dogs. Participants engaged in a structured three-round dialogue with GPT-5 that advocated for the target belief/policy using scientific evidence while inviting questions. Beliefs and policy support were measured pre- and post-intervention on 0–100 slider scales using validated scales. Analysis used multiple linear regression controlling for baseline attitudes and actively open-minded thinking (AOT), with interaction terms for moderators, Bonferroni correction within outcome families, mixed-effects robustness checks, and exploratory GPT-5-based classification of 11 persuasive strategies.

## Findings

- All interventions increased beliefs and policy support in their targeted domains; largest main effects were for economic inequality (belief +6.36 pps; estate tax support +8.98 pps), where baseline attitudes were lowest.
- Climate belief rose ~3.5–3.7 pps and carbon tax support up to 4.80 pps; vaccination effects were smaller (reflecting high baseline support), with mandatory-vaccination policy shifts not surviving multiple-testing correction.
- Treatment effects grew as baseline attitudes declined — dialogues were most effective among skeptics, exceeding what regression to the mean would predict.
- Combined (belief+policy) interventions generally did not outperform outcome-specific interventions; belief-targeted interventions produced only small spillover effects on policy.
- Trust in science consistently amplified effects (roughly doubling them at +1 SD); trust in AI was weaker, trust in government inconsistent, and AOT was not a reliable moderator.
- GPT-5 most often used iterative collaborative dialogue and person-centered alignment, deploying prebunking/debunking and steelmanning more with skeptics and bridging-to-action strategies more with supporters.

## Connections

This paper builds directly on work showing LLM dialogues can durably reduce conspiracy beliefs, most closely [[Costello2024-bg]], and extends that dialogic paradigm from belief correction to contested policy attitudes across multiple domains. It contrasts with findings on static, message-based AI persuasion such as [[Hackenburg2025-dj]], and intersects with the science-trust and inoculation literature reflected in [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]]. Its skeptic-targeting results also speak to depolarization and attitude-change debates addressed in [[Voelkel2026-lc]].
