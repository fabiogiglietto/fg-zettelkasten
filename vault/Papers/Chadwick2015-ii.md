# Politics in the age of hybrid media

> Chadwick, A., Dennis, J., & Smith, A. P. (2015). Politics in the age of hybrid media. *The Routledge Companion to Social Media and Politics*, 7–22. https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315716299-2
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315716299-2)

## Summary

This chapter advances the **hybrid media system** approach as an interpretive lens for contemporary political communication. Its central argument is that older and newer media logics increasingly interact and interpenetrate rather than functioning as separate, discrete systems. Chadwick, Dennis, and Smith position this interdependence as the defining characteristic of an increasingly dynamic and volatile political information environment. As a conceptual review, the chapter synthesises emerging research across three domains — news and journalism, election campaigning, and engagement and mobilisation — to demonstrate the framework's explanatory value.

## Key Contributions

- Consolidates and reviews scholarship applying the hybrid media system framework across three central fields of political communication.
- Articulates the ontological foundations of the approach — the interaction, rather than separation, of established and emergent media logics.
- Identifies additional theoretical pillars built atop this basic ontology that illuminate news, campaigning, and mobilisation.

## Methods

This is a conceptual and theoretical review chapter rather than an empirical study. It proceeds by synthesising and reviewing illustrative examples drawn from the growing body of research that employs the hybrid media system approach, organising this literature around the three thematic domains.

## Findings

- The hybrid media system approach sheds light on developments in news and journalism, election campaigning, and engagement and mobilisation.
- Political communication environments are increasingly dynamic and volatile precisely because of the interaction between older and newer media.
