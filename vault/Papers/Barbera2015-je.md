# Birds of the Same Feather Tweet Together: Bayesian Ideal Point Estimation Using Twitter Data

> Barberá, P. (2015). Birds of the Same Feather Tweet Together: Bayesian Ideal Point Estimation Using Twitter Data. *Political Analysis*, *23*, 76–91. https://doi.org/10.1093/pan/mpu011
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/pan/mpu011)

## Summary

This paper introduces a method for estimating the ideological positions of both political elites and ordinary citizens from the structure of their networks on Twitter. Departing from the ideal point estimation tradition in political science—which conventionally relies on legislative roll-call votes—the author leverages the sociological principle of homophily: people tend to follow political actors whose views they share. From this premise, a Bayesian Spatial Following model treats ideology as a latent variable recoverable from an individual's pattern of following ties. The result is a scalable approach that places politicians and the mass public on a common ideological scale.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces a scalable Bayesian ideal point estimation approach grounded in social network (following) structure rather than roll-call votes or text analysis.
- Extends ideal point estimation beyond legislators to ordinary citizens, situating elites and the public within a shared ideological space.
- Demonstrates the value of large-scale social media data as a source for measuring political ideology.

## Methods

The core is a **Bayesian Spatial Following model** in which each user's ideology is a latent variable inferred from which political actors they choose to follow. The model exploits the assumption of network homophily to link the observed following matrix (citizens by politicians) to positions on a latent ideological dimension, and is applied to Twitter network data connecting citizens and politicians.

## Findings

- Following ties on Twitter exhibit ideological homophily, making it possible to recover latent ideological positions.
- The method jointly estimates positions for both elites and the mass public in a single common space (specific validation results not available in the summary).

## Connections

This work is a foundational contribution to the use of social media network structure for measuring political ideology, and it underpins later empirical studies of cross-cutting exposure and elite influence such as [[Bakshy2015-rn]] and [[Barbera2015-fw]]. The homophily assumption at its core connects to broader debates about echo chambers and selective exposure examined in [[Bail2018-fk]], [[Flaxman2016-lm]], and [[Gonzalez-Bailon2023-uy]], while its measurement agenda relates to methodological reflections on social media data in political science like [[Lazer2018-mm]].
