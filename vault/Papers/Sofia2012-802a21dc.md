# Moving towards a socially-driven internet architectural design

> Sofia, R., Mendes, P., Damásio, M. J., Henriques, S., Giglietto, F., Giambitto, E., & Bogliolo, A. (2012). Moving towards a socially-driven internet architectural design. *ACM SIGCOMM Computer Communication Review*. https://doi.org/10.1145/2317307.2317316
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/2317307.2317316)

## Summary

This interdisciplinary position paper argues that future Internet architecture should be designed with social capital principles as first-class considerations alongside technological ones. Its central motivation is the rise of the "prosumer" — the user who simultaneously produces and consumes content and network resources — and its extension into the "network prosumer," a role embodied in User-centric Networks (UCNs) that pushes user empowerment down into the lower OSI layers. The authors diagnose today's Internet supply chain as structurally asymmetric and contend that a symmetric redesign is needed to unlock new business models and knowledge exchange. They propose operationalizing social capital properties (trust, influence, reach, engagement) into networking metrics and critically revisit how centrality measures are (mis)applied across networking and social capital modeling.

## Key Contributions

- Articulates a vision and design guidelines for a **socially-driven Internet architecture** that merges social capital theory with networking design.
- Extends the prosumer concept into the network layer via the **"network prosumer"** notion grounded in User-centric Networks.
- Proposes a **symmetric Internet supply-chain model** that explicitly includes prosumers and UCNs as stages, contrasted with the conventional asymmetric model.
- Provides a comparative mapping between social capital centrality concepts and their networking counterparts, identifying specific misalignments.
- Identifies **reach, engagement, and influence** as the most actionable social properties for incorporation into Internet design, with candidate networking metrics for each.

## Methods

A conceptual, interdisciplinary position paper drawing on computer networking, social sciences, communication studies, and economics. The authors conduct a literature review across related Future Internet projects (GENI, EIFFEL, SocialNets, ULOOP) and social capital theorists (Bourdieu, Coleman, Putnam, Burt). Analytically, they rely on two framing devices: a comparative analysis of centrality definitions (degree, betweenness, closeness) across social capital and networking contexts (summarized in a comparison table), and a 7-stage Internet supply-chain model used to diagnose asymmetry and propose a symmetric variant. They further sketch a socially-driven design cycle linking social media, open datasets, social behavior models, and new media/business practices.

## Findings

- Vertical integration by operators clashes with the modular TCP/IP stack, limits innovation, and misaligns costs with price models.
- In the 7-stage supply chain, stages 2, 3, 5, and 7 have benefited from traffic growth, while stages 1, 4, and 6 stagnated due to weak price signals.
- User-centric Networks rely on four properties — resource sharing, cooperation, trust, and self-organization — pushing prosumer behavior to lower OSI layers.
- Networking and social capital interpretations of centrality diverge: high-betweenness nodes are social "bridgers" but mere relays in networking; high-closeness nodes imply good diffusion socially yet can become bottlenecks in networks.
- The "bridger" role and the transitivity of strong ties are poorly represented in current pervasive networking models.
- Prosumer activity exhibits power-law properties, evidencing the coexistence of consumer and prosumer models.
