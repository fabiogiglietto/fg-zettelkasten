# Demystifying hashtag hijacking in the public opinion game: attention, narratives, and social bots

> Zhao, B., Ren, W., He, Y., & Zhang, H. (2026). Demystifying hashtag hijacking in the public opinion game: attention, narratives, and social bots. *Information, Communication & Society*, 1–23. https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118x.2025.2531130
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118x.2025.2531130)

## Summary

This paper theorizes and analyzes "hashtag hijacking" — the strategic appropriation or redirection of trending hashtags — as a deliberate move in online public opinion contests rather than as incidental noise. Published in *Information, Communication & Society*, the study integrates three analytic strands: the dynamics of attention capture, the framing and substitution of narratives, and the role of automated agents (social bots). The authors argue that these three forces interact to determine whether a hijacking attempt succeeds in reshaping the discursive frame attached to a hashtag, situating the phenomenon within broader debates on platform politics and computational propaganda.

## Key Contributions

- Provides a framework for understanding hashtag hijacking as a strategic public opinion tactic.
- Integrates attention dynamics, narrative framing, and automated agents into a single analytic lens.
- Advances scholarship on the role of social bots in contested online discourse.

## Methods

Conceptual and empirical analysis of hashtag hijacking episodes. The available abstract does not specify the particular datasets, platforms, or computational techniques used.

## Findings

- Hijacking succeeds through a combination of attention capture and narrative substitution.
- Bot-driven amplification is implicated in shifting the trajectory of hashtag conversations.
- Attention, narrative, and bot activity operate jointly rather than independently.
