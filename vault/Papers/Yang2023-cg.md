# Visual misinformation on Facebook

> Yang, Y., Davis, T., & Hindman, M. (2023). Visual misinformation on Facebook. *Journal of Communication*. https://doi.org/10.1093/joc/jqac051
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/joc/jqac051)

## Summary

This paper offers the first large-scale empirical study of image-based political misinformation on Facebook, addressing a blind spot in a literature that has focused overwhelmingly on text and links. Drawing on more than 13 million posts from over 14,000 pages and 11,000 public groups collected during the run-up to the 2020 U.S. election, the authors combine computational image analysis with manual coding to estimate how much political visual content contains misinformation. Their central argument is that images are a substantial and understudied vector of political misinformation, and that understanding the misinformation ecosystem requires taking visual media seriously.

## Key Contributions

- First large-scale empirical study of image-based political misinformation on Facebook.
- Demonstrates the use of perceptual hashing and computer vision to detect duplicate images and political figures at scale across a very large corpus.
- Provides an empirical prevalence estimate for misinformation in political images, extending the misinformation literature beyond text and links.

## Methods

- Collected 13,723,654 posts from 14,532 pages and 11,454 public groups between August and October 2020, covering nearly all engagement with U.S. public political content on Facebook.
- Applied perceptual hashing to identify duplicate images across the corpus.
- Used computer vision techniques to detect and identify political figures within images.
- Manually coded a sample of 1,000 political images to assess the prevalence of misinformation.

## Findings

- Twenty-three percent (roughly a quarter) of the sampled political images (N = 1,000) contained misinformation.
- Image-based misinformation is widespread within U.S. public political content on Facebook during the 2020 election period.
- Visual misinformation constitutes a significant component of the misinformation ecosystem that prior text- and link-focused work has largely overlooked.

## Connections

This work extends foundational misinformation scholarship that has centered on textual and link-based content, complementing platform-scale analyses of Facebook political content such as [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] and [[Allcott2025-jb]]. Its computational, image-centered approach relates to other emerging studies of visual and AI-generated political imagery, including [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]], and to research on coordinated image-sharing on Facebook such as [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] and [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]].
