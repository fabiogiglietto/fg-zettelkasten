# Quantifying the impact of misinformation and vaccine-skeptical content on Facebook

> Allen, J., Watts, D. J., & Rand, D. G. (2024). Quantifying the impact of misinformation and vaccine-skeptical content on Facebook. *Science*, *384*, eadk3451. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk3451
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adk3451)

## Summary

This paper challenges the dominant narrative that COVID-19 vaccine hesitancy in the US was primarily driven by outright social media misinformation. Combining large-scale lab experiments, crowdsourced content evaluation, and machine learning, Allen, Watts, and Rand estimate the causal impact of thousands of vaccine-related Facebook URLs on the vaccination intentions of roughly 233 million US users. Their central finding is a reframing: unflagged, non-"false" content that nonetheless encouraged vaccine skepticism had a far larger aggregate effect on reducing vaccine uptake than content flagged as false — roughly 46 times greater. The mechanism is one of reach rather than per-exposure potency: legal-but-skeptical content reaches vastly more people, so its lower individual persuasive effect compounds into a larger population-level harm.

## Key Contributions

- Introduces a scalable framework integrating lab experiments, crowdsourcing, and machine learning to estimate causal effects of online content at population scale.
- Empirically reframes the misinformation-and-vaccination debate by shifting attention from overtly false content to legal-but-skeptical content.
- Quantifies the relative real-world impact of different categories of vaccine-related social media content across ~233 million users.

## Methods

- Lab experiments with a total N = 18,725 participants measuring the effect of content on vaccination intentions.
- Crowdsourcing to assess and characterize vaccine-related content.
- Machine learning to scale per-item experimental estimates across 13,206 vaccine-related URLs.
- Causal estimation combining measured per-exposure effects with observed exposure/reach data, applied to a population of ~233 million US Facebook users.

## Findings

- Unflagged vaccine-skeptical content had roughly a 46-fold greater aggregate impact on vaccination intentions than flagged misinformation.
- This gap arises from reach: unflagged content had far wider exposure despite lower per-exposure persuasive impact, so aggregate effect is dominated by exposure volume rather than item-level falsity.

## Connections
