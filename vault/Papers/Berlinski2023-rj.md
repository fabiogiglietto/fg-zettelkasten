# The effects of unsubstantiated claims of voter fraud on confidence in elections

> Berlinski, N., Doyle, M., Guess, A. M., Levy, G., Lyons, B., Montgomery, J. M., Nyhan, B., & Reifler, J. (2023). The effects of unsubstantiated claims of voter fraud on confidence in elections. *Journal of Experimental Political Science*, *10*, 34–49. https://doi.org/10.1017/xps.2021.18
>
> [View paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/xps.2021.18)

## Summary

This paper examines whether unsubstantiated elite claims of voter fraud erode public confidence in the electoral system. Drawing on a nationwide survey experiment fielded in the United States after the 2018 midterm elections, the authors provide causal evidence that exposure to fraud allegations diminishes trust in elections. The work is framed within the broader phenomenon of elite-driven election delegitimization, foreshadowing the significance of Donald Trump's post-2020 efforts to discredit and overturn election outcomes through false fraud claims.

## Key Contributions

- Provides new experimental evidence on the *causal* impact of elite fraud claims on public trust in elections.
- Demonstrates that delegitimizing rhetoric, even when unsubstantiated, has a measurable corrosive effect on confidence in electoral institutions.
- Connects an early (2018-era) study to the wider arc of election delegitimization that intensified after the 2020 US presidential election.

## Methods

- Nationwide US survey experiment fielded after the 2018 midterm elections.
- Experimental design exposed respondents to unsubstantiated voter fraud claims and measured resulting confidence in elections (specific treatment wording and outcome measures not detailed in the abstract).

## Findings

- Exposure to unsubstantiated fraud claims reduces confidence in the election system.
- The effect is presented as a causal consequence of exposure to elite fraud rhetoric, not merely a correlation.
- Full quantitative estimates are not available in the provided text, but the study reports clear "new evidence" of a corrosive effect.
