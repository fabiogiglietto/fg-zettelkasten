# Information Disorder & Disinformation Narratives

## From False Facts to Cultural Narratives

A decisive theoretical move across this corpus is the reconceptualization of disinformation away from discrete false claims toward *cultural narratives* and *collective sensemaking*. [[Marwick2026-qd]] argues that successful disinformation operates as identity-affirming, cathartic stories signaled by catchphrases and travelling across genres — a framing extended by [[Sadler2025-vu]]'s hermeneutic-realist critique of the true/false binary and by [[Suau_Martinez2026-lv]]'s finding that cumulative narrative exposure across the hybrid ecosystem drives belief. [[Starbird2025-jj]] and [[Prochaska2025-ef]] operationalize this narrative turn in the context of U.S. elections, showing that misleading rumors emerge from the *interaction* of (often accurate) evidence with distorted frames, and that "deep stories" performed collaboratively by influencers, elites, and audiences render single-tweet analyses inadequate. [[Goel2025-iq]] provides the empirical counterpart: mainstream articles are systematically repurposed to feed misleading narratives, invisible to domain-level detection. [[Waight2025-al]] responds methodologically, using LLMs to measure narrative similarity across languages where lexical reuse methods fail. Together these papers dismantle the older paradigm epitomized by fact-checking studies and push toward what [[Frischlich2025-vn]] terms a complexity-aware, multilevel understanding.

## Taxonomies, Definitions, and Conceptual Reflexivity

Alongside the narrative turn runs a persistent debate over how to define and label the phenomenon itself. [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] laid early groundwork by shifting focus from creators' intent to propagators' judgments in a hybrid news system. Recent contributions push further: [[Yoo2026-ev]] and [[Mosca2026-yh]] show that "fake news" is itself a rhetorical weapon deployed by hyperpartisan outlets and politicians for strategic delegitimization, while [[Farkas2026-lr]] documents how fact-checkers defend their legitimacy against precisely such accusations. [[Thiele2025-ol]] critiques Meta's "coordinated inauthentic behavior" concept as analytically muddled and offers a rational-choice typology of coordinated manipulation. [[Frischlich2025-vn]] indicts the dominant "infodemic," "warfare," and "pollution" analogies, and [[Mahl2026-hc]] reports Delphi consensus that terminological disputes may distract from more urgent research questions. Cross-cutting reviews by [[Budak2024-ef]] and [[Spampatti2026-kx]] press the field to reckon with overstated harms, WEIRD-centrism, and lessons from more mature domains like climate psychology — themes echoed by [[Humprecht2025-ml]] in its call for comparative work.

## Ecosystems, Hybridity, and Mainstreaming

A cluster of studies traces how radical and misleading content moves through hybrid media ecosystems rather than remaining in fringe enclaves. [[Rothut2026-or]] introduces "protest-facilitated mainstreaming" using Querdenken Telegram data, while [[Askanius2026-de]] maps Sweden's YouTube "alternative influence network" and [[Marino2023-9137f448]] shows how Italian Facebook amplifies an "Intellectual Dark Web" via legacy-media remediation. [[Copland2025-em]] traces Sky News Australia's transformation into a transnational right-wing propaganda node, and [[Bennett2025-xs]] provides the meta-theoretical scaffolding: "digital surrogate organizations" pull conservative parties toward illiberalism through connective action. [[Marwick2025-vx]] and [[Marwick2025-ov]] examine adjacent socialization processes — "processual redpilling" in far-right forums and "populist knowledge production" on ConspiracyTok — where disinformation functions as ritualized evidence for identity work. [[Grusauskaite2026-po]] shows how "Great Reset" communities perform symbolic boundary work across six platforms, and [[Bailard2024-pj]] links online framing to offline Proud Boys violence. [[Rossini2026-jn]]'s Brazilian panel shows the downstream cost: electoral misinformation belief erodes political tolerance itself.

## Elite Drivers, Coordination, and State Actors

Where earlier misinformation research emphasized ordinary users, this corpus increasingly foregrounds elites, coordinated networks, and states. [[Tornberg2025-ir]] provides the clearest cross-national verdict: radical-right populism — not populism or right-wing ideology alone — drives party-level misinformation across 26 countries; [[Tai2026-qk]] finds parallel institutional-ideological asymmetries among U.S. officials, and [[Renault2025-uh]] shows that even X's Community Notes flag Republicans 2.3× more often. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] opens the black box of Russia's IRA through OSINT analysis of employee CVs, while [[Kim2026-wg]] documents targeted digital voter suppression in 2016, and [[Kim2026-br]] traces two decades of suspected foreign trolling in Korean comment sections. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] shows contrarian coordination amplifying credentialed anti-consensus experts in COVID-19 discourse, [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] and [[Graham2025-gp]] examine coordinated activity around China's 2022 protests and #IStandWithPutin respectively, and [[Song2025-yh]] shows that coordinated link-sharing is ideologically agnostic. Bastos2025-ol adds a visual dimension via Twitter's Moderation Research Consortium data, and [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] emphasizes how manipulation campaigns persist by appropriating emerging events.

## Platforms, Algorithms, and Governance

Platform-level dynamics form a third recurring axis. [[Donovan2025-ws]] offers a critical history in which "misinformation-at-scale" is a structural feature of engagement-driven business models; [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] and [[Rossi2023-847d5a9f]] quantify Facebook diffusion in the U.S. and Europe respectively, and [[Vincent_undated-re]] tracks TikTok's rising prevalence of low-credibility content under DSA-style monitoring. [[Mosleh2024-op]] documents cross-platform divergence in partisan and low-quality news engagement, while [[Bosch2024-hj]] highlights TikTok's neglected sonic affordances and [[Zhao2025-ny]] examines hashtag hijacking. Governance interventions produce mixed evidence: [[Simeone2025-vo]] finds Twitter's 2021 deplatforming of election-audit accounts caused network-wide ripple effects, [[Cazzamatta2026-lo]] documents fact-checkers' rejection of Meta's post-2025 "censorship" framing, and [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] shows social bots functioning as agenda-builders against campaigns. Measurement infrastructure itself is contested: [[Luhring2025-od]] audits NewsGuard, [[Scalco2026-bd]] operationalizes "information voids," and [[Hourigan2026-oc]] uses digital diaries to show that everyday users flag mainstream outlets — not fringe sites — as their primary sources of perceived misinformation.

## AI, Generative Media, and New Vectors

Generative AI is emerging as both threat and instrument. [[Emilio2026-ik]] theorizes "synthetic realities" as a stack of content, identity, interaction, and institutions, and [[Waight2026-ts]] shows that state-controlled media are memorized and reproduced by commercial LLMs. [[Triedman2025-uy]] audits Musk's Grokipedia, finding derivative content with elevated citation of unreliable sources and novel "LLM auto-citogenesis." Yet [[Hameleers2026-mc]] tempers alarmism: AI-generated images do not universally outperform textual disinformation, and low-tech video decontextualization can be more persuasive. [[Choi2026-bz]] traces modality-congruent carryover effects from deepfake exposure. On the mitigation side, [[DeVerna2025-dl]] finds LLMs unreliable at political fact-checking without curated RAG, while [[Costello2024-bg]] provides striking evidence that personalized GPT-4 dialogues durably reduce conspiracy beliefs by ~20%.

## Effects, Vulnerabilities, and Interventions

The corpus documents both real harms and important boundary conditions. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] estimates that antivaccine tweets caused thousands of vaccine refusals; [[Lyons2026-ca]] shows low-credibility health content is rare in aggregate but heavily concentrated among older adults; [[Adam2026-tz]] combines panel data with web tracking to distinguish contagion from mitigation effects of media exposure on COVID-19 conspiracy beliefs. [[Rohrbach2026-rc]] implicates journalistic strategies themselves in conspiracy formation, and [[Xue2025-bp]] documents the emotional turn in fact-checking. [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]] shows influencer sourcing amplifies toxicity around brand misinformation. Individual-difference and contextual moderators recur: [[Van_Erkel2026-mk]] documents a "hostile misinformation effect" mirroring the hostile media effect, [[Gattermann2025-yx]] links far-right electoral success to public disinformation concerns, and [[Nenno2025-xa]] shows news values in flagged content vary across WEIRD/non-WEIRD contexts. On the intervention side, [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]] demonstrates that Instagram prebunking ads produce durable five-month effects at scale, while [[Lieu2025-nl]] finds CARDS content categories matter more than logical fallacy type for climate misinformation, and [[Ventura2025-sw]] uses WhatsApp deactivation to isolate messaging-app effects in Brazil. [[Gardam2025-er]] and [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]] extend the terrain to visual climate denial and politicized science reporting.

## An Emerging Consensus

Taken together, these papers describe a field moving decisively away from artifact-level, atomized, and WEIRD-centric analysis toward multilevel, narrative, and ecosystem-oriented inquiry. The center of gravity is shifting: from individual false claims to cultural narratives; from platform effects to institutional and elite drivers; from cognitive corrections to complexity-aware intervention portfolios. As [[Frischlich2025-vn]], [[Mahl2026-hc]], and [[Spampatti2026-kx]] all argue in different registers, no single analogy, actor, or intervention will suffice — and as [[Budak2024-ef]] warns, neither will inflated claims about social media's harms. The most productive frontier appears to lie precisely where these strands converge: in tracing how narratives, elites, platforms, and generative technologies co-produce contemporary information disorder.
