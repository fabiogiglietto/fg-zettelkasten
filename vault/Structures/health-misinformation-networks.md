# Health Misinformation & Problematic Content Networks

## Two Levels of Analysis: Individuals and Ecosystems

The papers filed here split, roughly, into two lenses on the same problem — one asking *who is exposed to problematic health content and why*, the other asking *how does problematic content circulate at scale, and who moves it*. [[Adam2026-tz]] and [[Lyons2026-ca]] anchor the individual-level lens. Adam2026-tz's panel-plus-web-tracking design in Germany and Switzerland shows that COVID-19 conspiracy belief is never simply "exposure causes belief": alternative media exposure produces contagion, mainstream debunking produces mitigation, but populist and mistrustful individuals selectively engage with the former and avoid the latter — and when they do encounter debunking, it can backfire into reinforcement rather than correction. This complicates any simple supply-side story of misinformation, since it shows predispositions actively curating exposure. Lyons2026-ca extends the same tracking logic to health content in the US and delivers a structurally similar but demographically distinct finding: exposure to low-credibility health sites is rare in aggregate but concentrated in a small, self-selecting population — disproportionately older adults with poor discernment and conspiracist worldviews — and it travels through habitual referral chains among low-credibility sites rather than through search or social media. Read together, these two papers suggest that health misinformation exposure is less a mass contagion event than a matter of *selective, self-reinforcing minority uptake*, with age and political predisposition as the key sorting variables.

## Measuring the Supply Side: Voids, Balance, and Overabundance

[[Scalco2026-bd]] pivots from individual exposure to the structural conditions that make problematic content more likely to fill the gap. By formalizing "information voids" as anomalous imbalances between public demand (Wikipedia views, Google Trends) and credible supply (Facebook, Twitter, GDELT), the paper operationalizes a concept — the data/information void — that has circulated informally in infodemiology discourse but lacked a measurement apparatus. Its empirical payoff directly complements the exposure-side papers: during voids, the share of highly credible content drops and misinformation prevalence rises, and voids persist longer than periods of overabundance. This gives a supply-side mechanism for what Lyons2026-ca documents from the demand side — that low-credibility content finds an audience partly because credible content fails to meet demand at key moments (vaccine roll-outs, safety-suspension events). Scalco2026-bd thus functions as connective tissue in this cluster: it explains *when and why* the ecosystem becomes vulnerable, complementing the *who is vulnerable* findings elsewhere.

## Coordinated Networks: Actors, Tactics, and National Variation

A distinct sub-cluster — [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], [[Marino2023-9137f448]], and [[Song2025-yh]] — shifts from exposure and ecosystem dynamics to the *organized* circulation of problematic health information, using coordinated link sharing behavior (CLSB) as a shared methodological backbone (the CooRnet toolkit runs through all three). Giglietto2022-0e951ac5 is foundational here: it documents the emergence of an Italian covid-skeptic coordinated network as an offshoot of pre-existing political coordination (League, Five Star Movement), catalogs evasion tactics (link laundering, first-comment link placement, religious-group infiltration), and demonstrates that a content-agnostic, behavioral signal (coordination itself) can surface problematic content faster than manual fact-checking. Marino2023-9137f448 builds directly on this network, reclassifying the same covid-skeptic ecosystem around the public figures it amplifies — showing that an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster achieves outsized share-driven engagement, and, crucially, that legacy media and mainstream journalists remain the central nodes recirculating this content via remediation of TV and newspaper appearances. This is a important corrective to any account that treats coordinated misinformation networks as separate from "legitimate" media: the two are structurally entangled.

Song2025-yh broadens the comparative frame beyond Italy, contrasting UK and US Facebook vaccine discourse. Its central move is to show that CLSB is *ideologically agnostic* — pro-vaccine communities coordinate just as densely as anti-vaccine ones, but with opposite credibility profiles (institutional sourcing vs. "Unknown" content) and different national framings (UK anti-vaccine discourse emphasizes safety, US emphasizes individual liberty). Together, these three papers push the collection's coordinated-network strand toward a more nuanced claim than "coordination equals malice": coordination is a technique that both misinformation and legitimate public-health communication exploit, with national political culture and pre-existing partisan infrastructure determining which narratives it amplifies.

## From Circulation to Consequence

[[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] closes the causal loop that the network papers leave implicit. Where Giglietto2022-0e951ac5, Marino2023-9137f448, and Song2025-yh document *how* problematic vaccine content circulates and Adam2026-tz and Lyons2026-ca document *who* absorbs it, Bollenbacher2026-vz asks whether that absorption translates into offline harm. Using a compartmental SIRVA epidemic model fit to county-level tweets, vaccinations, and case data, it estimates a specific causal chain — antivaccine tweets to vaccine refusals to additional cases and deaths — providing a rare quantitative bridge from platform-level speech to epidemiological outcome. This paper functions as the collection's terminus: it is the piece that most directly answers why the exposure and network dynamics documented elsewhere matter beyond the platforms themselves.

## Gatekeepers Old and New

[[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] sits somewhat apart from the misinformation-specific papers but supplies necessary context: it examines Google News Italia as an algorithmic gatekeeper shaping the health agenda that eventually reaches Facebook. Its finding that emotionally charged, "popular" health topics (celebrity illness, healthcare malpractice) generate disproportionate engagement relative to their share of aggregated coverage anticipates the engagement dynamics that Marino2023-9137f448 later identifies within explicitly problematic networks — high-engagement, share-driven content clusters around figures and scandals rather than technical accuracy. Read as a pair, Ducci2022-10cb5d70 and Marino2023-9137f448 suggest that the attention economics privileging emotionally resonant health content operate continuously across the "legitimate" and "problematic" ends of the media ecosystem, making the boundary between mainstream health journalism and alternative influence networks more porous than the labels imply.

## The Arc

Taken as a whole, this set of papers moves from micro to macro and back: individual predispositions and demographic vulnerabilities (Adam2026-tz, Lyons2026-ca) determine who absorbs problematic content; ecosystem-level supply-demand imbalances (Scalco2026-bd) determine when that content finds an opening; coordinated actor networks (Giglietto2022-0e951ac5, Marino2023-9137f448, Song2025-yh) determine how it is manufactured and amplified across national contexts; gatekeeping dynamics (Ducci2022-10cb5d70) show that the attention logics rewarding sensational content are not confined to fringe actors; and causal epidemic modeling (Bollenbacher2026-vz) closes the loop by quantifying what all of this circulation costs in vaccinations, cases, and deaths. The throughline is a rejection of any single-cause story of health misinformation: it is jointly produced by predisposed audiences, porous gatekeeping structures, opportunistic coordinated networks, and structural gaps in the credible information supply — and it has measurable, non-trivial public-health consequences.
