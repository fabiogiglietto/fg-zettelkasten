# Computational Methods & LLMs for Content Analysis

## From Ad Hoc Scripts to Validated Pipelines

The papers in this collection trace a field moving from opportunistic use of large language models toward deliberately engineered, validated measurement infrastructures. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] is the methodological anchor here: it documents a three-stage "LLMs-in-the-loop" pipeline — fine-tuned classification, embedding-based clustering, LLM-generated cluster labels — built for Italian election Facebook data, and it is candid about the validation problems this creates (variable narrative granularity, the inadequacy of crowdsourced raters against models that now outperform them). Its companion study [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] supplies the empirical backbone for one stage of that pipeline, showing that OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large consistently yields more semantically coherent clusters of Italian political news than the language-specific UmBERTo model, and that clustering algorithm choice (K-means vs. HDBSCAN) matters as much as embedding choice. Together these two papers model a template other contributions extend: [[Larsson2026-ro]] applies GPT-4 zero-shot sentiment classification with human validation to a decade of Norwegian Progress Party posts, explicitly framing this as a demonstration of LLM-assisted content analysis outside English; [[Ober2026-vd]] pushes the hybrid logic into qualitative research, combining topic modeling, LLM-assisted labeling, and iterative human codebook refinement on teacher interview transcripts; and [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] shows the same "LLM beats specialized model" pattern holding in the visual domain, where GPT-4o outperforms purpose-built face-recognition and person-counting systems for analyzing Instagram campaign imagery. [[Meher2025-qb]] and [[Bailard2024-pj]] represent an adjacent, lower-resource-friendly track: rather than prompting proprietary APIs, they fine-tune open (ConflLlama via QLoRA) or transformer (DeBERTa) models for domain-specific classification of conflict events and collective-action frames, arguing that efficient, auditable fine-tuning can rival or precede prompt-based LLM approaches while running on consumer hardware.

## Embeddings as Measurement Instruments

A second cluster treats embeddings themselves as the object of methodological scrutiny, asking what they measure and how that measurement can be corrupted or refined. [[Fan2025-ut]] formalizes a widespread but underexamined problem — that pretrained embeddings encode confounds like source or language that bias similarity-based analyses — and shows that linear concept erasure (LEACE) can dramatically improve clustering and retrieval without degrading out-of-domain performance, effectively offering a general-purpose preprocessing fix for the kind of pooled, multi-source corpora common in computational communication research. [[Arminio2025-tw]] extends embedding-based clustering into the visual register, arguing that CNN pipelines capture only denotative image content and proposing a VLLM-to-text-to-embedding pipeline that better recovers *connotative* meaning in climate-related Instagram images, with interpretability gains from TF-IDF summaries of the intermediate text. [[Waight2025-al]] and [[Elfes2026-jb]] use LLMs to operationalize theoretical constructs that embeddings alone cannot capture: narrative similarity as shared claims-about-events (validated against text reuse, topic modeling, and Relatio baselines, and applied to trace Russian "biolab" narratives across outlets) and Greimas' actantial model as a measure of "narrative polarisation" in YouTube discourse on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, respectively. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] offers a simpler but related move — asking instruction-tuned LLMs to place texts on ideological dimensions and averaging many such judgments — while [[Lai2024-to]] pursues a structurally similar latent-variable goal without LLMs, using correspondence analysis of Reddit sharing behavior to scale YouTube videos ideologically. [[Bruns2025-fz]] rounds out this cluster with "practice mapping," proposing vector embeddings of network actions as an alternative to conventional, hairball-prone network visualization for multimodal platform interactions.

## Probing What LLMs Can and Cannot Do as Coders

A distinct methodological throughline interrogates the reliability of LLMs as annotators rather than assuming it. [[Brown2025-jk]] is the most systematic entry: across four contentious annotation tasks and three LLMs, it finds that demographic bias effects are small and dataset-specific rather than model-specific, while item difficulty (human label entropy) is by far the dominant predictor of LLM-human disagreement — a finding that reframes fairness debates around task properties rather than model choice. [[Paci2025-ag]] identifies a harder ceiling: even strong models fail to reliably interpret implicatures and presuppositions in Italian political speech, producing fully correct explanations in only about a quarter of open-ended cases, which suggests pragmatic competence remains a genuine bottleneck for LLM-based discourse coding. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] finds an analogous ceiling in fact-checking — standard and reasoning-enabled LLMs perform poorly on PolitiFact claims unless given curated retrieval context, and even search-augmented models show a citation skew toward left-leaning sources — while [[Lee2026-je]] demonstrates a capability that cuts the other way: LLMs can reliably infer users' political alignment even from ostensibly nonpolitical text, outperforming supervised baselines and raising privacy concerns about scalable inference from innocuous content. [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys this emerging annotation-and-synthesis literature as a field in its own right, and [[Balluff2026-if]] supplies the corpus's most pointed counterweight, arguing that communication researchers have adopted LLMs largely without reckoning with reproducibility, environmental, and bias costs, and urging a trade-off mindset that favors smaller, open, task-appropriate models over default LLM use — a call that several of the above papers implicitly answer by rigorously benchmarking rather than assuming LLM adequacy.

## LLMs as Objects and Agents of Persuasion

Several papers shift focus from LLMs as analytic tools to LLMs as communicative agents whose effects on belief and trust are themselves objects of content-analytic and experimental study. [[Kotz2026-lk]] finds that brief evidence-based dialogues with GPT-5 shift beliefs and policy support across climate, vaccination, and inequality domains, with effects concentrated among skeptics and moderated by trust in science — while [[Szabo2026-rd]] shows a chatbot-based "conversational inoculation" outperforming static reading/writing inoculation against misinformation, crediting adaptability and trust-building rather than argument content alone. [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] complicates this optimism: perceived political bias in the same underlying model substantially attenuates its persuasive power, operating through user pushback rather than disengagement, showing that persuasion is contingent on perceived neutrality of the messenger. [[Mattis2026-gu]] extends the trust question to journalistic transparency, finding that disclosing AI use in any newsroom task — especially fact-checking — reduces perceived trustworthiness, with heterogeneous audience segments rather than a single uniform reaction. Read together, these four papers suggest that the same generative capacities that make LLMs useful coding tools also make them consequential, trust-sensitive communicators whose effects require the same rigor of measurement as any other persuasive intervention.

## Detecting Coordination, Inauthenticity, and Synthetic Content at Scale

A parallel set of papers turns computational content-analysis techniques toward detecting manipulation and AI-generated material itself. [[Luceri2025-tr]] and [[Yang2025-iv]] both advance coordination-detection methodology beyond text-centric, timing-based signals — adapting network-based detection to TikTok's video-first structure, and exploiting statistical regularities in link-sharing speed and frequency to make Facebook coordination detection harder to evade. Bastos2025-ol applies computer-vision tools to profile imagery in the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium dataset, asking whether troll farms carry identifiable visual signatures by country of origin, extending inauthenticity detection from text and network structure into the visual register that [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] and [[Arminio2025-tw]] also foreground. [[Triedman2025-uy]] pushes this detection logic toward a new target — AI-generated knowledge platforms themselves — using embedding-based similarity and citation analysis to show that Grokipedia is heavily derivative of Wikipedia yet systematically skews toward lower-quality and ideologically aligned sources, particularly on controversial topics, illustrating how the same embedding-comparison toolkit developed for clustering political news ([[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]]) can audit AI-generated corpora for quality and bias.

## Substantive Applications: Framing, Narrative, and News Values

Several papers apply these computational and LLM-assisted tools to long-standing communication-research questions about framing and discourse quality. [[Nguyen2026-vm]] combines computational and manual content analysis to map mentalistic-agentic framing of LLMs in global news coverage, finding techno-capitalist master frames dominate but anthropomorphic language serves varied — including critical — rhetorical functions. [[Arora2025-tx]] and [[Sarmiento2025-as]] both push framing analysis beyond fixed, predefined frame sets: the former extracting cross-modal (textual, visual) frames from gun-violence coverage, the latter proposing an unsupervised ML/network/NLP pipeline for surfacing emergent, subtle frames in polarized online discussion. [[Nenno2025-xa]] operationalizes news values computationally across 24 countries to test whether misinformation exploits conflict, negativity, and proximity differently in WEIRD versus non-WEIRD contexts, finding small but significant differences and challenging the cross-cultural generalizability of Western news-values typologies. [[Gardam2025-er]] argues text-centric methods are fundamentally insufficient for image-driven platforms and proposes a visual-first approach to climate-denial discourse on Instagram. [[Hartmann2025-px]] steps back from any single method to synthesize 129 echo-chamber studies, concluding that much of the field's empirical disagreement stems from inconsistent conceptualization and operationalization rather than divergent underlying reality — a diagnosis that resonates with this whole topic's concern for methodological validation before substantive inference.

## The Infrastructural Ground Beneath Computational Content Analysis

A substantial subset of papers steps back from analytic technique to interrogate the data infrastructure that any computational or LLM-based pipeline depends on. [[Yang2026-tq]] itself models the topic's methodology reflexively, using a human-in-the-loop LLM annotation pipeline to conduct a systematic review of social-media-based social science, finding that empirical reliance on Twitter/X and Facebook has plateaued since API restrictions began — a finding that motivates the rest of this cluster. [[Rieder2025-ju]] audits YouTube's search API and finds it "forgetful by design," with severe temporal decay in retrievability that undermines historical and DSA-relevant research; [[Ulloa2024-jm]] shows that web-scraping methodology itself, independent of time delay, introduces systematic, non-randomly distributed distortion into measures of news consumption; and [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]] documents a platform-wide artifact in Meta's URL Shares Dataset whereby a simple anonymization threshold, interacting with feed-ranking changes, produces spuriously similar cross-country trends. [[Peters2026-mo]] and [[Bruns2026-pn]] examine the regulatory and infrastructural responses to these problems — the contested, politically negotiated status of "data quality" in the EU Digital Services Act, and the way "clean room" access environments (like the Meta Content Library) privilege quantitative, code-based, well-resourced research while excluding qualitative and cross-platform work. [[Zheng2026-bi]] offers a constructive counterpoint, building TubeStats and TokStats as open tools for genuinely random platform sampling to solve the "denominator problem" that opportunistic samples cannot. [[Fan2026-af]] closes this cluster by arguing that even where digital trace data are available, most communication research aggregates away their most valuable feature — fine-grained temporality — and proposes sequence-, process-, and embedding-based methods to preserve it.

## Reflexive Turns: AI Evaluating Science, and Science Evaluating AI

The collection ends by folding back on itself, asking what role AI should play in producing and validating knowledge in the first place. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] benchmarks AI coding agents' ability to reproduce published social-science findings, showing surprisingly high accuracy alongside troubling susceptibility to sycophantic specification search under confirmatory prompting — a direct empirical test of the reproducibility worries [[Balluff2026-if]] raises abstractly. [[Jayaram2026-wd]] extends this logic to peer review itself, describing an agentic tool that substantially improves error detection in mathematical and computer-science manuscripts and proposing a taxonomy of AI's expanding role in scientific verification. [[Matias2025-px]] provides the normative counterweight running through much of this topic: that the scientific rigor of AI evaluation itself depends on participatory, lived-experience input at multiple stages (equipoise, measurement, explanation, inference, interpretation), not merely on technical benchmarking. Taken together with [[Balluff2026-if]]'s critique of unreflective adoption, these papers suggest the field's next methodological frontier is not simply building better LLM-based pipelines for content analysis, but building the meta-level infrastructure — validation protocols, reproducibility benchmarks, participatory oversight — needed to trust them.
