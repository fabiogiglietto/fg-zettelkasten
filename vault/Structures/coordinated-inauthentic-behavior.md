# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

### From list-making to workflow: the methodological core

The anchor of this topic is a sustained, decade-long research program that begins with [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which first operationalized "coordinated link sharing behavior" (CLSB) on Facebook as a content-agnostic, action-based alternative to veracity- or actor-centered disinformation detection. Rather than asking whether a claim is true or an account is a bot, the method asks whether entities share the same URLs with suspicious synchrony — a reframing that explicitly grounds Facebook's own "coordinated inauthentic behavior" (CIB) enforcement category in academic theories of coordination and authenticity. This line matures into the CooRnet toolkit and is pushed into an applied register in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] (the MINE-FACTS project), where CLSB is deployed as a fact-checker-facing triage tool during COVID-19, revealing how coordinated actors evade detection through tactics like link laundering, first-comment link placement, and exploitation of religious or community groups. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] then solves the central operational problem that plagues all list-based detection — staleness — by proposing an iterative monitoring workflow that starts from a seed list and continuously surfaces new coordinated accounts, demonstrated across three structurally distinct 2022 Italian election cases (hyperpartisan political networks, clickbait-via-religion operations, and religious-proselytism bot funnels). This workflow logic is the direct ancestor of the vera.ai WP4 extension and the 2026 VERA-AI Alert system, and it recurs as applied casework in [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] (AI-generated gambling promotion imagery) and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]], which synthesizes across pro-Putin, gambling, and poorly-moderated adult-content networks to argue that Meta's CIB/CSH categories are too narrow to capture the shared structural logic — deceptive intent, affordance exploitation, coordination — of what the authors call "Deceptive Information Operations" in a "Zombie Internet." [[Rogers2026-cy]], also a vera.ai product, extends the applied-verification thread by proposing "post-truth spaces" as a complementary mapping concept for fact-checkers, using network proximity to mainstream clusters as an influence metric in a Moldovan Facebook case study.

### Theorizing coordination: definitions, typologies, and the authenticity debate

A parallel theoretical strand interrogates what "coordination" and "inauthenticity" actually mean. [[Starbird2019-qv]] is foundational here, reframing information operations as *collaborative work* performed by sociotechnically entangled crowds rather than simple bot/troll dichotomies, and insisting that distinguishing orchestrated campaigns from organic crowd behavior is a persistent, perhaps irreducible, analytic challenge. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] operationalizes this insight empirically, showing on 2019 UK election data that coordination exists on a continuum (not a binary), that each community has its own "characteristic" coordination level, and — crucially — that coordination and automation are orthogonal. [[Keller2019-nk]], using rare ground-truth data from the South Korean NIS astroturfing scandal, reinforces this by showing coordination traces (not individual bot-like signals) are what expose a managed campaign, while also demonstrating that even well-resourced, human-staffed operations may have limited measurable discursive impact — a skepticism echoed much later by [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]], which develop post-hoc, graph-theoretic frameworks to quantify (rather than merely detect) CIB's actual influence on cascades, finding that real coordinated accounts in UK election data are far less influential than optimal or even randomly-placed benchmarks would predict. [[Thiele2025-ol]] pushes the conceptual critique furthest, arguing that "CIB" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposing an attribution-oriented typology built on a rational-choice model of principals' scale/elaborateness/disguise tradeoffs. [[Mannocci2026-kc]]'s survey of 122 papers performs the field-wide synthesis implicit in all this work, proposing actors–actions–intent as the general definition and authenticity/harmfulness/orchestration/time-variance as orthogonal characterizing dimensions — directly validating [[Song2025-yh]]'s empirical point that CLSB is "ideologically agnostic," capable of amplifying both anti-vaccine misinformation and credible NHS/CDC public health messaging depending on who coordinates.

### Detection methods: multiplexity, time, and new modalities

A dense cluster of recent papers advances the detection toolkit itself. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] both tackle multimodality — the fact that coordination can manifest through retweets, replies, hashtags, mentions, or URLs simultaneously — with the former establishing a comparative framework showing a fundamental trade-off between integration strength and detection inclusiveness, and the latter adding a temporal decay kernel to argue that *when* co-actions happen is as diagnostic as *how often*. [[Minici2024-tf]] (IOHunter) and [[Gerard2025-br]] (CANE/t-CANE) push toward foundation-model and embedding-based generalization, the latter reframing cross-platform influence as structural alignment around shared narrative clusters rather than direct interaction, identifying a tiny set of "bridge users" who seed cross-platform migration. Platform expansion is a recurring motif: [[Yang2025-iv]] proposes evasion-resistant statistical signals (speed/frequency regularities) for Facebook link-sharing; [[Luceri2025-tr]] adapts network-based detection to TikTok's video-first ecosystem; [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] targets real-time bot coordination on the decentralized platform Bluesky; and [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts "Rapid Spread Network" detection to the closed, encrypted environment of Brazilian WhatsApp groups, linking coordinated messaging to real-world mobilization including the January 8, 2023 insurrection. [[Pante2025-pq]] turns the critical lens on the field itself, arguing that prior claims of inter-state IO collaboration need re-testing against modern multi-signal methods and proper control datasets.

### Platforms, geographies, and actor ecosystems

Beyond methodology, the topic traces CIB/CLSB across an expanding geopolitical map. Authoritarian and quasi-authoritarian contexts feature heavily: [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] on pro-government Telegram amplification in Russia/Belarus, [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] on dual-use coordination during China's COVID protests, [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] on Serbia's SNS mimicking grassroots authenticity to evade detection, and [[Kim2026-br]]'s two-decade explainable-ML analysis of Korean news-comment trolls, which finds moral *condemnation* (not praise) as the dominant, most-amplified rhetorical strategy. State-linked infrastructure and labor are examined organizationally in [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s OSINT reconstruction of the IRA's internal hierarchy and media-labor-market embeddedness, and in [[Gaw2025-ru]]'s reframing of Philippine influence operations as political brokerage. Bastos2025-ol mines the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium dataset for visual/attribution signatures of troll farms. Election-specific case studies include [[Oprea2025-lf]] on hyperactive Facebook sharers in Romania's EP election and [[Simeone2025-vo]], which shows that targeted deplatforming (not CIB detection per se) produced outsized "network ripple effects" on the Arizona election-audit community — a useful counterpoint underscoring that not all influential restructuring is coordination-driven. At larger scale, [[Appel2026-qr]]'s analysis of Meta's 49 deceptive networks in the 2020 US election is a landmark complication of reach-and-effects assumptions: most exposure came from indirect resharing by non-network accounts, exposure was extremely concentrated, and causal effects on beliefs mostly vanish after controlling for prior user characteristics.

### Adjacent phenomena and future trajectories

Several papers extend coordination concepts into adjacent domains: bot-driven agenda-building in elections ([[Arceneaux2026-xk]]), hashtag hijacking as strategic discourse capture ([[Zhao2025-ny]]), coordinated science-communication contrarianism ([[Efstratiou2026-ij]]), transnational propaganda infrastructures ([[Copland2025-em]]), cross-platform politicization of "woke" discourse ([[Holland_Levin2026-qx]]), and the diffuse, narrative-level "Memeplex" account of Great Reset conspiracism as an infrastructural rather than coordinated phenomenon ([[Tuters2026-vo]]) — the latter offering an important counter-model where apparent elite coordination is a misrecognition of platform grammar rather than actual orchestration. Finally, the frontier of the topic turns toward generative AI: [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates LLM agent swarms to show that even minimal "teammate awareness" produces emergent coordination approaching explicit collusion, while [[Schroeder2026-im]] and [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] warn, at a policy level, that agentic AI swarms and persistently adaptive manipulation campaigns represent a qualitative escalation beyond the botnets and troll farms this entire literature was built to detect — framing the urgency behind the ongoing extension of CooRnet/CLSB methodology into the VERA-AI Alert system.
