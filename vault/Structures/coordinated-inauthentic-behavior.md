# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior Detection

## The line of argument: from problem definition to detection to interpretation

This corpus traces the emergence, consolidation, and diversification of coordinated inauthentic behavior (CIB) as both a theoretical object and a set of operational tools. Its throughline is the researcher's own methodological signature — coordinated link sharing behavior (CLSB) and the CooRnet package — but the notes around it show how that contribution sits within a much larger, contested field.

### Founding the paradigm: from content to coordination

The origin point is [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which reframes disinformation research away from adjudicating content veracity or unmasking "bad actors" and toward detecting *coordinated collective action* — operationalized as near-simultaneous, repeated URL sharing across Facebook pages, groups, and profiles. This move is explicitly grounded in CSCW's participatory framing of information operations as *work* ([[Starbird2019-qv]]), and answers early network-based coordination detection on Twitter ([[Nizzoli2020-cf]], [[Keller2019-nk]]) with a Facebook-native, content-agnostic method. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] extends CLSB into applied fact-checking during COVID-19, showing how coordinated networks evolve tactics (link laundering, first-comment link-hiding, religious-group infiltration) to evade detection, while [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] solves the staleness problem of static actor lists by building a live workflow that iteratively expands coordinated-account registries — a direct ancestor of the VERA-AI Alert system. This lineage culminates in applied case studies under the vera.ai umbrella: [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] on AI-amplified gambling promotion, [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] on "Deceptive Information Operations" spanning propaganda, gambling, and adult-content networks, and [[Rogers2026-cy]] on mapping "post-truth spaces" for verification specialists — all sharing the diagnostic instinct that platform affordances, not just bad content, are the exploit surface.

### Contesting and refining the concept

A second cluster interrogates CIB/CLSB's conceptual foundations. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] offers the field's most systematic reckoning, arguing coordination is an orthogonal dimension (authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, time-variance) that platform-derived CIB definitions collapse too narrowly. [[Thiele2025-ol]] pushes further, contending CIB conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposing an attribution-focused typology centered on principals' strategic incentives. [[Song2025-yh]] complicates the moral valence directly within the CLSB tradition, showing coordinated link-sharing can carry credible public-health messaging as readily as misinformation — coordination is ideologically agnostic. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] and [[Graham2026-fb]] each destabilize the authentic/inauthentic binary from opposite ends: Serbian SNS networks mimic grassroots authenticity so well they evade detection, while Reddit's "botting" culture shows ostensibly bot-like repetition is often human participatory performance, not deception at all.

### Methodological innovation beyond Facebook links

A dense technical cluster extends coordination detection across modalities and platforms. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] formalize multimodal and temporal-multiplex representations, arguing single-modality or flattened-network approaches lose or dilute real coordination signals — a direct methodological complement to CLSB's single-modality (URL-based) design. [[Yang2025-iv]] proposes evasion-resistant detection via sharing-speed statistical regularities rather than timing alone, addressing the same adversarial-adaptation problem [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] documents empirically. Platform-specific adaptations proliferate: [[Luceri2025-tr]] for TikTok's video-first ecosystem, [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] for real-time bot detection on Bluesky, [[Kansaon2025-id]] for closed WhatsApp networks in Brazil, [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] for Telegram forwarding in Russia/Belarus, and [[Minici2024-tf]] introducing a graph foundation model (IOHunter) for cross-campaign generalization. Bastos2025-ol adds a visual-identity dimension to attribution, using computer vision on troll-farm profile images.

### Skepticism and the question of impact

A counter-current questions whether detected coordination actually drives influence. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] models cascade influence formally and finds real coordinated accounts perform far below optimal or even random-matched benchmarks, suggesting CIB is less pivotal than assumed. [[Simeone2025-vo]] similarly finds that deplatforming's dramatic discourse effects on the Arizona Election Review community were *not* explained by bot-like coordination. [[Pante2025-pq]] retroactively re-audits claims of inter-state IO collaboration and finds them unsupported without proper control datasets. Together with [[Appel2026-qr]]'s massive Meta-collaboration study — showing indirect resharing by non-network accounts, not direct network posting, drives most exposure, and that correlational harms mostly vanish after controlling for prior user characteristics — these papers form a sobering empirical check on inflated claims about coordinated networks' causal power.

### Case studies, actors, and infrastructures

A rich descriptive layer documents specific coordinated ecosystems: the IRA's organizational anatomy via OSINT ([[Poliakoff2026-fa]]), Philippine influence operations as political brokerage ([[Gaw2025-ru]]), Sky News Australia as a transnational propaganda node ([[Copland2025-em]]), Korean troll rhetoric over two decades ([[Kim2026-br]]), pro-Kremlin hashtag campaigns exploiting "infrastructures of truth" ([[Graham2025-gp]]), Chinese COVID-protest coordination amid digital repression ([[Kulichkina2026-zk]]), Romanian election hyperactivity ([[Oprea2025-lf]]), and bot-driven agenda-building in US midterms ([[Arceneaux2026-xk]]) and hashtag hijacking ([[Zhao2025-ny]]). [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] extends coordination analysis into science communication, tracing how coordinated contrarian networks intersect with news media pathways.

### The horizon: generative AI and platform politics

The newest strand anticipates how generative AI reshapes coordination itself. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates LLM agents spontaneously reproducing IO coordination signatures without human direction; [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into "malicious AI swarms" as a systemic democratic threat; [[Tang2026-gu]] documents matrix-account, AI-engineered virality as industrialized creative labor in China; and [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] empirically anchors this shift, showing an exponential post-ChatGPT surge in AI-generated gambling content within coordinated Facebook networks. Bookending the corpus, [[Bruns2019-nr]] and [[Donovan2025-ws]] supply the political-economic and infrastructural context — API restrictions choking independent research, and platforms' preference for behavioral (CIB-style) moderation over truth-adjudication as a structural feature of engagement-driven business models — explaining both why CLSB-style, content-agnostic detection became necessary and why it remains institutionally precarious.
