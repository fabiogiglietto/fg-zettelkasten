# Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour

## Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour

### The methodological spine: from CooRnet to workflow-based monitoring

Giglietto's programme begins from a deceptively simple methodological wager: rather than trying to adjudicate the truth or falsity of content, or to unmask individual bad actors, disinformation research should detect *coordination itself* as a behavioural signature. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] establishes this founding move — coordinated link sharing behaviour (CLSB) during the 2018 and 2019 Italian elections — showing that near-simultaneous, repeated URL sharing across pages, groups and profiles correlates strongly with the circulation of problematic domains, and that the internal composition of a network (openly political vs. deceptively "non-political") predicts its sharing strategy. This becomes the CooRnet lineage. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] operationalises CLSB for real-world fact-checking use (the MINE-FACTS prototype), demonstrating that a content-agnostic, behavioural filter surfaces a higher proportion of problematic content than routine fact-checking, while also cataloguing the evasive tactics coordinated actors develop (link laundering, first-comment link placement, cross-posting into image macros, exploitation of religious groups). [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] then addresses the central weakness of any list-based detection system — obsolescence — by proposing an iterative workflow that starts from a seed list and continuously surfaces new coordinated accounts, applied across three structurally distinct cases (a partisan hyperpartisan network, a clickbait-for-profit operation, a religious proselytism network) that together demonstrate the framework's actor- and motive-agnosticism. This lineage anticipates the vera.ai-era work: [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] extends the same detection logic to define "Deceptive Information Operations" as a category defined by the convergence of deceptive content, platform-affordance exploitation, and coordination — explicitly arguing that Meta's CIB/CSH categories are too narrow to capture the "Zombie Internet" of pro-Putin propaganda, gambling promotion, and adult-content/fraud networks his team uncovers. [[Rogers2026-cy]], also from vera.ai, complements this with the "post-truth space" concept, mapping how fringe clusters gain influence not through their own centrality but through proximity to "borderline" clusters that bridge them to the mainstream — a spatial, cartographic counterpart to the workflow-based detection line.

### Substantive case studies: what coordinated networks are used for

A second cluster of Giglietto-adjacent papers uses the CLSB/CooRnet apparatus to characterise specific covid-era and adjacent phenomena. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] traces how "Alternative Influence Networks," and especially an Intellectual Dark Web cluster, achieved disproportionate share-driven engagement in circulating covid-skeptic narratives, with legacy media and journalists remaining central nodes of amplification — a hybridity finding. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] pushes the programme into the generative-AI era, documenting an exponential post-volume explosion in coordinated gambling-promotion networks after ChatGPT's launch, and arguing that generative AI intensifies rather than replaces existing persuasion drivers (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, FOMO, cultural localisation) while exploiting Meta's regulatory gap between paid and organic content. These case studies collectively model the throughline from CLSB-as-method to CLSB-as-diagnostic-lens for emergent harms (health misinformation, gambling, synthetic media).

### The broader field: convergent and competing detection paradigms

Sitting alongside and increasingly citing this lineage is a dense contemporary literature on coordination detection that both extends and challenges the CooRnet approach. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] offers the field's most ambitious synthesis, surveying 122 papers to propose a unifying definition of coordinated online behaviour along four axes (authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, time-variance) and explicitly critiquing the platform-driven CIB framing that Giglietto's own papers have worked within and against. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] push the technical envelope on multimodality and temporality — showing that single-modality, time-agnostic detection (the kind CLSB originally relied on) misses coordination that manifests through replies, mentions, or hashtags, and that multiplex, time-weighted representations outperform monomodal ones. [[Yang2025-iv]] directly addresses a vulnerability in timing-based methods like CooRnet by proposing detection based on the statistical regularity of sharing speed and frequency rather than post-timing alone, explicitly framing itself as a response to the evasion risk inherent in the CooRnet-style approach. [[Thiele2025-ol]] and [[Mannocci2026-kc]] both critique "coordinated inauthentic behaviour" as a platform-derived concept that conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent — a conceptual tension Giglietto's own workflow papers implicitly navigate by defining coordination behaviourally rather than by actor authenticity.

### Platform and modality extensions

The detection line has proliferated across platforms beyond Facebook and Twitter/X, testing whether CLSB-style logics transfer. [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts the video-first architecture of TikTok, arguing text-centric methods do not straightforwardly transfer. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] pushes toward real-time bot-coordination detection on the decentralised Bluesky platform. [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts a Rapid Spread Network approach to WhatsApp's closed, encrypted ecosystem in Brazil, showing coordinated messaging fed real-world mobilisation around the January 8, 2023 protests. [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] applies coordination detection to Telegram forwarding networks amplifying Russian and Belarusian state narratives. [[Minici2024-tf]] and [[Gerard2025-br]] represent the graph-machine-learning frontier: IOHunter combines language models and GNNs for cross-campaign generalisation, while CANE/t-CANE reframes cross-platform influence as narrative-cluster affiliation rather than direct interaction, identifying small sets of "bridge users" who seed migrating narratives between Truth Social and X.

### Contesting the paradigm: does coordination matter, and how much?

A skeptical countercurrent questions the causal weight typically assigned to coordinated activity. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] formally models the influence of coordinated nodes within retweet cascades and finds that real coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election achieve far less influence than optimal or even random placement would predict, suggesting CIB may be less pivotal to diffusion than assumed. [[Appel2026-qr]] and its companion [[noauthor_undated-bm]] — large-scale Meta/FIES studies of 49 deceptive networks in the 2020 US election — similarly find that most reach comes from ordinary users resharing content rather than from network accounts themselves, and that correlations between exposure and downstream political outcomes evaporate once pre-exposure characteristics are controlled for, cautioning against causal overreach. [[Simeone2025-vo]] complicates the CIB frame from another angle, showing that the ripple effects of Twitter's deplatforming of election-audit accounts were *not* attributable to bot-like coordinated behaviour, but to authentic human disengagement following the loss of hub/authority structure — a useful reminder that not all impactful network phenomena are coordinated, and not all coordination is impactful. [[Pante2025-pq]] extends this scrutiny to claims of *inter-state* coordination, arguing prior findings of collaboration between state-backed operations do not hold up against modern multi-signal methods and proper control datasets.

### Actors, motives, and organisational infrastructure

Several papers shift focus from detection technique to the human and organisational infrastructure behind coordination. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] offers a rare organisational ethnography of the IRA via leaked CVs, revealing it as a labour-market-embedded media operation rather than a purely covert unit. [[Gaw2025-ru]] reconceptualises Philippine influence operations as political brokerage connecting clients, platforms, and voters. [[Oprea2025-lf]] and [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] examine how ruling parties (in Romania and Serbia respectively) use hyperactive users and grassroots-mimicking tactics that evade standard bot-centric detection, echoing Thiele's point that manipulative intent, not superficial inauthenticity, is the analytically salient feature. Bastos2025-ol looks for attributable visual signatures in troll-farm profile imagery, while [[Kim2026-br]] and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] extend detection to state-linked comment trolling in Korea and protest-related coordination in China, respectively — both finding coordination serving dual repressive/mobilising functions in authoritarian contexts.

### Emerging horizon: generative AI as coordination's next frontier

The most recent papers converge on generative AI as both a new object and a new enabler of coordinated inauthentic behaviour, extending directly from [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]'s findings on AI-amplified gambling promotion. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates LLM agents in information-operation scenarios, finding that mere "teammate awareness" among AI agents produces coordination levels nearly matching explicit collective deliberation — a sobering echo of the human coordination this whole research line has spent a decade learning to detect. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a policy register, warning of "malicious AI swarms" that fuse LLM reasoning with multi-agent architectures to manufacture synthetic consensus at a scale and adaptability beyond prior botnets. [[Tang2026-gu]] documents the industrialisation of AI-generated viral content via "matrix account systems" in China, and [[Graham2025-gp]] and [[Zhao2025-ny]] examine how propaganda campaigns (#IStandWithPutin, hashtag hijacking) exploit trust infrastructures and attention dynamics — together sketching the next problem set the CooRnet-to-VERA-AI Alert lineage will need to confront: coordination that is synthetic not just in account identity but in the very content and reasoning driving it.
