# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The CooRnet lineage: from election monitoring to a general workflow

The backbone of this topic is a single methodological lineage that begins with [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which reframes disinformation research away from content-veracity judgments and toward coordinated collective action: pages, groups, and profiles sharing identical URLs in suspiciously tight time windows. This "coordinated link sharing behavior" (CLSB) operationalization, built into the open-source CooRnet package, becomes the thread the rest of the researcher's own work pulls on. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] turns the method into an applied fact-checking tool during the COVID-19 infodemic, showing that CLSB surfaces problematic content at a higher hit-rate than routine manual fact-checking and documenting how coordinated actors launder links, hide them in first comments, or cross-post into unrelated religious and community groups to evade detection. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] then solves the staleness problem inherent to any list-based approach: a workflow that starts from a seed of known coordinated accounts and iteratively expands the list by monitoring over-performing posts, catching ideologically, commercially, and religiously motivated operations side by side during the 2022 Italian election. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends the same corpus of covid-skeptic coordinated accounts to trace how "Alternative Influence Networks," and especially an Intellectual Dark Web cluster, remediate legacy-media and TV statements into shareable conspiracy content — showing that coordination studies need to look past the network itself to the hybrid media ecosystem that feeds it.

This program culminates in the VERA-AI-funded strand of work. [[Rogers2026-cy]] generalizes the detection logic into the "post-truth space" concept, combining network mapping with digital provenance investigation and an influence metric based on proximity to mainstream clusters, demonstrated on Moldovan Facebook discourse about the Russia-Ukraine war — crucially showing that the most consequential nodes are often not the post-truth clusters themselves but "borderline" bridges between them and the mainstream. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] pushes the CooRnet-style monitoring workflow onto three structurally disparate Facebook operations (pro-Putin propaganda, gambling promotion, poorly moderated adult-content groups) to argue that Meta's narrow CIB/CSH categories miss a broader class of "Deceptive Information Operations" defined by the convergence of deceptive intent, platform-affordance exploitation, and coordination — regardless of whether false content is present at all. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] applies the same monitoring infrastructure to document how generative AI has transformed one of these zombie-internet strands, gambling promotion, producing an exponential, dateable step-change in volume after ChatGPT's launch and new AI-generated visual persuasion repertoires that blend with established drivers like FOMO and manufactured trust. Read together, these five papers trace an arc from "detect coordinated link-sharing" to "build a living monitoring infrastructure" to "generalize the concept of what counts as problematic coordination" to "track how generative AI reshapes the phenomenon in real time" — the core trajectory this whole topic radiates from.

## Conceptual foundations and definitional debates

A second cluster interrogates what "coordination" and "inauthenticity" actually mean, largely predating or running parallel to the CooRnet program but essential to situating it. [[Starbird2019-qv]] supplies the foundational reframe that disinformation is collaborative *work*, performed partly by orchestrated accounts and partly by authentic, unwitting participants, making the orchestrated/organic boundary empirically blurry — a problem every detection paper in this topic must eventually confront. [[Keller2019-nk]] gives this a rigorous political-economic grounding via principal-agent theory, using South Korea's NIS Twitter campaign as rare ground truth to show that coordination traces, not bot-like individual features, are the most robust signal of astroturfing, while also showing that even professionalized human-staffed campaigns had modest discourse-level impact. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] formalizes coordination as a continuum rather than a binary, demonstrating on the 2019 UK election that coordination and automation are largely orthogonal and that the most tightly coordinated clusters are often small activist fringes rather than mainstream party machines.

These empirical papers set up the more recent theoretical reckonings. [[Thiele2025-ol]] directly critiques the CIB concept as conflating inauthenticity with manipulative intent, proposing instead a rational-choice attribution framework built around scale, elaborateness, and disguise as signals of a hidden principal's resources and stakes. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] performs the field-wide synthesis, reconciling platform and academic definitions into a general three-part model (actors, synergic actions, intent) and four orthogonal dimensions (authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, time-variance), explicitly arguing that coordination research should decouple from the threat-centric CIB framing that platforms like Meta popularized — a direct theoretical complement to the empirical expansions of CIB attempted by [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] and [[Rogers2026-cy]].

## Methodological innovation: beyond timestamps and single platforms

A dense cluster of recent papers extends coordination-detection methodology technically, largely in dialogue with the CooRnet/Nizzoli lineage. [[Yang2025-iv]] attacks the core vulnerability of timing-based detection (trivially manipulable) by exploiting statistical regularities in sharing speed and frequency instead. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] both tackle multimodality and temporality: the former builds a time-decayed multiplex collaboration model that outperforms single-modality baselines across 26 benchmark datasets, while the latter systematically compares five ways of integrating modalities (hashtags, retweets, mentions, URLs) and finds an inherent trade-off between integration strength and detection inclusiveness, with multiplex community detection as the most balanced compromise. [[Minici2024-tf]] moves toward foundation-model generalization, combining language models and graph neural networks (IOHunter) to detect information-operation drivers across heterogeneous state-sponsored campaigns, including out-of-distribution and few-shot settings. [[Gerard2025-br]] reframes cross-platform coordination entirely around shared narrative participation rather than direct interaction ties, identifying a tiny set of "bridge users" who seed the majority of narratives migrating between Truth Social and X. Platform-specific adaptations round out this cluster: [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] pushes toward real-time bot-coordination detection on the decentralized Bluesky platform, and [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts the video-first affordances of TikTok, arguing that text-centric detection frameworks cannot be naively transplanted.

## Does coordination matter? Questioning CIB's causal weight

A notable counter-current within the topic empirically interrogates whether detected coordination actually drives outcomes, complicating any assumption that finding a coordinated network is equivalent to finding an influential one. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] develop post-hoc, graph-theoretic frameworks for quantifying coordinated accounts' actual influence within retweet cascades, finding that real coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election dataset achieve influence far below optimal or even resource-matched greedy benchmarks — their placement resembles random assignment more than strategic targeting. [[Simeone2025-vo]] arrives at a structurally similar conclusion from a different angle: the dramatic network collapse following Twitter's 2021 deplatforming of election-fraud accounts was not attributable to coordinated inauthentic behavior (only ten accounts met coordination thresholds) but to the removal of a few authentically central human hubs. [[Pante2025-pq]] extends this skepticism to the inter-state level, arguing that prior claims of collaboration between state-backed information operations do not hold up once modern multi-signal detection and proper control datasets are applied. [[Appel2026-qr]], working with Meta's own platform-level exposure data on 49 US 2020 election networks, complicates the picture further: most reach came not from network accounts directly but from ordinary users resharing their content, and correlations between exposure and downstream beliefs largely disappear once pre-exposure characteristics are controlled — a sobering empirical check on inflated causal narratives about CIB's political effects that pairs naturally with the cascade-influence skepticism of the Di Marco papers.

## Geographically and institutionally situated cases

A broad set of case studies applies coordination detection to specific national and platform contexts, each adding texture to how coordination manifests under different political-economic conditions. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] reconstructs the organizational anatomy of the St Petersburg IRA from leaked employee CVs, showing it functioned as an ordinary media-labor-market firm rather than a state-embedded unit, and that it predates and outlives the 2016 US election as its supposed raison d'être. Bastos2025-ol mines the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium's troll-farm archive for visual, rather than textual or network, signatures of national origin. [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] traces message-forwarding coordination among pro-government Telegram channels in Russia and Belarus, while [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] identifies over 13,000 coordinated accounts straddling both protest mobilization and state-aligned repression during China's 2022 COVID protests — a dual-use framing of coordination infrastructure. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues that Serbia's ruling SNS party represents a qualitatively subtler, harder-to-detect form of digital authoritarianism than centralized bot farms, mimicking grassroots support rather than mechanically amplifying it — a direct empirical challenge to detection methods tuned for more overtly synchronized campaigns. [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as a form of political brokerage mediating between clients, platforms, and voters, foregrounding organizational infrastructure over content. [[Kim2026-br]] contributes a two-decade, explainable-ML analysis of suspected state-linked trolling in Korean news comments, showing that morally condemning rhetoric — not direct praise of foreign actors — is the dominant and most algorithmically rewarded strategy. [[Kansaon2025-id]] extends coordination detection into WhatsApp's encrypted ecosystem in Brazil, linking coordinated messaging networks directly to real-world mobilization including the January 8 insurrection.

## Substantive domains: health, elections, and discourse contests

Several papers apply coordination methods to specific content arenas. On health communication, [[Song2025-yh]] uses CooRnet itself to compare pro- and anti-vaccine CLSB networks across the UK and US, finding that coordination is ideologically agnostic — serving both misinformation and credible public-health messaging depending on who deploys it — while [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] traces how a small, non-automated but tightly coordinated contrarian retweet network amplifies credentialed anti-consensus experts in COVID science discourse, with news coverage typically following rather than leading superspreader activity. On elections, [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive-user sharing patterns across Romanian party pages during the 2024 EP campaign, and [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] shows bots functioning as agenda-builders that most strongly shape campaign sentiment rather than press or public discourse. [[Zhao2025-ny]] examines hashtag hijacking as a strategic maneuver combining attention capture, narrative substitution, and bot amplification, while [[Holland_Levin2026-qx]] traces the cross-platform, partisan co-optation of "woke" discourse, finding coordinated asymmetries between YouTube's content "supply" role and Twitter's "demand" role for conservative content. [[Goel2025-iq]] complicates the whole misinformation-detection paradigm by showing that mainstream, factually accurate articles are frequently co-shared to support misleading narratives — a phenomenon invisible to source-level or coordination-only methods. [[Graham2025-gp]] offers a case study of #IStandWithPutin as propaganda that exploits, rather than evades, the platform infrastructures built to support credible information.

## The frontier: generative AI and the destabilization of "inauthentic"

A forward-looking cluster asks what coordination detection means once content generation itself becomes automated and adaptive. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates emergent coordination among LLM agents in a generative agent-based model, finding that mere mutual awareness among agents — without explicit deliberation — is nearly sufficient to produce real-world-like coordination signatures (network density, narrative convergence, synchronized amplification). [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a policy register, warning that LLM-powered multi-agent "swarms" constitute a qualitative escalation beyond prior botnets, capable of manufacturing consensus and poisoning future training data, and arguing existing detection and governance paradigms are inadequate to the coming threat. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] contributes the simpler but important observation that manipulation campaigns persist by continually appropriating new emerging events, a form of longitudinal adaptivity detection workflows like [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] are explicitly designed to track. Set against these speculative and simulated threats, [[Graham2026-fb]] offers a deliberately destabilizing counterpoint: Reddit's "Anus_Fungi" phenomenon shows that what looks like a bot invasion can be a human-driven participatory meme practice, arguing that the human/bot and authentic/inauthentic binaries this entire topic depends on are themselves culturally negotiated rather than technical facts — a provocation that echoes Thiele's and Mannocci's definitional critiques from the opposite, more whimsical direction. Finally, [[Donovan2025-ws]] historicizes the whole enterprise, showing how "coordinated inauthentic behavior" itself emerged as a politically palatable, truth-agnostic moderation category that let platforms act on behavioral signals without adjudicating veracity — a genealogy that explains why so much of this topic's methodological energy, from CooRnet onward, has been invested in behavioral rather than content-based detection in the first place.
