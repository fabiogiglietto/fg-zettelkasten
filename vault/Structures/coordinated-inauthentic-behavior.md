# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The CooRnet lineage: from link-sharing detection to a research program

The methodological backbone of this topic is the sequence of papers built around Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) and the CooRnet toolkit. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] lays the conceptual foundation, arguing that disinformation research should pivot from adjudicating content veracity or hunting "bad actors" to detecting *coordinated collective action*—operationalizing Facebook's own "coordinated inauthentic behavior" language through networks of pages, groups, and profiles that share identical URLs near-simultaneously and repeatedly. Applied to the 2018 and 2019 Italian elections, this reveals that coordinated networks disproportionately amplify problematic domains and previously flagged disinformation sources, and that the balance of openly political versus deceptively "non-political" entities in a network predicts distinct sharing strategies. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] operationalizes this approach for real-world fact-checking during COVID-19, showing CLSB surfacing a covid-skeptic network that grew out of pre-existing Italian political clusters and cataloguing the evasive tactics (link laundering, first-comment link hiding, religious-page cross-posting) that coordinated actors use to survive platform enforcement. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] then solves the staleness problem inherent to any list-based detection: a workflow that iteratively expands and updates coordinated-account lists in near-real time, applied to the 2022 Italian snap election to surface not only political hyperpartisanship but also click-economy and religious-proselytism operations—demonstrating that the same behavioral signature (coordination) cuts across ideological, commercial, and religious motives. Most recently, [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] extends the program into the generative-AI era, using the vera.ai project's identification of coordinated Facebook gambling networks to show how ChatGPT's arrival triggered an exponential, structurally-detectable jump in coordinated posting volume, and how generative visual content intensifies rather than replaces earlier persuasion drivers. Read together, these four papers narrate the CLSB/CooRnet program's own arc: from conceptual grounding, to applied fact-checking tool, to a dynamic monitoring workflow, to confronting the AI-augmented next generation of the same underlying behavior.

## Extending and interrogating the detection methodology

A cluster of papers treats coordination detection itself as an open technical problem, often explicitly building on or benchmarking against the CooRnet-style approach. [[Yang2025-iv]] responds directly to a core CLSB vulnerability—reliance on post-timing, which is trivially manipulable—by exploiting statistical regularities in sharing speed and frequency across 11.2 million Facebook link posts. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] push the field toward multimodality and temporality: the former systematically compares five ways of operationalizing coordination across retweets, replies, mentions, hashtags, and URLs, finding an inherent trade-off between integration strength and detection inclusiveness; the latter proposes a time-aware multiplex model that outperforms single-modality and time-agnostic baselines across 26 benchmark datasets. [[Minici2024-tf]] moves detection into the foundation-model paradigm with IOHunter, combining language and graph neural models to generalize across state-sponsored campaigns even in few-shot settings. [[Gerard2025-br]]'s CANE/t-CANE framework reframes cross-platform influence structurally, linking users through shared narrative-cluster participation rather than platform-specific interaction traces, and using this to trace how a small set of "bridge users" seed narrative migration between Truth Social and X. Platform-specific adaptation is its own sub-thread: [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts the video-first architecture of TikTok, [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] targets real-time bot-coordination detection on the decentralized Bluesky, and [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts a "Rapid Spread Network" approach from Twitter research to the encrypted, closed ecosystem of Brazilian WhatsApp groups, connecting coordinated messaging to real-world mobilization around the January 8, 2023 attacks. [[Pante2025-pq]] turns methodological scrutiny inward, re-testing prior claims of inter-state CIB collaboration with more rigorous controls and finding earlier conclusions may not hold up—a useful corrective note within a field prone to overclaiming coordination from thin signals.

## Conceptual stock-taking: what "coordination" and "inauthenticity" even mean

Running alongside the technical papers is a conceptual critique of the field's foundational vocabulary. [[Thiele2025-ol]] argues that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and is poorly suited to platforms without real-name policies; it proposes a rational-choice model and an eight-type typology that reconstructs the strategic decisions of covert campaign "principals" from observable features (scale, elaborateness, disguise) rather than content. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] contributes a thinner but complementary observation—that manipulation campaigns persist by appropriating emerging events rather than dying out. At the boundary of the concept, [[Graham2026-fb]]'s study of Reddit "botting" (mushroom-emoji spam) explicitly destabilizes the human/bot and authentic/inauthentic binaries that underwrite CIB detection, arguing that authenticity is a culturally negotiated performance rather than a technical property—a useful caution against over-mechanizing what counts as "coordination worth flagging."

## Case studies across regimes, platforms, and issues

A large empirical layer applies coordination detection (often explicitly CLSB-derived) to specific political and health contexts. [[Song2025-yh]] uses CooRnet directly to compare pro- and anti-vaccine CLSB networks in the UK and US, finding CLSB is ideologically agnostic—equally capable of amplifying misinformation or scaling credible public-health messaging depending on who wields it. [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive-user amplification on Facebook during Romania's 2024 European Parliament campaign, while [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] traces coordinated forwarding among pro-government Telegram channels in Russia and Belarus, and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] analyzes multilingual coordinated Twitter activity around China's 2022 COVID protests as simultaneously enabling mobilization and state-aligned repression. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] extends the geography further, arguing Serbia's ruling party runs a subtler, harder-to-detect form of CIB that mimics authentic grassroots support rather than relying on centralized bot farms—an implicit challenge to detection methods tuned to more mechanical coordination signatures. [[Kim2026-br]] applies an explainable ML framework to two decades of Korean news comments, finding suspected state-linked trolls favor morally condemning rhetoric over direct praise, which is disproportionately amplified by engagement-based ranking. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] steps back from network detection entirely to reconstruct the organizational and labor-market realities of the St Petersburg IRA via OSINT on employee CVs, complementing behavioral detection with production-side ethnography, while [[Gaw2025-ru]] similarly reframes Philippine influence operations as a form of political brokerage grounded in qualitative fieldwork rather than network traces. [[Graham2025-gp]] examines #IStandWithPutin as propaganda that exploits rather than evades the platform "infrastructure of truth," and Bastos2025-ol mines the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium's troll-farm dataset for visual (rather than textual or network) signatures of state origin. [[Simeone2025-vo]] offers a valuable counter-note: analyzing Twitter's 2021 deplatforming of Arizona "election audit" accounts, it explicitly finds CIB was *not* a meaningful driver of that network's activity, and that targeted removal of human hub accounts—not coordinated inauthenticity—explains the community's collapse.

## Scale, causality, and skepticism about impact

A distinct strand asks whether detected coordination actually matters for outcomes. The paired Meta-collaboration studies [[Appel2026-qr]] and [[noauthor_undated-bm]] analyze 49 deceptive networks (CIB plus financially-motivated operations) around the 2020 US election, showing reach is concentrated in a handful of networks, overwhelmingly driven by reshares from unaffiliated accounts rather than direct network posting, and that apparent associations with political outcomes evaporate once pre-exposure user characteristics are controlled for—a sobering methodological check on causal overreach. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] formalizes this skepticism graph-theoretically, showing that real coordinated accounts in UK election retweet cascades achieve influence far below optimal or even resource-matched benchmarks, and that their placement resembles random assignment rather than strategic targeting. [[Goel2025-iq]] complicates the picture from another angle, showing that domain-level misinformation metrics miss a larger phenomenon of factually true mainstream content being co-shared to support misleading narratives—suggesting coordination-and-authenticity frameworks need to look beyond source reliability to narrative repurposing.

## The emerging frontier: LLM-driven coordination

A final cluster looks forward to how generative AI reshapes the phenomenon itself. [[Schroeder2026-im]] maps the threat of "malicious AI swarms"—LLM-driven multi-agent systems capable of persistent, adaptive, cross-platform coordination that could manufacture synthetic consensus beyond the reach of earlier botnets, and proposes layered governance responses. [[Orlando2025-ul]] provides an empirical complement via generative agent-based modeling, showing that LLM agents spontaneously reproduce real-world coordination mechanics (narrative convergence, synchronized amplification, hashtag adoption) as a function of mutual awareness alone, without explicit deliberation—an early experimental bridge between the swarm-threat literature and the detection methodologies developed for human-operated networks. Read against [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]'s empirical finding of an AI-driven volume explosion in gambling promotion, these papers suggest the CLSB/CIB paradigm's next challenge is less about refining detection signals for human-coordinated networks and more about anticipating an entirely AI-native mode of coordination.

## Adjacent currents: bots, attention, and authenticity performance

Rounding out the topic are papers on bot-adjacent dynamics that inform but sit slightly outside core CIB detection: [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] treats social bots as agenda-builders shaping campaign sentiment via algorithmic amplification; [[Zhao2025-ny]] examines hashtag hijacking as a bot-assisted narrative-capture tactic; and [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] traces how a small, non-automated but genuinely coordinated contrarian network amplifies credentialed anti-consensus experts in COVID science discourse, later picked up by low-trust news outlets—illustrating how coordination detection intersects with expertise, credibility, and cross-media diffusion well beyond the original CLSB use case.
