# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The Italian origins and the CooRnet lineage

This line of work begins, methodologically, in the run-up to the 2018 and 2019 Italian elections, where [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] reframes disinformation research around *coordinated link sharing behavior* (CLSB) rather than content veracity or actor blacklisting — an ecological, action-based move that becomes the field's signature contribution. The paper shows that networks sharing URLs near-simultaneously are dramatically more likely to circulate problematic domains, and that the balance of openly political versus deceptively "non-political" entities in a network predicts different sharing strategies. This becomes the CooRnet toolkit, extended into a continuously-updating workflow in [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], which tracks the 2022 Italian snap election and demonstrates that static blacklists quickly become stale; three case studies (a hyperpartisan M5S network, a religious-page clickbait operation, and a Church of Almighty God proselytism network) show how actor-agnostic, behavior-first detection surfaces ideologically, economically, and religiously motivated operations alike. The COVID-19 period produces a parallel application in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], where CLSB is paired with fact-checkers (MINE-FACTS/Facta.news) to speed up identification of an emergent Italian covid-skeptic coordinated network, documenting tactics — link laundering, first-comment link placement, religious-group piggybacking — that manipulators use to evade enforcement. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends this corpus by tracing how "Alternative Influence Network" and Intellectual Dark Web figures are remediated through legacy media into these same coordinated clusters, showing hybridity between mainstream and fringe actors rather than a clean separation.

Running alongside the Italian CooRnet strand is a parallel, convergent lineage from Starbird, Nizzoli, and Keller that supplies the field's conceptual vocabulary. [[Starbird2019-qv]] reframes information operations as *collaborative work* sustained by crowds, not just bots and trolls, insisting that orchestrated and organic participation are often empirically inseparable. [[Keller2019-nk]], using rare ground-truth data from the Korean NIS astroturfing scandal, shows that coordination traces — not bot-like individual signatures — are the most robust and hardest-to-hide fingerprint of a managed campaign, while also finding that even a well-resourced, human-staffed operation had only modest measurable impact. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] formalizes coordination as a continuum rather than a binary, demonstrating on 2019 UK election data that coordination and automation are orthogonal, and that the most tightly coordinated communities are often small activist or antagonist clusters rather than mainstream parties.

## Does coordination actually move the needle?

A recurring and increasingly prominent thread questions whether detected coordination translates into real influence. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] develop post-hoc, graph-theoretic frameworks for quantifying coordinated accounts' causal contribution to cascade diffusion, finding that real coordinated accounts in UK election data exert influence far below optimal or even randomly-matched benchmarks — suggesting CIB may be less pivotal than assumed, due to scarce resources and unstrategic placement. [[Simeone2025-vo]] complicates the picture from the content-moderation side: a targeted 2021 Twitter deplatforming of election-audit accounts produced network-wide "ripple effects" disproportionate to the accounts removed, even though the affected community showed almost no coordinated inauthentic behavior — implying that authentic human mobilization, not bot coordination, was doing the structural work. [[Pante2025-pq]] applies modern multi-signal coordination detection with control datasets to re-examine prior claims of inter-state collaboration between information operations, arguing such claims were methodologically premature. [[Appel2026-qr]], working from Meta's FIES collaboration data on 49 deceptive 2020-election networks, shows that exposure is extremely concentrated (three networks drove most reach, largely via *reshares by unaffiliated users* rather than direct network posting), and that correlations between exposure and downstream beliefs mostly evaporate once pre-exposure characteristics are controlled — a sobering empirical check on causal narratives about influence-operation impact. Together these papers mark a maturing, more skeptical phase of the field: detection has outpaced causal evaluation, and the latter increasingly undercuts maximalist claims about CIB's power.

## Methodological escalation: multiplex, temporal, and foundation-model detection

A dense cluster of recent papers pushes CLSB-style detection methodology forward technically. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically compares five ways of operationalizing *multimodal* coordination (retweets, replies, mentions, hashtags, URLs), finding a fundamental trade-off between integration strength and detection inclusiveness, with multiplex community detection as the best balance — a finding sharpened by [[Iannucci2025-eg]], which adds a temporal decay kernel atop multiplex layers and validates it against 26 benchmark datasets, outperforming single-modality and fixed-time-window baselines, particularly against dilution tactics. [[Yang2025-iv]] attacks the timing-manipulability problem directly, proposing statistical regularities in sharing *speed and frequency* as a more evasion-resistant Facebook coordination signal than post-timing alone. [[Minici2024-tf]] brings graph foundation models (language models plus GNNs) to bear on cross-campaign generalization, achieving strong out-of-distribution and few-shot detection of IO "drivers." [[Gerard2025-br]]'s CANE/t-CANE framework abandons interaction-based graphs altogether in favor of latent narrative-cluster affiliation, identifying a tiny set of "bridge users" responsible for most narrative migration between Truth Social and X around the 2024 US election — a structural, discourse-centric alternative to behavioral coordination networks. [[Mannocci2026-kc]]'s PRISMA survey of 122 papers steps back to synthesize this entire methodological landscape, proposing actors/actions/intent as the field's common denominator and arguing coordination should be studied as orthogonal to authenticity and harm, not conflated with platform-defined "CIB."

## Beyond Twitter and Facebook: new platforms, new publics

The methodology is increasingly ported to platforms it was not built for. [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts TikTok's video-first ecosystem, where text-centric network-similarity approaches do not transfer cleanly. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] pushes toward real-time bot-coordination detection on the decentralized Bluesky. [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts rapid-spread-network detection to WhatsApp's closed, encrypted Brazilian political groups, linking coordinated messaging directly to real-world mobilization (the January 8, 2023 attacks). [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] applies forwarding-network analysis to pro-government Telegram channels in Russia and Belarus, and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] examines multilingual coordinated Twitter activity around China's 2022 COVID protests as dual-use infrastructure for both mobilization and state-aligned repression. Each extension confirms the core CooRnet insight — coordination is behaviorally detectable independent of content — while surfacing platform-specific affordances (forwarding limits, encrypted groups, video similarity) that complicate direct transfer of Facebook/Twitter-era techniques.

## Case studies: elections, health, and authoritarian information ecosystems

A substantial empirical layer applies coordination detection to specific political and health contexts. [[Song2025-yh]] compares pro- and anti-vaccine CLSB in the UK and US, finding CLSB is ideologically agnostic — serving both misinformation and credible public-health messaging depending on who deploys it. [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive-user amplification across all major parties in Romania's 2024 EP election. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues Serbia's ruling SNS party deploys a subtler, harder-to-detect mimicry-based CIB than centralized bot farms, evading standard detection. [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as a form of political brokerage grounded in interviews with practitioners, emphasizing political-economic infrastructure over content. [[Kim2026-br]] offers a two-decade, cross-national view of suspected state-linked trolling in Korean news comments, showing morally condemning rhetoric (rather than direct boosting) is the dominant and most-amplified strategy. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] turns an OSINT lens on the organizational history of the St Petersburg Internet Research Agency itself, recasting it as a labor-market actor rather than a purely covert operation. [[Zhao2025-ny]] examines hashtag hijacking as a narrative-capture tactic, and [[Graham2025-gp]]'s #IStandWithPutin case study argues propaganda now exploits, rather than evades, the platform infrastructures of truth-verification. [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] extends agenda-building theory to show bots function as a genuine fourth communicator class shaping US campaign sentiment. [[Copland2025-em]] and [[Holland_Levin2026-qx]] trace cross-platform propaganda circuits — Sky News Australia's dual Facebook/YouTube strategy bridging Australian and US right-wing ecosystems, and the politicization of "woke" discourse synergistically across Twitter and YouTube. Bastos2025-ol mines the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium dataset for visual signatures of troll-farm profile images as an attribution signal.

## Theorizing coordination and the attribution problem

Several papers step back to interrogate the field's conceptual foundations. [[Thiele2025-ol]] argues that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent, and proposes a rational-choice typology linking observable campaign features (scale, elaborateness, disguise) to principals' hidden strategic calculus — a direct challenge to platform-derived categories. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] similarly argues Meta's CIB/CSH categories are too narrow, proposing "Deceptive Information Operations" as a convergence of deceptive content, platform-affordance exploitation, and coordination, illustrated through pro-Putin, gambling, and poorly-moderated-group case studies that together evoke a "Zombie Internet" where human/automated and authentic/manipulated boundaries dissolve. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] contributes the observation that manipulation campaigns persist by opportunistically appropriating emerging events rather than remaining static.

## The generative-AI frontier

The newest stratum confronts how LLMs reshape both the threat and its detection. [[Schroeder2026-im]] theorizes "malicious AI swarms" — agentic, persistent, cross-platform coordination that qualitatively exceeds botnet-era threats — and proposes layered governance responses. [[Orlando2025-ul]] empirically simulates this scenario with generative agent-based modeling, showing that mere *awareness* of teammates among LLM agents produces coordination levels nearly matching explicit collective deliberation, with network density, narrative homogeneity, and hashtag adoption all escalating as operational structure increases. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]], working within the VERA-AI project, documents a real-world correlate: an exponential, structurally-detected surge in coordinated Facebook gambling-promotion volume immediately following ChatGPT's launch, with generative AI intensifying (not replacing) existing visual persuasion drivers. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] shows a non-automated but still coordinated contrarian network amplifying credentialed anti-consensus experts in COVID science discourse, with news media typically following rather than leading superspreader activity — underscoring that coordination's amplification role often precedes and shapes mainstream coverage rather than vice versa.

## VERA-AI and applied monitoring infrastructure

Finally, [[Rogers2026-cy]] exemplifies the applied, verification-oriented arm of this agenda: the "post-truth space" concept, developed for the vera.ai project, combines network mapping, source-provenance investigation, and an influence metric to help fact-checkers locate not just fringe clusters but the "borderline" content bridging them into the mainstream — demonstrated on Moldovan Facebook discourse around the Russia-Ukraine war. Read together with [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] and [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], this constitutes the line's most consistent practical output: not merely academic detection methods but operational monitoring systems (CooRnet, the vera.ai Alert pipeline) designed to keep pace with manipulators who adapt faster than static lists or content classifiers ever could — the same ambition that, a decade on from the 2018 Italian election, now has to contend with LLM-driven swarms as its newest adversary.
