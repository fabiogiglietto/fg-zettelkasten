# Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour

## The CooRnet lineage: from CLSB to platform-agnostic monitoring

The methodological backbone of this collection runs through [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which first operationalised "coordinated link sharing behaviour" (CLSB) as a content-agnostic alternative to actor- or veracity-based disinformation detection: networks of pages, groups, and profiles sharing identical URLs near-simultaneously and repeatedly. That paper's ecological turn — treat coordination itself as the signal, regardless of what is being shared — becomes the throughline for everything that follows under this topic. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] operationalises the same logic as a working tool (MINE-FACTS, built on the CooRnet R package) for Italian COVID-19 fact-checkers, showing CLSB-flagged content is substantially more likely to be problematic than routine fact-checking baselines, while cataloguing the evasive tactics coordinated actors use (link laundering, first-comment link placement, religious-group camouflage). [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] then addresses the central weakness of any list-based approach — obsolescence — by turning detection into a continuously updating workflow, seeding from known coordinated accounts to surface new ones during the 2022 Italian election and uncovering not just political but click-economy and religious-proselytism operations. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends the pipeline from network detection to downstream content analysis, showing how "Alternative Influence Network" and Intellectual Dark Web figures are laundered into mainstream Italian Facebook discourse via legacy-media remediation.

This lineage's most recent instalments show the paradigm moving from academic prototype to applied infrastructure and confronting new adversarial dynamics. [[Rogers2026-cy]], conducted under the vera.ai project, generalises CLSB-adjacent mapping into the "post-truth space" concept, adding provenance investigation and an influence metric to locate fact-checking targets in a Moldovan Russia-Ukraine case — explicitly positioning the toolkit as service infrastructure for verification professionals (the conceptual ancestor of the VERA-AI Alert system named in this topic). [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] then confront generative AI as a force multiplier: the former documents an exponential, ChatGPT-coincident explosion in coordinated gambling-promotion imagery exploiting Meta's paid/organic regulatory gap, while the latter proposes "Deceptive Information Operations" as a framework broader than Meta's own CIB/CSH categories, arguing that pro-Kremlin propaganda, gambling networks, and poorly moderated adult-content groups share a common structural logic of concealment, affordance exploitation, and multi-account coordination that content-based enforcement cannot capture. Together these mark the signature methodology's maturation from single-platform, single-country detection into a general framework for auditing platform governance failures.

## Detection methodology as a contested, evolving craft

A large cluster of papers treats detection itself as the object of methodological innovation and critique, in dialogue with rather than direct extension of the CooRnet line. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] is the field-defining survey here, synthesising 122 papers to argue that coordination is an orthogonal dimension — separable from authenticity, harm, and orchestration — that most CIB-focused work conflates, and it maps the shared toolkit (co-action networks, similarity functions, time-window choices, community detection) that every other paper in this set instantiates in some form. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] operationalises the multiplex problem the survey identifies, comparing five ways of handling multimodal coordination (retweets, replies, mentions, hashtags, URLs) and finding a fundamental trade-off between integration strength and detection inclusiveness, with multiplex community detection the most balanced compromise. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] pushes further by adding a temporal decay kernel to multiplex modelling, arguing that both synchronicity and frequency are needed to distinguish deliberate coordination from coincidence, and validating against 26 benchmark datasets. [[Yang2025-iv]] attacks the same fragility from a different angle, proposing detection based on the statistical regularity of sharing *speed and frequency* rather than post-timing, precisely because timing is trivially manipulable by sophisticated actors.

Several papers extend detection to new platforms and representational strategies. [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts the video-first ecosystem of TikTok, arguing text-centric detection frameworks do not transfer. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] targets real-time bot coordination on the decentralised platform Bluesky. [[Gerard2025-br]] proposes a genuinely platform-agnostic solution — Cluster Affiliation Network Embedding — that links users through shared latent narrative participation rather than platform-specific interaction signals, enabling it to trace "bridge users" who seed narrative migration between Truth Social and X with far less data than interaction-based methods require. [[Minici2024-tf]] takes the foundation-model route, combining language models and graph neural networks (IOHunter) to generalise IO-driver detection across six state-sponsored campaigns, addressing the perennial problem that labelled ground truth is scarce and campaign-specific.

## Pushing back: does coordination detection over-claim?

A skeptical countercurrent interrogates whether detected coordination is as consequential, or as robustly identified, as the field assumes. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] formally models influence in retweet cascades and finds that real coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election achieve influence far below optimal or even resource-matched greedy benchmarks — their placement looks statistically like random assignment, suggesting CIB may be less pivotal to diffusion than commonly assumed. [[Pante2025-pq]] revisits published claims of inter-state CIB collaboration and argues, using modern multi-signal detection and control datasets, that prior findings were methodologically fragile. [[Simeone2025-vo]] complements this from the moderation side: in the Arizona Election Review deplatforming case, only 10 accounts met coordination thresholds, meaning the dramatic post-ban collapse of mobilization was driven by ordinary human disengagement, not by removing bot networks — a caution against assuming CIB explains observed network effects. [[Thiele2025-ol]] mounts the most systematic conceptual challenge, arguing "coordinated inauthentic behaviour" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and is poorly suited to platforms without real-name policies; it proposes a rational-choice attribution model and an eight-type typology built around *intent* rather than inauthenticity per se, explicitly positioning itself against Meta-derived CIB vocabulary. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]], more briefly, insists on the temporal durability of manipulation campaigns as they latch onto emerging events — a reminder that detection snapshots understate persistence.

## Case studies: elections, authoritarianism, and closed platforms

A substantial share of the corpus applies coordination detection to specific political contexts, testing the methodology's portability. The Meta-collaborative FIES study — appearing as both [[Appel2026-qr]] and [[noauthor_undated-bm]] — is the most data-rich case, showing that 49 deceptive networks reached tens of millions of US Facebook/Instagram users in 2020, that reach is extremely concentrated and driven mainly by reshares from *non-network* accounts, and, notably, that engagement/exposure correlations with political outcomes largely vanish after controlling for pre-exposure characteristics — a sobering methodological lesson for causal claims elsewhere in the literature. [[Oprea2025-lf]] finds comparable amplification dynamics at Facebook-page level during Romania's 2024 EP campaign via hyperactive-user sharing patterns. [[Kansaon2025-id]] extends coordination detection into the closed ecosystem of WhatsApp, tracing Brazilian coordinated groups from misinformation dissemination to real-world mobilization around the January 8 attacks. [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] apply coordination detection to authoritarian information ecosystems — pro-government Telegram amplification in Russia/Belarus and dual-use protest/repression Twitter activity around China's COVID protests — while [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues Serbia's SNS-linked networks represent an evolutionarily *harder* case, mimicking authentic grassroots support so convincingly that standard detection frameworks (implicitly, CIB-style tools) struggle to catch them, calling for new analytical categories beyond bot-farm centralism.

Several papers examine coordination's organisational and human infrastructure rather than its network signature. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] reconstructs the IRA's internal labour-market structure via leaked CVs, complicating the "troll factory as covert intelligence unit" image with evidence of ordinary media-industry employment dynamics. [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as political brokerage, emphasising economic infrastructure over content. Bastos2025-ol mines the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium dataset for visual self-presentation signatures that might permit attribution by country of origin. [[Kim2026-br]] applies explainable ML to two decades of Korean news comments, finding suspected state-linked trolls rely disproportionately on morally condemning rhetoric — amplified by engagement-based ranking — rather than direct praise of foreign actors, a finding that resonates with [[Graham2025-gp]]'s argument that propaganda succeeds by exploiting, not evading, the platform infrastructures built to signal trustworthiness (illustrated via #IStandWithPutin).

## Coordination beyond the "bad actor" frame

A final cluster complicates the assumption that coordination equals malice. [[Song2025-yh]] shows CLSB networks promoting *pro*-vaccine, credible public-health content just as readily as anti-vaccine misinformation, arguing coordination is ideologically agnostic and context-dependent — a direct empirical test of the [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] framework's premise that coordination, not content, is the meaningful unit of analysis. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] finds a contrarian-dominated coordinated retweet network amplifying credentialed anti-consensus COVID experts, notably *not* explained by bot automation, and shows news coverage typically follows rather than precedes this superspreader activity. [[Zhao2025-ny]] and [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] examine adjacent tactics — hashtag hijacking and bot-driven agenda-building — where coordination interacts with organic discourse in more diffuse, less clearly "inauthentic" ways; Arceneaux's Granger-causality analysis is notable for finding campaigns more vulnerable to bot agenda-setting than the press. [[Holland_Levin2026-qx]] traces cross-platform coordinated amplification (Twitter/YouTube) in the politicisation of "woke," showing conservative actors' synergistic cross-platform coordination without invoking bot/CIB machinery at all.

## The emerging frontier: generative and agentic AI

The newest arc concerns what happens when coordination becomes automatable end-to-end. [[Orlando2025-ul]] uses generative agent-based modelling to show that LLM agents spontaneously reproduce real-world IO coordination signatures — network density, narrative convergence, synchronized amplification — with mere teammate-awareness sufficing to approximate deliberate collective coordination, a striking demonstration that the behavioural signatures this whole topic is built to detect can emerge without any human orchestration at all. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a governance warning, arguing that LLM-plus-multi-agent architectures constitute a qualitative escape from the cost and cadence constraints that limited human-run troll farms like the IRA, and proposing layered detection, provenance, and observatory-style responses. Read alongside [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]'s empirical documentation of a ChatGPT-coincident surge in AI-generated gambling propaganda, these two papers suggest the CooRnet/CLSB-descended detection paradigm — built to find synchrony and repetition among human-operated accounts — is now being tested against adversaries that can synthesize convincing variation at will, setting the central methodological challenge for the next generation of this research line.
