# Coordinated Inauthentic Behaviour

## The methodological core: from Coordinated Link Sharing to a living workflow

The anchor of this line of work is the original coordinated link-sharing behaviour (CLSB) methodology, first laid out in [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] on the 2018 Italian election and the 2019 European election. That paper made the foundational argumentative move that structures everything downstream: disinformation research should stop asking "is this content false?" or "is this actor a bot?" and instead ask "are these accounts acting together?" Coordination, not falsity or automation, becomes the diagnostic signal, and the paper demonstrates empirically that coordinated networks disproportionately carry problematic domains. This ecological, action-based framing is operationalised in the CooRnet package and tested against a real crisis in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] (MINE-FACTS), which shows CLSB surfacing a far higher rate of problematic COVID-19 content than routine fact-checking, while also cataloguing the evasive tactics — link laundering, first-comment link-hiding, religious-group camouflage — that coordinated actors develop in response to detection. [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]] complicates the whole enterprise methodologically, showing that Meta's own 100-shares anonymisation threshold on the URL Shares Dataset introduces a platform-wide artifact (traceable to Feed-ranking changes) that can masquerade as a substantive cross-country social signal — a cautionary companion piece for any CLSB-adjacent study relying on Meta-provided data. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends the method from network detection to actor-level content classification, mapping an "Alternative Influence Network" of Intellectual-Dark-Web figures inside the Italian covid-skeptic coordinated cluster. The line matures into infrastructure in [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], which reframes static lists of known coordinated accounts as perpetually stale and proposes a near-real-time monitoring workflow — seed list, overperforming-post detection, near-duplicate expansion — validated on the 2022 Italian snap election and surfacing hyperpartisan, click-economy, and religious-proselytism operations side by side. This is the direct methodological ancestor of the VERA-AI Alert system.

## VERA-AI, the Alert system, and theorising "Deceptive Information Operations"

A second cluster pushes the workflow from detection tool to conceptual framework under vera.ai WP4. [[Rogers2026-cy]] introduces "post-truth spaces" — hard-to-verify or known-disinformation clusters located via co-sharing, provenance investigation, and an influence metric based on proximity to mainstream discourse — and demonstrates the approach on Moldovan Facebook discourse about the Russia-Ukraine war, explicitly building a tool for fact-checkers rather than a purely descriptive account. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] takes the argument furthest: analysing pro-Putin propaganda, coordinated gambling promotion, and poorly moderated adult-content groups together, it proposes "Deceptive Information Operations" (DIOs) as a category that supersedes CIB by foregrounding the *convergence* of deceptive content, platform-affordance exploitation, and coordination, rather than treating inauthenticity or falsity as necessary conditions — this is the paper that coins "Zombie Internet" for the resulting structural condition of Facebook's engagement-driven ecosystem. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] applies the same detection pipeline to gambling promotion specifically, documenting how generative AI produces an exponential, structurally-detectable break in posting volume after ChatGPT's launch and arguing that AI intensifies rather than replaces existing persuasion drivers. Together these three papers mark the project's most current theoretical move: away from platform-defined "authenticity" violations and toward structural, harm-agnostic accounts of manipulation.

## Platform governance, policy effects, and the data-access problem

A closely related strand turns the same instruments on platform *policy* itself. [[Giglietto2025-1765bb4f]] uses breakpoint detection on 2.5 million Meta Content Library posts to show that Meta's political-content-reduction policy hit Italian parliamentarians' Facebook reach roughly ten months before its announced global rollout, recovered only partially after reversal, and — critically — was compensated for by extremist accounts through higher posting frequency rather than higher reach per post, exposing a transparency gap in Meta's own disclosures. This sits alongside a wider set of papers interrogating the post-API research environment shaped by the EU Digital Services Act: [[Philipp2026-tl]] surveys the new DSA-mandated data portals for election research, [[Pierri2025-hm]] and [[de-Vreese2026-zx]] both assess Article 40 vetted-researcher access as a fraught but consequential lever against platform power, and [[Schulte2026-df]] documents the practical bottlenecks of multi-platform electoral-discourse research (X plus TikTok) around the 2025 German election, arguing that platform observability itself must be treated as reusable infrastructure. [[Jurg2025-ur]] audits YouTube's content-moderation claims during the 2024 EU elections and finds inconsistent application of authority-labelling across languages and broadcasters; [[Hurcombe2025-cs]] performs a discourse analysis of Meta's own Newsroom communications, showing how the "coordinated inauthentic behaviour" label functions rhetorically as a floating signifier that externalises blame from platform design. [[Inacio-da-Silva2026-zf]] and [[Oprea2025-lf]] extend the transparency-auditing impulse to Brazilian ad practices and Romanian hyperactive-user amplification respectively, both concluding that platform self-policing is insufficient absent independent, often crowdsourced, monitoring.

## Comparative case studies: coordination across regimes

Running parallel to the Italian-anchored programme is a widening set of national case studies that test whether CLSB-style coordination signatures travel across political systems. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues Serbia's ruling SNS party exemplifies a more mimetic, harder-to-detect form of digital authoritarianism than centralised bot farms; [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] finds message-forwarding coordination among pro-government Telegram channels in Russia and Belarus; [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] identifies over 13,000 coordinated accounts straddling protest mobilisation and state-aligned repression during China's 2022 COVID protests; [[Kim2026-br]] applies an explainable ML pipeline to two decades of Korean news comments, finding that suspected troll accounts favour morally condemning rhetoric over direct foreign-state praise, with condemnation disproportionately amplified by engagement-based ranking; and [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as a form of political brokerage grounded in interviews with IO practitioners, foregrounding the political-economic infrastructure rather than the content itself. [[Kansaon2025-id]] extends coordination detection into the encrypted WhatsApp ecosystem, tracing Brazilian coordinated messaging from the 2022 election through the January 8 insurrection. [[Song2025-yh]] applies CLSB directly to UK/US vaccine discourse, showing that coordination itself is ideologically agnostic — used by both pro- and anti-vaccine communities, with divergent content-credibility profiles. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] supplies organisational grounding for much of this literature, using leaked CVs to reconstruct the Internet Research Agency as a labour-market institution rather than a purely tactical entity, while Bastos2025-ol mines the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium's troll-farm dataset for visual, rather than textual or network, signatures of state origin.

## Computational advances: multiplex, multimodal, and foundation-model detection

A distinct methodological front, less tied to any single case, works to generalise and stress-test coordination-detection techniques. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] both argue that single-modality, time-agnostic detection is inadequate: the former proposes a time-decayed multiplex collaboration model, the latter systematically compares five multimodal operationalisations and finds multiplex community detection the best-balanced. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] consolidates the field in a PRISMA-guided survey, proposing a general definition built from actors, synergic actions, and intent, and insisting that coordination is orthogonal to authenticity and harm — a direct theoretical bridge to the DIO reframing in [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]]. [[Yang2025-iv]] targets a specific evasion vector, showing that link-sharing speed and frequency resist the timing manipulation that undermines earlier CLSB-style signals. [[Minici2024-tf]] brings graph foundation models to bear on cross-campaign generalisation, while [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] offers a sobering counterpoint: formally modelling influence in UK 2019-election retweet cascades, it finds real coordinated accounts achieve influence far below optimal placement and close to random, suggesting CIB may be less pivotal to diffusion than assumed. [[Thiele2025-ol]] tackles the attribution problem head-on, arguing CIB conflates inauthenticity with intent and proposing a rational-choice typology of principals instead. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] and [[Luceri2025-tr]] extend detection to new platform environments (Bluesky, TikTok's video-first ecosystem), [[Gerard2025-br]] models cross-platform narrative migration between Truth Social and X via shared discourse clusters rather than direct interaction, [[Pante2025-pq]] re-examines prior claims of inter-state coordination against stronger controls, and [[Zhao2025-ny]] analyses hashtag hijacking as a related but distinct attention-capture tactic involving bots and narrative substitution.

## US elections, exposure, and the limits of causal inference

A US-centric cluster interrogates how much coordinated and inauthentic activity actually mattered for outcomes, using unusually rich exposure data. [[Appel2026-qr]] and its companion draft [[noauthor_undated-bm]] analyse 49 Meta-identified deceptive networks from the 2020 election, finding reach highly concentrated in a few networks, driven mostly by reshares from unaffiliated users, and showing that naive associations with political outcomes disappear once pre-exposure characteristics are controlled — a methodologically cautious counterweight to alarmist readings of CIB's electoral effect. [[Kim2026-wg]] pushes back from a different angle, using direct ad-exposure tracking to show that 2016 IRA-linked voter-suppression ads exhibited real geo-racial targeting and produced measurable, heterogeneous turnout declines that average-treatment-effect designs would miss. [[Efstratiou2025-gs]] and [[Simeone2025-vo]] examine platform-level interventions rather than coordinated actors per se: the former shows Twitter's post-Musk algorithm rewarded agitation and Musk-proximity more than partisanship, the latter shows that deplatforming eight accounts produced network-wide "ripple effects" on the Arizona Election Review community that (notably) were *not* attributable to coordinated inauthentic behaviour, since only ten accounts met coordination thresholds. [[Bruns2026-yv]] provides the essayistic bookend, narrating Twitter's institutional decline and the migration of "participatory disinformation" tactics to successor platforms that have yet to solve the same governance problems.

## Beyond link-sharing: narratives, generative AI, and new threat models

A final set of papers pushes the object of study past URL-level coordination toward narrative-level and synthetic threats. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] and [[Ramos2026-qo]] both argue that claim-level fact-checking is insufficient against campaigns that persist by appropriating emerging events or by mutating into evolving narrative structures — the latter proposing "argument-checking" as a complement, grounded in the Valencia DANA flood case. [[Waight2025-al]] offers a scalable LLM-based method for measuring narrative (not lexical or topical) similarity across languages, applied to the diffusion of Russian "biolabs" claims into low-quality US outlets, and [[Goel2025-iq]] shows that domain-reliability classifications miss a large phenomenon of genuinely true mainstream content being repurposed to support misleading narratives. [[Graham2025-gp]] frames the #IStandWithPutin campaign as propaganda that exploits, rather than evades, the platform infrastructures of truth-verification. Looking forward, [[Schroeder2026-im]] and [[Orlando2025-ul]] anticipate a step-change in coordination itself: the former maps how LLM-driven "malicious AI swarms" could manufacture consensus autonomously, and the latter provides early empirical grounding via generative agent-based modelling, showing that mere mutual awareness among simulated IO agents is nearly sufficient to reproduce dense, synchronized, real-world-like coordination signatures — a foreshadowing of the detection challenges implied by [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]'s AI-driven gambling case. [[Donovan2025-ws]] historicises the whole arc, tracing how "misinformation-at-scale" and the CIB framework emerged from platforms' preference for behavioural over veracity-based moderation, and warning that the retreat of trust-and-safety investment since 2021 leaves this entire research programme increasingly reliant on external, adversarial data access.

## Adjacent currents

A wider penumbra of papers touches coordination and inauthenticity without making it the central object, but usefully triangulates the topic. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] traces a contrarian coordinated retweet network amplifying credentialed experts in COVID science communication; [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]] and [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] examine, respectively, influencer-driven toxicity and social bots as agenda-builders in organisational and campaign communication; [[Knupfer2025-vt]] and [[Copland2025-em]] analyse elite-media amplification dynamics (US "CRT Republicans," Sky News Australia's transnational propaganda pipeline) that are structurally adjacent to coordinated amplification without being CIB per se; [[Bouchafra2026-ts]] documents cross-platform visual securitisation tactics by the Sweden Democrats that include disinformation-adjacent footage manipulation; [[Smith2025-kc]] proposes a causal mechanism ("attention brokerage") for how amplification by high-degree nodes reshapes network structure, relevant to understanding why coordinated seeding matters; and [[Iannelli2018-ebd918b7]] — methodologically distant but germane to the broader toolkit — develops a Facebook-ad-based survey-recruitment procedure for reaching conspiracy-theory-adjacent populations, illustrating the same platform-native, ad-tooling ingenuity that runs throughout this research programme.
