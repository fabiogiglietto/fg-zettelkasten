# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior Detection

## The line of inquiry: from Italian elections to a global monitoring infrastructure

This body of work traces a single methodological arc: the invention, refinement, defense, and institutionalization of coordination-based (rather than content- or actor-based) detection of manipulated social media discourse. At its origin sits [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which reframes disinformation research around "coordinated link sharing behavior" (CLSB) — the observation that groups of Facebook pages, groups, and profiles sharing identical URLs in tight temporal windows are a more tractable and less gameable signal than adjudicating truth or unmasking bad actors one by one. This is the foundational move: content is unreliable and actors are opaque, but *behavior* — the choreography of near-simultaneous sharing — leaves statistical traces. The CooRnet tool that operationalizes this insight becomes the backbone of everything that follows in this cluster.

## Consolidating and productizing the method

[[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] extends CLSB into an applied fact-checking tool (MINE-FACTS), showing that content-agnostic coordination detection surfaces a higher hit-rate of problematic material than routine manual fact-checking, while also cataloguing the tactics — link laundering, first-comment link placement, religious-group camouflage — that coordinated actors use to evade enforcement. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] answers the obvious next problem: static lists of known coordinated accounts decay as actors adapt or get banned. The proposed workflow iteratively re-detects and updates coordination lists in near-real time, and is demonstrated on the 2022 Italian election, uncovering a hyperpartisan political network, a religious-page clickbait economy, and a covert proselytism operation — evidence that the same behavioral signature (coordination) underlies motivationally disparate operations (political, commercial, religious). This actor-agnostic, motive-agnostic stance becomes a signature commitment of the whole programme, later crystallized as the "Zombie Internet" framework in [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]], which explicitly argues that Meta's narrow CIB/CSH categories miss operations whose harm isn't reducible to "inauthenticity plus coordination," and proposes "Deceptive Information Operations" as a broader diagnostic spanning content, platform-affordance exploitation, and participatory dynamics. The same detection infrastructure is then pointed at new empirical targets under the vera.ai umbrella: [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] tracks how coordinated Facebook gambling networks weaponized generative AI (documenting a 13,000%+ post-volume surge after ChatGPT's release), and [[Rogers2026-cy]] develops the adjacent concept of "post-truth spaces" for locating influential disinformation clusters for fact-checkers, applied to Moldovan Facebook discourse on the Russia-Ukraine war — both direct descendants of the CooRnet lineage, now aimed at building an operational alert system for verification specialists rather than a one-off academic study.

## Vindication and stress-testing from outside

The CLSB/coordination paradigm's validity is corroborated and complicated by adjacent empirical work. [[Song2025-yh]] applies CooRnet directly to pro- and anti-vaccine Facebook discourse, confirming that CLSB is ideologically neutral machinery — it can carry misinformation or credible public-health messaging depending on who wields it — a finding that echoes the motive-agnosticism established in the Italian election studies. Meta's own large-scale audits, [[Appel2026-qr]] and its companion [[noauthor_undated-bm]], validate the coordination framework's core intuitions at platform scale: deceptive networks reach millions primarily through *reshares by unaffiliated users*, not direct network posting, meaning coordination detection must account for downstream, informal amplification, not just the seed network — a structural challenge for CLSB-style methods focused on core coordinated clusters. Other papers press on the *limits* of the paradigm: [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] finds that observed coordinated accounts in UK election cascades are far less influential than optimal placement models predict, suggesting CIB is sometimes overstated as a causal driver of diffusion; [[Simeone2025-vo]] similarly finds that a consequential authority-structure collapse after Twitter deplatforming was *not* attributable to coordinated bot behavior, cautioning against reflexively invoking CIB to explain network effects.

## Methodological competitors and refinements

A cluster of technically-oriented papers works the same problem space with different tools, effectively benchmarking against and refining the CLSB tradition. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] both push toward multimodal and temporally-weighted coordination networks, arguing single-modality, time-agnostic approaches (of which timing-based CLSB is an example) miss complementary coordination signals across retweets, mentions, hashtags, and URLs. [[Yang2025-iv]] directly targets a weakness in timing-based detection — its vulnerability to trivial evasion — by exploiting statistical regularities in sharing *speed and frequency* instead, a refinement squarely within the Facebook link-sharing detection lineage. [[Minici2024-tf]] pushes toward foundation-model generalization across campaigns and platforms, while [[Luceri2025-tr]] and [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] extend coordination detection to new platform ecologies (TikTok's video-first structure, Bluesky's decentralized architecture) where existing text-and-network methods don't straightforwardly transfer. [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts "rapid spread network" coordination detection to WhatsApp's encrypted, closed-group context in Brazil, showing coordinated messaging fed real-world mobilization. [[Mannocci2026-kc]], a comprehensive survey, synthesizes this entire proliferation of methods, arguing for a general, theory-grounded definition of coordinated online behavior along axes of authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, and time-variance — implicitly cataloguing the very CLSB/CIB tradition this note traces as one instantiation of a broader phenomenon.

## Conceptual and normative pushback

A final layer interrogates the *concept* of coordinated inauthentic behavior itself. [[Thiele2025-ol]] argues CIB conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposes attribution-focused typologies grounded in principal-agent theory instead. [[Donovan2025-ws]] historicizes CIB as a platform-governance category invented precisely because it lets companies act on behavior without adjudicating truth — a structural, business-model-driven choice rather than a neutral scientific category. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] shows how sophisticated state-linked actors (Serbia's SNS) evolve tactics specifically to *evade* existing coordination-detection frameworks by mimicking authentic grassroots activity, while [[Graham2026-fb]] challenges the human/bot binary underlying much of this detection work altogether, arguing authenticity is a culturally negotiated performance rather than a technical property machine classifiers can cleanly resolve. Frontier-facing pieces — [[Orlando2025-ul]] on emergent coordination among LLM agents and [[Schroeder2026-im]] on "malicious AI swarms" — signal where the detection arms race is heading next: generative AI agents that can produce humanlike, adaptive coordination patterns evading the synchrony-based signatures (like CLSB) that this entire research programme was built to catch, setting up the next methodological challenge for CooRnet-style tools and the VERA-AI Alert system alike.
