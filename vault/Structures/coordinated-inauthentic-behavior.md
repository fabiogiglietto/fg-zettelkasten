# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The methodological spine: coordinated link sharing and CooRnet

The anchor of this topic is a specific analytic move: treating coordination itself — rather than content veracity or actor identity — as the unit of detection. [[Giglietto2019-0bd8b768]] lays the conceptual groundwork, arguing that the post-2016 reckoning with disinformation moved through stages (fake news → bad actors → behavior) that culminate in Facebook's loosely defined notion of "coordinated inauthentic behavior," which the paper operationalizes as entities sharing identical URLs within improbably short time windows. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] extends this empirically across the 2018 and 2019 Italian elections, showing that coordinated networks are many times more likely to share problematic domains and that a network's "politicalness" inversely predicts the concentration of what it shares — the first demonstration that CLSB is a durable, measurable signature rather than an artifact of one campaign. This signature becomes a tool: [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] turns it into the MINE-FACTS prototype for fact-checkers during COVID-19, using the CooRnet package to map Italian "nefarious" actors at macro, meso, and micro levels and to document how coordinated clusters evade enforcement (link-laundering, first-comment links, religious-group cover). [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] then converts the one-off detection into a living workflow — iteratively re-monitoring known coordinated accounts to surface new ones — applied to the 2022 Italian election and yielding three structurally distinct case studies (a hyperpartisan M5S network, a clickbait click-economy operation, and a religious-proselytism network), underscoring that static blacklists systematically understate the scale of coordinated activity.

The same lineage radiates outward in two directions. First, into substantive applications: [[Marino2023-9137f448]] uses CLSB-identified accounts to trace how an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster and legacy-media remediation jointly amplified COVID-19 problematic content in Italy; [[Song2025-yh]] exports CooRnet to UK/US vaccine discourse, showing CLSB is ideologically agnostic — capable of scaling both anti-vaccine misinformation and credible public-health messaging depending on who coordinates; [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] push the method into new terrain (AI-generated gambling-promotion imagery, and a broader "Deceptive Information Operations" framework spanning pro-Putin propaganda, gambling, and adult-content networks), arguing that generative AI intensifies rather than replaces established coordination tactics and that Meta's narrow CIB/CSH categories cannot recognize harm that isn't reducible to coordination-plus-inauthenticity. Second, into infrastructure for practitioners: [[Rogers2026-cy]], part of the same vera.ai ecosystem, introduces "post-truth spaces" as a mapping technique for fact-checkers, explicitly situating CLSB-style coordination detection alongside alternative influence networks, fake-news engagement spaces, and participatory propaganda as one of several lenses on the same underlying phenomenon. Together these papers trace an arc from definitional groundwork, to detection workflow, to applied tooling (CooRnet, VERA-AI Alert) — the "core methodological signature" that the rest of the topic's papers either build on, compete with, or test against.

## Competing and complementary detection architectures

A parallel strand of network-science work treats coordination detection as a general computational problem independent of any single platform or case. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] reframes coordination as continuous rather than binary, showing in the 2019 UK election that coordination and automation are orthogonal and that the most tightly coordinated communities are small activist/antagonist groups, not mainstream partisans — a finding that complicates any assumption that coordination equals inauthenticity. [[Mannocci2026-kc]]'s survey of 122 papers consolidates this into a general framework (actors, synergic actions, intent; authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, time-variance as orthogonal axes), explicitly positioning coordination as broader than the threat-centric "CIB" framing that structures the Giglietto lineage. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] push on the multimodality problem the surveys identify — comparing monomodal, flattened, and multiplex operationalizations and showing that time-aware multiplex representations recover more coordination than single-signal methods like CLSB's URL-timing approach, while [[Yang2025-iv]] directly targets a weakness of timing-based detection (its manipulability) by proposing speed-and-frequency regularities as a more evasion-resistant signal for Facebook link sharing. [[Iannucci2026-my]] goes furthest in formalizing the problem, recasting coordination detection as hypothesis testing against explicit null models via the planted-submatrix problem, and delivering a sobering methodological corrective: dense, modularity-detected clusters are not evidence of coordination unless benchmarked against a null, a caution that bears directly on CLSB-style pipelines that treat detected density as prima facie suspicious.

Other papers extend detection into new modalities and platforms that the original CooRnet approach was not built for: [[Minici2024-tf]]'s IOHunter fuses language models and graph neural networks for cross-campaign generalization; [[Gerard2025-br]]'s CANE/t-CANE models users via shared narrative clusters rather than interaction ties, surfacing "bridge users" who seed cross-platform migration between Truth Social and X; [[Luceri2025-tr]] and [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] confront the video-first ecosystem of TikTok and the real-time, decentralized structure of Bluesky, respectively, both arguing that text/timestamp-based methods need adaptation rather than direct transplant. [[Pante2025-pq]] turns the toolkit reflexively on prior findings, re-testing claims of inter-state IO collaboration against modern multi-signal detectors and control datasets — a direct methodological check on the kind of inference CLSB-adjacent work produces. A final cluster asks not whether coordination is detected but whether it matters: [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] model coordinated accounts' causal influence within retweet/cascade trees and find that real coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election dataset achieve far less influence than optimal or even randomly-matched placements would predict — a skeptical counterweight suggesting that CIB's detectability (the field's dominant preoccupation) should not be conflated with its diffusion impact.

## Case studies across platforms, regimes, and elections

A third cluster applies coordination detection — often explicitly derived from or benchmarked against the CooRnet/Nizzoli lineage — to specific campaigns, platforms, and political contexts, building out the comparative empirical record the topic demands. On encrypted and alternative platforms: [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts a "Rapid Spread Network" approach to 13 million WhatsApp messages in Brazil, tracing coordinated accounts from misinformation dissemination into real-world mobilization around the January 8 attacks; [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] maps coordinated amplification among pro-government Telegram channels in Russia and Belarus. On established platforms under authoritarian or hybrid-regime pressure: [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] quantifies over 13,000 coordinated Twitter accounts around China's 2022 COVID protests, read through frameworks of connective action and digital repression; [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues Serbia's SNS party exemplifies a more mimetic, harder-to-detect mode of digital authoritarianism than centralized bot farms, calling for new frameworks beyond standard CIB detection. Electoral contexts proliferate the comparative base: [[Appel2026-qr]] uses Meta-provided data on 49 deceptive networks in the US 2020 election to show that most exposure occurs via reshares by non-network accounts rather than direct network posting — a finding that resonates with CLSB's emphasis on amplification networks over originating actors; [[Simeone2025-vo]] shows that Twitter's 2021 deplatforming of Arizona "audit" accounts produced network-wide authority collapse not attributable to CIB, since only ten accounts met coordination thresholds — a useful negative case; [[Klinger2023-qu]] traces how German anti-immigration framing laundered from the fringe Identitarian Movement into AfD's official campaign via intermediary and superspreader networks, without formal coordination; [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive-user amplification across all major parties in Romania's 2024 EU campaign; [[Copland2025-em]] shows Sky News Australia's platform-specific bifurcated strategy (native Facebook vs. YouTube) functioning as transnational network propaganda.

## Actors, attribution, and organizational infrastructure

A related set of papers shifts from detecting coordination to explaining who runs it and why, addressing the "attribution problem" that detection alone cannot solve. [[Thiele2025-ol]] offers a theoretical corrective, arguing that CIB-style definitions conflate inauthenticity with manipulative intent, and proposing a rational-choice typology linking campaign scale, elaborateness, and disguise to principals' resources and stakes. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] and [[Keller2019-nk]] supply ground-truth organizational detail that grounds such typologies empirically: Poliakoff's OSINT analysis of IRA employee CVs reveals a conventional, Crimea-era media-labor organization rather than a 2016-election-specific operation, while Keller's canonical study of the South Korean NIS campaign demonstrates that coordination traces — not bot-like individual behavior — are the most robust and hardest-to-hide signature of astroturfing, a methodological insight that anticipates the entire coordination-detection paradigm this topic surveys. [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as political brokerage, foregrounding economic infrastructure over content; Bastos2025-ol looks for country-of-origin visual signatures in troll-farm profile imagery; [[Kim2026-br]] applies explainable ML to two decades of Korean news comments, finding that state-linked trolling relies more on morally condemning rhetoric engineered for algorithmic visibility than on direct pro-regime messaging — extending attribution research into affect and ranking dynamics. [[Graham2025-gp]] and [[Zhao2025-ny]] examine tactics (hashtag exploitation, infrastructural mimicry of "truth" signals) that any attribution framework must account for.

## Theoretical anchors and emerging frontiers

Two earlier papers function as conceptual touchstones for nearly everything above. [[Starbird2019-qv]] insists that information operations are collaborative "work" performed partly by authentic, unwitting participants, making the orchestrated/organic boundary — which every detection method in this topic ultimately tries to draw — inherently blurry. This tension resurfaces in [[Efstratiou2026-ij]], which finds a genuinely coordinated, non-automated network amplifying contrarian COVID experts, and in [[Jurg2025-ur]], which audits YouTube's algorithmic and labeling infrastructure rather than user coordination per se, broadening the topic's scope to platform governance. Finally, a forward-looking cluster asks what happens when coordination is manufactured by generative AI rather than organized humans: [[Orlando2025-ul]] shows LLM agents spontaneously reproducing IO coordination signatures (density, narrative homogeneity, synchronized amplification) once given mere mutual awareness, without explicit instruction; [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a governance-oriented warning about "malicious AI swarms"; and [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] and [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] gesture toward the adaptive persistence of manipulation campaigns and the agenda-building power of bots, respectively. Read together, the topic traces a field moving from Keller and Starbird's foundational insight that coordination (not automation) is the real signature of inauthentic operations, through the Giglietto/CooRnet program that operationalized this insight into a reusable, content-agnostic workflow now embedded in VERA-AI tooling, outward into a proliferating ecology of competing detection architectures, cross-platform case studies, attribution theory, and — on the horizon — a new generation of synthetic coordination that may demand the next methodological leap.
