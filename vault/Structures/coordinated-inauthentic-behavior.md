# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The methodological core: from CLSB to CooRnet

The throughline of this collection begins with [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which reframes disinformation research away from content- or actor-level detection and toward *coordinated collective action* as the diagnostic unit — the paper that operationalizes "coordinated link sharing behaviour" (CLSB) against the Italian 2018 and 2019 elections and demonstrates that networks sharing URLs near-simultaneously are disproportionately linked to problematic domains and known disinformation entities. This becomes the CooRnet methodology proper, extended in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] into a content-agnostic tool for COVID-19 fact-checking, where the same behavioral signature surfaces covid-skeptic clusters, "link-laundering" tactics, and religious-proselytism networks that content analysis alone would miss. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] pushes the method from a static snapshot into a living workflow — iteratively updating lists of coordinated accounts across the 2022 Italian snap election — explicitly answering the critique that list-based detection goes stale as actors adapt. Two more recent applications extend the method into new harms: [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] traces how generative AI has industrialized coordinated gambling promotion (a post-ChatGPT explosion in post volume), and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] proposes "Deceptive Information Operations" as an umbrella concept spanning a pro-Putin propaganda network, gambling promotion, and poorly moderated adult-content groups — arguing Meta's CIB/CSH categories are too narrow to capture operations whose harm isn't reducible to coordination-plus-inauthenticity. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] and [[Rogers2026-cy]] round out the Italian/vera.ai-adjacent cluster: the former shows how "Alternative Influence Networks" and legacy media co-produce covid-skeptic virality via remediation, the latter proposes "post-truth spaces" as a mapping concept for fact-checkers, with a Moldova case study showing that borderline (not purely fringe) clusters carry the greatest network centrality.

## Refining and contesting the detection apparatus

A parallel, more technical strand interrogates and extends the coordination-detection toolkit itself. [[Yang2025-iv]] argues that timing-based coordination signals are trivially gameable and proposes statistical regularities in sharing speed/frequency as more robust; [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] independently converge on multiplex, multimodal representations (retweets, replies, hashtags, URLs) as superior to monomodal or naively flattened networks, with the latter adding a temporal-decay kernel; [[Minici2024-tf]] generalizes further with a graph-foundation-model (IOHunter) that fuses language and structural signals for cross-campaign, out-of-distribution detection. [[Gerard2025-br]] reframes coordination entirely around shared narrative participation rather than interaction ties, identifying a tiny set of "bridge users" who ferry narratives between Truth Social and X. Platform-specific adaptations follow: [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts TikTok's video-first ecosystem, [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] pushes toward real-time bot-coordination detection on Bluesky, [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts Rapid Spread Network logic to WhatsApp's closed, encrypted Brazilian ecosystem, and [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] applies forwarding-network analysis to pro-government Telegram channels in Russia and Belarus. Together these papers treat the original CLSB/CooRnet insight — coordination as a behavioral, not content, signature — as a template to be reinvented for each platform's affordances.

## Skepticism and recalibration: does coordination actually matter?

A notable counter-current questions whether detected coordination translates into real influence. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] formally models the influence of coordinated nodes in retweet cascades and finds that real-world coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election dataset perform far below optimal or even greedy benchmarks — coordination is present but largely non-strategic and marginal to cascade dynamics. [[Simeone2025-vo]] similarly finds that the ripple effects of Twitter's 2021 deplatforming of "Stop the Steal" accounts cannot be attributed to CIB (only ten accounts met coordination thresholds), implicating instead the structural loss of hub authority among ordinary human users. [[Appel2026-qr]] and its companion entry [[noauthor_undated-bm]] — reporting the same FIES study of 49 Meta-identified deceptive networks around the 2020 US election — deliver the most consequential recalibration: exposure is massively concentrated in a handful of networks, driven mostly by *reshares from unaffiliated accounts* rather than direct network posting, and the apparent correlations between exposure and downstream political outcomes evaporate once pre-exposure user characteristics are controlled for. [[Pante2025-pq]] extends this skeptical turn to claims of *inter-state* coordination, arguing that prior findings of collaboration between state-linked operations don't survive rigorous benchmarking against control datasets. Read together, this cluster complicates the field's default assumption that detecting coordination is equivalent to demonstrating impact — a necessary corrective to the more triumphalist detection literature.

## Conceptual and typological work

Several papers sit above the empirical fray, working to sharpen or contest the field's core concepts. [[Thiele2025-ol]] mounts the most direct theoretical challenge, arguing that "coordinated inauthentic behaviour" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposing a rational-choice attribution framework (scale, elaborateness, disguise) with an eight-type campaign typology instead. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] examines how manipulation campaigns persist by opportunistically appropriating emerging events, while [[Graham2025-gp]]'s #IStandWithPutin case study reframes propaganda as parasitic on the "infrastructure of truth" — exploiting rather than evading verification systems. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] and Bastos2025-ol approach attribution from different angles: the former via OSINT analysis of IRA employee CVs revealing an organizational, labor-market structure rather than a purely ideological one; the latter via computer-vision analysis of troll-farm profile imagery as a novel attribution signal.

## Country and campaign case studies

The topic's comparative ambition is visible in a run of national case studies. Beyond the Italian core, [[Kansaon2025-id]] documents WhatsApp-coordinated mobilization culminating in Brazil's January 8 attacks; [[Inacio-da-Silva2026-zf]] audits irregular Facebook electoral ads in the 2018 Brazilian election via volunteer browser-plugin crowdsourcing; [[Oprea2025-lf]] quantifies "hyperactive user" amplification on Romanian party pages during the 2024 EU election; [[Bouchafra2026-ts]] traces the Sweden Democrats' platform-tailored securitizing visual campaign; [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues Serbia's SNS practices a subtler, harder-to-detect mimicry of grassroots support than centralized bot farms; [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] applies coordination detection to the 2022 China COVID-protest Twitter sphere; [[Kim2026-br]] offers a two-decade, explainable-ML analysis of suspected state-linked trolling in Korean news comments, finding condemnation rhetoric — not praise — drives engagement; and [[Gaw2025-ru]] reconceives Philippine influence operations as political brokerage rather than isolated disinformation. [[Copland2025-em]] traces Sky News Australia's transformation into a transnational right-wing propaganda node bridging Australian and US audiences.

## The AI-native frontier

A final, forward-looking cluster anticipates how generative AI reshapes coordination itself. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates networked LLM agents in an information-operations testbed, showing that mere *awareness* of teammates — without explicit deliberation — produces coordination levels near those of fully collective decision-making, a striking finding for detection theory. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a policy-forum argument about "malicious AI swarms" threatening democratic epistemics at scale, proposing layered governance responses. Adjacent but not identical, [[Zhao2025-ny]] examines hashtag hijacking as a bot-assisted narrative-capture tactic, [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] tests whether bots function as agenda-builders against organizational messaging in the 2022 Ohio midterms, and [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]] and [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] extend coordination analysis into adjacent territories — influencer-driven toxicity and COVID science-communication pathways — showing how the CIB/CLSB toolkit increasingly informs research well beyond its original electoral-disinformation home, from vaccine discourse ([[Song2025-yh]]) to the broader information ecosystem's fusion of coordinated amplification, credentialed experts, and news media.
