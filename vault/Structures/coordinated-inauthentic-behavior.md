# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior Detection

## The Detection Problem: From Content to Coordination

The intellectual core of this collection is a methodological wager, first made explicit in [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]]: rather than chasing individual pieces of false content or trying to unmask "bad actors" one by one, disinformation research should detect the *collective action* itself — accounts that share the same links, images, or messages in improbably rapid, repeated succession. This reframing, developed through Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) and operationalized in the open-source CooRnet package, treats coordination as a content-agnostic, actor-agnostic signal that can surface manipulation regardless of its ideological or commercial motivation. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] establishes the empirical case on Italian election data; [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] extends it to COVID-19 "infodemic" monitoring in partnership with fact-checkers, showing CLSB catches proportionally more problematic content than manual fact-checking alone; [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] turns the method into a living workflow — seed lists that self-update by iteratively discovering new coordinated accounts — solving the problem that static blacklists decay as actors adapt, get banned, or rebrand. This lineage of work is the backbone of the researcher's signature contribution and the methodological engine behind the VERA-AI Alert system, later demonstrated on gambling promotion networks ([[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]) and on composite "Deceptive Information Operations" spanning propaganda, gambling, and adult-content networks ([[Giada2026-fc9a3833]]). [[Rogers2026-cy]], also produced under vera.ai, complements this actor-network detection with a source-provenance and "post-truth space" mapping technique for fact-checkers, showing how the same verification mission can be pursued through adjacent but distinct methodological lenses.

## Theorizing Coordination: Definitions, Spectra, and Typologies

A second cluster of papers works to give this operational practice conceptual ballast. [[Starbird2019-qv]] is foundational here, reframing information operations as *collaborative work* — participatory, sociotechnical, and stubbornly resistant to a clean bots/trolls-versus-authentic-crowd binary. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] pushes this insight into network science, showing coordination is a continuous spectrum rather than a binary label, and crucially that coordination and automation are orthogonal — bot detection is neither necessary nor sufficient for CIB detection. [[Keller2019-nk]]'s ground-truthed study of NIS Twitter astroturfing in South Korea makes the same point from a different angle: coordination traces (not individual bot-like behavior) are the durable signal, since campaigns are principal-agent problems that leave detectable fingerprints regardless of whether the agents are human or automated. [[Mannocci2026-kc]]'s survey synthesizes this entire trajectory, proposing a general framework built on actors/actions/intent and four orthogonal dimensions (authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, time-variance) — explicitly arguing that coordination research must outgrow the narrow, industry-driven "coordinated inauthentic behavior" framing that platforms like Meta introduced. [[Thiele2025-ol]] pursues a parallel but distinct project: an attribution-focused typology asking not "is this coordinated?" but "who commissioned it?", critiquing CIB's conflation of inauthenticity with intent. Together these theoretical papers trace an arc from CSCW-style participatory framing, through network-science operationalization of "how coordinated," to typological and attributional questions of "coordinated for whom and why."

## Methodological Refinement: Modalities, Time, and Robustness

A dense technical subliterature refines the mechanics of detection itself, largely in critical dialogue with the CooRnet/CLSB and Nizzoli traditions. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically compares five ways of handling multimodal coordination (retweets, replies, mentions, hashtags, URLs), finding multiplex community detection the most balanced against the field's default practice of flattening layers. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] adds temporal weighting to this multiplex logic, arguing that both proximity and frequency of co-action are needed to separate deliberate coordination from coincidence, and benchmarks favorably against prior state-of-the-art. [[Yang2025-iv]] targets a specific evasion vector — the vulnerability of timing-based detection to trivial manipulation — proposing statistical regularities in sharing speed and frequency as a more robust signal, a direct methodological rejoinder to the timing-dependence built into earlier CLSB approaches. [[Minici2024-tf]] moves toward foundation-model-based detection (IOHunter), combining language and graph neural models for cross-campaign generalization, addressing a limitation that recurs throughout this literature: methods trained on one platform or one campaign often fail to transfer. That transfer problem motivates several platform-specific extension papers: [[Luceri2025-tr]] on TikTok's video-first ecosystem, [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] on real-time Bluesky bot-coordination detection, and [[Kansaon2025-id]]'s adaptation of "Rapid Spread Network" methods to WhatsApp's closed, encrypted architecture in Brazil — each showing that coordination signals must be reinvented as platform affordances change. [[Pante2025-pq]] performs a skeptical audit of this whole enterprise, re-testing claims of inter-state IO collaboration against control datasets and finding earlier findings may not survive more rigorous, multi-signal scrutiny — a useful corrective note within a field prone to over-claiming from single-signal analyses.

## Does Coordination Matter? Contesting Impact and Influence

Running alongside the technical refinement is a countercurrent questioning whether detected coordination actually *does* much. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] formally models the counterfactual influence coordinated accounts could have achieved in real cascades and finds observed coordinated accounts perform far below optimal or even resource-matched benchmarks, their placement statistically indistinguishable from random — suggesting CIB may be less pivotal to diffusion than prevailing narratives assume. [[Simeone2025-vo]] complements this from the moderation side: the 2021 Arizona Election Review deplatforming produced network-wide "ripple effects" that were not explained by CIB (only 10 accounts met coordination thresholds), implying that authentic human disengagement, not bot removal, drove the collapse. [[Appel2026-qr]]'s large-scale Meta-partnered study of 49 deceptive US 2020 election networks similarly complicates simple coordination-equals-harm assumptions: most exposure occurred via *reshares by non-network accounts*, not direct network posting, and correlational links between exposure and downstream beliefs largely evaporate under proper controls. Together these three papers form a skeptical wing within the topic, warning against conflating detectability with consequentiality.

## Substantive Applications: Platforms, Actors, and Contexts

A large group of papers puts coordination-detection methods (or ad hoc adaptations of them) to work across substantive cases, testing the method's portability and its interpretive payoff. On Facebook specifically — the platform CooRnet was built for — [[Song2025-yh]] shows CLSB is ideologically agnostic, capturing both anti-vaccine misinformation networks and legitimate institutional pro-vaccine amplification, differentiated by density and fact-check verification rates across the UK and US. [[Oprea2025-lf]] examines Romanian EU election pages manually via an "Authenticity Matrix," converging with CLSB-style findings on hyperactive user amplification while noting how restrictive API access blocks independent computational verification. State and quasi-state actors recur throughout: [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] (IRA organizational history via CVs), [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] (pro-government Telegram amplification in Russia/Belarus), [[Kim2026-br]] (two decades of suspected state-linked trolling in Korean news comments), [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] (Serbia's SNS mimicking grassroots authenticity to evade detection), [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] (dual-use coordination in Chinese COVID-protest discourse), Bastos2025-ol (visual/image-based attribution of troll farms), and [[Gaw2025-ru]] (Philippine influence operations reframed as political brokerage) each extend coordination analysis into new geopolitical or modal territory, often stressing that authoritarian and hybrid-regime actors evolve past the "obvious bot farm" signature the original detection paradigm was built to catch. [[Copland2025-em]] and [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] extend the frame to media ecosystems — Sky News Australia's transnational propaganda pipeline and COVID science communication's contrarian coordinated retweet cluster, respectively — showing coordination analysis productively applied beyond overtly "disinformation" content into mainstream and quasi-mainstream discourse. [[Zhao2025-ny]], [[Arceneaux2026-xk]], and [[Graham2025-gp]] round out the applications with hashtag hijacking, bot-driven agenda-building in US midterms, and the #IStandWithPutin case, each reinforcing that coordinated amplification exploits legitimate platform infrastructure rather than existing outside it — a theme also central to [[Donovan2025-ws]]'s historical account of how "misinformation-at-scale" and the CIB concept itself emerged from platforms' engagement-driven business models.

## The Coming Challenge: Generative AI and Emergent Coordination

A final, forward-looking cluster signals where the topic is heading. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] documents an exponential, ChatGPT-triggered explosion in coordinated gambling-promotion volume, arguing generative AI intensifies known persuasion drivers rather than replacing them. [[Tang2026-gu]] describes Chinese "AI slop" creators industrializing virality via matrix-account systems, and [[Schroeder2026-im]] and [[Orlando2025-ul]] look ahead to agentic LLM swarms — the latter empirically demonstrating in simulation that mere mutual awareness among LLM agents is nearly sufficient to produce coordination indistinguishable from deliberate campaigns. [[Graham2026-fb]]'s Reddit "botting" case study offers a useful counterweight, insisting that the human/bot and authentic/inauthentic binaries are themselves culturally negotiated rather than technical facts — a reminder, echoed in [[Mannocci2026-kc]]'s survey and [[Thiele2025-ol]]'s attribution model, that as detection methods mature, the field's next task is less about finding coordination than about deciding what kind of coordination — and whose — actually warrants concern.
