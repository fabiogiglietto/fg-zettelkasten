# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## From Discovery to Contestation: A Decade of CIB Research

The concept of "coordinated inauthentic behavior" has hardened from a platform policy category into a full research programme — and, increasingly, into a contested one. The founding move in this literature was to shift attention away from content veracity and toward *behavioral traces* of collective action: [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] operationalizes CLSB across the 2018–2019 Italian elections, arguing that coordinated link sharing is empirically tied to problematic domains and that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" can be grounded in scholarship on connective action and authenticity. That workflow is progressively refined — into a rolling, near-real-time monitoring pipeline in [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]], into an infodemic-facing fact-checking tool in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], and, more recently, into an analysis of generative-AI-driven gambling promotion in [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]], where the same behavioral lens tracks the industrial scaling of visual persuasion after ChatGPT.

## Methodological Refinement: Beyond Timing, Beyond Single Modalities

A second wave sharpens the detection apparatus. [[Yang2025-iv]] argues that timing signals — the workhorse of early CLSB detection — are trivially evadable, and turns to statistical regularities in link-sharing speed and frequency. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] both push toward multiplex, multimodal formulations, showing that aggregating retweets, hashtags, mentions and URLs into a single flattened layer discards structurally important nodes; multiplex community detection with temporal decay emerges as the most balanced approach. [[Minici2024-tf]] takes a different route via graph foundation models, promising generalization across campaigns, while [[Gerard2025-br]] abandons interaction graphs altogether in favour of latent narrative affiliations, uncovering "bridge users" across Truth Social and X. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] extends real-time detection to Bluesky, and [[Luceri2025-tr]] confronts the harder problem of translating text-centric methods to TikTok's video-first ecosystem. Across these, the field is converging on the view that no single modality or timing signal is sufficient — coordination must be triangulated.

## Widening the Empirical Map

If the early CLSB work was Italy-centric, the topic now spans a striking geographic and platform range. [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts coordination detection to encrypted WhatsApp groups during Brazil's 2022 election, linking online coordination to the January 8 attacks. [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] maps forwarding coordination across pro-regime Telegram channels in Russia and Belarus; [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] identifies coordinated activity on both sides of China's 2022 COVID protests, treating platforms as dual-use infrastructure for mobilization and repression. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues Serbia's SNS represents a more mimetic, decentralized authoritarianism that evades standard detectors, while [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine election IOs as political brokerage embedded in political-economic infrastructures. [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive users amplifying all four leading Romanian parties during the 2024 EP election, and [[Kim2026-br]] traces two decades of suspected troll activity in Korean news comments. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] complements these platform-side analyses with an OSINT-based reconstruction of the IRA as a labor-market actor with a five-level hierarchy.

## Content Domains and Cross-Cutting Cases

Substantive case studies show CIB operating well beyond electoral politics narrowly conceived. [[Song2025-yh]] uses CooRnet to compare pro- and anti-vaccine CLSB in the UK and US, showing that coordination is ideologically agnostic. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] finds a contrarian coordinated retweet network amplifying credentialed anti-consensus experts in COVID science communication. [[Askanius2026-de]] maps the Swedish far-right YouTube "alternative influence network," where guest appearances and hyperlinking hybridize activism with influencer economics. [[Graham2025-gp]] reads #IStandWithPutin as propaganda parasitizing platform trust infrastructures, and [[Zhao2025-ny]] treats hashtag hijacking as strategic play in public opinion games. Bastos2025-ol opens a visual-identity front on the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium data.

## The Sceptical Turn: How Much Does CIB Actually Matter?

A striking counter-current has emerged questioning whether coordinated networks meaningfully shape outcomes. [[Appel2026-qr]] and its pre-registered companion [[noauthor_undated-bm]] leverage Meta-internal exposure data from the 2020 FIES to show that although 49 deceptive networks reached ~37M Facebook users, most exposure was driven by *reshares from unaffiliated accounts*, network content averaged only 0.3% of political content views for exposed users, and associations with downstream attitudes largely dissolve under adjustment for pre-exposure characteristics. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] independently models cascade influence and finds that coordinated accounts on X are placed close to randomly, exerting roughly 10% of the influence an optimal deployment would achieve. [[Pante2025-pq]] similarly re-examines and scrutinizes claims of *inter-state* coordination, questioning earlier findings once modern controls are applied. Together, these papers press the field to distinguish existence of coordination from consequence — a distinction the earlier detection-focused literature largely bracketed.

## Conceptual and Definitional Reckoning

This empirical maturation has forced conceptual re-examination. [[Thiele2025-ol]] argues that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" — a Meta-derived category — conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and is poorly suited to comparative research; it proposes a rational-choice typology built on scale, elaborateness and disguise. [[Hurcombe2025-cs]] shows discursively how Meta's Newsroom has used precisely this ambiguity, treating "authenticity" and "coordination" as floating signifiers that displace responsibility from platform affordances to external bad actors, while the term itself has migrated into academic and regulatory vocabularies. [[Goel2025-iq]] challenges the domain-list paradigm from another angle, showing that mainstream articles co-shared with fake news disproportionately carry misleading narratives — coordination as *narrative repurposing* rather than fabrication. [[Graham2026-fb]] destabilizes the human/bot binary itself, treating "botting" as participatory culture rather than technical inauthenticity. And [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] adds a temporal dimension, showing how campaigns persist by appropriating emerging events.

## The Next Horizon: Synthetic and Emergent Coordination

Two papers signal where the field is headed. [[Orlando2025-ul]] shows through generative agent-based modelling that LLM agents *spontaneously* reproduce known IO signatures — dense retweet networks, homogeneous narratives, synchronized amplification — with only teammate awareness, not explicit deliberation. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates the policy stakes, arguing that agentic "malicious AI swarms" collapse the cost, cadence and iteration constraints that limited human troll farms, and calling for provenance infrastructure, an international observatory, and incentive-based interventions. Read against [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s portrait of the IRA as a bounded, hierarchically staffed labour organization, these papers mark a genuine phase change: from coordination as *organized human labour* mediated by platforms, to coordination as an *emergent property* of networked generative agents — a shift that will test whether the behavioral-signature detection paradigm built over the last decade can hold.
