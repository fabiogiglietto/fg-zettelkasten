# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## From link-sharing to a field: the CooRnet lineage

The methodological spine of this topic runs directly from [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which reframed disinformation research around *coordinated link sharing behavior* (CLSB) rather than content veracity or actor blacklists — the move that eventually becomes the CooRnet package. Its key insight, that networks of pages, groups, and profiles sharing identical URLs near-simultaneously are disproportionately linked to problematic domains, set the template for everything that follows: content-agnostic, behavior-first detection grounded in near-duplicate timing rather than semantic judgment. [[Starbird2019-qv]] supplies the theoretical scaffolding this empirical move needed, reconceiving information operations as *collaborative work* performed by hybrid crowds of orchestrated, cultivated, and organic participants — a framing that explains why CLSB networks are never cleanly "bot farms" and why the boundary between authentic and inauthentic is perpetually blurred. [[Keller2019-nk]], using the rare ground-truth case of the Korean NIS astroturfing campaign, independently converges on the same diagnosis: coordination patterns, not bot-like individual traits, are the durable signature of managed campaigns, because principal-agent dynamics leave traces no individual-level mimicry can erase. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] then formalizes coordination as a continuum rather than a binary, showing on UK election data that automation and coordination are orthogonal and that the most tightly coordinated communities are often small activist cores, not mainstream partisan bulk.

## Operationalizing and hardening detection

A second cluster works to keep the CooRnet-style approach current against evasion and platform diversification. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] turns the static list of coordinated accounts into a live monitoring workflow — iteratively re-detecting new coordinated entities from a seed list — directly anticipating the logic later embodied in the VERA-AI Alert system, and demonstrating its value across ideological (Five Star Movement), economic (clickbait), and religious (Church of Almighty God) coordination simultaneously. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] (MINE-FACTS) applies the same CooRnet lineage to COVID-19 in partnership with Italian fact-checkers, documenting how coordinated actors adapt tactics — link laundering, first-comment link placement, cross-posting via image macros — in direct response to platform mitigation, a cat-and-mouse dynamic that motivates continuous rather than one-off detection. [[Yang2025-iv]] pushes the core signal itself forward, arguing that timing-based coordination detection is trivially gameable and proposing statistical regularities in sharing *speed and frequency* as a more evasion-resistant alternative, tested on 11.2 million Facebook link posts.

Parallel efforts generalize coordination detection beyond single modalities and single platforms. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] both tackle the multimodality problem — the former systematically comparing five ways of integrating retweet, reply, mention, hashtag, and URL co-actions and finding multiplex community detection the best-balanced compromise; the latter adding a temporal decay kernel to Newman's collaboration model and validating a multiplex time-aware approach across 26 benchmark datasets. [[Minici2024-tf]] moves to graph foundation models, combining language and graph neural signals (IOHunter) to generalize detection across heterogeneous state-sponsored campaigns, including out-of-distribution and few-shot settings. Platform expansion is its own front: [[Luceri2025-tr]] adapts network-similarity detection to TikTok's video-first affordances, [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] targets real-time bot coordination on the decentralized Bluesky, and [[Gerard2025-br]] proposes CANE/t-CANE, linking users through shared latent narrative clusters rather than platform-specific interaction traces, to reconstruct cross-platform discourse networks (Truth Social–X) at a moment when API access has degraded the interaction-based methods this whole tradition was built on.

## From detecting coordination to measuring its consequences

A distinct and more skeptical strand asks whether detected coordination actually matters for diffusion. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] develop post-hoc, graph-theoretic frameworks for quantifying the *influence* coordinated accounts exert within retweet cascades, finding — provocatively — that real coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election dataset achieve influence far below optimal or even resource-matched greedy placements, resembling random assignment more than strategy. This dovetails with [[Simeone2025-vo]], which shows that in the Arizona Election Review community CIB was nearly absent (only 10 accounts met coordination thresholds) while network-level deplatforming of a few central *human* accounts nonetheless produced outsized ripple effects — a reminder that coordination detection and causal impact are separate questions. [[Pante2025-pq]] applies similar methodological caution to claims of *inter-state* coordination, arguing that earlier reports of state-to-state collaboration in IOs do not survive rigorous benchmarking against control datasets. Together these papers push the field, per [[Di_Marco2026-xu]]'s own framing, from an overemphasis on detection toward quantifying downstream effect.

## Consolidation, critique, and attribution

[[Mannocci2026-kc]]'s PRISMA survey of 122 papers is the field's current stock-taking: it reconciles industry and academic definitions, proposes authenticity, harmfulness, orchestration, and time-variance as orthogonal axes, and insists that coordination is a general sociotechnical phenomenon (spanning activism to disinformation), not inherently a threat category — directly echoing the orthogonality arguments of [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] and the "collaborative work" framing of [[Starbird2019-qv]]. [[Thiele2025-ol]] pushes further into critique, arguing that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" as a category conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and proposing instead a rational-choice attribution model keyed to scale, elaborateness, and disguise as signals of a campaign's hidden principal. [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] makes a cognate point empirically: Serbia's SNS-linked networks evade CIB detection precisely by mimicking authentic grassroots support rather than operating as centralized bot farms, suggesting detection frameworks built on IRA-style botnets undercount more adaptive authoritarian manipulation.

## Applications across political and platform contexts

The methodology's portability is demonstrated across a wide range of cases. On Facebook, [[Song2025-yh]] extends CLSB to compare pro- and anti-vaccine coordination in the UK and US, showing coordination is ideologically agnostic — it can scale credible public-health messaging as readily as misinformation. [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive-user amplification across Romanian party pages during EP elections, while [[Marino2023-9137f448]] traces how an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster, identified via the same coordinated-network seed lists, drove Covid-skeptic engagement on Italian Facebook through legacy-media remediation. On Telegram and WhatsApp, [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] maps pro-regime channel coordination in Russia/Belarus and [[Kansaon2025-id]] adapts a Rapid-Spread-Network approach to the encrypted Brazilian WhatsApp ecosystem, linking coordinated messaging to real-world mobilization around January 8, 2023. On Twitter/X, [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] detects coordination amid China's COVID protests as dual-use infrastructure for both mobilization and repression, [[Copland2025-em]] traces Sky News Australia's two-pronged propaganda strategy across YouTube and Facebook, and [[Zhao2025-ny]] examines hashtag hijacking as strategic narrative capture. State-actor organizational structure is examined directly in [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s OSINT study of the IRA's labor-market integration, Bastos2025-ol's visual-identity analysis of troll-farm profile imagery, [[Kim2026-br]]'s two-decade explainable-ML study of Korean troll comment campaigns, and [[Gaw2025-ru]]'s reframing of Philippine influence operations as political brokerage — all pushing the field past network traces toward the organizations and incentives behind them. At platform scale, [[Appel2026-qr]]'s analysis of Meta-identified deceptive networks around the US 2020 election is the closest any of these papers come to causal exposure data, showing reach is concentrated in a few networks and travels mainly through reshares by non-network accounts — a sobering empirical check on how much coordinated seeding alone explains population-level exposure.

## The VERA-AI Alert line

A tight cluster of papers constitutes the live monitoring-system descendant of this methodology. [[Rogers2026-cy]] introduces "post-truth spaces" as a vera.ai deliverable for fact-checkers, combining network mapping, provenance investigation, and an influence metric to locate not just coordinated clusters but the more consequential "borderline" clusters that bridge them to the mainstream (demonstrated on Moldovan Facebook war discourse). [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] generalizes this into "Deceptive Information Operations," using the same automated coordinated-sharing detection workflow to analyze three structurally convergent but motivationally distinct Facebook cases — pro-Putin propaganda, gambling promotion, and poorly moderated adult-content groups — arguing that Meta's CIB/CSH categories are too narrow to capture operations whose harm isn't reducible to coordination-plus-inauthenticity. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] narrows in on one of those cases, documenting an exponential, AI-driven escalation of coordinated gambling promotion following ChatGPT's release, with generative AI intensifying rather than replacing established persuasion drivers. These three papers together show the methodology's evolution from static detection toward a live alerting infrastructure tracking how coordinated actors exploit regulatory gray zones and generative tools in near-real time.

## The frontier: coordination in the age of generative AI

A final, forward-looking cluster asks what coordination detection must become as LLMs enter the loop. [[Schroeder2026-im]] and [[Orlando2025-ul]] are complementary: the former is a policy forum mapping how agentic LLM "swarms" could achieve persistent, adaptive, cross-platform coordination beyond anything botnets managed, while the latter empirically demonstrates, via generative agent-based modeling, that LLM agents spontaneously reproduce key coordination signatures (network density, narrative homogeneity, synchronized amplification) once given mere mutual awareness — no explicit deliberation required. This has direct implications for the detection apparatus built across this topic: if coordination can emerge from awareness alone, synchrony-based and similarity-based detectors optimized on human/botnet traces may need rethinking. Adjacent work gestures at where this dynamic already surfaces: [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] finds non-automated but tightly coordinated contrarian retweet networks shaping COVID science communication pathways into the press; [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]], [[Arceneaux2026-xk]], and [[Holland_Levin2026-qx]] extend coordination-adjacent dynamics into influencer-driven toxicity, bot agenda-building, and cross-platform culture-war framing respectively; and [[Graham2026-fb]] and [[Graham2025-gp]] push back against purely technical bot/propaganda framings, arguing for culturally negotiated and infrastructurally parasitic understandings of inauthenticity that complicate any purely network-signature approach. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]]'s observation that manipulation campaigns persist by opportunistically appropriating emerging events closes the loop: detection systems built on this topic's methodological line must be continuously renewed, not because the underlying signal (synchronized, patterned co-action) has changed, but because the actors generating it keep finding new material and, increasingly, new non-human collaborators to generate it with.
