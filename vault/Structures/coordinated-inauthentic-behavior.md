# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The Shape of the Field: From Content to Behavior to Impact

This is the researcher's deepest and longest-running vein of work, and the papers filed here trace a coherent intellectual arc: from establishing *behavior* rather than *content* as the proper unit of analysis for platform manipulation, through building and refining detection methodologies, to interrogating whether coordination actually matters for opinion change — and finally confronting a new generation of AI-driven threats.

### Foundations: coordination as an ecological signal

The line of argument begins with [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which reframes disinformation research away from adjudicating truth/falsity or hunting "bad actors" toward detecting *coordinated collective action* — operationalizing what Facebook had called "coordinated inauthentic behavior" using near-simultaneous, repeated link-sharing across pages, groups, and profiles during Italian elections. This CooRnet/CLSB methodology becomes the backbone of an entire research program, extended into pandemic-era monitoring in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] (the MINE-FACTS workflow feeding Italian fact-checkers), into dynamic list-updating in [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] (2022 Italian snap election, surfacing hyperpartisan, click-economy, and religious-proselytism networks), into influencer ecosystem analysis in [[Marino2023-9137f448]] (Alternative Influence Networks and the "Intellectual Dark Web" remediated through legacy media), and most recently into visual/generative-AI persuasion in [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] (gambling promotion networks) and the broader synthesis in [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]], which proposes "Deceptive Information Operations" as a category that subsumes CIB, deception theory, and strategic information operations under a single structural logic. Parallel conceptual work from Starbird's tradition — [[Starbird2019-qv]] — insists disinformation is *collaborative work*, blurring orchestrated and organic participation, a theme echoed decades later in [[Starbird2025-jj]]'s evidence-frame analysis of election-integrity rumoring, where misinformation is shown to live in the interaction between accurate evidence and partisan framing rather than in false facts per se.

### Methodological refinement: what counts as coordination?

A second cluster interrogates and extends the CLSB/coordination-detection toolkit itself. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] argues coordination is a continuum, not a binary, and demonstrates it is orthogonal to automation — a claim central to the field's self-understanding. [[Keller2019-nk]] uses rare ground-truth data from South Korea's NIS scandal to show coordination traces, not bot-like features, are the most reliable signal, while also introducing a principal-agent lens later formalized by [[Thiele2025-ol]], whose typology of eight campaign types tackles the "attribution problem" — who commissions manipulation — as analytically distinct from detecting inauthenticity. Technical refinements proliferate: [[Mannocci2025-ig]] and [[Iannucci2025-eg]] both push toward multimodal and temporal-aware detection, showing single-modality or flattened-network approaches miss or dilute real coordination; [[Yang2025-iv]] proposes evasion-resistant statistical signals of link-sharing speed/frequency; [[Minici2024-tf]] introduces a graph foundation model (IOHunter) for cross-campaign generalization; [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] and [[Luceri2025-tr]] extend detection to new platform contexts (Bluesky, TikTok) where text-centric assumptions break down. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] caps this thread with a PRISMA survey proposing a unifying definition built on actors, synergic actions, and intent, explicitly decoupling coordination from harm. Skepticism about interstate and platform-based coordination claims appears in [[Pante2025-pq]], which re-tests prior inter-state collaboration claims against control datasets.

### Does coordination matter? The causal reckoning

A striking counter-current questions whether detected coordination and exposure actually shift attitudes or behavior. [[Eady2023-xg]] finds IRA exposure in 2016 was concentrated among already-partisan Republicans and dwarfed by domestic media exposure, with no measurable attitudinal effect — a finding structurally paralleled by [[Appel2026-qr]]'s FIES analysis of 49 deceptive 2020 networks, where correlations with discernment and polarization evaporate after controlling for pre-exposure characteristics. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] formalize this skepticism graph-theoretically, showing real coordinated accounts in UK election cascades achieve influence far below optimal or even random-matched benchmarks. Against this, [[Kim2026-wg]] recovers a strong causal signal by attending to heterogeneous, geo-racially targeted effects invisible to average-treatment-effect designs, and [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] links antivaccine Twitter exposure causally to compartmental epidemic outcomes. [[Karlsson2026-hd]] adds a further twist: even when interference doesn't change votes, *media narratives about* interference can independently polarize audiences along partisan lines.

### Domain and country case studies

The topic's comparative breadth is anchored by case studies spanning the named countries and beyond. Italy dominates via the CooRnet lineage above; health misinformation is examined comparatively in [[Song2025-yh]] (UK/US anti- and pro-vaccine CLSB) and [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] (COVID science communication pathways); Brazil appears via [[Kansaon2025-id]]'s WhatsApp coordination analysis linking online mobilization to the January 8 attacks; Nigeria and India are less directly represented here but sit within the broader Global South brokerage frame of [[Gaw2025-ru]] (Philippines) and the electoral-authoritarian frame of [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] (Serbia); Ukraine/geopolitical cases include [[Waight2025-al]]'s narrative-similarity tracing of Russian "biolabs" claims, [[Graham2025-gp]]'s #IStandWithPutin case study, [[Bosch2024-hj]] on TikTok sound-based Ukraine disinformation, and [[Rogers2026-cy]]'s "post-truth spaces" mapping of Moldovan Facebook discourse. Russian and adjacent state operations are historicized organizationally in [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s OSINT reconstruction of the IRA's labor structure, and extended to Telegram in [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] (Russia/Belarus) and to Korea in [[Kim2026-br]]'s two-decade troll-detection study. China's protest-repression dynamics appear in [[Kulichkina2026-zk]].

### Adjacent phenomena and platform politics

Several papers push the topic's boundaries productively. [[Simeone2025-vo]] shows deplatforming produces network-wide "ripple effects" independent of CIB per se; [[Graham2026-fb]] and Bastos2025-ol interrogate the human/bot and visual-identity boundaries that underpin detection assumptions; [[Zhao2025-ny]] and [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] examine bots as agenda-builders and hashtag-hijackers in more conventional political communication terms; [[Holland_Levin2026-qx]], [[Knupfer2025-vt]], [[Copland2025-em]], and [[Bouchafra2026-ts]] trace cross-platform politicization and propaganda dynamics (culture-war discourse, connective factions, Sky News Australia, Sweden Democrats) that intersect with but aren't reducible to coordination detection. [[Gerard2025-br]] offers a structural innovation — narrative-cluster embeddings identifying "bridge users" who seed cross-platform migration between Truth Social and X. [[Jurg2025-ur]] and [[Oprea2025-lf]] turn to platform accountability, auditing YouTube's moderation and documenting unaddressed hyperactive-user amplification on Facebook during EU elections. [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]] extends coordination-adjacent thinking into marketing, showing influencer-sourced misinformation generates distinct toxic response patterns.

### The AI horizon

Finally, a forward-looking cluster confronts generative AI as both a subject and a tool of coordinated manipulation. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates emergent LLM-agent coordination, showing mere mutual awareness among synthetic agents approximates explicit collusion; [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a governance-oriented warning about "malicious AI swarms"; and [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] notes the adaptive persistence of manipulation campaigns across emerging events — together suggesting that the CooRnet-era behavioral signatures this research program pioneered will need continual reinvention as the actors themselves become synthetic, adaptive, and increasingly difficult to disentangle from authentic participation.
