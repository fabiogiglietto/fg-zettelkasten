# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

### From CLSB to a field: methodological genealogy

The structure note's methodological spine runs directly from [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which first operationalized "coordinated link sharing behavior" (CLSB) on Facebook during the 2018–2019 Italian elections, through the operational hardening of that approach in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] (the MINE-FACTS fact-checking prototype) and [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] (a continuously-updating workflow for the 2022 Italian election). These papers establish the signature move of this line: shift attention from content veracity or actor identity to *collective behavioral signatures*—near-simultaneous, repeated link-sharing across accounts—as a content-agnostic, evasion-resistant proxy for inauthentic coordination. This lineage culminates in the vera.ai-funded present: [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] (gambling promotion and generative-AI visual persuasion) and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] (the "Zombie Internet" synthesis of pro-Putin propaganda, gambling, and adult-content networks), both explicitly framed as outputs of the same CooRnet-descended detection pipeline feeding the VERA-AI Alert monitoring system. [[Rogers2026-cy]], also a vera.ai product, extends the lineage laterally into "post-truth spaces," combining network mapping with source-provenance investigation to serve fact-checkers directly—showing the applied, interventionist orientation that distinguishes this tradition from purely academic detection work.

### Theoretical foundations: what "coordination" even means

Running parallel to the applied CLSB line is a more conceptual strand asking what coordination *is* and whether "coordinated inauthentic behavior" (Meta's own term) is theoretically coherent. [[Starbird2019-qv]] reframes disinformation as participatory, collaborative "work," insisting that the hard problem is distinguishing orchestration from organic crowd behavior—a provocation that haunts nearly everything downstream. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] operationalizes coordination as a continuum rather than a binary, showing coordination and automation are orthogonal. [[Keller2019-nk]], using rare ground-truth data from the Korean NIS astroturfing scandal, shows coordination traces (not bot-like features) are the most robust signal, while also demonstrating that even well-resourced campaigns can have strikingly limited discourse-level impact. This skepticism about impact resurfaces powerfully in [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its sequel [[Di_Marco2026-xu]], which formally quantify (rather than merely detect) how much influence coordinated accounts actually exert in cascades—finding real CIB accounts perform close to random placement, far below optimal influence benchmarks. [[Thiele2025-ol]] pushes the conceptual critique furthest, arguing CIB conflates inauthenticity with intent and proposing an attribution-focused typology grounded in principal-agent theory. [[Mannocci2026-kc]], a PRISMA survey of 122 papers, is the keystone synthesis here, proposing actors/actions/intent as the general definition and authenticity/harmfulness/orchestration/time-variance as orthogonal characterizing dimensions—effectively canonizing the field this structure note maps.

### Detection methods: multiplex, temporal, statistical, and foundation-model advances

A dense cluster of 2025–2026 papers pushes detection methodology beyond single-modality, time-window heuristics. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematically compares five multimodal operationalizations on the 2019 UK election dataset, finding multiplex community detection the most balanced. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] independently converges on multiplex, time-decayed representations, validated against the 26-dataset benchmark later used by [[Iannucci2026-my]], whose hypothesis-testing reframing—coordination detection as a planted-submatrix problem—delivers the field's most rigorous detectability boundaries and a sobering lesson: dense recovered clusters are not evidence of coordination unless benchmarked against explicit null models. [[Yang2025-iv]] attacks the timing-manipulability weakness directly, proposing speed/frequency regularities as more evasion-resistant signals. [[Minici2024-tf]] (IOHunter) and [[Pante2025-pq]] extend detection into graph foundation models and cross-state control-dataset rigor, respectively, the latter explicitly reassessing—and complicating—prior claims of inter-state IO collaboration. Platform-specific adaptation is its own emerging front: [[Luceri2025-tr]] on TikTok's video-first ecosystem, [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] on real-time Bluesky bot detection, and [[Kansaon2025-id]] extending Twitter-derived "Rapid Spread Network" logic into WhatsApp's encrypted ecosystem.

### Empirical case studies: platforms, polities, and actors

A broad empirical layer tests these methods against concrete campaigns. On Facebook, [[Song2025-yh]] uses CooRnet-style CLSB to contrast pro- and anti-vaccine coordination in the UK and US, and [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive-user amplification in Romanian EP election pages. On Twitter/X, [[Simeone2025-vo]] shows deplatforming's "network ripple effects" independent of CIB, while [[Appel2026-qr]] offers an unusually large-scale, platform-collaborative analysis of Meta-identified deceptive networks' real exposure during the 2020 US election—crucially finding that *resharing by unaffiliated accounts*, not direct network posting, drives most reach, and that correlational "effects" on beliefs evaporate under controls. State-linked operations receive sustained attention: [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] reconstructs the IRA's organizational and labor-market structure via OSINT; Bastos2025-ol probes visual self-presentation signatures across the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium dataset; [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] and [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] examine Telegram/Twitter coordination in Russian-Belarusian and Chinese authoritarian contexts respectively; [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues Serbia's SNS exemplifies a subtler, harder-to-detect mimetic authoritarianism; and [[Kim2026-br]] applies explainable ML across two decades of Korean comment data to find condemning rhetoric, not direct praise, is the dominant and most-amplified troll strategy. Beyond elections, [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as political brokerage infrastructure, and [[Copland2025-em]] traces Sky News Australia's propaganda role via network and topic analysis.

### Science communication, platform governance, and generative AI as new frontier

Several papers push the topic's boundaries outward. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] shows coordinated amplification of contrarian science communicators intersecting with news-media pathways, while [[Zhao2025-ny]] and [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] examine bots as agenda-builders via hashtag hijacking and campaign messaging. Governance-adjacent work—[[Jurg2025-ur]] auditing YouTube's moderation during EU elections—complements the detection literature by interrogating platform accountability itself. Most consequentially for the field's near future, a cluster of 2025–2026 papers anticipates generative AI reshaping coordination: [[Orlando2025-ul]] shows LLM agents spontaneously reproduce IO coordination signatures even without central control; [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates to "malicious AI swarms" as a democratic threat requiring new governance; [[Tang2026-gu]] documents AI-driven "matrix account" virality engineering in China; and [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] notes manipulation campaigns' persistence through event-appropriation. Together with [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]'s empirical documentation of ChatGPT-triggered volume explosions in gambling networks, these papers mark the line's newest arc: from detecting human-coordinated inauthenticity to anticipating synthetic, agentic coordination at a scale and adaptivity existing detection frameworks were not built for—precisely the challenge the VERA-AI Alert system is now built to confront.
