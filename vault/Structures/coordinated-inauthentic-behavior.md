# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior

## The CooRnet lineage: from link-sharing to a research program

The anchor of this topic is a methodological lineage that begins with [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]], which reframes disinformation research away from adjudicating truth or unmasking "bad actors" and toward detecting *coordinated collective action*—pages, groups, and profiles that share the same URLs near-simultaneously and repeatedly. This ecological, behavior-first move, formalized as Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) and operationalized in the open-source CooRnet package, becomes the conceptual chassis for nearly everything else filed here. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] extends the approach to the COVID-19 infodemic, showing how a covid-skeptic Italian network grew out of pre-existing political CLSB clusters and cataloguing the evasive tactics (link laundering, first-comment link placement, religious-group cover) that coordinated actors use against platform enforcement. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] then solves CLSB's central operational weakness—that static lists of coordinated accounts decay as actors adapt or get banned—by building a workflow that continuously re-seeds itself from known coordinated accounts to surface new ones, applied to the 2022 Italian snap election and yielding three strikingly different case studies (a hyperpartisan M5S network, a religious clickbait economy, and a Church of Almighty God proselytism operation). The most recent entries in this line, [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]], turn the same detection infrastructure onto new objects: coordinated gambling promotion supercharged by generative AI, and a broader typology of "Deceptive Information Operations" that argues Meta's own CIB/CSH categories are too narrow to capture manipulation that exploits platform affordances and engagement-driven ranking without ever requiring false content. [[Rogers2026-cy]], produced under the same vera.ai umbrella, complements this by proposing "post-truth spaces" as a mapping concept for fact-checkers, showing—via a Moldovan Facebook case study—that fringe CLSB clusters often exert influence indirectly, through proximity to a more centrally located "borderline" cluster rather than through their own network centrality.

## Pressure-testing and extending the detection paradigm

A second cluster treats coordination detection itself as an open methodological problem, largely in dialogue with (and sometimes in tension with) the CooRnet tradition. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] both argue that single-modality, time-agnostic coordination signals are insufficient: the former proposes a time-aware multiplex model that outperforms prior approaches across 26 benchmark datasets, while the latter systematically compares five ways of operationalizing multimodal coordination and shows that naive "flattening" of multiple action-layers—still the field's dominant practice—discards structurally important actors. [[Yang2025-iv]] attacks a related vulnerability directly, showing that CooRnet-style reliance on post-timing is trivially evadable and proposing speed/frequency regularities as a more robust Facebook-specific signal. [[Minici2024-tf]] and [[Gerard2025-br]] push detection toward generalization and cross-platform reconstruction: IOHunter combines language models with graph neural networks to identify IO drivers across heterogeneous campaigns, while CANE/t-CANE models users as distributions over latent narrative clusters to trace how manipulation migrates between platforms (finding a tiny set of "bridge users" responsible for the bulk of Truth Social–to–X narrative diffusion). [[Luceri2025-tr]] and [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] extend the paradigm to underexamined venues—TikTok's video-first ecosystem and real-time bot detection on Bluesky—arguing methods built for text-centric platforms don't transfer cleanly.

Not all of this pressure-testing is additive, however. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] uses a post-hoc influence-quantification framework on UK election retweet cascades to argue that observed coordinated accounts achieve influence far below optimal or even resource-matched benchmarks, their placement statistically indistinguishable from random—a sober counterpoint suggesting CIB may be less pivotal to diffusion than commonly assumed. [[Pante2025-pq]] performs a similar corrective service for claims of *inter-state* coordination, arguing that prior findings of state-to-state collaboration don't survive re-analysis with modern multi-signal methods and proper control datasets. [[Simeone2025-vo]] complicates the picture from another angle: analyzing the ripple effects of Twitter's 2021 deplatforming of election-audit accounts, it finds explicitly that CIB was *not* the mechanism at work (only 10 accounts met coordination thresholds), yet network-level authority structures still collapsed—implying that structural interventions can matter independently of whether bot-like coordination is present.

## Conceptual reckonings: what should "coordination" and "inauthenticity" mean?

Several papers step back to interrogate the field's foundational categories. [[Thiele2025-ol]] is the most systematic: it argues that "coordinated inauthentic behavior" conflates inauthenticity with manipulative intent and is poorly suited to platforms without real-name policies, proposing instead a rational-choice attribution model built around scale, elaborateness, and disguise as signals of a covert principal's resources and stakes. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] arrives at a cognate diagnosis from the empirical side, proposing "Deceptive Information Operations" as a category that decouples manipulation from the presence of false content. [[Song2025-yh]] pushes in the opposite normative direction, using CooRnet itself to show that CLSB is ideologically agnostic—UK and US vaccine discourse reveals coordinated networks on *both* the pro- and anti-vaccine sides, with credible public-health communicators using the same coordinating tactics as misinformation spreaders, differing chiefly in content credibility rather than coordination structure per se. Read together with [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]], which documents Serbian ruling-party networks that succeed specifically by mimicking authentic grassroots support rather than behaving like detectable bot farms, this cluster collectively insists that coordination detection cannot be treated as a solved technical problem separable from questions of intent, harm, and mimicry.

## Scale and consequence: how much does any of this matter?

A distinct empirical strand asks about downstream reach and effects rather than detection mechanics. The paired Meta-collaboration studies [[Appel2026-qr]] and [[noauthor_undated-bm]] (evidently duplicate entries of the same US 2020 FIES analysis) offer the most rigorously causal treatment in the collection: 49 deceptive networks reached tens of millions of users, overwhelmingly via reshares from unaffiliated accounts rather than direct network posting, but associations between exposure and downstream political outcomes largely evaporate once pre-exposure user characteristics are controlled for—a valuable corrective against overclaiming persuasive effects from mere exposure data. [[Kansaon2025-id]] extends the reach question to WhatsApp in Brazil, tracing coordinated messaging to real-world consequences including the January 8, 2023 attacks, while [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] shows coordinated retweet amplification of contrarian scientific experts feeding into (rather than following) news coverage of COVID-19 science.

## Geographic and platform expansion

The topic's tools are increasingly ported to new national contexts and platforms. [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]] applies coordination detection to pro-government Telegram channels in Russia and Belarus; [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] to multilingual Twitter activity around China's 2022 COVID protests; [[Oprea2025-lf]] to hyperactive Facebook sharing during Romania's 2024 EP election; [[Kim2026-br]] to two decades of Korean news comments, using explainable ML to show state-linked trolls favor morally condemning rhetoric over direct praise; and [[Gaw2025-ru]] reframes Philippine influence operations as a form of political brokerage rather than isolated disinformation. [[Poliakoff2026-fa]] and Bastos2025-ol mine large released troll datasets—OSINT CVs and the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium's imagery—to recover organizational and visual-identity dimensions of coordination that behavioral network analysis alone cannot see. [[Copland2025-em]] and [[Graham2025-gp]] examine adjacent propaganda dynamics (Sky News Australia's transnational amplification pipeline; the #IStandWithPutin campaign's exploitation of platform trust infrastructure) that sit at CIB's conceptual border without being CLSB studies proper. [[Zhao2025-ny]] and [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] similarly probe bot-adjacent phenomena—hashtag hijacking and algorithmic agenda-building—that extend the topic's concerns into narrower tactical registers.

## The generative-AI horizon

The newest arc anticipates how large language models reshape both the threat and its study. [[Orlando2025-ul]] shows LLM agents in simulated information operations spontaneously reproducing real-world coordination signatures—denser networks, converging narratives, synchronized amplification—once given mere awareness of teammates, without explicit instruction. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a governance argument about "malicious AI swarms" that fuse LLM reasoning with persistent multi-agent architectures to manufacture consensus at unprecedented scale, arguing detection alone cannot keep pace and calling for provenance infrastructure and international oversight. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] grounds this speculative arc empirically, documenting an exponential, AI-driven surge in coordinated gambling-promotion imagery after ChatGPT's release—demonstrating that generative AI does not displace the coordination mechanisms this whole topic has mapped, but intensifies and recombines them. [[FitzGerald2025-nv]], read alongside these, frames the underlying continuity: manipulation campaigns persist by adaptively appropriating whatever new affordance or event becomes available, of which generative AI is only the latest instance.
