# Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior Detection

## The founding move: from content to coordinated action

The line of argument running through this topic begins with a deliberate methodological pivot away from adjudicating truth or falsity toward detecting *behavior*. [[Giglietto2020-9d8acdd7]] inaugurates this program during the 2018–2019 Italian elections, arguing that disinformation research should shift from content/actor detection to tracking coordinated collective action — operationalized as entities sharing identical URLs in anomalously tight temporal windows. This CLSB (Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior) approach, implemented in the open-source CooRnet package, becomes the signature contribution anchoring this entire topic, and its theoretical scaffolding is echoed by [[Starbird2019-qv]], which reframes information operations as *collaborative work* straddling orchestrated and organic participation, and by [[Keller2019-nk]], whose ground-truthed South Korean NIS case shows that coordination traces, not individual bot-like markers, are the most robust signal of astroturfing. [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] pushes the conceptual move further, showing coordination is a continuum rather than a binary and that it is largely orthogonal to automation — a finding that reverberates throughout later work questioning bot-centric detection.

## Sustaining and scaling the CooRnet/CLSB lineage

A cluster of papers extends the original CLSB machinery toward operational, real-world deployment. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] (MINE-FACTS) adapts CooRnet into a content-agnostic tool for Italian COVID-19 fact-checkers, documenting how coordinated covid-skeptic networks evolve tactically (link laundering, first-comment link placement, religious-group infiltration) to evade enforcement. [[Giglietto2023-fa71a001]] addresses the inherent staleness of static coordinated-account lists, proposing an iterative monitoring workflow that surfaced hundreds of new coordinated actors during the 2022 Italian election, from hyperpartisan political networks to clickbait and religious-proselytism operations. This infrastructure culminates in the VERA-AI ecosystem: [[Rogers2026-cy]] introduces "post-truth spaces" as a complementary mapping concept for verification specialists, demonstrating on Moldovan Facebook data that borderline clusters bridging post-truth and mainstream discourse carry more network influence than the fringe clusters themselves; [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] turns the same detection pipeline onto coordinated gambling promotion, showing how generative AI intensifies and industrializes persuasive visual strategies after ChatGPT's release; and [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] synthesizes three VERA-AI case studies (pro-Putin propaganda, gambling, unmoderated adult-content groups) into the broader concept of "Deceptive Information Operations," arguing that Meta's narrow CIB/CSH categories cannot capture manipulation sustained by platform affordances rather than overt inauthenticity alone.

## Theorizing coordination: definitions, typologies, attribution

Running parallel to this applied lineage is a more conceptual strand interrogating what "coordination" and "inauthenticity" actually mean. [[Thiele2025-ol]] criticizes the CIB construct itself for conflating inauthenticity with manipulative intent, proposing a rational-choice typology oriented around attribution of covert principals. [[Mannocci2026-kc]] offers the most comprehensive reconciliation yet, surveying 122 papers to build a general definition around actors, synergic actions, and intent, and explicitly decoupling detection from characterization — a distinction that organizes much of the methodological literature surveyed below. Together with [[Nizzoli2020-cf]] and [[Starbird2019-qv]], these papers insist that coordination is not inherently malicious (a claim empirically reinforced by [[Song2025-yh]], which finds both pro- and anti-vaccine Facebook communities using CLSB, with harm determined by source credibility rather than coordination per se).

## Advancing the network-science toolkit

A dense methodological frontier extends the original CooRnet-style approach along several axes. Temporal and modal richness is the focus of [[Iannucci2025-eg]] (time-decayed multiplex collaboration networks) and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] (a systematic comparison of multimodal operationalizations, finding multiplex community detection the most balanced). [[Yang2025-iv]] attacks the timing-manipulability problem directly, proposing speed-and-frequency regularities as more evasion-resistant signals for Facebook link sharing. Generalization across campaigns and platforms motivates [[Minici2024-tf]]'s graph foundation model (IOHunter) and [[Gerard2025-br]]'s platform-agnostic CANE/t-CANE framework, which reconceives cross-platform influence as shared narrative-cluster affiliation rather than direct interaction, identifying a small set of "bridge users" driving Truth Social–to–X narrative migration. [[Luceri2025-tr]] and [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] push detection into under-studied video-first (TikTok) and decentralized (Bluesky) ecosystems, while [[Pante2025-pq]] applies modern multi-signal detection with control datasets to show that some previously claimed inter-state coordination may not survive rigorous re-analysis — a methodological corrective in the same spirit as the field's broader push toward statistical rigor.

## Does coordination matter? Influence, impact, and skepticism

A distinct but closely related strand asks not whether coordination can be detected but whether it actually *matters* for diffusion and attitudes. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] and its companion [[Di_Marco2026-xu]] formalize post-hoc influence evaluation on retweet cascades, finding that real coordinated accounts in the 2019 UK election dataset achieve influence far below optimal or even greedy benchmarks — suggesting CIB is often resource-poor and badly placed rather than devastatingly effective. This skepticism echoes earlier survey-linked findings: [[Eady2023-xg]] shows IRA exposure in 2016 was extremely concentrated among already-partisan users and dwarfed by domestic media exposure, with no measurable attitudinal effect, while [[Appel2026-qr]] extends this to 49 Meta-identified networks in 2020, showing reach is driven mostly by organic resharing rather than direct network activity, and that apparent correlations with outcomes evaporate after controlling for pre-exposure characteristics. [[Simeone2025-vo]] complicates the picture from the moderation side, showing that targeted deplatforming (not CIB per se) can flip an entire community's authority structure — a reminder that network interventions, not just detection, are part of the causal story.

## Platform, regime, and geographic diversification

The detection paradigm has been exported widely beyond its Italian/Facebook origins, testing its portability across platforms and political contexts. WhatsApp's closed ecosystem is tackled by [[Kansaon2025-id]] in Brazil, linking coordinated messaging to real-world mobilization around January 8. Telegram's pro-government channels in Russia and Belarus are mapped by [[Kuznetsova2025-nu]], while [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] examines dual-use coordination (protest and repression) during China's COVID-19 protests. Electoral contexts recur throughout: [[Oprea2025-lf]] documents hyperactive-user amplification in Romanian EP elections, and [[Jovanovic-Harrington2026-ze]] argues Serbia's SNS deploys a more mimetic, harder-to-detect form of digital authoritarianism than centralized bot farms — a claim that sits in productive tension with detection-optimism elsewhere in the topic. Organizational and labor-market perspectives on troll production appear in [[Poliakoff2026-fa]]'s OSINT reconstruction of the IRA's internal hierarchy and [[Gaw2025-ru]]'s brokerage framing of Philippine influence operations, while Bastos2025-ol adds a visual-identity dimension to troll-farm attribution. Thematic and discourse-level coordination studies — [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] on COVID science communication, [[Copland2025-em]] on Sky News Australia's network-propaganda role, [[Zhao2025-ny]] on hashtag hijacking, [[Arceneaux2026-xk]] on bot agenda-building, [[Holland_Levin2026-qx]] on the politicization of "woke," and [[Kim2026-br]]'s two-decade machine-learning study of Korean troll rhetoric — collectively show the method's reach into agenda-setting, platform policy, and cross-national information contestation.

## The emerging frontier: generative and agentic AI

The topic's most recent arc turns toward how generative and agentic AI reshape coordination itself. [[Orlando2025-ul]] uses generative agent-based modeling to show that LLM agents spontaneously reproduce IO-like coordination once mutual awareness is introduced, with teammate awareness alone nearly matching explicit deliberation. [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a governance-oriented warning about "malicious AI swarms" capable of manufacturing consensus and poisoning training data at scale, while [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] notes the more mundane but consequential adaptive persistence of manipulation campaigns as they appropriate emerging events. Read alongside [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]'s empirical documentation of AI-amplified gambling promotion, these papers suggest the CooRnet/CLSB tradition's next methodological challenge: detecting coordination whose actors are no longer merely inauthentic humans or crude bots, but adaptive, narrative-generating AI agents operating at a pace and scale the original detection paradigm was not built to anticipate.
