# Generative AI and Media

## From hype to infrastructure: how AI imaginaries are produced

A first cluster of work treats generative AI less as a technology than as a discursive and political formation. [[Dodds2026-df]] frames AI hype as a *systemic infrastructure* that redistributes legitimacy and resources rather than a passing bubble, and [[Vertesi2026-lv]] sharpens this by arguing that even well-meaning critique can be enrolled as a "decoy" that stabilizes the underlying Project of AI. [[Hepp2026-oi]] complements the loud futuring of CEOs with the "quiet futuring" of Bay Area pioneer communities — Effective Altruism, Rationalists, lab houses — that supply the imaginative reservoir. [[Stanusch2026-ec]] traces how the Altman controversy let the industry occupy the future through *premediation* and *preclusion*, while [[Weinbrand2026-sf]] shows the same logic in Google's framing of AI Overviews as seamless, inevitable infrastructure. Cross-nationally, [[Wang2025-zy]], [[Nguyen2026-vm]] and [[Richter2026-bt]] map how newspapers and Twitter discourse have varied across the UK, US, China, India and Germany, finding that mentalistic-agentic framings, utopian/dystopian tropes, and stakeholder composition are more nationally and organizationally patterned than any uniform "AI narrative" would suggest. [[Fattorini2026-bo]] grounds this in Italian public opinion as *critical ambivalence* rather than technophobia, and the Anthropic user survey in [[UnknownUnknown-db]] supplies a worker-level counterpart, linking exposure to displacement concern.

## Persuasion: from speculative panic to mechanism

A second, fast-consolidating line interrogates whether and how conversational AI actually persuades. [[Costello2024-bg]] opened the door by showing durable, generalizing reductions in conspiracy belief through tailored GPT-4 dialogue. [[Lin2025-xp]] extended this to real elections across the US, Canada, and Poland, finding effects that exceed conventional political ads and are driven primarily by *facts*, not psychological manipulation or personalization. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] decomposes the levers — post-training, prompting, and especially *information density* — and surfaces a sharp persuasion–accuracy trade-off. [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] then escalates the stakes: frontier systems out-persuade not only laypeople but elite debaters and professional canvassers, with the advantage collapsing only when AI is throttled to human throughput. [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] adds a crucial boundary condition: perceived partisan bias in the model attenuates persuasion by roughly a quarter, repoliticizing what prior work had treated as an apolitical capacity. [[Szabo2026-rd]] turns persuasion defensively into *conversational inoculation*, while [[Schroeder2026-im]] warns that the same capacities, embedded in multi-agent swarms, could fabricate consensus at scale — a possibility [[Orlando2025-ul]] demonstrates in simulation, where mere teammate awareness suffices to produce coordinated influence dynamics.

## Synthetic media, misinformation, and epistemic security

A third strand asks what generative outputs do to the information environment. [[Emilio2026-ik]] articulates the umbrella concept of *synthetic reality* and the paradox that pervasive synthesis raises the cost of truth itself. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] documents the financial-fraud edge of this transformation and pushes liability upstream to platforms. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] usefully deflates some of the alarm, finding that AI-generated still images do not reliably out-persuade text — decontextualized real video remains the more potent vector. [[Choi2026-bz]] adds a metacognitive twist: difficulty authenticating one deepfake carries over modality-congruently into susceptibility to the next. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] grounds these dynamics empirically, showing an exponential post-ChatGPT surge in AI-generated gambling promotion that intensifies, rather than replaces, prior persuasion drivers. On the corrective side, [[DeVerna2025-dl]] shows that LLM fact-checking succeeds only with curated RAG, not via web search or reasoning alone, and [[Dierickx2026-tw]] proposes "emergent facts" as the epistemic category required to evaluate such outputs. [[Triedman2025-uy]] offers a striking case study of Grokipedia as a politically tilted, AI-mediated alternative to Wikipedia, citing blacklisted sources and even Musk's own tweets. [[Waight2026-ts]] complements this by tracing how state-controlled media gets *laundered* into commercial LLM outputs, a structural form of bias distinct from explicit model design.

## LLMs as research instruments

A fourth cluster repositions LLMs as instruments in the social-science toolkit. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] and [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] develop and validate LLMs-in-the-loop pipelines for political discourse on Facebook, with the latter showing that OpenAI embeddings outperform Italian BERT variants for clustering. [[Arminio2025-tw]] extends this visual-side, using VLLMs to capture *connotative* meaning that CNN-based pipelines miss; [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] similarly shows GPT-4o outperforming specialized face-recognition models on Instagram campaign images. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] and [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] use LLMs for political scaling and survey-response measurement, while [[Lee2026-je]] illustrates the double edge: LLMs reliably infer political alignment even from non-political text, an analytical asset and a privacy threat. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] pushes the agenda further, showing that frontier coding agents can reproduce a majority of published social-science findings — but also exhibit sycophancy under confirmatory prompts. The survey by [[Tan2024-vl]] situates these moves within a broader landscape of LLM-based annotation and synthesis. Against this enthusiasm, [[Balluff2026-if]] urges reflexivity about reproducibility, environmental cost, and corporate dependency; [[Brown2025-jk]] finds demographic bias in LLM annotation small relative to item difficulty; and Paci2026-pu (in Italian political discourse) and [[Paci2025-ag]] show clear limits in pragmatic reasoning that warn against treating LLMs as drop-in human coders.

## Toward a post-social media studies

Several papers zoom out to ask what these developments mean for the field itself. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] proposes that the *social media* paradigm is dissolving into three post-social formations — algorithmic broadcasting, semi-private spheres, and AI-mediated communication — each requiring new concepts and methods. [[Ng2026-og]] argues, formally, that agentic AI demands social theory as a structural prior rather than a single-task framing. [[Gillespie2026-aa]] reads red-teaming as the next chapter of content moderation's sociotechnical labor problem. And [[Manovich2026-ih]] offers a media-theoretic counterpoint: generative AI is the first medium that *itself thinks*, with built-in media cognition and an inherent aesthetic conservatism. Read together, these papers suggest an emerging consensus: generative AI is not simply a new tool for studying media, nor merely a new media object — it is reshaping the very objects, methods, infrastructures, and labor arrangements through which media research is done, and the discipline is still catching up to that fact.
