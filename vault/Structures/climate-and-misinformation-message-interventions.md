# Message-Based Interventions for Climate Communication and Misinformation Resistance

## The arc: from static messages to adaptive dialogue

The papers filed here trace a shift in how climate and misinformation communication research conceives of "the message." At one end sit single-shot, broadcast-style interventions — a framed sentence, a short video, a fake headline — whose effects are measured across large, representative samples. At the other end sit interactive, personalized dialogues, increasingly powered by large language models, that adapt in real time to what a listener actually says. Read together, the set documents both the limits of the first approach and the growing evidence for the second, while a framing piece ([[Spampatti2026-kx]]) argues that the whole enterprise of misinformation research should borrow maturity from climate psychology's decades-long struggle with exactly this tension between informational and motivational approaches.

## Testing the limits of static framing

[[Voelkel2026-lc]] is the most sobering entry here, and arguably the keystone empirical result for the "broadcast message" paradigm. Pre-registering a megastudy on the ten most-cited climate messaging strategies, the authors find that even the most celebrated framings in the literature move attitudes by only a few percentage points, show little evidence of partisan-tailored heterogeneity, and fail entirely to shift costly behaviour (donations), sometimes backfiring. This complements [[Lieu2025-nl]], which manipulates the *content* and *logical structure* of misinformation itself rather than corrective messaging, and finds that fallacy type is largely inert while the substantive content category (attacks on climate solutions, attacks on scientists) drives both perceived veracity and political polarization. Together these two studies suggest that the field has over-invested in parametrically varying message wording and rhetorical form, while under-appreciating that what matters is the *topic* being attacked and the *audience's* prior commitments — a point [[Spampatti2026-kx]] makes explicitly when it argues that misinformation research has overemphasized cognitive manipulation checks and under-measured real, high-stakes behavioural outcomes, and that effects vary enormously across countries, domains, and online/offline contexts.

## Inoculation: from lab demonstration to field deployment

A second strand follows inoculation theory's trajectory toward ecological validity. [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]] is a milestone: a 19-second prebunking video deployed as an actual Instagram ad to over 375,000 users, showing a durable (five-month) 21-point gain in participants' ability to spot emotional manipulation, alongside tripled click-through to further information. This moves inoculation out of the survey panel and into the feed itself, addressing exactly the "real-world behavioural outcome" gap that [[Spampatti2026-kx]] flags as a systemic weakness of the field. [[Szabo2026-rd]] pushes inoculation further toward interactivity, introducing "Conversational Inoculation" via an LLM chatbot (MindFort/"Forty") and comparing it against traditional reading- and writing-based refutation. The chatbot condition outperforms static formats once individual baseline susceptibility is controlled, and qualitative analysis locates the mechanism not in argument content per se but in interactional features — adaptability, trust-building, scaffolding of independent thought — that static inoculation materials cannot provide. This is a conceptual bridge between the prebunking literature and the LLM-dialogue literature that dominates the rest of the topic.

## Conversational AI as the new persuasive modality

The most striking convergence across this folder is the emergence of LLM dialogue as a qualitatively more powerful intervention than single-shot messaging. [[Costello2024-bg]] is the foundational result: three-round, personalized GPT-4 conversations reduce conspiracy belief by roughly 20%, durably (≥2 months), with generalization to unrelated conspiracies — directly challenging motivational accounts that treat conspiracy belief as impervious to evidence. [[Kotz2026-lk]] extends this logic across domains (climate, vaccination, inequality) with GPT-5, showing that dialogue-based persuasion is not conspiracy-specific but generalizes to contested policy attitudes, and — crucially — works best precisely on the skeptics that static campaigns like those in [[Voelkel2026-lc]] struggle to move, with trust in science as the key amplifying moderator. Read against [[Voelkel2026-lc]]'s null results on behavior and limited partisan reach, these two papers suggest that the "depth and personalization" missing from decades of brief messaging may be the active ingredient, not merely a delivery format.

[[Dubey2026-bl]] complicates this optimism in an instructive way. Testing a "balanced" chatbot that presents mainstream and conspiratorial climate content side by side, it finds — counter to the reactance/motivated-reasoning predictions one might draw from [[Lieu2025-nl]] or [[Costello2024-bg]]'s framing debates — that higher-conspiracy-belief users actually trust and prefer the chatbot *more* than low-belief users, with perceived usefulness and trust (not ease of use) driving acceptance. This is a useful caution: conversational AI's persuasive power in [[Costello2024-bg]] and [[Kotz2026-lk]] came from tailored counter-evidence, not mere balance or format; [[Dubey2026-bl]]'s balanced-chatbot design achieves acceptance without necessarily achieving belief change, reminding the field that trust and engagement are necessary but not sufficient conditions for correction.

## Synthesis

Taken as a whole, the topic traces an evolution from measuring whether a cleverly worded sentence moves a dial ([[Voelkel2026-lc]], [[Lieu2025-nl]]) toward asking whether an adaptive, trust-building conversation can change minds durably and at scale ([[Costello2024-bg]], [[Kotz2026-lk]], [[Szabo2026-rd]], [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]]), with [[Dubey2026-bl]] cautioning that acceptance of a conversational format is not the same as belief revision. [[Spampatti2026-kx]] supplies the meta-level diagnosis that ties the set together: the discipline needs richer behavioural measurement, cross-context generalizability, and integration of motivational/social drivers alongside cognitive ones — precisely the gaps that the conversational-AI and field-deployed-inoculation studies in this folder are beginning, tentatively, to fill.
