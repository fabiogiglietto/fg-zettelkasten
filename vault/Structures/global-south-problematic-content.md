# Problematic Content in the Global South

## Beyond the Anglophone Feed: Coordination in Southern Information Ecosystems

Research on coordinated manipulation has long orbited Twitter and Facebook in North American and European elections. The papers gathered here push that agenda outward — geographically, to Brazil and the Philippines, and infrastructurally, into encrypted messaging apps and the political-economic scaffolding around campaigns. Together they suggest that a coordination framework built on public-feed platforms captures only a fraction of what "problematic content" looks like where WhatsApp is the dominant civic infrastructure and where election-time manipulation is organized as an industry.

### Messaging Apps as the Missing Layer

Two of the three papers converge on WhatsApp as the analytic frontier. [[Ventura2025-sw]] approaches the platform experimentally, deactivating users' accounts during the Brazilian election to establish that closed messaging materially shapes exposure to misinformation and political beliefs — a causal counterpart to observational feed studies. [[Kansaon2025-id]] approaches the same terrain forensically, adapting Twitter-derived Rapid Spread Network methods to 13 million messages across 1,444 public groups, and demonstrating that coordinated inauthentic behavior thrives inside encrypted environments once treated as methodologically inaccessible. Read together, they make a joint case: the private-messaging layer is neither a marginal supplement nor a black box, but a first-order site of political influence — one where platform countermeasures designed for feeds (forwarding limits, account bans) largely miss the mark, since, as [[Kansaon2025-id]] shows, most coordinated messages bypass forwarding altogether.

### From Content to Consequence

A recurring thread is the traceability of online coordination into offline political mobilization. [[Kansaon2025-id]] documents how coordinated WhatsApp activity fed directly into the doxxing of Supreme Court justices and the January 8, 2023 attacks in Brasília, with specific messages tracked from coordinated dissemination through group-level amplification to in-person harassment and street protests calling for military intervention. This moves the coordination literature past its familiar reliance on engagement metrics: the outcome variable is not virality but a Supreme Court under siege. [[Ventura2025-sw]] complements this by supplying the individual-level mechanism — that WhatsApp exposure does in fact shift beliefs — closing part of the loop between coordinated supply and political consequence.

### Coordination as Political Economy

[[Gaw2025-ru]] shifts registers, offering a theoretical reframing that the Brazil papers implicitly need. Drawing on interviews with influence-operation leads in the 2022 Philippine elections, it recasts IOs not as ad hoc propaganda but as *brokerage* — enterprises that sit between political clients, platforms, and publics, sustained by durable political-economic infrastructures. This reframing complicates the "coordinated network" as the unit of analysis in [[Kansaon2025-id]]: the 1,575 coordinated accounts identified computationally may be the visible surface of a brokerage economy invisible in the trace data. Conversely, [[Gaw2025-ru]]'s qualitative account gains empirical purchase when read alongside the network patterns [[Kansaon2025-id]] documents — concentrated activity (27% of accounts producing 80% of messages) is precisely what a brokerage model would predict.

### An Emerging Southern Research Program

Across the three papers a distinct research program is taking shape. Methodologically, it triangulates experimental (Ventura), computational-network (Kansaon), and ethnographic-interview (Gaw) approaches, refusing to let any one method define the object. Substantively, it treats the Global South not as a testing ground for imported frameworks but as a site where those frameworks are stress-tested and revised — WhatsApp forces new detection methods, Philippine campaign economies force new theories of what an "influence operation" even is. The open question the cluster leaves is integrative: how do the brokerage infrastructures [[Gaw2025-ru]] describes actually produce the coordinated traces [[Kansaon2025-id]] measures, and how do both aggregate into the belief shifts [[Ventura2025-sw]] estimates? A comparative design spanning Brazil, the Philippines, and cases yet to be added (India, Nigeria) is the natural next move.
