# Social Media Engagement & Reactions

## The Emotional Architecture of Platform Engagement

At the center of this cluster sits a deceptively simple observation: platforms encode affect as data, and that data circulates, structures visibility, and is read back by users and researchers as a signal of political and social meaning. [[Anwar2024-34dba628]] provides the connective tissue for the whole topic, synthesizing 64 studies on Facebook's Reactions feature and establishing the foundational finding that recurs across nearly every other paper here: negative, arousing reactions—especially anger—drive engagement and virality more reliably than positive ones, and populist/far-right actors are disproportionately skilled at eliciting them. This review essentially sets the research agenda that the empirical papers in the set go on to test, extend, or complicate.

[[Kalsnes2025-zb]] offers a direct empirical elaboration of this agenda in a comparative Scandinavian setting, showing that 'Angry' reactions positively predict sharing while 'Love' reactions suppress it—confirming the anger-virality link at scale while adding nuance about cross-national variation (Sweden's more balanced emotional economy versus Norway's stronger anger-share coupling) and about supply-side asymmetries between populist and mainstream parties. Read alongside [[Anwar2024-34dba628]], it demonstrates how a systematic review's aggregate claims cash out in a specific multi-country dataset, and how affective reactions function as a genuine *affordance*—not just a byproduct—shaping what political content survives in the feed.

## From Aggregate Signals to Community Dynamics

Where [[Kalsnes2025-zb]] and [[Anwar2024-34dba628]] treat reactions largely as outcome variables, [[Marino2026-he]] pushes the inquiry longitudinally, asking what affective engagement looks like *inside* a single hyperpartisan community over time. Its three-year study of pro-Bolsonaro Facebook networks is the most direct challenge in the set to static echo-chamber assumptions: rather than finding stable, impermeable affective patterns, it documents volatility keyed to political events (Lula's inauguration, the January 2023 coup attempt) and even evidence of adversarial "brigading" that destabilizes reaction patterns from within. This paper effectively asks the question that a systematic review like [[Anwar2024-34dba628]] cannot: not just *which* reactions dominate, but how their meaning and intensity evolve as political context shifts, reframing partisan communities as dynamic and event-sensitive rather than fixed.

[[Green2025-ap]] complicates the picture from a different angle, arguing that the very act of measuring partisanship at the level of engagement or source obscures what is actually happening at the level of the individual story. Its concept of "curation bubbles" suggests that homogeneity in reactions and sharing is not necessarily an echo-chamber pathology but an artifact of unbundling and rebundling content through social networks—a claim that sits in productive tension with [[Marino2026-he]]'s finding of instability and with [[Kalsnes2025-zb]]'s cross-national reaction patterns, since all three are wrestling with how to characterize the true unit of partisan signal (post, story, or community) amid engagement metrics that can mislead if read at the wrong level of aggregation. [[Messing2014-jc]], though earlier and experimental rather than observational, supplies a foundational counterpoint to the echo-chamber narrative that both [[Green2025-ap]] and [[Marino2026-he]] engage with: social endorsement cues, it finds, can override partisan source selection entirely, suggesting engagement signals (likes, endorsements) may sometimes *increase* cross-cutting exposure rather than reinforce affective silos.

## Weaponized and Ambivalent Affect

A second thread examines how affective engagement is strategically manufactured and moralized. [[Nangle2026-yo]] extends the anger-centric account of far-right virality found in [[Anwar2024-34dba628]] by showing that positive affect and humour—not hostility—are the operative tools of contemporary far-right normalization on Instagram, describing an "affective-discursive ecology" where algorithmic proximity amplifies softened extremist content. This nuances the anger-dominant story: engagement-optimized affect is not monolithically negative but strategically selected for platform and audience.

[[Di-Domenico2026-zq]] and [[Xue2025-bp]] both interrogate the underside of engagement-driven communication ostensibly aimed at correcting or informing. [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]] shows that misinformation from influencers generates qualitatively different, more homogeneous toxicity than the same content from regular users, implicating credibility and parasocial bonding as infrastructural amplifiers of collective antagonism—a source-based complement to [[Anwar2024-34dba628]]'s content-based account of what drives negative engagement. [[Xue2025-bp]] turns the lens on fact-checking itself, finding that even this ostensibly neutral genre relies on emotional framing to secure engagement, suggesting that the anger/love economy documented across Kalsnes, Anwar, and Nangle penetrates corrective as well as adversarial discourse.

## Engagement as Relational and Infrastructural Labour

A final set of papers reframes engagement signals not as expressions of political sentiment but as social and infrastructural labor. [[Farci2017-2a88c00e]]'s earlier ethnography of "networked intimacy" anticipates the logic later papers apply to political contexts: Facebook's low-cost affective signals (liking, tagging) function as relational currency, a claim that helps explain *why* reactions carry such interpretive weight in political contexts explored by [[Kalsnes2025-zb]] and [[Marino2026-he]]. [[Volpe2026-um]] shows how creators strategically perform care and community to satisfy platform demands, echoing [[Nangle2026-yo]]'s and [[Di-Domenico2026-zq]]'s concern with affect as manufactured rather than spontaneous. Finally, [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] returns to the aggregate, gatekeeping level, demonstrating that engagement (shares vs. comments) tracks emotional resonance and topic salience—celebrity and scandal outperforming technical health content—reconfirming, in a non-political domain, the same emotionality-drives-engagement logic that structures the entire topic cluster, from Reactions research to hyperpartisan community dynamics.
