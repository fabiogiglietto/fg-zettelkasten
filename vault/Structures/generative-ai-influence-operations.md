# Generative AI & Influence Operations

## The Shape of a New Research Front

This cluster documents a field caught mid-pivot: the vocabulary and methods of disinformation studies, built for a social-media era of shared feeds and human-authored content, are being retrofitted for a landscape of generative synthesis, conversational persuasion, and algorithmically distributed AI slop. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] supplies the framing thesis for the whole collection — the "social media" paradigm is dissolving into three post-social formations (algorithmic broadcasting, semi-private micro-communities, and AI-mediated communication) — and much of what follows can be read as case studies filling in each of those three boxes.

## Conversational Persuasion: Mechanisms, Limits, and Asymmetries

The most coherent thread is a rapidly maturing experimental literature on LLMs as persuaders. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] establishes the foundational finding that post-training and prompting, not scale or personalization, drive persuasiveness, with information density as the master variable — a claim [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] pushes further by showing frontier AI out-persuades even elite human debaters and professional canvassers, again traceable to fact throughput rather than rapport. [[Lin2025-xp]] corroborates this across four real elections, finding AI persuades via evidence rather than manipulation, though with a troubling asymmetry: right-leaning AI advocacy is systematically less accurate. [[Costello2024-bg]] and [[Kotz2026-lk]] extend the paradigm to conspiracy beliefs and contested policy domains (climate, vaccines, inequality), showing durable, generalizing effects concentrated among skeptics — a hopeful counter-note to the manipulation narrative. Yet this optimism is bounded by two important complications. First, the persuasion-accuracy trade-off: [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] shows the very techniques that maximize persuasiveness degrade factual accuracy. Second, persuasion is not politically inert: [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] demonstrates that perceived partisan bias in the messenger cuts belief correction by roughly a quarter, and [[Rauchfleisch2026-fa]] shows that transparency about *identity* (AI-vs-human) does nothing to blunt persuasion, whereas disclosing *intent* halves it — a distinction with direct regulatory stakes (EU AI Act Article 50) that reframes what "meaningful transparency" should mean. [[Szabo2026-rd]] rounds out this thread by flipping the persuasion tool into a defensive one: conversational inoculation against misinformation, where interactivity itself — not just content — is the active ingredient.

## Synthetic Visuals, Coordinated Networks, and the New Persuasion Ecosystem

A second cluster examines generative AI not as a chat-based persuader but as an industrial input into coordinated visual and multi-agent campaigns — the module's titular concern. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] is the anchor case study: coordinated Facebook gambling networks show an exponential, ChatGPT-coincident surge in synthetic visual persuasion, exploiting Meta's organic/paid regulatory gap. This sits alongside [[Tang2026-gu]] and [[Galip2026-ix]], which examine the labor and aesthetics of "AI slop" as an industrialized creative practice (matrix accounts, benchmarked virality) and as a culturally ambivalent "gimmick" (Ngai) that is simultaneously effortless and overworked. Orlando2025-uy moves from empirical platform analysis to simulation, modeling how networked LLM agents spontaneously develop IO-like coordination — narrative convergence, synchronized amplification — merely from mutual awareness, without explicit orchestration, which resonates with [[Schroeder2026-im]]'s policy-forum warning about "malicious AI swarms" fabricating consensus at scale. Visual platform politics reappear in [[Bouchafra2026-ts]] (Sweden Democrats' platform-tailored securitizing memes on TikTok, including decontextualized foreign footage) and [[Hollingshead2026-vx]] (TikTok immigration discourse, where affordances cut both toward inclusion and xenophobia, with GenAI-enabled "speculative worldbuilding" as an emerging risk). [[Bosch2024-hj]] adds the underexamined sonic dimension of TikTok propaganda around the Ukraine invasion. Notably, [[Hameleers2026-mc]] complicates the assumption that synthetic visuals are inherently more potent: AI images did not consistently outperform text, and low-tech decontextualized video proved more credible for salient conflicts — a useful check on techno-deterministic alarm.

## Deepfakes, Fraud, and the Sociotechnical Erosion of Trust

Where the previous thread treats visuals as persuasion inputs, another set of papers treats them as fraud and epistemic-erosion vectors. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] maps the deepfake financial-fraud ecosystem and argues for gatekeeper (not victim) liability, converging with [[Emilio2026-ik]]'s theoretical claim that the real danger is not isolated deepfakes but "synthetic realities" — coherent, interactive fabricated environments producing a paradox whereby societies rationally discount all digital evidence. [[Wack2026-bt]]'s Kenya-based field experiment operationalizes this erosion empirically: deepfake impact is not fixed at production but "socially negotiated" through surrounding audience comments, with partisanship strongly moderating credulity — a finding that speaks directly to Ferrara's institutional-layer concerns and to Global South contexts underrepresented elsewhere in the cluster (also present in [[Beacken2026-zb]]'s comparative six-country study of GenAI propaganda in weakened democracies, which cautions against assuming uniform AI effects).

## Trust, Disclosure, and Journalism's Adaptation

A cross-cutting concern is how audiences and institutions respond once AI involvement is disclosed. [[Suk2026-ai]] offers the meta-theoretical scaffolding — a multilevel (individual/institutional/societal) framework porting media-trust theory to generative AI. Empirically, [[Gilardi2026-hw]] finds AI disclosure produces a curiosity bump in engagement without quality penalties or lasting acceptance, while [[Mattis2026-gu]] shows disclosure effects are task-specific, harshest for fact-checking, and segmented across at least five distinct audience profiles — jointly suggesting that "AI disclosure" is not a monolithic treatment, echoing [[Rauchfleisch2026-fa]]'s identity-vs-intent distinction from the persuasion literature.

## LLMs as Research Instruments: Classification, Annotation, and Epistemic Infrastructure

The teaching module's other pole — LLMs as tools for studying political discourse — is represented by methods-forward papers testing what these models can and cannot reliably do. [[Arminio2025-tw]] shows VLLM-generated textual descriptions substantially improve *connotative* (meaning-level) image clustering over CNN pipelines, with interpretability gains directly useful for computational social science on visual persuasion (complementing the gambling-image work of [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]). [[Paci2025-ag]] delivers a sobering counterpoint: even strong LLMs fall well short of expert humans at interpreting implicature and presupposition in real Italian political speech, exposing a pragmatic-competence ceiling relevant to any pipeline using LLMs to annotate manipulative discourse. [[Kasianenko2026-tn]] audits a different kind of "AI as infrastructure" — Google's AI Overviews — finding uneven guardrails that debunk established conspiracies (chemtrails) more reliably than emergent ones (15-minute cities), sometimes inadvertently legitimizing the latter. [[Triedman2025-uy]] extends this audit logic to Grokipedia, showing it is Wikipedia-derivative yet systematically skews toward unreliable/blacklisted sourcing on controversial topics — a case of an AI system functioning simultaneously as content generator and knowledge-infrastructure competitor. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] supplies the epistemological vocabulary tying these instrumental concerns together, proposing "emergent facts" as a category for evaluating outputs that are plausible but neither verified nor straightforwardly false — directly relevant to any effort to use LLMs as classifiers of political truth-claims.

## Structural and Governance Undercurrents

Underneath the empirical papers runs a governance conversation about what should be done. [[Waight2026-ts]] identifies an indirect, structural influence pathway — state-controlled media leaking into training corpora and shaping model valence by query language — that reframes "influence operations" as something that can be encoded before any campaign is even launched. [[Unknown2025-qj]] examines red-teaming as a public-interest safety practice for surfacing such harms. [[Goldberg2026-eb]] offers the most constructive institutional vision, cataloguing AI-enabled technologies (bridging systems, collective dialogue tools, community moderation) that could push digital public squares toward deliberation rather than manipulation — a deliberately optimistic bookend to [[Schroeder2026-im]]'s swarm-threat warning, and a fitting close to a topic whose papers collectively trace the arc from "can AI persuade and deceive" toward "how should our concepts, methods, and institutions be redesigned now that it demonstrably can."
