# Computational Approaches to Social Network Structure and Community Formation

## Redefining the Network: From Explicit Ties to Latent Structure

A first line of inquiry in this cluster asks what counts as a "tie" once platforms curate exposure algorithmically rather than through explicit follow/friend graphs. [[Gerbaudo2026-fo]] stakes out the theoretical ground, arguing that TikTok-style recommender systems have replaced Granovetter-style "networked publics" with statistically-inferred "clustered publics" — item-centric neighborhoods built from implicit behavioral signals like watch time rather than people-centric graphs of declared relationships. This reframing sets the stage for the methodological papers in this group, which can be read as attempts to operationalize exactly this kind of latent, behaviorally-inferred structure. [[Bruns2025-fz]] proposes "practice mapping," using vector embeddings of user actions to escape the "furball" problem of conventional network visualization by representing commonality of practice rather than only pairwise connection — a direct methodological cousin of Gerbaudo's clustered-publics thesis. [[Gerard2025-br]] pushes the same logic further, modeling users as distributions over latent narrative clusters (CANE/t-CANE) rather than through interaction edges, and showing that discourse-based affiliation reveals cross-platform "bridge users" invisible to behavior- or metadata-based graphs. [[Fan2026-af]] complements this by arguing that communication research has historically flattened time out of digital trace data, and proposes treating timestamped user-sequences — via network analysis, HMMs, process mining, and embedding models — as a first-class object, revealing platform-switching dynamics and modularity structures (e.g., TikTok/YouTube vs. Facebook/Instagram communities) that pure snapshot graphs would miss.

## Scaling and Engineering Self-Supervised Community Detection

A second thread is squarely architectural: how to detect communities and coordinated actors at scale while preserving semantic and temporal richness. [[Ferrara2026-io]] is the most purely methodological contribution, diagnosing a "Semantic Wall" (over-smoothing in GNN-based community detection) and a "Systems Wall" (O(N²) memory costs) and proposing ECHO, a self-supervised architecture that routes graphs to different encoders based on structural heuristics and achieves million-node scalability with state-of-the-art NMI. [[Minici2024-tf]] tackles an adjacent scaling problem — generalizing detection of coordinated information-operation drivers across heterogeneous campaigns — via a graph foundation model (IOHunter) that fuses language-model and GNN signals, explicitly framing graph foundation models as the natural extension of the foundation-model paradigm to network-structured social data. Both papers treat community/coordination detection as a representation-learning problem whose central challenge is generalizing embeddings across topologies and domains rather than hand-tuning per-dataset heuristics, situating them as technical infrastructure underlying the more applied coordination-detection work discussed next.

## Detecting Coordination: Temporal and Multiplex Refinements

A closely related set of papers treats coordinated inauthentic behavior as a structural, multi-layered phenomenon requiring more than a single co-action signal. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] extends Newman's collaboration model with an exponential temporal-decay kernel and a multiplex representation separating hashtag, retweet, mention, and URL layers, showing that combining temporal and modal information outperforms time-agnostic or single-modality baselines, particularly against dilution tactics. [[Mannocci2025-ig]] systematizes this problem space further, comparing five ways of operationalizing multimodal coordination (monomodal, independent layers, union flattening, multiplex detection, intersection flattening) and demonstrating a structural trade-off between integration strength and inclusiveness, with multiplex community detection emerging as the most balanced choice — a finding that directly corroborates Iannucci's design decision to keep modalities as separate layers rather than flattening them. Both papers thus converge empirically on multiplex, temporally-aware network construction as the methodological frontier for coordination detection, bridging back to [[Minici2024-tf]]'s goal of generalizable IO-driver identification.

## Mapping Movements: Community Formation and Transnational Ties in Practice

The remaining papers apply graph-based and mixed-methods network analysis to empirically map how extremist and hyperpartisan communities form, cohere, and cross borders, functioning as case studies in which the structural concepts above (clustering, bridging, multiplexity) are observed in the wild rather than engineered. [[Askanius2026-de]] maps a Swedish far-right "alternative influence network" on YouTube using guest-appearance, hyperlink, and mention ties, finding high connectivity organized around bridging hosts and alternative-media hubs — a star-like, hub-and-broker structure echoing the "bridge user" concept central to [[Gerard2025-br]]. [[Kakavand2026-kt]] extends the comparative lens across five platforms, showing that the same actor set produces markedly different network topologies (dense, reciprocal, fragmented on Instagram; sparse, broadcast-like on Twitter) depending on platform affordances, reinforcing the idea — shared with [[Gerbaudo2026-fo]] — that structural form is co-produced by platform design rather than simply read off a fixed social reality. [[Groebner2026-pc]] and [[Heft2021-ky]] both examine transnational network formation among right-wing media, using co-commenting and hyperlink graphs respectively to show that national outlets interlink into coherent transnational structures with the US (or US-oriented content) occupying a central hub position — a finding paralleling the centrality dynamics and asymmetric diffusion directionality that [[Gerard2025-br]] identifies between Truth Social and X. Finally, [[Tuters2026-vo]] uses co-hashtag network analysis across a large tweet corpus to show how conspiratorial alignment around the "Great Reset" emerges through patterned, modular circulation rather than coherent narrative — a community-formation process that is structural and infrastructural first, ideational second, closely mirroring Gerbaudo's claim that algorithmically-patterned adjacency, not explicit affiliation, is now the primary engine of online community formation.

## Synthesis

Read together, these thirteen papers trace an arc from theory (what does "network" mean when platforms curate by inferred similarity rather than declared tie? — [[Gerbaudo2026-fo]]) through method (how do we detect such latent, multiplex, temporally-structured communities at scale, self-supervisedly and efficiently? — [[Ferrara2026-io]], [[Minici2024-tf]], [[Bruns2025-fz]], [[Gerard2025-br]], [[Fan2026-af]], [[Iannucci2025-eg]], [[Mannocci2025-ig]]) to application (what communities, bridges, and transnational structures do these methods reveal in extremist and hyperpartisan media ecosystems? — [[Askanius2026-de]], [[Kakavand2026-kt]], [[Groebner2026-pc]], [[Heft2021-ky]], [[Tuters2026-vo]]). The throughline is a shared conviction that community and coordination are emergent, inferred structural phenomena — not given by platform-native edges — and that capturing them responsibly requires embedding-based, multiplex, and temporally-sensitive computational tools rather than single-snapshot, single-modality graphs.
