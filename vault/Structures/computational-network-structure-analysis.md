# Computational Approaches to Social Network Structure and Community Formation

## From Networked to Clustered Publics

The topic's conceptual anchor is [[Gerbaudo2026-fo]], which argues that platforms like TikTok have replaced explicit, person-to-person "networked publics" with algorithmically inferred "clustered publics" built from implicit behavioral signals rather than follows or friendships. This reframing matters for the rest of the set because it recasts community detection from a description of *user-declared* structure into an attempt to reverse-engineer a *platform-inferred* structure that is otherwise opaque. [[Bouchaud2026-lr]] operationalizes exactly this problem empirically: by reconstructing an approximation of X's 256-dimensional recommendation embedding space, it shows that a purely engagement-driven system inadvertently encodes a near-perfectly separable left–right ideological axis, orthogonal to other user attributes. Read together, these two papers frame the central stakes of the topic — platforms build latent, non-consensual social geometries, and the analyst's task is to recover and interrogate them.

## Embedding-Based Methods for Mapping Structure

A cluster of papers develops the computational machinery for this recovery task. [[Ferrara2026-io]] (ECHO) tackles the scalability and over-smoothing problems that have historically kept self-supervised graph neural networks from attributed community detection at million-node scale, using a topology-aware router and memory-sharded contrastive objective to combine semantic and structural signals. [[Bruns2025-fz]] proposes a complementary, lighter-weight logic — "practice mapping" — that embeds users' *actions* rather than raw ties, explicitly aiming to escape the illegibility of the network "hairball" when interactions are multimodal. [[Gerard2025-br]] extends this embedding logic across platforms: its Cluster Affiliation Network Embedding (CANE) reconstructs user-user ties not from reposts or follows but from shared participation in latent narrative clusters, enabling discovery of "bridge users" who structurally connect otherwise disconnected platform ecosystems (Truth Social and X). Across Ferrara, Bruns, and Gerard, embeddings of behavior or content substitute for embeddings of declared ties — a direct methodological response to the platform opacity that Gerbaudo and Bouchaud diagnose.

## Causal Mechanisms of Tie and Structure Formation

A second strand moves from *describing* clusters to explaining *why* transitive, triadic, and diffusion structures emerge in the first place. [[Smith2025-kc]] introduces the "attention broker" (tertius amplificans) mechanism and uses a difference-in-differences design on follower-event data to show that amplification by high-degree accounts causally accelerates triad closure among their followers — a local, exposure-based mechanism for the classically observed tendency toward transitivity in directed social networks. [[Di-Marco2025-aa]] complements this from the diffusion side: using a post-hoc optimal-placement framework on retweet cascades, it finds that real coordinated accounts achieve influence far below what strategic placement would predict, suggesting coordination is often structurally inert rather than causally pivotal. [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]] provides the macro-scale diffusion backdrop, characterizing how misinformation's reach and virality on Facebook diverge from other content at the scale of a billion re-shared posts. Together these three works push the topic beyond static community labels toward causal and cascade-level accounts of how structure is generated and how much particular actors actually matter.

## Detecting Coordination: Multiplex, Temporal, and Foundation-Model Approaches

A closely related methodological cluster targets the applied problem of detecting *coordinated inauthentic behavior* as a special case of community/cluster formation. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] both argue that single-modality, time-agnostic collaboration networks miss real coordination: Iannucci introduces a time-decayed, multiplex extension of Newman's collaboration model, while Mannocci systematically compares five ways of integrating modalities (monomodal, flattened, multiplex, intersected), showing a fundamental trade-off between integration strength and detection inclusiveness, with multiplex community detection emerging as the most balanced compromise. [[Minici2024-tf]] pushes the same problem toward generalization, proposing IOHunter, a graph foundation model combining language and graph neural representations to detect IO drivers across heterogeneous campaigns, including in few-shot and out-of-distribution settings. These three papers collectively refine the community-detection toolkit specifically for adversarial, engineered clustering, in contrast to the "organic" clustering addressed by Ferrara, Bruns, and Gerard.

## Empirical Ecosystems: Mapping Far-Right and Alternative Media Networks

A substantial applied cluster turns these graph-based tools onto the far-right and alternative-media ecosystem, testing how practice- and audience-based clustering manifest empirically across platforms and countries. [[Askanius2026-de]] maps a Swedish far-right "alternative influence network" on YouTube, showing how guest appearances, hyperlinks, and mentions weave commercial influencer practices together with political propaganda into a cohesive but ideologically diverse cluster. [[Kakavand2026-kt]] generalizes this across five platforms, arguing that far-right actors do not reproduce one network but adapt distinct connectivity, replicability, and scalability patterns per platform's affordances — a structural finding that echoes Gerbaudo's claim that "the social" is platform-specific rather than a fixed property of users. [[Groebner2026-pc]] extends the frame transnationally, using co-commenting overlap networks across four English-language hyperpartisan YouTube channels to show a US-centered, integrated transnational far-right audience space. [[Zhu2026-tn]] and [[Balluff2026-ev]] shift from influencer/channel networks to audience- and entity-overlap networks between alternative and mainstream outlets, respectively finding that structural isolation of alternative media is politically and institutionally contingent (Zhu) and that alternative and legacy outlets reference overlapping entities but organize them into more cohesive, less modular relational structures (Balluff). Read as a group, these five papers show the practice-mapping and community-detection logic of the methodological cluster being applied at ecosystem scale, revealing that "clustering" in far-right media is neither uniform nor platform-independent but emerges from the interaction of affordances, political context, and actor strategy.

## Extending the Toolkit: Temporal Sequences and Agentic Systems

Two papers push the topic's methods toward new data modalities and new populations. [[Fan2026-af]] argues that communication research has under-used the fine-grained temporal structure of digital trace data, reviewing six computational approaches (including network analysis and embedding models) for mining "user-sequences" that preserve hyper-longitudinal information — a methodological complement to the largely cross-sectional graph-clustering techniques used elsewhere in this set. Finally, [[Ng2026-og]] extrapolates the entire research program to a new kind of network: multi-agent AI systems. Its Multi-Agent Social Systems framework formalizes agentic AI populations as an information-exchange/influence-dynamics/network triple, and validates structural priors like network-constrained dependence and co-evolution using degree-partition and karma-diffusion analysis on a real AI social network. This closing paper suggests that the graph-based, embedding-driven, and causal-network methods developed across this topic — originally built to explain human community formation — are already being repurposed as the structural prior for understanding, and governing, emergent social structure among artificial agents.
