# Computational Approaches to Social Network Structure and Community Formation

## Self-supervised embeddings and the scaling problem

The technical core of this collection concerns how to detect community and practice structure in networks that are too large, too heterophilic, or too multimodal for classical modularity-based methods. [[Ferrara2026-io]] frames this most explicitly as a dual bottleneck—a "Semantic Wall" of over-smoothing in GNN-based community detection and a "Systems Wall" of O(N²) memory costs—and answers both with a topology-aware router that switches encoders per graph and a memory-sharded InfoNCE objective, pushing attributed community detection to million-node scale. This is a direct methodological escalation of the concerns raised by [[Bruns2025-fz]], whose "practice mapping" proposal diagnoses the same underlying problem in cruder form: conventional network visualization collapses into an uninterpretable "hairball" once multiple interaction modes are layered together, and vector embeddings of user actions are proposed as a way to recover legible structure. Where Bruns sketches the idea programmatically, [[Ferrara2026-io]] and [[Gerard2025-br]] operationalize it at scale—the latter's CANE/t-CANE framework abandons platform-specific interaction signals (retweets, follows) entirely in favor of embedding users as distributions over latent discourse clusters, explicitly built for the "post-API," cross-platform, fragmented-data environment that Bruns and [[Zheng2026-bi]] independently worry about.

A second cluster of papers applies this embedding logic specifically to the problem of coordinated, inauthentic structure. [[Iannucci2025-eg]] and [[Mannocci2025-ig]] both argue that single-modality or flattened multi-layer representations misrepresent coordination, converging on multiplex, Louvain-style community detection as the more faithful operationalization—Iannucci adding a temporal decay kernel to distinguish deliberate synchrony from coincidence, Mannocci mapping out the precise trade-off between integration strength and detection inclusiveness. [[Minici2024-tf]]'s IOHunter extends the same graph-plus-attribute philosophy into a foundation-model paradigm, fusing language-model and GNN representations to generalize IO-driver detection across campaigns that share no common labels—an ambition structurally analogous to ECHO's attempt to generalize community detection across graph morphologies without hand-tuned heuristics.

## From structure to causal mechanism

A second line of inquiry asks not just how to detect emergent structure but why it emerges. [[Smith2025-kc]] is the most methodologically distinctive paper in the set, using a novel exploit of API-derived follower events and a difference-in-differences design to show that triad transitivity—long treated as a static structural regularity—has an identifiable local causal driver: "attention brokers" whose amplification of content measurably accelerates their followers' tie formation to the amplified account. This causal-inference register sits in productive tension with [[Di-Marco2025-aa]], which asks a related counterfactual question—how much cascade influence do coordinated accounts actually exert relative to optimal or random placement—and finds that real coordinated accounts underperform strategic benchmarks, resembling random placement more than deliberate design. Together these two papers push back against purely descriptive network-structure claims (whether about brokerage or about coordination) by insisting on counterfactual or optimal baselines, complementing the detection-oriented papers above: knowing that a multiplex community exists ([[Iannucci2025-eg]], [[Mannocci2025-ig]]) is analytically distinct from knowing what causal work that structure is doing ([[Smith2025-kc]], [[Di-Marco2025-aa]]).

## Structure as diagnostic of fragmentation

A third thread uses graph-analytic tools less to advance methodology than to characterize substantive patterns of political and subcultural fragmentation. [[Dehghan2026-sy]] applies overlap and cross-posting measures across eleven political subreddits to show that structural connectivity between ideologically adjacent communities is far lower than expected, arguing that this "sedimentation of polarisation" is irreducible to discourse or platform design alone. [[Zhu2026-tn]] performs an analogous audience-overlap network analysis across six countries' alternative and mainstream media ecosystems, finding that the structural marginality of alternative outlets is not a fixed property but is conditioned by media-system type and populist parties' institutional access—densely isolated enclaves (Sweden) versus blurred boundaries (Austria, France). [[Askanius2026-de]] maps a comparable phenomenon at the level of individual actors rather than outlets, using guest-appearance, hyperlink, and mention networks to show how the Swedish far-right YouTube ecosystem cohered into a professionalized "alternative influence network" during the pandemic. [[Wang2026-ub]] extends this diagnostic use of network analysis to platform migration, showing that even a well-connected internal network among academic Twitter emigrants to Mastodon was insufficient to prevent large-scale reversion, with retention instead explained by cross-server engagement diversity—a finding that echoes Zhu's point that network position, not mere connectivity, determines an actor's durability within an ecosystem.

## Measurement infrastructure and the limits of inferred structure

A final set of papers, while less directly about community detection, bears on the topic as cautionary or complementary infrastructure: any claim about emergent network structure is only as reliable as the trace data and sampling procedures underlying it. [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]] shows that Meta's 100-share anonymization threshold introduces a platform-wide algorithmic artifact that can masquerade as cross-national behavioral convergence—a direct warning against over-interpreting apparent structural similarity in derived networks. [[Zheng2026-bi]] addresses the complementary "denominator problem" for YouTube and TikTok, arguing that opportunistic or keyword-based samples used to build most social networks in this literature systematically oversample popular, algorithmically amplified nodes. [[Eady2025-vm]] and [[Green2025-ap]] both push structural analysis of political communication down from the outlet/domain level to the level of individual users and stories, showing—via ideology-scaling of shared URLs and story-level audience heterogeneity, respectively—that aggregate structural measures (a domain's or a user's apparent partisan position) can conceal fine-grained heterogeneity that would matter for any embedding built on such signals. [[Gaisbauer2025-by]] makes a parallel methodological argument for multi-level ("story, outlet, content") mapping of news circulation. [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] and [[Fan2026-af]] extend this reflexive concern to definitional and temporal dimensions: the former argues that propagation dynamics, not creator intent, should be the unit of analysis for false-information cascades, while the latter proposes treating fine-grained timestamped trace data as sequences rather than aggregated cross-sections—a call for exactly the kind of dynamic, path-sensitive representation that the causal-mechanism papers ([[Smith2025-kc]], [[Di-Marco2025-aa]]) and the temporal-multiplex detection papers ([[Iannucci2025-eg]]) already operationalize in narrower settings.

Read together, the collection traces an arc from methodological innovation in representing structure (embeddings, multiplex layers, foundation models) through causal interrogation of what that structure does, to substantive mapping of fragmentation across platforms and ecosystems, closing with a reflexive layer insisting that every one of these structural claims is conditioned by the sampling, thresholding, and temporal-aggregation choices built into the underlying trace data.
