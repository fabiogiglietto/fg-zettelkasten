# Health Information Ecosystems Online

## Mapping the Ecosystem: Supply, Demand, and Gatekeeping

The papers filed here trace a single online health-information ecosystem from several vantage points: what determines the availability and credibility of content in circulation, who consumes low-quality material, how it spreads through coordinated actor networks, and what downstream harms it produces. Together they suggest that the "infodemic" is best understood not as a single contagion but as a system with structural pressure points, differentiated audiences, and identifiable amplification mechanisms.

**Structural gaps and the supply-demand frame.** [[Scalco2026-bd]] provides the most systemic account, operationalizing the idea that misinformation exploits "information voids" — moments where public demand (Wikipedia views, Google Trends) outstrips reliable supply (Facebook, Twitter, GDELT news). Its five-regime taxonomy (Void, Lack, Balance, Abundance, Overabundance) gives a formal vocabulary for what other papers in this set observe empirically: during voids triggered by events like EMA vaccine authorizations, credible content share drops and unreliable content share rises. This economic metaphor of the information market underlies much of what follows — coordinated networks and gatekeepers are, in effect, competing suppliers filling (or exploiting) these gaps.

**Who falls into the gap: audience-side concentration.** [[Lyons2026-ca]] complicates any narrative of ambient, evenly-distributed exposure. Using passive web-tracking rather than self-report, it shows that exposure to low-credibility health content is rare in aggregate but sharply concentrated — the top 10% of users account for 77% of exposure — and disproportionately borne by older adults with weaker discernment and conspiracist leanings. Crucially, this exposure is not driven by search engines or social media referral (a finding that qualifies the platform-centric emphasis of the other papers) but by habitual navigation between low-credibility sites themselves, suggesting durable consumption niches rather than incidental algorithmic exposure. This finding sits in productive tension with [[Scalco2026-bd]]'s ecosystem-level view: voids may explain *when* bad content proliferates, but [[Lyons2026-ca]] shows that only a narrow, self-selecting slice of the audience actually absorbs it.

**From exposure to consequence.** [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] pushes the causal question furthest, building a SIRVA compartmental model to link geolocated antivaccine tweets to measurable reductions in US county-level vaccination and resulting excess cases and deaths. Its contribution is methodological as much as substantive: it moves the field from correlational or experimental demonstrations of misinformation's effects on attitudes toward a population-scale causal estimate of real epidemiological cost, giving empirical teeth to the abstract "misinformation prevalence" that [[Scalco2026-bd]] associates with information voids.

**Coordinated amplification networks.** Two papers examine the organized, network-level machinery that produces and circulates this content. [[Song2025-yh]] analyzes coordinated link-sharing behavior (CLSB) on Facebook in the UK and US, showing that both pro- and anti-vaccine camps coordinate, but with divergent structures and content credibility: anti-vaccine CLSB is denser in the UK and skewed toward unverifiable sources, while US networks lean pro-vaccine and institutionally grounded. Its central claim — that CLSB is ideologically agnostic, a tool that can serve misinformation or credible public-health messaging depending on context — reframes coordination itself as a neutral technology rather than an inherent marker of malice, a nuance that complicates simpler "bad actor network" framings. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] narrows in on the Italian case, identifying an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster within a broader Alternative Influence Network that generates disproportionate share-driven engagement, and — significantly — shows that legacy journalists and mainstream outlets remain the central nodes for circulating covid-skeptic content, via a remediation process that recycles TV and newspaper statements into shareable social posts. Read alongside [[Song2025-yh]], this suggests that coordinated networks are not free-standing alternative-media phenomena but are structurally entangled with legacy institutions, and that comparative national context (UK vs. US vs. Italy) shapes both the political framing (safety concerns vs. individual freedom vs. anti-elite conspiracism) and the media architecture through which problematic content travels.

**Platform and aggregator gatekeeping.** [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] shifts the lens from coordinated grassroots or influencer networks to algorithmic curation, examining Google News Italia as a gatekeeper for health content later amplified on Facebook. It finds that while medical-scientific and healthcare-system topics dominate what gets aggregated, it is emotionally charged categories — celebrity health stories, healthcare malpractice — that generate the highest engagement, particularly comments. This echoes the demand-side dynamics implicit in [[Scalco2026-bd]]'s void framework (attention gravitates to emotionally salient content when credible technical content underperforms) and offers a partial explanation for the audience behavior [[Lyons2026-ca]] documents: gatekeepers curate technical material, but engagement infrastructure rewards its more sensational, less epistemically rigorous adjacent categories, creating structural incentives that dovetail with the coordinated-amplification strategies described by [[Song2025-yh]] and [[Marino2023-9137f448]].

**Synthesis.** Across these six papers, a coherent arc emerges: structural imbalances between information supply and demand ([[Scalco2026-bd]]) create openings that gatekeepers' engagement-driven curation ([[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]]) and coordinated actor networks ([[Song2025-yh]]; [[Marino2023-9137f448]]) exploit, with real but unevenly distributed audience uptake ([[Lyons2026-ca]]) that nonetheless translates into measurable public-health harm ([[Bollenbacher2026-vz]]). The comparative national dimension — UK, US, and Italy in the network studies — underscores that the mechanics of coordination and gatekeeping are structurally similar across contexts even as the substantive narratives (freedom vs. safety vs. anti-elite conspiracism) are locally inflected, a comparative sensitivity that any future extension of this note to other national contexts (e.g., India and Nigeria) would need to sustain.
