# Survey Data Quality, Comprehension, and Recruitment

## The comprehension gap at the root of survey inference

The most basic question a structure note on survey methodology can ask is also the one most often skipped: do respondents understand the questions the way researchers intend? [[Hinck2026-yj]] makes this the organizing indictment of the field, arguing that survey research routinely proceeds without any empirical demonstration of shared meaning between researcher and respondent. This is a foundational claim precisely because it is upstream of every other methodological concern in this collection — sample quality, recruitment strategy, and measurement operationalization all presuppose that the instrument itself is being read as intended. The paper's call for systematic comprehension testing sets the evidentiary bar that the rest of this literature, often implicitly, fails to clear.

## Opt-in samples: not one thing, but many

[[Stagnaro2025-pz]] supplies the empirical counterpart to that critique at the level of sample composition rather than item wording. Testing nine opt-in platforms along response validity, representativeness, and "professionalism," it dismantles the convenient fiction that opt-in samples are interchangeable: quota-based platforms (Lucid, Forthright) buy representativeness at the cost of response validity, driven substantially by mobile-phone respondents, while curated panels (Prolific, Connect) show the reverse trade-off. Its most actionable finding — that two trivial front-end attention checks meaningfully raise validity without eroding representativeness — offers a low-cost fix analogous in spirit to Hinck's call for comprehension evidence, but operating on attentiveness rather than semantic understanding. Critically, Stagnaro shows that post-stratification weighting can restore main effects but not heterogeneous treatment effects, meaning that the choice of sample source is not fully correctable after the fact — a warning that resonates with the specification-curve findings discussed below.

## Recruitment as its own methodological problem: reaching hard-to-survey populations

Where Stagnaro evaluates existing panel infrastructures, [[Iannelli2018-ebd918b7]] tackles the prior problem of how to build a sample at all when the target population is niche or stigmatized. Using Facebook's ad-targeting and Pixel-based conversion tracking to recruit Italian conspiracy-theory sympathizers, it demonstrates that platform advertising can be efficient (a 3.28% conversion rate, sub-€0.50 per respondent) even as its *effectiveness* — whether interest-based targeting actually captures ideologically distinctive respondents — remains unproven: the Facebook-recruited sample did not differ meaningfully from a general-population CAWI benchmark in conspiracy endorsement. This null result is itself methodologically important, echoing Stagnaro's caution that demographic or interest-based quotas do not guarantee attitudinal representativeness, and it foreshadows a recurring theme across this topic: technical sophistication in *acquiring* respondents does not automatically translate into validity of what is *measured* from them.

## When measurement choices move the answer

A cluster of papers extends the comprehension/representativeness problem to the operationalization stage, showing that even with a fixed sample, how variables are measured can drive substantive conclusions as much as the data themselves. [[Schemer2026-mh]] applies specification curve analysis across 504 combinations of media-slant and polarization measures in US panel data and finds that methodological choices swing coefficient magnitudes by up to sevenfold, with audience-based slant scores systematically inflating estimates relative to content-based ones — a direct demonstration of researcher degrees of freedom degrading inferential stability, paralleling Stagnaro's demonstration that sample choice alters detectable heterogeneity. [[Luhring2025-od]] performs an analogous audit one level up the pipeline, showing that source-trustworthiness databases like NewsGuard are reasonably stable when used as continuous scores but that binarizing them at a threshold can flip empirical conclusions merely because a handful of major outlets cross the cutoff — a caution about measurement discretization that mirrors Stagnaro's attention-check filtering results. [[Ulloa2024-jm]] pushes the same logic into the domain of behavioral trace data adjacent to self-report: it shows that ex-situ web scraping (the standard method for reconstructing news-exposure content post hoc) introduces systematic, non-random distortion dominated by collection environment rather than by delay, undermining any study that treats scraped content as equivalent to what respondents actually saw. Together, these three papers generalize the sample-source problem identified by Stagnaro into a broader principle: instrument, database, and data-collection-pipeline choices are never neutral, and validity claims must be pressure-tested against alternative specifications.

## Extending measurement with computational tools

Two papers explore how large language models might address, or complicate, the comprehension and scaling problems raised above. [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] proposes pairing LLMs with paired-comparison methods to convert open-ended survey text into continuous latent scales, explicitly framed as a solution to the tradeoff between the limited depth of closed-ended items and the labor cost of manual open-ended coding — a direct methodological response to the comprehension concerns in [[Hinck2026-yj]], since open text arguably offers better evidence of what a respondent actually understood. [[UnknownUnknown-db]] applies a related computational strategy at scale: Anthropic's survey of ~81,000 Claude.ai users infers occupation and productivity/displacement perceptions from open-ended text via LLM classifiers, successfully identifying occupation for only 39% of respondents. This illustrates both the promise and the fragility of LLM-mediated measurement — rich qualitative self-report can be linked to behavioral usage data, but a large share of respondents' underlying attributes remain unrecoverable from free text, an empirical instantiation of the same comprehension-and-inference gap that motivates [[Hinck2026-yj]] and the paired-comparison approach of [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]].

## Downstream stakes: what rides on getting this right

The remaining papers in this collection are less about survey methodology per se than about substantive findings whose credibility depends entirely on the foundations laid above, and they usefully illustrate why the methodological questions matter. [[Voelkel2026-lc]]'s megastudy on climate messaging persuasion depends on large opt-in online samples and finds that pilot-study effects shrink or vanish under adequately powered, pre-registered conditions — a substantive echo of Stagnaro's caution that heterogeneous effects are sample- and power-dependent. [[Fattorini2026-bo]] reports nationally representative survey data on Italian AI attitudes, offering a comparison case where quota-based representativeness (unlike Stagnaro's opt-in platforms) is achieved through traditional probability-sample design, useful as a benchmark for what "representativeness" is meant to approximate. [[Lyons2026-ca]] links self-report survey data to passive web-tracking to study low-credibility health content exposure, a triangulation strategy that directly addresses the comprehension/validity problem by checking self-reported constructs (discernment, conspiracist worldview) against behavioral trace data rather than relying on self-report alone — a design strategy Hinck's argument implicitly recommends. Finally, [[Dubey2026-bl]]'s TAM-based survey study of chatbot trust among conspiracy believers exemplifies how attitude and intention measures (positive predictors explaining 90%+ of variance) can behave in psychometrically clean ways even while producing substantively counterintuitive results, underscoring that strong model fit is no guarantee that a survey instrument is capturing the construct researchers assume it does — precisely the gap [[Hinck2026-yj]] identifies as endemic to the field.

## Synthesis

Read together, these twelve papers trace an arc from foundational skepticism about whether respondents understand survey items ([[Hinck2026-yj]]), through empirical stress-tests of sample quality ([[Stagnaro2025-pz]]) and recruitment innovation ([[Iannelli2018-ebd918b7]]), to demonstrations that measurement and pipeline choices — in media-slant coding, source databases, and trace-data scraping — can be as consequential as sampling itself ([[Schemer2026-mh]], [[Luhring2025-od]], [[Ulloa2024-jm]]). Computational methods offer partial remedies by extracting richer signal from open text ([[DiGiuseppe2025-es]], [[UnknownUnknown-db]]), while a set of substantive applications ([[Voelkel2026-lc]], [[Fattorini2026-bo]], [[Lyons2026-ca]], [[Dubey2026-bl]]) shows both the promise and the fragility of survey-based inference once these foundational issues are — or are not — addressed. The throughline is a discipline-wide argument for methodological humility: representativeness, response validity, comprehension, and measurement operationalization are separable dimensions of quality, each independently capable of distorting conclusions, and no single fix (weighting, attention checks, quota sampling, or LLM-assisted coding) substitutes for treating all of them as objects of explicit evidence rather than assumption.
