# Italian Media Ecosystem (MINE)

## The MINE Arc: From Mapping Attention to Diagnosing Weaponisation

The papers filed here trace a coherent trajectory in the Mapping Italian News Ecosystem (MINE) programme: beginning with how partisan audiences engage news, moving to how coordinated actors and alternative influencers exploit that engagement, and culminating in how "fake news" itself becomes a rhetorical instrument in the mainstream press. Read together, they chart the Italian information environment as a hybrid, polarised-pluralist system in which insularity, coordination, and delegitimisation reinforce one another.

### Insularity and the comments-to-shares signature

The empirical spine of the programme is [[Giglietto2019-882f1900]], which establishes that Italian populist audiences — especially those aligned with the Five Star Movement and the League — cluster around more insular news sources, and that insularity translates into a distinctive engagement fingerprint on Facebook: more shares per comment (amplification) versus more comments per share (contestation). This ratio becomes a portable analytic device across the MINE corpus. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] reuses it to show that Intellectual Dark Web influencers and covid-skeptic experts generate share-heavy, amplification-driven engagement, while journalists' pages attract contested, comment-heavy interaction. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] then repurposes the same signature as a *ranking* signal for fact-checkers, arguing that problematic content is systematically share-dominant. What began as a descriptive finding about the 2018 campaign matures into an operational heuristic for infodemic triage.

### From audience patterns to coordinated infrastructures

Where the 2018 election paper focuses on how partisan publics *interact* with news, [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] shifts the object of analysis to the coordinated actors *supplying* it. Using Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB) and the CooRnet package, the MINE-FACTS project maps a covid-skeptic network that grew out of pre-existing League- and M5S-adjacent coordinated clusters — a striking empirical bridge to the populist-insularity dynamics documented earlier. The paper also catalogues evasion tactics (first-comment links, image-macro cross-posting, link laundering through reputable outlets) that show how these networks actively adapt to platform enforcement, turning the ecosystem into a moving target for both researchers and fact-checkers.

### Alternative Influence Networks and the persistence of legacy media

[[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends the coordinated-actor analysis by asking *who* these networks amplify. The answer complicates any simple "alternative vs. mainstream" dichotomy: the most engaging covid-skeptic posts are dominated by mainstream-affiliated journalists (Porro, Paragone) and right-wing newspapers (La Verità, Libero), with micro-celebrity influencers riding on remediated TV and print material. This resonates with [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]]'s observation that "unprincipled celebrities" like Montagnier or Viganò offer fact-check-proof soundbites precisely because their claims were genuinely uttered on legacy platforms. Hybridity in the Italian case is thus less a story of digital insurgents displacing old media than of alternative networks *parasitising* legacy authority through remediation.

### Fake news as rhetoric, not just content

[[Mosca2026-yh]] closes the arc by inverting the object of study once more. If earlier MINE papers ask how problematic information circulates, this one asks how *accusations* of fake news are themselves deployed strategically in the Italian press. The Strategic Delegitimization and Selective Amplification (SDSA) model reframes fake news as a rhetorical weapon in negative campaigning, with newspapers acting as uneven amplifiers according to political alignment. This complements the coordinated-network findings: where [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] and [[Marino2023-9137f448]] document bottom-up and lateral amplification through Facebook clusters, [[Mosca2026-yh]] identifies a top-down amplification logic operating within elite print media.

### Convergences and open tensions

Across the four papers, three cross-cutting claims emerge. First, the Italian ecosystem is not neatly bifurcated between mainstream and alternative — legacy outlets, coordinated pages, and micro-celebrity influencers form a single circulatory system. Second, engagement metrics carry political meaning: the comments-to-shares ratio functions simultaneously as a descriptor of partisan style ([[Giglietto2019-882f1900]]), an amplification signature of alternative influence ([[Marino2023-9137f448]]), and a triage signal for fact-checking ([[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]]). Third, populist and hyperpartisan advantage is procedural as much as ideological, resting on coordinated infrastructures and rhetorical routines that predate any specific piece of content. A productive tension remains between the behavioural, content-agnostic stance of the CooRnet-based work and the discursive, rhetoric-centred stance of [[Mosca2026-yh]] — two complementary lenses on the same polarised-pluralist ecology that MINE has set out to map.
