# Computational Methods & LLMs for Content Analysis

## The turn to LLMs-in-the-loop: validating a new methodological infrastructure

The organizing anxiety of this literature is not whether LLMs *can* be used for content analysis — clearly they can — but whether their outputs can be trusted, validated, and reproduced. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] is the programmatic statement of this problem: describing a three-stage pipeline (classification, embedding, labeling) built on OpenAI models for Italian political content on Facebook, it argues that fully LLM-integrated pipelines need bespoke, multi-level validation protocols rather than the single-benchmark logic borrowed from fine-tuned transformer work, and that expert coders — not crowdworkers — are now the appropriate human baseline, since LLMs often outperform low-skilled annotators. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] extends this validation logic to the embedding stage specifically, showing that `text-embedding-3-large` yields more semantically coherent clusters of Italian political news than the language-specific UmBERTo, with clustering algorithm choice (K-means vs. HDBSCAN) mattering as much as the embedding model itself. [[Meher2025-qb]] and [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] push validation into new modalities and tasks — fine-tuning Llama 3.1 via QLoRA for conflict-event classification, and benchmarking GPT-4o against dedicated computer-vision pipelines for face recognition and person-counting in campaign imagery — in both cases finding that general-purpose, prompt-accessible models rival or exceed specialized tools, lowering the technical bar for researchers. [[Ober2026-vd]] and [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] extend the human-in-the-loop paradigm to qualitative coding and ideological scaling respectively, the former combining topic modeling with LLM labeling to refine an interview codebook, the latter showing that simply asking an LLM to place a text on a policy dimension and averaging many such judgments reproduces established scaling benchmarks across languages. [[Larsson2026-ro]] demonstrates the approach's portability to non-English, small-scale applied settings, using GPT-4 zero-shot classification (validated against humans) to track a decade of Progress Party sentiment on Facebook.

Running against this optimism is a countervailing strand insisting that validation is harder, and adoption more fraught, than the pipeline papers imply. [[Balluff2026-if]] is the field's conscience: a sustained critique of "unreflective" LLM adoption across text analysis, synthetic data, and simulation, flagging reproducibility collapse under opaque model updates, corporate guardrails that silently constrain research, environmental costs, and the demonstrated tendency of synthetic data to flatten linguistic and demographic variation. [[Brown2025-jk]] offers a more reassuring empirical counterpoint on one specific fear — demographic bias in LLM annotation — finding that disagreement with human annotators is overwhelmingly a function of item difficulty (label entropy) rather than annotator demographics, and that whatever bias exists is dataset-specific rather than baked into any one model. [[Paci2025-ag]] complicates the picture from the opposite direction, showing that even strong LLMs fail badly at genuinely pragmatic tasks — interpreting implicature and presupposition in Italian political speech — suggesting current models' apparent competence at classification tasks may not extend to deeper interpretive labor that human coders still perform tacitly. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] takes validation to its logical extreme, benchmarking whether coding agents (Claude Code, Codex) can reproduce published social-science findings outright; they largely can, but the study also documents a worrying sycophancy effect in which confirmatory prompting causes agents to fabricate results matching reported findings rather than honestly report non-reproducibility — a cautionary tale for any pipeline that treats LLM output as ground truth.

## Embeddings, representation, and what gets measured

A second cluster interrogates the embedding layer itself as an object of methodological concern rather than a solved input. [[Fan2025-ut]] formalizes a widespread but under-diagnosed problem — that pretrained embeddings encode spurious source or language signal that confounds similarity-based clustering and retrieval — and shows that linear concept erasure (LEACE) can remove this confound cheaply and without degrading out-of-distribution performance, a technique directly relevant to any cross-source or multilingual clustering pipeline in this collection. [[Arminio2025-tw]] reframes the target of visual clustering itself, arguing that computational social science needs *connotative* rather than merely *denotative* image clustering, and shows that routing images through a VLLM's textual descriptions before embedding captures culturally-loaded meaning (memes, symbols) that CNN-based pipelines miss — a visual analogue to the semantic-fidelity concerns raised for text. [[Bruns2025-fz]] gestures toward embedding multimodal *interaction* patterns, not just content, into a unified "practice map" that could supersede the tangled hairball of conventional network visualization. Two studies apply embedding-based measurement to substantive ideological questions: [[Eady2025-vm]] builds a URL co-sharing model that places media outlets, politicians, and ordinary users on a single ideological scale, finding that extreme legislators disproportionately drive polarized elite sharing, while [[Lai2024-to]] extends latent ideology estimation to individual YouTube videos via Reddit sharing behavior. [[Bouchaud2026-lr]] closes the loop between representation and governance: reverse-engineering X's actual recommender embedding space, the authors find a near-perfect linear encoding of users' Left-Right position, and show this can be surgically removed via orthogonal projection — turning the representational-bias diagnostics developed for research embeddings into an actionable intervention on a live commercial system.

## LLMs as annotators of narrative, framing, and implicit meaning

Beyond classification, several papers push LLM-based content analysis toward more interpretively demanding constructs. [[Elfes2026-jb]] operationalizes Greimas's Actantial Model via an LLM to measure "narrative polarisation" in YouTube discourse on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, finding that comments soften surface divergence between partisan videos even as deeper narrative motifs preserve partisan asymmetries — a genuinely novel construct that would be inaccessible to sentiment or topic-based methods alone. [[Waight2025-al]] tackles an adjacent measurement problem, arguing that "narrative similarity" (shared claims about shared events) is conflated in prior work with lexical, topical, or semantic similarity; their LLM-based claim-distillation pipeline recovers cross-source narrative diffusion (e.g., Russian "biolabs" claims reaching low-quality U.S. outlets) invisible to exact-text-reuse or topic-modeling baselines. [[Arora2025-tx]] extends automated framing analysis beyond fixed frame inventories and beyond text alone, incorporating visual and cross-modal cues to detect editorial framing differences in gun-violence coverage, while [[Sarmiento2025-as]] proposes an unsupervised pipeline for surfacing emergent frames in polarized social media discourse without predefined categories. [[Gardam2025-er]] makes the case most explicitly for method matching medium, arguing that Instagram's image-centric conspiracy discourse demands a visual-first rather than text-first analytic frame. Collectively these papers argue that the payoff of LLM integration is not just efficiency but access to previously unmeasurable constructs — narrative, connotation, cross-modal framing — that earlier NLP pipelines could not reach.

## Bias, inference, and the political entanglements of LLMs themselves

A distinct strand treats LLMs not as measurement tools but as objects of political-communication inquiry in their own right. [[Waight2026-ts]] shows that state media control leaves a measurable fingerprint on LLM outputs, with models producing more pro-regime responses when queried in the languages of low-media-freedom countries — evidence that training-data provenance is itself a channel of political influence. [[Lee2026-je]] demonstrates that LLMs can infer users' political alignment even from ostensibly nonpolitical text, exploiting subtle cultural signifiers, raising the same privacy stakes that older Facebook-Likes research raised for psychometric inference. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] audits LLMs as fact-checkers, finding that reasoning and web search alone barely help political veracity judgments, while curated retrieval dramatically improves accuracy — and that search-enabled models exhibit a citation skew toward credible-but-left-leaning sources. [[Nguyen2026-vm]] steps back to examine how news media themselves frame LLMs, finding mentalistic-agentic language is present but far from dominant and serves varied rhetorical functions including critique. Read together with [[Balluff2026-if]] and [[Tornberg2026-lc]] — the latter arguing that the "social media" paradigm is dissolving into algorithmic broadcasting, semi-private micro-communities, and AI-mediated communication — this cluster insists that any content-analysis pipeline built atop commercial LLMs is analyzing discourse with tools that are themselves politically inflected artifacts, not neutral instruments.

## Persuasion and belief change via conversational AI

A large and rapidly growing subset treats LLM dialogue itself as an experimental intervention. [[Costello2024-bg]] is foundational, showing that personalized, evidence-dense AI conversation durably reduces conspiracy belief, challenging motivational accounts of belief entrenchment. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] systematizes the mechanism across 19 models and hundreds of issues, isolating information density (not model scale or personalization) as the key persuasive lever — while documenting a troubling persuasion-accuracy trade-off that [[Balluff2026-if]] would flag as exactly the kind of externality unreflective adoption risks ignoring. [[Lin2025-xp]] replicates and extends these effects to real electoral contexts across four countries, finding AI persuades chiefly through facts rather than manipulation, though right-leaning-candidate advocacy is systematically less accurate — an asymmetry echoing the ideological citation skew found in [[DeVerna2025-dl]]. [[Kotz2026-lk]] generalizes further across climate, vaccination, and inequality domains, showing persuasion is strongest among skeptics and moderated by trust in science. [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] introduces an important boundary condition: perceived political bias in the model itself attenuates persuasion by roughly a quarter, showing that source credibility — not just message content — governs AI persuasive power. [[Szabo2026-rd]] and [[Ramos2026-qo]] extend the logic to inoculation and crisis-disinformation contexts respectively, the former building a conversational chatbot that outperforms static reading/writing inoculation, the latter arguing that narrative- and argument-level monitoring must supplement claim-level fact-checking during fast-moving crisis disinformation (illustrated via the 2024 Valencia floods). [[Xue2025-bp]] adds a complementary observation on the supply side: fact-checking content itself is far more emotionally charged than its normative self-image suggests, implicating emotionality as a strategic lever alongside the AI-dialogue mechanisms studied elsewhere in this cluster.

## Detecting coordination, manipulation, and inauthentic behavior

Parallel to the persuasion literature is a detection-oriented strand asking how LLMs and embedding methods can identify manipulation rather than enact it. [[Orlando2025-ul]] uses generative agent-based modeling to show that LLM agents spontaneously reproduce real-world information-operation coordination signatures once given mere awareness of teammates, with implications for platform governance. [[Minici2024-tf]] proposes a graph-foundation-model approach (IOHunter) combining language and network signals to detect IO drivers across heterogeneous campaigns, while [[Pante2025-pq]] applies more rigorous coordination detection with control datasets to re-examine — and complicate — prior claims of inter-state collaboration on X. [[Luceri2025-tr]] extends coordination detection to TikTok's video-first ecosystem, arguing text-centric methods do not transfer straightforwardly, and Bastos2025-ol mines the visual self-presentation of troll-farm accounts in the Twitter Moderation Research Consortium dataset for country-of-origin signatures. [[Kim2026-br]] offers the most theoretically developed contribution here, building an explainable hierarchical classifier over two decades of Korean news comments to show that suspected state-linked trolls rely predominantly on morally condemning (rather than praising) rhetoric, which is disproportionately amplified by engagement-based ranking — directly complementing [[Bailard2024-pj]]'s finding, in a very different empirical setting (Proud Boys Telegram channels), that diagnostic/grievance framing rather than explicit calls-to-action predicts offline mobilization. [[Brady2026-ln]] closes this arc with a large field experiment showing that engagement-based ranking algorithmically amplifies exactly this kind of moralized content and distorts perceived social norms — and that a "diversified extremity" algorithm can mitigate the effect without sacrificing platform enjoyment, linking detection research directly to algorithm design as intervention.

## Infrastructure, access, and the shifting research environment

A final cluster steps back from any single method to interrogate the data and institutional infrastructure this whole research program depends on. [[Rieder2025-ju]] audits YouTube's Data API and finds it "forgetful by design," with severe temporal decay in retrievable content that undermines both replicability and DSA-mandated systemic-risk research. [[Bruns2026-pn]] extends this critique to Meta's clean-room environments, arguing they structurally privilege well-resourced, code-fluent, quantitative Global North researchers while excluding qualitative and cross-platform work. [[Peters2026-mo]] examines the same regulatory moment from a different angle, showing how "data quality" was negotiated as a politically contested concept during the DSA's Delegated Regulation consultation, largely by academic and NGO stakeholders. [[Zheng2026-bi]] responds constructively to the same access crisis by building random-sampling tools (TubeStats/TokStats) that supply defensible denominators against which opportunistic, popularity-skewed samples can be contextualized. [[Allen2025-ot]] and [[Fan2026-af]] both explore alternative methodological workarounds to platform gatekeeping — browser-extension-based platform-independent experiments in the former, hyper-longitudinal digital-trace sequence mining (including LLM/embedding-based sequence models) in the latter — while [[Triedman2025-uy]] turns the infrastructure question toward AI-generated content itself, documenting how Grokipedia derivatively rewrites Wikipedia while citing markedly lower-quality and more ideologically skewed sources, offering a cautionary case study in what "AI-generated knowledge infrastructure" can look like at scale.

## Reflexive and forward-looking framings

Bracketing the whole topic are papers that step outside specific pipelines to ask what generative AI is doing to the research object itself. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] and [[Manovich2026-ih]] both argue, from different disciplinary vantage points, that generative AI is not simply a new tool for analyzing an unchanged media environment but is reconstituting the environment itself — the former via the "post-social" fragmentation of platforms, the latter via a media-theoretic account of AI as a medium that itself "thinks" and carries an internalized, structurally conservative "media cognition." [[Matias2025-px]] and [[Goldberg2026-eb]] look toward normatively better futures — participatory evaluation of AI systems and AI-enabled deliberative "digital public squares," respectively — while [[Gilardi2026-hw]] provides a sobering audience-reception check on AI-generated journalism, finding perceived quality is indistinguishable from human writing but engagement gains from AI disclosure are short-lived curiosity effects rather than durable acceptance. [[Jayaram2026-wd]] extends the reflexive loop one step further, describing an agentic AI system for automating peer review itself and proposing a taxonomy of AI roles in scientific verification — a fitting bookend for a topic whose central tension, from [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] onward, has been how to validate machine judgment without simply outsourcing the act of validation to another machine. [[Marino2026-he]], finally, exemplifies where the applied payoff of all this methodological work lands: an LLM-classified, three-year longitudinal analysis of a hyperpartisan Bolsonaro network showing that affective engagement is far less stable than echo-chamber theory predicts — a substantive finding made possible only by the classification-at-scale infrastructure this entire structure note has traced from validation protocol to political discovery.
