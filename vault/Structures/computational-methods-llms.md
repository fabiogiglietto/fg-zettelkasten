# Computational Methods & LLMs in Content Analysis

## Computational Methods & LLMs in Content Analysis

### The validation imperative

The dominant throughline across this collection is methodological: can LLMs and embedding models be trusted to do the analytical work that human coders and classical NLP pipelines used to do, and under what conditions? A cluster of papers treats this as a direct, benchmarked question. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] pits OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large against the Italian-specific UmBERTo for clustering political news, finding the general-purpose LLM embedding consistently superior—extending English-language findings to a non-English setting. [[Fan2025-ut]] tackles a quieter but pervasive problem with embeddings themselves: pretrained representations encode confounders like source or language that bias similarity measures, and linear concept erasure (LEACE) can strip this out cheaply and reliably across ten models and multiple languages. [[Meher2025-qb]] and [[Farjam2026-zg]] both push toward *accessible* fine-tuning—QLoRA-adapted Llama for conflict event classification, and a locally-run agentic Llama workflow replicating a published content-analysis study—demonstrating that strong, reliable performance need not depend on proprietary, cloud-hosted frontier models. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] delivers perhaps the sharpest validation finding: even reasoning- and search-augmented LLMs fact-check poorly on their own, and the decisive lever is curated retrieval, not model scale—a finding that resonates with [[Brown2025-jk]]'s result that item difficulty (human label entropy), not demographic bias or model choice, is the dominant predictor of LLM-human annotation agreement. Together these works reframe "is the LLM good enough?" as an engineering and validation question rather than a binary verdict.

### LLMs as collaborators, not oracles

A second thread resists treating LLMs as autonomous classifiers at all, instead theorizing them as partners in interpretive work. [[Ober2026-vd]] and [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] both develop human-in-the-loop workflows—topic modeling plus LLM-assisted labeling for interview transcripts, and an explicit "LLM Codebook Prompt" for deductive framing analysis—arguing that disagreements between LLM and human coders are not simply errors to be minimized but diagnostic moments that expose ambiguities and historically contingent assumptions buried in theoretical codebooks (e.g., what counts as "moral" framing in a digital-privacy era). [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys this broader shift toward LLM-based annotation and synthesis as an emerging methodological subfield. [[Balluff2026-if]] supplies the field's conscience, cataloguing the epistemic, environmental, and reproducibility costs of reflexively defaulting to large commercial LLMs and arguing for a trade-off mindset that privileges open, task-appropriate, less resource-intensive tools—a corrective in direct tension with papers that showcase ever-larger model capabilities.

### Scaling classical content-analysis questions

Many papers use LLMs or embeddings instrumentally, to scale long-standing communication-research questions rather than to interrogate the method itself. [[Larsson2026-ro]] applies GPT-4 zero-shot sentiment classification (validated against humans) to a decade of non-English Facebook posts to study negativity and engagement; [[Bailard2024-pj]] fine-tunes DeBERTa to classify collective-action frames at scale and links them to offline violence via Granger causality; [[Lai2024-to]] and [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] extend latent-ideology scaling—one via Reddit-sharing correspondence analysis of YouTube videos, the other via prompt-and-average LLM positioning of political texts—to new units of analysis. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] and [[Arminio2025-tw]] push into the visual domain, showing multimodal LLMs can outperform specialized computer-vision pipelines for face/person detection and can capture *connotative* (not just denotative) meaning in images, addressing a gap also flagged by [[Gardam2025-er]], [[Arora2025-tx]], [[Yang2023-cg]], and Bastos2025-ol, all of which argue that text-centric methods have systematically underserved image-heavy platforms and visual political communication. [[Waight2025-al]] and [[Elfes2026-jb]] extend this scaling logic to higher-order semantic constructs—narrative similarity and actantial role-assignment—arguing that LLMs finally make tractable estimands (narrative diffusion, narrative polarization) that prior lexical or topic-modeling approaches could only approximate; [[Altoe2026-wu]] similarly operationalizes narrative-level clustering for real-time virality triage. [[Nenno2025-xa]] and [[Sarmiento2025-as]] round out this group with large cross-national and unsupervised framing analyses that depend on computational scale to surface cross-cultural or emergent patterns invisible to small hand-coded samples.

### LLMs as social actors, not just instruments

A distinct subset treats LLMs not as analytical tools but as objects of study or as interactive social agents—raising questions about inference, persuasion, and trust that bear directly on how such tools should be used in research. [[Lee2026-je]] shows LLMs can infer political alignment from nonpolitical text with unsettling accuracy, a capability with direct implications for the privacy of LLM-coded corpora. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]], [[Costello2024-bg]], and [[Lin2025-xp]] establish that conversational LLMs are highly persuasive—on political attitudes, conspiracy beliefs, and voting intentions respectively—while [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] uncovers a persuasion-accuracy trade-off and [[Lin2025-xp]] documents partisan asymmetries in claim accuracy, both cautioning that the same mechanisms enabling rich computational content analysis (information density, fluent argumentation) are what make these systems risky real-world actors. [[Szabo2026-rd]] and [[Dubey2026-bl]] study LLM-chatbots as misinformation-resistance tools, finding that interactional design (trust, adaptability) matters as much as content. [[Paci2025-ag]] complicates the optimism by showing LLMs still struggle with pragmatic inference (implicature, presupposition) in real political speech, tempering claims about near-human annotation competence. [[Nguyen2026-vm]] and [[Manovich2026-ih]] step back to examine how LLMs/GenAI themselves are represented and theorized—as agentic news subjects and as a novel cognitive medium—offering a reflexive frame for the methodological enterprise.

### Infrastructure, detection, and the limits of platform-based data

A final set addresses the empirical infrastructure underlying all this work. [[Freelon2018-ao]] and [[Allen2025-ot]] chart the "post-API age," arguing that platform-independent methods (scraping, browser-extension experiments) are now methodologically necessary rather than optional. [[Fan2026-af]] proposes a temporal/sequence-based reorientation for digital trace data, reviewing six computational approaches (including language-based models) for capturing hyper-longitudinal user behavior. Detection-focused papers—[[Pante2025-pq]], [[Iannucci2026-my]], and [[Kim2026-br]]—converge on a methodological critique that echoes the validation theme: coordination and influence-operation detection have relied too heavily on descriptive pattern-matching without statistical null models or control datasets, a gap [[Iannucci2026-my]] formalizes via planted-submatrix hypothesis testing and [[Kim2026-br]] addresses through explainable, span-level rationale classifiers. [[Xue2025-bp]], [[Ramos2026-qo]], and [[Ahuja2025-ku]] extend the substantive concerns of this topic—emotional framing in fact-checks, narrative-level disinformation countermeasures, and regulatory mapping of manipulative design—into adjacent normative and policy territory, suggesting that computational content-analysis methods are increasingly expected not just to describe discourse but to inform intervention and governance.

Across all four threads, the papers collectively narrate a field in rapid, somewhat uneven transition: embedding models and LLMs are proving powerful enough to scale analyses of framing, ideology, narrative, and visual meaning that were previously bottlenecked by manual coding, but nearly every substantive application paper is shadowed by a validation, bias, or infrastructure paper insisting that these gains are conditional—on curated context, appropriate null models, human oversight, or honest accounting of what the tools cannot yet do.
