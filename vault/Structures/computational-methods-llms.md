# Computational Methods & LLMs for Content Analysis

## Computational Methods & LLMs for Content Analysis

The papers gathered here trace an arc from methodological enthusiasm to methodological reckoning. They collectively document the rapid, often improvised incorporation of large language models into the empirical machinery of political communication and computational social science — as annotators, embedding generators, survey proxies, persuaders, fact-checkers, and even peer reviewers — while simultaneously accumulating evidence about where these tools succeed, fail, and introduce new forms of bias or fragility.

### Validating LLMs as coders and classifiers

A substantial cluster concerns whether LLMs can substitute for human coders in content analysis, and under what conditions. [[Bailard2024-pj]] fine-tunes a supervised DeBERTa model to classify collective-action frames in extremist Telegram discourse, exemplifying the older but still-dominant supervised-classifier paradigm against which newer LLM-based approaches are often benchmarked. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] shows GPT-4o outperforming specialized computer-vision pipelines for face recognition and person-counting in political imagery, while [[Meher2025-qb]] demonstrates that efficient QLoRA fine-tuning of open-weight Llama models dramatically improves conflict-event classification over zero-shot baselines, emphasizing accessibility on consumer hardware — a concern echoed by [[Farjam2026-zg]], whose agentic, locally-run Llama pipeline replicates and extends a classic climate-coverage study across languages with a structured Instructor–Classifier–Judge architecture. [[Larsson2026-ro]] and [[Ober2026-vd]] both document hybrid human-validated LLM workflows — zero-shot sentiment classification and topic-modeling-plus-LLM-labeling, respectively — that aim to preserve interpretive rigor while scaling. [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] pushes this further, reframing LLMs not as classifiers but as "analytical collaborators" whose disagreements with human coders expose latent theoretical ambiguities in framing codebooks, a stance that resonates with [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]]'s reflection on validating a fully LLM-integrated pipeline (classification, embedding, clustering, labeling) for Italian political news, where the paper argues each stage needs bespoke validation and that expert — not crowdsourced — annotators are now required since LLMs often outperform low-skilled human coders. [[Marino2026-he]] applies such a pipeline longitudinally to Brazilian hyperpartisan Facebook networks, showing LLM-classified affective engagement reveals unstable, event-driven dynamics rather than static echo chambers.

Several papers push into harder inferential territory. [[Lee2026-je]] shows LLMs can infer political alignment even from nonpolitical text, exploiting subtle cultural cues, raising privacy concerns that parallel [[Waight2026-ts]]'s finding that state media control seeps into LLM training data and skews outputs by query language. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] proposes a simple "ask-and-average" prompting method for scaling political texts on ideological dimensions, extending the text-scaling tradition into the LLM era, while [[Lai2024-to]] extends latent ideology estimation to YouTube videos via Reddit sharing networks — a reminder that not all scaling work in this space is LLM-based. Limits are equally well documented: [[Paci2025-ag]] finds current multilingual LLMs struggle badly with implicature and presupposition in Italian political speech, and [[DeVerna2025-dl]] shows that even reasoning- and search-augmented LLMs fail at political fact-checking without curated retrieval, though a RAG system built on fact-checker summaries yields dramatic gains — while also surfacing an ideological skew toward left-leaning credible sources in web-search citations.

### Bias, fairness, and representational validity in LLM annotation

A second thread interrogates whether LLM judgments are systematically biased. [[Brown2025-jk]] finds demographic biases in LLM annotation of contentious content are dataset-specific rather than model-specific, and dwarfed by item difficulty (label entropy) — a carefully calibrated counterpoint to alarmist narratives. [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys the broader landscape of LLM-based annotation and synthesis methodologies, while [[Balluff2026-if]] offers the field's most sustained critique, cataloguing epistemic, environmental, and ethical costs of unreflective LLM adoption — reproducibility threats from opaque corporate updates, demographic skew in synthetic data, and the frequent sufficiency of smaller, cheaper models — arguing for a trade-off mindset rather than either rejection or uncritical adoption. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] approaches validity from an epistemological angle, arguing that generative AI outputs require a new category of "emergent facts" irreducible to evidence-based, interpretative, or rule-based conceptions of factuality, offering indicators (accuracy, verifiability, contextual relevance, consistency) for practical assessment.

### Embeddings, clustering, and representational infrastructure

A methodologically dense cluster concerns embedding models as the substrate of downstream text (and image) analysis. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] benchmarks OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large against the Italian-specific UmBERTo for clustering political news, finding the proprietary multilingual model consistently superior — a finding operationalized in [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]]'s pipeline above. [[Fan2025-ut]] tackles a subtler problem: embeddings encode confounders like source or language that bias similarity measures, and shows that linear concept erasure (LEACE) can be applied broadly as a low-cost corrective without degrading out-of-domain performance. [[Arminio2025-tw]] extends embedding-based clustering to the visual domain, showing that vision-LLMs generating connotative textual descriptions of images outperform CNN-based pipelines for socially meaningful (rather than merely denotative) clustering, with interpretability gains via TF-IDF summaries. [[Bruns2025-fz]] proposes "practice mapping" via vector embeddings of network actions as an alternative to conventional, hairball-prone network visualization, and [[Waight2025-al]] develops an LLM-based pipeline for measuring cross-lingual "narrative similarity" — distilling texts into claims and subjects before comparison — validating it against text-reuse, topic-modeling, and Relatio baselines and demonstrating its superior sensitivity in tracking Russian disinformation narratives into U.S. media. [[Elfes2026-jb]] similarly uses an LLM (DeepSeek-R1) to operationalize Greimas' Actantial Model for measuring "narrative polarisation" in YouTube/comment discourse on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, with LLM-human coding agreement matching human-human agreement. [[Arora2025-tx]] and [[Sarmiento2025-as]] extend automated framing analysis toward multimodal and unsupervised approaches respectively, both critiquing the field's historical reliance on fixed, text-only frame sets. [[Gardam2025-er]] makes a parallel, non-LLM methodological argument for visual-first analysis of Instagram conspiracy discourse.

### LLMs as causal agents: persuasion, belief change, and manipulation

A distinct set of papers studies LLMs not as analytic instruments but as causal interventions in discourse itself. [[Costello2024-bg]] shows personalized AI dialogue durably reduces conspiracy beliefs by ~20%, challenging motivational accounts of belief rigidity. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] systematically decomposes the levers of conversational AI persuasion, identifying information density as the central mechanism and documenting a troubling persuasion-accuracy trade-off, while its companion study [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] shows AI systems out-persuade even elite human debaters and professional canvassers, with the advantage collapsing only when AI is throttled to human throughput — reinforcing the fact-density mechanism. [[Kotz2026-lk]] extends this paradigm across contested societal domains (climate, vaccines, inequality), finding effects concentrated among skeptics and moderated by trust in science. [[Szabo2026-rd]] operationalizes a related logic defensively, building a chatbot for "conversational inoculation" against misinformation. Collectively these papers, alongside [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]]'s causal linkage of antivaccine tweets to offline vaccination and mortality outcomes, establish an evidence base for treating online/AI-mediated discourse as having measurable downstream behavioral consequences — a throughline back to [[Bailard2024-pj]]'s offline-violence framing analysis.

### Platform audits, infrastructure, and data access

Running parallel to tool-validation is a strong infrastructural-critique literature, much of it about what computational methods can even be applied to, given shrinking and reshaped data access. [[Murtfeldt2025-wu]], [[Cullen2026-cb]], [[Bruns2026-pn]], [[Peters2026-mo]], and [[Annabell2026-ev]] together chart the post-API transition — from Twitter's API closure and CrowdTangle's shutdown to the EU Digital Services Act's Article 40 researcher-access regime — showing how platform-controlled infrastructures (APIs, clean rooms, data catalogs) shape not just what data are available but what research questions, methods, and even epistemic communities are possible, with data quality and the very definition of "data" revealed as politically contested rather than neutral technical matters. [[Rieder2025-ju]] offers a concrete audit demonstrating YouTube's search API's severe temporal decay and inconsistency problems, directly undermining DSA-related research reliance on it, while [[Zheng2026-bi]] responds with alternative infrastructure — random-sampling tools (TubeStats/TokStats) that supply defensible denominators absent from platform APIs. [[Allen2025-ot]] discusses a complementary workaround: LLM-powered browser-extension experiments that sidestep platform gatekeeping entirely, situating this innovation against the FIES collaboration and debates about ecological validity. [[Bouchaud2026-lr]] and Bastos2025-ol extend auditing to recommender systems and troll-farm visual identities respectively, showing embeddings can reveal (and, for Bouchaud, be surgically corrected for) inadvertent political profiling.

### Governance, trust, and the societal reception of AI-mediated analysis

A final set of papers steps back to ask how AI-generated or AI-assisted content is perceived and institutionally governed. [[Nguyen2026-vm]] maps how news media themselves frame LLMs, finding mentalistic-agentic framing coexists with critical and technical registers rather than dominating. [[Gilardi2026-hw]] experimentally shows readers judge AI-generated news as equivalent in quality to human-written news, with disclosure boosting only short-term curiosity. [[Triedman2025-uy]] audits Grokipedia against Wikipedia, finding heavy derivativeness alongside a troubling increase in unreliable and blacklisted sourcing on controversial topics — a cautionary case study in AI-generated knowledge infrastructure. [[Kasianenko2026-tn]] audits Google's AI Overviews' uneven handling of conspiratorial queries. Meanwhile, [[Alizadeh2026-es]], [[King2026-av]], and [[Jayaram2026-wd]] turn the lens on science itself: AI coding agents can now reproduce a large share of published social-science findings (with sycophancy risks under confirmatory prompting), AI-generated peer reviews reportedly exceed even strong human reviews, and agentic verification tools like Google's PAT are being piloted at scale in conference review — developments that, alongside [[Miller2026-cp]]'s account of open, community-governed AI note-writing on X and [[Altoe2026-wu]]'s streaming pipeline for pre-peak misinformation triage, suggest LLMs are moving from research instruments into the institutional fabric of knowledge production and verification itself. Read together, this topic's papers argue that the central methodological task of the current moment is not whether to use LLMs, but how to validate, bound, and govern their use responsibly — a lesson [[Balluff2026-if]] and [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] state most explicitly, and that nearly every other paper here tests empirically.
