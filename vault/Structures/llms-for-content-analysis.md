# LLMs for Political Content Analysis

## From ad hoc prompting to validated pipelines

The methodological core of this literature is the move from treating LLMs as convenient text classifiers to treating them as instruments requiring the same validation discipline as any measurement device. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] is the programmatic statement here: an "LLMs-in-the-loop" pipeline for Italian election data that chains a fine-tuned political-content classifier, embedding-based clustering, and LLM-generated cluster labels, and that argues explicitly for task-specific, expert-coder validation at each stage rather than a single omnibus check. Its companion study, [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]], supplies the embedding-layer evidence for that pipeline, showing that OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large yields more semantically coherent clusters of Italian political news than the language-specific UmBERTo model — a comparative-embedding result that other papers in this set implicitly build on when choosing embedding backends. [[Larsson2026-ro]] extends the same human-in-the-loop logic to a decade of Norwegian Facebook data, pairing GPT-4 zero-shot sentiment classification with human validation to study negative campaigning, demonstrating that the hybrid approach travels across languages and time horizons. [[Yang2026-tq]] scales the paradigm up again, using an LLM annotation pipeline not to study political content directly but to conduct a meta-scientific census of social-media research itself — evidence that the validated-annotation approach is becoming infrastructure for computational social science broadly, not just a niche technique. [[Tan2024-vl]] situates all of this within the wider machine-learning literature on LLM-based annotation and synthesis, offering the taxonomy (generation, assessment, utilization) against which the more applied papers here can be read.

## Measuring meaning: scaling, positioning, and narrative

A second cluster treats LLMs as instruments for constructing latent measures rather than discrete labels. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] shows that simply asking an LLM to place a text on an ideological dimension and averaging many such judgments reproduces established scaling benchmarks across languages, echoing Wordscores/Wordfish-style ambitions with a prompting-and-averaging logic. [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] pushes the same idea into open-ended survey analysis, using LLM-paired comparisons to build continuous scales from free text that would otherwise be too costly to hand-code. [[Lai2024-to]] and [[Elfes2026-jb]] extend latent-measurement thinking to genuinely new units of analysis — YouTube videos ideologically positioned via Reddit sharing behavior, and Greimas-style "narrative polarisation" extracted from YouTube transcripts and comments via DeepSeek-R1 — both suggesting that polarization and ideology are better captured as structural, narrative properties than as simple valence scores. [[Waight2025-al]] formalizes narrative similarity as a distinct estimand from lexical or topical overlap, building an LLM-based claim-and-subject distillation pipeline that reveals narrative diffusion (e.g., pro-Kremlin "biolabs" claims) invisible to exact-text-reuse methods — a direct methodological argument, in the Grimmer-and-King validation tradition, that estimator choice changes substantive conclusions. [[Meher2025-qb]] complements this scaling agenda from the fine-tuning side, showing that QLoRA-adapted Llama models can outperform zero-shot LLMs at multi-label conflict-event classification on consumer hardware, arguing for domain adaptation as a third path alongside prompting and embedding-based pipelines.

## Beyond text: multimodal and embedding-level innovations

The pipeline logic extends naturally into the visual and network domains. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] finds that GPT-4o substantially outperforms specialized computer-vision models at face recognition and person-counting in Instagram campaign imagery, while [[Arminio2025-tw]] shows that VLLM-generated textual descriptions of images enable more *connotative* (versus merely denotative) clustering than CNN pipelines, with the added benefit of TF-IDF interpretability — both papers arguing that multimodal LLMs lower the technical barrier to visual political communication research without sacrificing, and often improving, validity. [[Arora2025-tx]] pursues a parallel argument for framing analysis, insisting that news framing is inherently multi-modal and that discourse-analytic frame categories can be extracted jointly from text and image. [[Bruns2025-fz]] approaches the same embedding-based turn from the network side, proposing "practice mapping" — vector embeddings of interaction types — as a way past the interpretive limits of conventional network visualization. Underpinning much of this embedding-centric work is a quieter methodological contribution from [[Fan2025-ut]], which shows that linear concept erasure (LEACE) can strip confounding source- or language-signal from pretrained embeddings, dramatically improving multilingual and multi-source clustering/retrieval without degrading unrelated benchmarks — a general-purpose fix directly relevant to any pipeline, like [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]]'s or [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]]'s, that pools texts across sources or languages.

## Interrogating the instrument: bias, politics, and epistemic limits

A skeptical counter-current asks whether these pipelines measure what researchers think they measure. [[Brown2025-jk]] finds that LLM-annotator disagreement with human coders on contentious classification tasks is driven far more by item difficulty (label entropy) than by annotator demographics, complicating simple "LLMs are demographically biased" narratives while still finding dataset-specific directional biases. [[Paci2025-ag]] cuts the other way, showing that even strong multilingual LLMs fail to reliably recover the implicatures and presuppositions of real Italian political speech, suggesting a pragmatic-competence ceiling that pure scaling has not solved — a caution for any annotation pipeline that assumes LLMs grasp subtext. [[Waight2026-ts]] raises a structural version of the bias question: state-coordinated media is memorized in training corpora and measurably shifts commercial LLM outputs toward pro-regime valence when queried in lower-media-freedom languages, meaning the "ground truth" LLMs bring to annotation tasks can itself be politically contaminated. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] supplies the epistemological vocabulary for this problem, arguing that generative outputs constitute a new category of "emergent facts" not captured by evidence-based, interpretative, or rule-based notions of factuality, which bears directly on how confidently researchers should treat LLM-coded "facts" as ground truth. [[Balluff2026-if]] draws these threads together into an explicit methodological warning to the field: reproducibility, corporate dependency, environmental cost, and demographic bias in synthetic data all argue for reflexivity and for preferring smaller, open, task-appropriate models over reflexive LLM adoption — a direct rejoinder to the enthusiasm implicit in much of the pipeline literature above.

## Downstream stakes: persuasion, fact-checking, and the information ecosystem

A final group examines what happens once LLM-mediated content analysis meets live political communication and its manipulation risks — showing why validated annotation matters substantively, not just methodologically. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] and [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] jointly map the mechanics and limits of conversational AI persuasion: information density and post-training, not scale or personalization, drive persuasiveness, but that persuasion collapses when audiences perceive partisan bias in the model — and the same levers that boost persuasion erode factual accuracy, a trade-off with obvious implications for any application using LLMs to correct political misperceptions. [[Szabo2026-rd]] and [[Ramos2026-qo]] explore constructive uses of this persuasive capacity, respectively piloting LLM-driven "conversational inoculation" against misinformation and arguing that fact-checking must be supplemented with narrative monitoring and argumentation-theoretic "argument-checking" to counter evolving conspiracy narratives (illustrated via the Valencia DANA floods). [[DeVerna2025-dl]] tests the fact-checking capacity of LLMs directly, finding that neither scale nor reasoning nor web search substitutes for curated retrieval context, and that search-enabled models show a leftward citation skew — a finding that dovetails with [[Waight2026-ts]]'s and [[Triedman2025-uy]]'s broader concerns about the political provenance of what LLMs treat as reliable knowledge, the latter documenting how Grokipedia systematically upweights unreliable and blacklisted sources relative to Wikipedia on political and biographical topics. [[Scalco2026-bd]], [[Rieder2025-ju]], and [[Allen2025-ot]] address the infrastructural conditions under which all this analysis happens: detecting "information voids" from demand–supply mismatches, auditing YouTube's search API as unreliable for retrospective political research, and defending platform-independent, LLM-assisted experimentation as a response to closing API access — concerns that echo [[Yang2026-tq]]'s finding that empirical social-media research is already contracting post-API. [[Lee2026-je]] and [[Sarmiento2025-as]] close the loop back to annotation proper, showing respectively that LLMs can infer political alignment from ostensibly nonpolitical text (raising the privacy stakes of exactly the classification pipelines this literature builds) and that unsupervised, non-predefined framing detection can surface latent polarization structure that fixed-category coding would miss.

## The pipeline turns on itself

Two outlying papers suggest where this methodological line is heading next: toward AI validating not just political content but the scientific process that studies it. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] shows that frontier coding agents can reproduce a large share of published social-science findings, while flagging that PDF context and confirmatory framing induce bias and sycophancy — a direct extension of the validation anxieties raised by [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] and [[Balluff2026-if]] into the realm of reproducibility itself. [[Jayaram2026-wd]]'s account of Google's Paper Assistant Tool, though focused on math/CS peer review, models the same trajectory: agentic, human-in-the-loop verification systems increasingly mediating the production and certification of knowledge, with all the grounding and hallucination risks this topic's core papers have already catalogued for political content.
