# MINE — Mapping Italian News Program

## Line of Inquiry

MINE is the umbrella under which the researcher's Italian election-and-media agenda has been pursued since 2017, and though only two papers are filed here directly, they mark the two temporal and conceptual endpoints of that agenda: an early study of the 2013 general election ([[Iannelli2015-e0818c3e]]) and a later study of the 2018 campaign ([[Giglietto2019-882f1900]]). Read together, they trace a shift in how the umbrella's core question — *how does social media reshape Italian political communication?* — gets answered as the media ecology itself changes.

## From TV-centred hybridity to platform-native insularity

[[Iannelli2015-e0818c3e]] approaches the question from a hybrid-media-systems perspective in which Twitter is fundamentally parasitic on television: audience "second-screening" during talk shows tracks the broadcast calendar, politicians and broadcasters barely engage with the resulting chatter, and the dominant genres of tweeting (opinion, requests for interaction) are reactive rather than agenda-setting. The paper's key intervention is methodological and theoretical at once — it insists that social media not be treated as an independent variable, but as one term in an interdependent TV–Twitter system, with participation narrowly accessible to a small, TV-anchored public.

[[Giglietto2019-882f1900]], filed under the same umbrella five years later, effectively picks up where that account leaves a gap: what happens once populist actors (M5S, the League) learn to operate social media as their *own* infrastructure rather than as a talk-show sidecar? Here Twitter and Facebook are no longer secondary screens reacting to broadcast agendas but primary arenas of partisan attention management, with measurable "insularity" scores, strategic amplification (shares) versus contestation (comments), and a demonstrable asymmetry in how M5S's supporters treat positive versus negative coverage compared to legacy parties (PD, Forza Italia, the League). The 2013 study's finding that M5S was "almost entirely absent" from TV-linked Twitter conversation is thrown into relief by the 2019 study's finding that M5S sources are, five years on, the *most* insular and the *most* strategically weaponized in the ecosystem — suggesting a trajectory from marginal, TV-parasitic actor to independent architect of a partisan hybrid ecology.

## Continuities: hybridity, narrow publics, and party-specific media logics

Despite the shift in emphasis, both papers share a commitment to the hybrid-media-system framework (explicitly citing Chadwick in both cases) and a skepticism toward flattening "social media effects" narratives. Both also converge on the finding that engagement is structurally uneven and party-specific rather than a generic feature of "the internet": in 2013, PDL tweets clustered around media-oriented commentary on Berlusconi, PD around policy streaming, and M5S around interactional requests; in 2018, M5S again stands apart, but now as the party best able to convert insularity and amplification into strategic advantage, with PD/Forza Italia/League showing the inverse pattern of contestation on negative news. This continuity — populist and non-populist actors inhabiting structurally different media logics — is arguably the throughline the MINE umbrella exists to track across its successive funded sub-projects, even as the empirical object shifts from talk-show second-screening to full-scale, multi-platform electoral news ecosystems.

## Structure note as scaffold

Because MINE is an institutional rather than a purely topical container, its coherence lies less in shared method (survey-based content analysis in [[Iannelli2015-e0818c3e]] vs. large-scale network/engagement analytics in [[Giglietto2019-882f1900]]) than in a persistent set of questions: who gets to shape the agenda on social media, how partisan and populist actors differ from mainstream parties in their media strategies, and how much continuity exists between broadcast-era and platform-era Italian political communication. Future sub-projects on public opinion and health information filed under this hub will likely need to be read against this baseline arc — from audiences reacting to television, to partisan communities actively engineering platform-level attention.
