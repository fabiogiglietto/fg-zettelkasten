# Social TV & Second-Screen Audience Engagement

## The Core Case: Twitter as Second Screen for Television

The empirical heart of this strand is [[Iannelli2015-e0818c3e]], a large-scale study of the 2013 Italian general election campaign that treats Twitter as a second screen layered onto political talk-show viewing. Working from a nearly complete corpus of 2.5 million tweets across 1,076 episodes of eleven talk shows, the paper operationalizes the now-standard social-TV distinction between participation *in* the media (shaping agendas, introducing new issues or sources) and participation *through* the media (commenting, requesting interaction, performing opinion). Its central finding — that access to these practices was narrow, that politicians and broadcasters largely failed to activate or respond to the connected audience, and that the bulk of tweeting was opinion/comment rather than substantive agenda-building — has become a durable reference point: it tempers the more utopian early social-TV literature by showing that television's broadcast logic continues to dominate hybrid conversations rather than being displaced by them. The paper's framing within hybrid-media-systems scholarship (Chadwick, Couldry, Blumler & Coleman) situates second-screen practice not as a new participatory public sphere but as a set of "spontaneous" audience behaviors bolted onto an unreformed broadcast industry — a claim that has aged well as a corrective to breathless accounts of Twitter-enabled political engagement.

## A Later, Oblique Coda: The Infrastructure Itself in Crisis

If [[Iannelli2015-e0818c3e]] represents the strand's classic empirical mode — content-analyzing tweet peaks against a broadcast calendar — [[Wang2026-ub]] enters this topic area from an unexpected angle, and marks how far the research interest has drifted from its original object. Rather than studying audience talk *about* television, it studies the platform infrastructure that made such talk analyzable in the first place: Twitter itself, and the failed attempt of a highly motivated community (academic early adopters) to relocate to Mastodon after Musk's 2022 acquisition. There is no television here, no second screen in the literal sense — but the paper is filed in this strand because it speaks directly to the fate of the ecosystem that social-TV research depended on. Its finding that even a well-networked, ideologically committed cohort could not sustain migration away from Twitter (79.7% remained active on the incumbent platform; only ~7.4% became persistent, exclusive Mastodon users) documents the entrenchment and eventual instability of the very platform that hosted a decade of social-TV/second-screen scholarship.

## Reading the Two Together

Placed side by side, the two papers trace an arc rather than a shared method. [[Iannelli2015-e0818c3e]] asks what happens *on* Twitter during television events and finds a constrained, broadcast-subordinated public. [[Wang2026-ub]] asks what happens *to* Twitter as a platform once its ownership and governance are disrupted, and finds that network effects and social capital (large pre-existing follower counts predicting higher attrition risk on Mastodon, HR=1.13) are nearly impossible to replicate elsewhere, even for a coordinated professional community with strong internal cohesion. Read as a pair, they bracket the lifespan of Twitter-centric social-TV research: the earlier paper captures the platform at its height as an object of hybrid-media analysis, while the later one — via a case that has nothing to do with television — implicitly closes the loop by showing why that empirical paradigm (built on freely available, comprehensive hashtag/event corpora) has become harder to sustain, both because the platform's data ecosystem has fragmented and because no successor platform has achieved comparable centrality. This is consistent with the topic's status as a "stable but now secondary" interest: the core claims about TV-Twitter hybridity established by [[Iannelli2015-e0818c3e]] remain settled reference points, while [[Wang2026-ub]] signals, obliquely, why fresh empirical work in this exact mode is unlikely to be replicated on any single platform going forward.
