# Disinformation Narrative Monitoring

## The arc from "false facts" to narratives

A conceptual throughline unites much of this literature: the recognition that disinformation is not usefully understood as discrete, verifiable falsehoods but as *narratives* — recurring, emotionally resonant stories that circulate across platforms, genres, and even languages. [[Marwick2026-qd]] makes this case most forcefully, showing through three US case studies that successful disinformation behaves like folklore, signaled by catchphrases and sustained by catharsis and identity affirmation rather than factual persuasion. [[Suau_Martinez2026-lv]] operationalizes this empirically, demonstrating survey evidence that cumulative exposure to a recurring narrative — not exposure to any single claim — predicts belief. [[Prochaska2025-ef]] and [[Starbird2025-jj]] extend the argument into the mechanics of collective sensemaking around US elections, showing how "deep stories" are performed collaboratively by influencers and audiences through quote-tweet framing dynamics, and how the *style* of telling (explicit explanation vs. bare artifact-sharing) itself carries meaning. [[Sadler2025-vu]] supplies the missing theoretical rigor here, arguing that disinformation studies has used "narrative" too loosely; his hermeneutic-realist framework, applied to pro-Kremlin narration of NATO enlargement, shows that content can be ethically and referentially suspect without being straightforwardly false — a direct challenge to the true/false binary that undergirds most fact-checking. [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] anticipates this shift from an institutional angle, arguing that "fake news" typologies fixated on creator intent miss how propagation itself, via cycles of micro-level trust judgments, generates emergent macro-level cascades irreducible to any single label.

## Measuring and tracking narratives at scale

A second cluster addresses the methodological challenge of detecting and quantifying narratives computationally — directly relevant to PROMPT/MINE-FACTS-style monitoring infrastructure. [[Waight2025-al]] offers perhaps the sharpest tool: an LLM-based pipeline that distills documents into claims and subjects to measure true narrative similarity (as opposed to lexical or topical overlap), validated against the diffusion of Russian "biolabs" claims from state media into low-quality US outlets — a result invisible to exact-text-reuse methods. [[Scalco2026-bd]] tackles a complementary problem, operationalizing "information voids" as quantifiable demand-supply imbalances across social media and search data, showing these voids predict reduced circulation of credible content and increased misinformation during COVID vaccine rollouts. [[Rogers2026-cy]], emerging from the sister vera.ai project, proposes "post-truth spaces" as a mapping concept for fact-checkers, using network analysis and provenance investigation to locate influential disinformation clusters, demonstrated on Moldovan Facebook discourse around the war in Ukraine. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] traces analogous pathways for COVID science communication, showing coordinated contrarian networks amplifying credentialed dissenting experts and news coverage typically *following* rather than leading superspreader activity on Twitter. Together these papers model the shift from ad hoc narrative description toward scalable, structurally grounded detection — the technical backbone that monitoring dashboards require.

## War, elections, and platform-specific propaganda: case studies

Several papers are tightly scoped case studies that populate the monitoring agenda's substantive beats — Ukraine, elections, and identity-based culture-war narratives. On the war in Ukraine: [[Graham2025-gp]] examines how #IStandWithPutin exploited rather than evaded platform trust infrastructures; [[Bosch2024-hj]] identifies sound as an under-studied, TikTok-specific propaganda vector reaching tens of billions of views; [[Makeev2026-ma]] theorizes Russian state media's dual domestic/foreign propaganda objectives; and [[FitzGerald2025-nv]] notes the adaptive, event-appropriating persistence of manipulation campaigns more generally. On elections: [[Rossini2026-mj]] shows how belief in electoral misinformation and reliance on messaging apps eroded trust in Brazil's 2022 election, even as mainstream news use bolstered it; [[Rodarte2026-dk]] documents Brazilian parliamentarians' competing claims to epistemic authority during the Manaus COVID crisis; and [[Gaber2022-bk]] theorizes "strategic lying" in Brexit and the 2019 UK election as an evolution of political spin, turbocharged by social media and paradoxically reinforced by corrective rebuttals. [[Copland2025-em]] traces how Sky News Australia became a transnational right-wing propaganda node via a dual Facebook/YouTube strategy, bridging Australian and US conspiracist content (election fraud, COVID, the Great Reset). [[Ramos2026-qo]], written by Maldita.es fact-checkers, is the volume's most directly operational paper: it documents the Valencia DANA flood conspiracy and proposes a hybrid workflow combining narrative monitoring and "argument-checking" — targeting fallacious reasoning patterns rather than individual claims — as a necessary complement to fact-checking, since corrected beliefs regress and narrative momentum persists beyond any single debunk.

## Fact-checking institutions under pressure

A final cluster turns from narrative content to the institutional and technical apparatus of verification itself, asking what fact-checking can and cannot do in this landscape. [[Farkas2026-lr]] examines how European fact-checkers rhetorically defend their dependence on platform funding, while [[Cazzamatta2026-lo]] shows empirically that Meta's post-verification actions (removal, labeling, inaction) are inconsistent and that fact-checkers largely reject deletion in favor of transparency and counter-speech — complicating Zuckerberg's "censorship" framing of the Third-Party Fact-Checking Program's demise. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] demonstrates a concrete technical finding for dashboard design: LLMs perform poorly at political fact-checking unless given curated retrieval context, a result directly relevant to any automated verification pipeline PROMPT or MINE-FACTS might build. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] pushes the epistemology further, arguing that generative AI's "emergent facts" require new evaluative categories beyond evidence-based, interpretative, or rule-based factuality. [[Xue2025-bp]] complicates the normative self-image of fact-checking by showing it relies heavily on emotional language despite its objectivity claims. [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] offers an adjacent gatekeeping case study, showing how Google News Italia's aggregation of health content shapes Facebook engagement independent of source identity.

## Theoretical bedrock: propaganda, polarization, and the disinformation order

Underpinning the whole topic is a foundational layer of theory explaining *why* contemporary information ecosystems are vulnerable in the first place. [[Benkler2018-lw]] remains the central reference, arguing the US "epistemic crisis" is asymmetric and institutional — rooted in an insulated, radicalized right-wing media ecosystem rather than technology or foreign interference per se. [[Bennett2018-ys]] complements this with the "disinformation order" concept, tying strategic disinformation to declining institutional trust and the blurring of journalism and propaganda formats. [[Freelon2020-yp]] offers the field's clearest conceptual stock-taking, distinguishing disinformation from misinformation and propaganda via intent and harm, while [[Humprecht2020-gd]] operationalizes resilience cross-nationally, finding the US structurally exceptional among Western democracies. [[Tsfati2020-uo]] and [[Lazer2018-mm]] both interrogate the paradox that correction and mainstream coverage can themselves amplify falsehoods, a finding echoed throughout the narrative-persistence literature above. Finally, [[Starbird2019-qv]] reframes information operations as *collaborative work* blending orchestrated and organic participation — a lens that, together with Benkler's and Bennett's institutional diagnoses, supplies the theoretical scaffolding against which the more applied monitoring and fact-checking tools surveyed above are implicitly or explicitly built.
