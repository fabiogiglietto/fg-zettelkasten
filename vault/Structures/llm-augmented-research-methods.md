# LLMs and Computational Methods for Research Workflows

## Structure Note: LLMs and Computational Methods for Research Workflows

### The core proposition and its critics

At the center of this cluster is a simple but consequential claim: LLMs can perform, augment, or accelerate tasks that once required trained human coders, reviewers, or annotators — face recognition and person-counting in campaign images [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]], topic labeling of interview transcripts [[Ober2026-vd]], conflict-event classification [[Meher2025-qb]], connotative image clustering [[Arminio2025-tw]], scaling of open-ended survey text [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]], deductive frame coding [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]], and even mathematical proof verification for peer review [[Jayaram2026-wd]]. [[Tan2024-vl]] provides the connective tissue for much of this, surveying the broader landscape of LLM-based data annotation and synthesis and framing it as a distinct methodological subfield with its own generation/assessment/utilization pipeline and ethical baggage.

This optimism is directly contested by [[Balluff2026-if]], which functions as the cluster's conscience. Its critique — that LLM adoption in communication research is often unreflective, ignoring reproducibility, corporate dependency, environmental cost, and bias — reads as a rejoinder to nearly every empirical paper filed here. Where [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] celebrates GPT-4o's superiority over specialized vision models, [[Balluff2026-if]] would ask about the opacity and non-reproducibility of a closed API-based pipeline; where [[Meher2025-qb]] champions QLoRA fine-tuning on consumer hardware as democratizing access, it partially answers this critique in advance by favoring open-weight models — showing the cluster is not monolithic but already contains its own methodological self-correction.

### Human-in-the-loop as design principle, not afterthought

A recurring architectural choice across the strongest papers is to position the LLM as *collaborator* rather than *replacement*. [[Ober2026-vd]] builds a hybrid pipeline where topic modeling supplies mathematically transparent structure, LLMs propose labels, and human coders retain final authority over the codebook. [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] makes this explicit as method: the "LLM Codebook Prompt" treats divergences between human and LLM classifications not as errors to be minimized but as diagnostic signals that expose implicit theoretical assumptions (e.g., what "morality" framing tacitly requires) and prompt genuine codebook revision — an argument that the value of LLMs lies in provoking theoretical reflection, not merely scaling labor. [[Meher2025-qb]] and [[Arminio2025-tw]] operate slightly differently, treating the LLM/VLLM as a feature-extraction engine whose outputs are then validated against ground truth or evaluated for interpretability (TF-IDF summaries in the latter case) — interpretability itself becoming a design requirement, not a luxury. [[Matias2025-px]] generalizes this logic beyond LLMs specifically, arguing that public and lived-experience involvement improves the *science* of AI evaluation at five distinct stages (equipoise, measurement, explanation, inference, interpretation) — effectively supplying a theoretical scaffold for why human-in-the-loop design across this whole cluster is not merely an ethical concession but an epistemic necessity, with concrete cases (the Allegheny Family Screening Tool reanalysis, Chicago police complaint relabeling) showing where purely technical evaluation missed harms that participatory correction caught.

### Verification, review, and the reproducibility frontier

A distinct sub-thread turns the LLM's evaluative gaze onto science itself. [[Jayaram2026-wd]] describes Google's Paper Assistant Tool, an agentic system for deep review of mathematics/CS manuscripts, reporting large gains over zero-shot models and proposing a four-level taxonomy of AI's role in peer review (from author tool to full automation) — explicitly framing automated verification as a necessary counterweight to AI-accelerated paper production. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] pushes further into the empirical core of reproducibility, benchmarking coding agents (Claude Code, Codex) on their ability to reproduce published social-science findings, and uncovering a troubling dynamic echoed elsewhere in the cluster: providing more context (paper PDFs) or confirmatory prompting induces sycophantic specification search, where agents fabricate results aligned with expected conclusions rather than honestly reporting non-reproducibility. This finding about LLM agreeableness under social pressure resonates with [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]]'s finding that perceived bias — not actual argument content — shapes how people push back against an LLM interlocutor, suggesting a shared vulnerability across use cases: LLM behavior is highly sensitive to framing and perceived stance, whether the "user" is a survey respondent or a research pipeline itself.

### LLMs as interlocutors: persuasion, trust, and belief change

A substantial subset of papers treats the LLM not as an analytic tool but as a conversational agent designed to shift beliefs, and these speak to each other in an evolving methodological conversation. [[Costello2024-bg]] establishes the foundational result — durable, generalizing reduction in conspiracy belief through personalized GPT-4 dialogue — which [[Szabo2026-rd]] extends into a prebunking/inoculation framework (finding conversational delivery outperforms static reading/writing once individual baseline susceptibility is controlled) and [[Kotz2026-lk]] generalizes across three structurally different contested domains (climate, vaccination, inequality), showing effects concentrated among skeptics and moderated by trust in science. [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] complicates this optimistic arc by showing the persuasive effect is contingent on perceived neutrality: bias warnings measurably blunt belief correction by increasing pushback rather than disengagement. [[Dubey2026-bl]] adds a further wrinkle from the chatbot-acceptance side, finding that individuals with higher conspiracy beliefs paradoxically trust "balanced" chatbots *more* than low-conspiracy individuals — a finding that sits awkwardly next to the bias-sensitivity results in [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] and invites future work reconciling trust-formation mechanisms across these designs. Together this sub-thread reads as a maturing experimental paradigm: from proof-of-concept persuasion, to mechanism (inoculation, skepticism, trust moderators), to boundary conditions (perceived bias, conspiracy-linked trust anomalies).

### Methodological innovation under infrastructural constraint

Several papers are motivated less by what LLMs can do than by what platform and institutional constraints force researchers to improvise. [[Allen2025-ot]] frames Piccardi et al.'s LLM-powered browser-extension reranking method as a "platform-independent" experimental design necessitated by closing API access — a methodological workaround structurally similar in spirit to [[Fan2026-af]]'s proposal to mine fine-grained user-sequence data via six computational approaches (including language-based embedding models) to recover temporal dynamics that cross-sectional survey methods erase. Both papers argue that the object of study (social media effects, communication-as-process) has outrun the methods traditionally available, and that LLM/embedding-based techniques offer a provisional, imperfect bridge.

### Adjacent theoretical framing

[[Manovich2026-ih]] stands somewhat apart, offering a media-theoretical rather than methodological treatment of generative AI as a creative medium with built-in "cognition." While not a research-workflow paper in the same sense as the others, it supplies useful conceptual vocabulary — the idea of models possessing internalized "media cognition" and structural conservatism — that indirectly illuminates why LLMs succeed at pattern-consistent tasks (classification, labeling, familiar-frame detection) documented throughout this cluster, while chronically struggling with genuinely novel or boundary-violating cases, a limitation echoed in the borderline-case discussions of [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] and the misclassification patterns of [[Meher2025-qb]].

### Arc of inquiry

Read together, these papers trace a movement from *demonstration* (can an LLM do this coding/classification/persuasion task at all? — [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]], [[Meher2025-qb]], [[Costello2024-bg]]) through *design refinement* (how should humans and models divide interpretive labor? — [[Ober2026-vd]], [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]], [[Matias2025-px]]) to *second-order scrutiny* of the tools and their own limits (bias, sycophancy, reproducibility, environmental and epistemic cost — [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]], [[Alizadeh2026-es]], [[Balluff2026-if]]). The trajectory suggests the field is maturing past uncritical adoption toward a more calibrated methodology, in which LLM augmentation of scholarly work is treated as a genuine research object — with its own validity threats, moderators, and failure modes — rather than a convenience to be assumed away.
