# LLMs and Computational Methods for Research Workflows

## The augmentation arc: from data scarcity to agentic verification

This body of work traces a common arc: LLMs are being asked to stand in for, or alongside, human researchers at nearly every stage of the scholarly pipeline—annotation, coding, classification, measurement, review, and even replication. What unites the papers is less a shared method than a shared question: under what conditions can a model's judgment substitute for, extend, or corrupt a human researcher's judgment, and how should that boundary be designed and evaluated?

### Motivating the turn to LLMs

Two papers set the stage by documenting the conditions that make LLM-based methods attractive. [[Yang2026-tq]] uses a human-in-the-loop LLM annotation pipeline not as an object of study but as infrastructure—applying it to classify tens of thousands of papers and show that empirical social-media research, long dependent on Twitter/Facebook API access, is contracting as that access disappears. This creates the very problem the rest of the topic responds to: as traditional data sources and manual-coding capacity shrink, LLMs are proposed as a way to keep pace, whether by augmenting classification of new corpora or by reproducing findings whose original replication materials have decayed. That reproduction-under-scarcity theme resurfaces sharply in [[Alizadeh2026-es]], where AI coding agents are benchmarked directly on their ability to resurrect published computational results—showing strikingly high success rates alongside sobering failure modes (metadata leakage, sycophantic specification search under confirmatory prompting) that recast "reproduction" as itself a fraught, gameable task.

### Human-in-the-loop qualitative and visual coding

A cluster of papers works out what human-in-the-loop actually means for interpretive tasks. [[Ober2026-vd]] proposes topic modeling as a transparent scaffold onto which LLM-generated labels are layered, with human analysts adjudicating codebook revisions—explicitly trading some automation for interpretive accountability. [[Arminio2025-tw]] extends this logic to images, arguing that vision-LLMs surface *connotative* meaning (cultural, symbolic) that CNN pipelines miss, and that the intermediate text representations LLMs produce are what make clusters interpretable to a human coder in the first place—interpretability, here, is not an afterthought but the payoff of routing perception through language. Both papers treat the LLM as a generator of intermediate, humanly legible artifacts (labels, descriptions) rather than a black-box classifier, which is precisely the design feature that [[Balluff2026-if]] argues is too often absent from careless LLM adoption: without such scaffolding and reflexivity, researchers inherit reproducibility, bias, and environmental costs they haven't reckoned with.

### Classification and measurement at scale

A second cluster asks whether fine-tuned or prompted LLMs can serve as domain-specific classifiers and measurement instruments outright. [[Meher2025-qb]] fine-tunes an open-weight Llama model via QLoRA for conflict-event classification, explicitly framing efficient fine-tuning as democratizing access to strong domain classifiers on consumer hardware—a direct rebuttal, in method if not in citation, to [[Balluff2026-if]]'s worry about corporate dependency. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] and [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] push LLMs into a different register: not classification but latent measurement, using prompt-and-average or LLM-paired-comparison schemes to scale political texts and open-ended survey responses onto continuous dimensions, implicitly arguing that LLMs can replace both hand-coding and older scaling algorithms (Wordscores/Wordfish) for measurement tasks. [[Tan2024-vl]] supplies the connective tissue for this cluster, surveying the broader landscape of LLM-based annotation and synthesis and formalizing the methodological choices (generation, assessment, utilization of LLM labels) that the empirical papers instantiate piecemeal.

Running beneath all classification work is the question of *whose* judgment the LLM approximates. [[Brown2025-jk]] interrogates this directly, finding that demographic bias in LLM annotation is real but small and highly dataset-specific, dwarfed by item difficulty (human annotator disagreement) as a predictor of LLM-human agreement—a finding that complicates both the optimism of the fine-tuning papers and the alarm of [[Balluff2026-if]] by suggesting the more actionable lever is task design, not model or demographic matching.

### From coding to reasoning and persuasion

Two papers push the human-in-the-loop paradigm toward higher-stakes, more autonomous applications. [[Costello2024-bg]] shows that personalized LLM dialogue can durably shift conspiracy beliefs, repositioning the LLM not as a coder of text but as an interlocutor whose "evidence marshaling" capacity is itself the object of scientific interest—a reminder that the tool/subject boundary in this literature is porous. [[Jayaram2026-wd]] closes the arc by moving from data-stage augmentation to the review stage itself: an agentic system for verifying mathematical and computational claims in peer review, deployed at real conferences, with an explicit taxonomy of AI autonomy (author tool → reviewer tool → total automation) that names the trajectory this whole topic is riding.

### The throughline: reflexivity as method

Read together, these papers describe less a settled toolkit than a live methodological negotiation. The optimistic pole—fine-tuning ([[Meher2025-qb]]), scaling ([[Le-Mens2025-qz]], [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]]), agentic verification ([[Jayaram2026-wd]], [[Alizadeh2026-es]])—keeps demonstrating that LLMs can do more, faster, and more accessibly than prior pipelines. The cautionary pole—[[Balluff2026-if]] most explicitly, but also the bias-auditing of [[Brown2025-jk]] and the interpretability emphasis of [[Ober2026-vd]] and [[Arminio2025-tw]]—insists that every gain in scale be paired with an accounting of what has been delegated, to whom, and at what epistemic cost. [[Yang2026-tq]] and [[Alizadeh2026-es]] bookend the set as meta-level demonstrations: LLMs are already reshaping how we know what the field has done and whether its results still hold, which makes the design choices debated throughout this topic—when to keep a human in the loop, and how to evaluate what the loop is actually verifying—a methodological question with stakes for the discipline's own self-knowledge.
