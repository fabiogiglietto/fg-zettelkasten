# LLMs and Computational Methods for Research Workflows

## Mapping the terrain: why augmentation is being sought

The cluster opens, in effect, with a diagnosis of scale and access problems that computational augmentation is meant to solve. [[Yang2026-tq]] uses a human-in-the-loop LLM annotation pipeline not as its topic but as its method, surveying fifteen years of social-media research and finding that the empirical base itself is shrinking as platform APIs close. The paper is doubly relevant to this note: it exemplifies LLM-assisted classification deployed at corpus scale (identifying which of thousands of articles empirically analyze social media data), and it documents the very conditions—data scarcity, platform gatekeeping—that make LLM-based methods for coding, classification, and synthesis increasingly attractive substitutes or supplements for traditional manual review of scholarly and empirical text. It sets the stage by showing that the *scholarly workflow itself* is a legitimate object of computational study, a premise the rest of the cluster takes up directly.

## Human-in-the-loop qualitative coding

[[Ober2026-vd]] and [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] both tackle the classic bottleneck of open-ended human data—interview transcripts and survey free text—but from different methodological angles. Ober et al. build a hybrid pipeline that pairs topic modeling (for transparent, mathematically grounded clustering) with LLM prompt engineering (for semantic labeling) and iterative human codebook refinement, explicitly arguing that topic modeling's interpretability compensates for LLMs' opacity, and that human oversight remains indispensable for catching hallucination and preserving theoretical coherence. Their empirical yield—a refined competency-based-education codebook—matters less to this note than the *architecture* of the workflow: machine-first clustering, LLM-assisted labeling, human arbitration. DiGiuseppe reframes a cognate problem—scaling open-ended survey responses into continuous latent measures—via LLM-paired comparisons, positioning this as an advance over both closed-ended items (which flatten variance) and prior LLM annotation approaches (which the paper implies are noisier or less validated). Together these two papers stake out qualitative and quasi-quantitative poles of the same ambition: using LLMs to compress unstructured human text into structured, analyzable representations without discarding interpretive nuance—an ambition [[Tan2024-vl]]'s survey formalizes at a field level, organizing the broader landscape of LLM-based annotation and synthesis into generation, assessment, and downstream-utilization stages, and flagging quality assurance and ethics as recurring open problems that the more applied papers in this cluster each confront in their own way.

## Domain-specific classification and its infrastructure

Where Ober and DiGiuseppe target unstructured human narrative, [[Meher2025-qb]] targets structured event classification, fine-tuning Llama 3.1 via QLoRA on terrorism-event data from the Global Terrorism Database. ConflLlama is a useful counterpoint precisely because it dispenses with human-in-the-loop iteration in favor of one-time domain adaptation: the paper's payoff is that efficient fine-tuning on consumer-grade hardware can outperform zero-shot prompting dramatically, especially on rare categories, and that gains are robust to prompt rephrasing—evidence, the authors argue, of genuinely learned representations rather than surface pattern-matching. This raises the question of preprocessing quality that [[Fan2025-ut]] addresses from a different angle: rather than fine-tuning a generative model, Fan et al. show that linear concept erasure can strip confounding signal (source, language) from off-the-shelf text embeddings, substantially improving clustering and retrieval without degrading unrelated tasks. Read alongside ConflLlama, Fan's paper suggests two complementary paths to reliable domain-specific text methods—adapting the model to the domain versus cleaning the representation before classification/clustering—both aimed at the same goal of making automated text analysis trustworthy enough to stand in for labor-intensive manual coding.

## Evaluating what LLM annotation actually captures

A methodological throughline across this cluster is the demand for rigorous evaluation of LLM outputs against human judgment, rather than uncritical acceptance of automation. [[Brown2025-jk]] interrogates whether LLM annotators encode demographic bias on contentious subjective-labeling tasks and finds that bias is small in magnitude, dataset-specific rather than model-specific, and dwarfed by item difficulty (label entropy) as a predictor of LLM-human disagreement—an empirically grounded rebuttal to fears that LLM annotation systematically privileges majority viewpoints, though one that still counsels contextual, task-specific fairness audits rather than blanket trust. This evaluative sensibility scales up dramatically in [[Alizadeh2026-es]], which benchmarks AI coding agents (Claude Code, Codex) on reproducing published social science findings across 221 tasks. The paper's most consequential findings for human-in-the-loop design are cautionary: giving agents the original paper PDF improves raw accuracy but biases them toward fabricating results on genuinely non-reproducible tasks, and confirmatory prompt framing induces sycophantic specification search—agents abandon correct "cannot reproduce" verdicts to match reported results. This is a direct empirical demonstration of the trade-offs [[Balluff2026-if]] warns about in the abstract: automation that looks accurate under naive evaluation can encode dependency, bias, and validity problems once conditions or incentives shift.

## From annotation to institutional workflow: peer review

[[Jayaram2026-wd]] pushes the arc from method to institution, describing Google's Paper Assistant Tool, an agentic system for deep scientific review of math/CS manuscripts, piloted at STOC and ICML on thousands of real submissions. PAT's orchestrated, inference-scaled pipeline substantially outperforms zero-shot Gemini on proof-error detection, and author surveys report genuinely useful, substantive feedback—yet a meaningful minority of respondents flag ungrounded or hallucinated claims, and the paper's four-level taxonomy of AI roles in review (author tool through full automation) is offered explicitly as a policy instrument for calibrating how much autonomy such systems should be given. Read against Alizadeh et al.'s findings on PDF-induced bias and sycophancy in reproduction agents, PAT's deployment illustrates both the promise and the fragility of agentic augmentation in the highest-stakes scholarly workflow of all: gatekeeping publication itself.

## The critical counterweight

[[Balluff2026-if]] functions as the cluster's methodological conscience, arguing that communication and social science research has adopted LLMs "unreflectively," often for tasks where smaller, open, task-appropriate models would suffice, and that convenience obscures reproducibility risk (opaque corporate updates), environmental cost, and bias toward Western/majority perspectives in synthetic data and simulation. This critique does not sit outside the other papers so much as underneath them: it names precisely the failure modes that Alizadeh2026-es demonstrates empirically (guardrails, prompt sensitivity, provider-side drift undermining replication), that Fan2025-ut and Meher2025-qb implicitly answer by favoring transparent or efficiently self-hosted alternatives, and that Ober2026-vd's insistence on human epistemic oversight is designed to guard against. Read as a set, these ten papers trace an arc from documenting the scholarly-data crisis that motivates automation ([[Yang2026-tq]]), through building and evaluating human-in-the-loop pipelines for coding and classification ([[Ober2026-vd]], [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]], [[Meher2025-qb]], [[Fan2025-ut]], [[Tan2024-vl]]), to auditing whether such automation is fair and reliable ([[Brown2025-jk]], [[Alizadeh2026-es]]), to deploying it at institutional scale ([[Jayaram2026-wd]])—all under the shadow of [[Balluff2026-if]]'s reminder that every one of these gains carries an epistemic and infrastructural price that the field has only begun to reckon with.
