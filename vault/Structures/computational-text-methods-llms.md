# Computational Methods & LLMs for Content Analysis

## The core problem: can we trust an LLM in the analytical pipeline?

The organizing anxiety across this cluster is not whether LLMs *can* classify political text — they clearly can — but whether and how that capability can be validated well enough to support inference. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] states the problem most directly: once LLMs are embedded at multiple stages of a pipeline (classification, embedding, cluster labeling), existing validation protocols built for single fine-tuned models no longer apply, because LLM outputs vary in granularity, cannot be adequately checked by crowdworkers who are now outperformed by the models themselves, and require bespoke, task-specific validation at every stage. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] supplies one empirical building block underneath that pipeline, showing that OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large outperforms the Italian-specific UmBERTo for clustering political news, and that clustering-algorithm choice (K-means vs. HDBSCAN) matters as much as the embedding model itself — a reminder that "the LLM" is never a single validation object but a chain of interacting choices. [[Marino2026-he]] shows what the validated pipeline looks like in production, applying LLM-based classification to 12 million Brazilian Facebook posts to track affective engagement dynamics, while [[Larsson2026-ro]] and [[Bailard2024-pj]] extend the same logic to sentiment classification of populist Facebook posts and DeBERTa-based framing classification of Proud Boys Telegram messages, respectively — both explicitly pairing automated classification with human validation as a condition of trust. [[Ober2026-vd]] pushes the human-in-the-loop principle into qualitative research, integrating topic modeling and LLM-assisted labeling with iterative human codebook refinement, arguing that transparency (via topic modeling) rather than pure LLM opacity should anchor hybrid workflows.

## What LLMs are good at, and where they break

A second cluster interrogates the boundaries of LLM competence directly, often via adversarial or out-of-distribution tests. [[Lee2026-je]] demonstrates a genuinely novel capability — LLMs can infer users' political alignment even from nonpolitical text, exploiting subtle culturally-politicized lexical cues, and outperform supervised baselines, raising acute privacy concerns alongside methodological promise. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] similarly shows LLMs are powerful persuasive agents, but reveals a persuasion–accuracy trade-off: the very levers (information density, post-training) that make models persuasive also degrade the factual accuracy of what they say — a finding with direct implications for any pipeline that uses LLMs to generate rather than merely classify text. Two papers stress-test factual and pragmatic competence and find real limits: [[DeVerna2025-dl]] shows that LLMs, even with reasoning and web search, fact-check political claims poorly unless given curated retrieval context, and exhibit a leftward skew in the sources they cite; [[Paci2025-ag]] shows LLMs struggle to interpret implicatures and presuppositions in Italian political speech, falling well short of human-expert ceilings despite chain-of-thought prompting. [[Brown2025-jk]] complicates the bias narrative further: demographic disagreement between LLM and human annotators is small and dataset-specific rather than systematic, while item difficulty (annotator disagreement itself) is by far the strongest predictor of LLM-human agreement — suggesting fairness audits of LLM annotation should center task difficulty, not just model choice.

## Beyond text: embeddings, vision, and narrative as measurement problems

A methodologically adjacent set of papers treats embeddings and multimodal representations themselves as objects requiring critique and innovation. [[Fan2025-ut]] shows that pretrained text embeddings encode confounds (source, language) that bias similarity measures, and that linear concept erasure (LEACE) can remove this contamination cheaply and generally — a foundational fix for any downstream clustering work in this topic area, including the Italian news-clustering studies above. [[Arminio2025-tw]] extends the representational question to images, showing that vision-language models generating connotative textual descriptions produce more socially meaningful (if less denotatively precise) clusters than CNN pipelines, with the added benefit of TF-IDF interpretability. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] similarly finds multimodal LLMs (GPT-4o) outperforming specialized computer-vision models for face recognition and person-counting in campaign imagery, applying the validated method to substantive questions about candidate visibility. On the narrative side, [[Waight2025-al]] and [[Elfes2026-jb]] both argue that existing text-as-data methods (topic modeling, embeddings, exact reuse) are the wrong estimand for narrative-level phenomena, proposing LLM-based claim-distillation and actantial-role extraction respectively to recover "who did what to whom" structures — with Waight validating against hand-coded gold standards and Elfes applying Greimas's actantial model to reveal that YouTube comments homogenize surface narratives on Israel-Palestine while deeper motifs remain partisan. [[Sarmiento2025-as]] and [[Arora2025-tx]] gesture toward the same unsupervised, non-predefined-category ethos for framing analysis, one on social media polarization generally and one on multimodal news framing of gun violence. [[Bruns2025-fz]] proposes embedding-based "practice mapping" as an alternative to hairball-prone network visualization, extending the embedding logic from text to interaction structure itself.

## Scaling and positioning as a distinct application

A narrower thread treats LLMs as instruments for ideological measurement rather than classification per se. [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] shows that simply asking instruction-tuned LLMs to place texts on ideological dimensions and averaging responses reproduces established scaling benchmarks across languages, while [[Lai2024-to]] takes a non-LLM but conceptually adjacent route, recovering YouTube video ideology as a latent variable from Reddit cross-posting behavior — together suggesting multiple converging paths to the same measurement goal, LLM-prompted and behavior-based.

## Domain applications that stress-test the pipeline in the wild

Several papers apply LLM-in-the-loop methods to substantive puzzles, implicitly serving as further validation exercises. [[Bailard2024-pj]] links classified collective-action frames to offline violence via Granger causality; [[Goel2025-iq]] uses narrative-matching (adjacent to Waight's method) to show mainstream news is co-opted into misinformation ecosystems more than domain-level fake-news metrics suggest; [[Nguyen2026-vm]] combines computational and manual content analysis to trace agentic framings of AI itself in news coverage; [[Meher2025-qb]] fine-tunes Llama with QLoRA for conflict-event classification, emphasizing accessibility on consumer hardware as a democratizing methodological contribution; [[Szabo2026-rd]] and [[Matias2025-px]] push outward from classification toward intervention and participatory evaluation — an LLM-powered chatbot for misinformation inoculation, and an argument that lived-experience public involvement improves the *science* of AI evaluation, not just its legitimacy.

## The data beneath the models: access, quality, and infrastructural precarity

Running parallel to the modeling questions is a second, infrastructural strand asking whether the data being fed into these pipelines is trustworthy at all. [[Freelon2024-sc]] periodizes the "post-API age," documenting how platform data access has become permanently contingent on corporate and regulatory whim; [[Bruns2026-pn]] extends this into the present "clean room" era (Meta Content Library), arguing that access restrictions privilege quantitative, well-resourced, Global-North researchers and structurally exclude qualitative and cross-platform work. [[Rieder2025-ju]] and [[Ulloa2024-jm]] supply granular empirical audits of exactly how such infrastructures distort measurement — YouTube's search API forgets older content within weeks, while ex-situ web scraping of news content diverges sharply and non-randomly from in-situ browsing, both threatening the validity of any downstream LLM analysis built on such corpora. [[Peters2026-mo]] shows that even regulatory frameworks meant to guarantee access (the EU's DSA) leave "data quality" contested and under-specified, a political rather than purely technical construct. [[Allen2025-ot]] frames platform-independent, LLM-assisted experimentation (browser-extension reranking) as a methodological response to exactly this closing data environment.

## Reflexive and meta-level accounting

Finally, several papers step back to assess the field's trajectory itself. [[Balluff2026-if]] offers the most direct critique, cataloguing the epistemic, environmental, and ethical costs of unreflective LLM adoption and urging a trade-off mindset favoring smaller, open models where possible — a useful counterweight to the enthusiasm elsewhere in this collection. [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys the broader landscape of LLM-based annotation and synthesis methodologies, while [[Hartmann2025-px]] shows, in an adjacent domain (echo chambers), that much scholarly disagreement stems from inconsistent conceptualization and operationalization rather than empirical reality — a cautionary parallel for LLM-based measurement debates. [[Fan2026-af]] argues for a temporal reorientation of trace-data analysis using sequence-based and embedding methods. At the frontier, [[Alizadeh2026-es]] and [[Jayaram2026-wd]] turn the lens on science itself, testing whether AI coding agents can reproduce published social-science findings and whether agentic review tools can catch errors in manuscripts — extending LLM-in-the-loop validation concerns from content analysis to the practice of research and peer review itself, and closing the loop on the topic's central question: as LLMs move from object of study to instrument of study to evaluator of studies, what, exactly, validates the validator?
