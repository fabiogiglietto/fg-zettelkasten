# Generative AI & Media

## From verification to persuasion: a moving research front

Work in this cluster began, in the vera.ai tradition, with a fairly narrow question — can machines help us detect and verify manipulated media? — and has expanded into a much broader inquiry into how generative systems reshape the *entire* information environment: what counts as a fact, who persuades whom, and which visual, textual, and epistemic infrastructures underwrite public discourse. The papers filed here trace that widening arc.

## LLMs-in-the-loop for content analysis

A first thread treats LLMs as measurement instruments for social-scientific text and image analysis. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] articulates the general challenge — validating fully LLM-integrated pipelines requires expert coders and task-specific protocols — and [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] shows concretely how OpenAI embeddings outperform BERT-based alternatives for clustering Italian political news. Extensions push into new modalities and tasks: [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] finds GPT-4o beats specialised computer-vision pipelines for candidate detection on Instagram, [[Arminio2025-tw]] uses VLLMs to move from denotative to *connotative* image clustering, [[Le-Mens2025-qz]] scales political texts by "asking and averaging", [[DiGiuseppe2025-es]] proposes LLM-paired-comparisons for open-ended survey responses, and [[Waight2025-al]] shows that LLM-distilled claims capture cross-lingual narrative similarity in ways lexical methods miss entirely. [[Elfes2026-jb]] takes a further step by operationalising Greimas' actantial model via an LLM to measure *narrative polarisation*. Together these papers make a cumulative case: prompting-and-averaging pipelines can produce validated measurements previously inaccessible to computational social science.

Yet the same thread carries built-in warnings. [[Balluff2026-if]] argues much of this adoption is unreflective — reproducibility suffers under corporate model churn, smaller encoders often suffice, and synthetic data introduces demographic distortion. [[Brown2025-jk]] finds that dataset-level item difficulty dwarfs demographic bias in LLM annotation. [[Paci2025-ag]] shows that pragmatic reasoning (implicature, presupposition) in Italian political speech remains far from human ceilings. [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys the broader annotation-and-synthesis landscape. [[Alizadeh2026-es]] extends the frame all the way to reproduction of published findings, showing that coding agents can now replicate most computational social science — but are also susceptible to sycophantic specification search when nudged toward "confirmatory" answers.

## Fact-checking, verification, and the epistemology of the "fact"

A second thread confronts fact-checking directly. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] delivers the strongest empirical result: standard LLMs, even reasoning- or search-enabled, fail at political fact-checking, but curated RAG raises F1 by ~233%. Access to verified evidence, not model capability, is the bottleneck. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] pushes this into philosophy of knowledge, arguing GenAI outputs constitute a new epistemic category — *emergent facts* — that neither positivist, constructivist, nor institutional accounts of factuality can accommodate. [[Emilio2026-ik]] widens the aperture again: the deeper risk is not any single fake but layered "synthetic realities" and a *generative AI paradox* whereby rational discounting of all digital evidence itself becomes the harm. [[Choi2026-bz]] adds a psychological mechanism — a modality-congruent carryover effect in how audiences authenticate deepfakes.

## Synthetic visual persuasion

Closely related is the emerging thread on synthetic visual persuasion. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] documents a 13,000% post-volume surge in coordinated gambling promotion after ChatGPT's launch, with GenAI intensifying rather than replacing established persuasion drivers (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, FOMO, celebrity endorsements). [[Hameleers2026-mc]] provides an important counterweight: AI-generated visual disinformation is not universally more credible than text, and low-tech decontextualised video often outperforms it — the threat of GenAI may be overstated relative to older manipulation techniques. [[Manovich2026-ih]] steps back to theorise generative AI as a *medium* with structural conservatism, "media cognition", and entangled style/content.

## Persuasion at scale

Perhaps the most striking convergence concerns LLM persuasion. [[Costello2024-bg]] launched this line by showing GPT-4 dialogues durably reduce conspiracy beliefs. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] then systematised the "levers", finding that *information density* — not scale or personalization — is the key mechanism, and that persuasion trades off against accuracy. [[Lin2025-xp]] extends the finding to real elections in the US, Canada, Poland, and to ballot measures, showing effects that exceed traditional ads and are driven mainly by facts rather than psychological manipulation. [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] delivers the capstone: AI now out-persuades even elite competitive debaters and professional canvassers, again via information throughput. But the picture is more contingent than a simple capabilities story: [[DiGiuseppe2026-pu]] shows that a mere warning about political bias attenuates persuasion by ~25%, meaning perceived neutrality is itself a lever. [[Lee2026-je]] documents the flip side — LLMs can infer political alignment from ostensibly nonpolitical talk, opening scalable microtargeting pathways.

## Agentic threats and geopolitical bias

Zooming out further, [[Schroeder2026-im]] warns that LLM-plus-multi-agent architectures enable *malicious AI swarms* that fabricate consensus, and [[Orlando2025-ul]] provides the first empirical demonstration that even minimal teammate awareness produces emergent coordination among LLM agents in simulated information operations. [[Waight2026-ts]] shows that state-controlled media leaks into training data and causes commercial LLMs to produce more pro-regime responses when queried in low-media-freedom languages — a distinct pathway of institutional influence. [[Triedman2025-uy]] catalogues how Grokipedia amplifies unreliable and blacklisted sources especially on political topics, an emerging case of ideologically shaped AI knowledge infrastructure.

## Journalism, imaginaries, and public perception

The final thread reframes generative AI as a media-cultural phenomenon in its own right. [[Nguyen2026-vm]] and [[Wang2025-zy]] map how news outlets frame LLMs and generative AI across countries, finding that a techno-capitalist master frame dominates while mentalistic-agentic framings serve multiple rhetorical functions. [[Dodds2026-df]] and [[Stanusch2026-ec]] theorise AI *hype* as a structuring infrastructure — with the AI industry using "premediation" and "preclusion" to occupy futures. [[Hepp2026-oi]] locates the reservoir of these imaginaries in Silicon Valley's "quiet futuring" among Effective Altruist and Rationalist communities. [[Weinbrand2026-sf]] examines how Google's AI Overviews are discursively constructed as both commercial platform and public information infrastructure. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] provides the widest reframing of all — arguing that social media itself is dissolving into three post-social formations, one of which is AI-mediated communication, requiring new methods and concepts.

Downstream reception studies complete the picture: [[Gilardi2026-hw]] finds readers cannot distinguish AI-written from human-written news, but disclosure spurs short-term curiosity without long-term acceptance; [[Suk2026-ai]] proposes a multilevel framework for trust in generative AI; [[Gottfried2026-ww]] documents that half of US adults now use chatbots even as majorities remain skeptical about AI's societal effects. [[Larsson2026-ro]] shows LLM-assisted content analysis is now viable for longitudinal, non-English political communication research.

## The connecting arc

Read together, these papers describe a field in transition. What began as verification tooling (curated RAG, embeddings, multimodal classifiers) has become an inquiry into the joint reconfiguration of measurement, persuasion, and epistemology. The recurring tension is between capability and legitimacy: LLMs are simultaneously the most powerful text-analytic instruments social scientists have ever had ([[Waight2025-al]], [[Alizadeh2026-es]]) *and* the most powerful persuasive agents we have ever built ([[Hackenburg2026-ud]], [[Lin2025-xp]]); simultaneously indispensable to fact-checking pipelines ([[DeVerna2025-dl]]) *and* producers of a new class of "emergent facts" that destabilise the very concept ([[Dierickx2026-tw]], [[Emilio2026-ik]]). The forward research programme, taken as a whole, is about learning to hold both sides of that tension methodologically, epistemically, and politically.
