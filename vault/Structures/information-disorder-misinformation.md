# Information Disorder & Misinformation

## From Fake News to Information Disorder: A Field Redefining Itself

The corpus begins, in a sense, with a definitional crisis. [[Giglietto2019-e9be81c1]] argues that the term "fake news" is itself a rhetorical trap — a creator-centric category that cannot capture how falsehood actually circulates through a hybrid news system of injectors, propagators, and audiences making situated truth-judgments. That skepticism about single-cause, single-actor definitions becomes a throughline: [[Mosca2026-yh]] shows how "fake news" accusations function less as descriptions of content than as rhetorical weapons of delegitimization in Italian political discourse, while [[Yoo2026-ev]] finds the same weaponization on the American hyperpartisan right and left, with the term reshaping Hallin's spheres of consensus and deviance rather than referring to any stable phenomenon. [[Sadler2025-vu]] pushes furthest, arguing that disinformation scholarship's own use of "narrative" has been analytically naive, and proposing a hermeneutic-realist alternative in which stories can be referentially and ethically suspect without being simply "false." Together these pieces mark a shift away from binary true/false accounting toward attention to strategic communicative function — a shift that anchors much of what follows.

## The Narrative Turn

A cluster of papers converges on narrative, rather than the discrete false claim, as the proper unit of analysis. [[Marwick2026-qd]] shows that successful disinformation behaves like cross-platform folklore — characters, catchphrases, cathartic payoffs — which is why domain-blocklists and fact-checking struggle to contain it. [[Suau_Martinez2026-lv]] and [[Goel2025-iq]] extend this empirically: repeated cross-outlet exposure to a narrative predicts belief better than any single claim, and — crucially — mainstream outlets' own true content is routinely repurposed to sustain misleading narratives, meaning source-level trust metrics miss a large share of the phenomenon. [[Waight2025-al]] supplies the methodological infrastructure this turn needs, an LLM-based pipeline for measuring "narrative similarity" that outperforms text-reuse and topic-modeling baselines, applied to trace how the Russian "biolabs" narrative bled from state media into fringe and even mainstream US outlets. Election-integrity scholarship extends the same logic: [[Starbird2025-jj]] and [[Prochaska2025-ef]] read election-fraud rumors as evidence-frame interactions and "deep stories" collaboratively performed by influencers and audiences on Twitter, where the *how* of telling is as constitutive as the *what*. [[Ramos2026-qo]], written from inside the Spanish fact-checker Maldita.es, operationalizes this insight as practice — proposing "argument-checking" and "narrative monitoring" as necessary supplements to claim-level fact-checking, illustrated through the Valencia flood conspiracy.

## Coordination, Networks, and Platform-Scale Manipulation

A parallel empirical tradition maps *how* problematic content is organized and amplified, largely through coordinated link/account behavior. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] — the MINE-FACTS project — is a hub text here: it operationalizes Coordinated Link Sharing Behaviour via the CooRnet tool to surface Italian COVID-skeptic networks faster than manual fact-checking, documenting tactics like link-laundering and first-comment link-hiding. [[Giada2026-fc9a3833]] generalizes this into "Deceptive Information Operations," a three-dimensional framework (deceptive content, platform-affordance exploitation, coordination) spanning pro-Putin propaganda, gambling scams, and adult-content networks united by a shared "Zombie Internet" logic that current CIB/CSH categories cannot see. [[Thiele2025-ol]] theorizes the attribution problem these detection efforts face, proposing a rational-choice typology linking campaign scale/elaborateness/disguise to a principal's stakes and resources. [[Rodriguez_Farres2025-sg]] extends detection to Bluesky, while [[Kulichkina2026-zk]] and [[Rothut2026-or]] examine coordination's protest-adjacent, "dual-use" character — Twitter activity around China's COVID protests, and Querdenken's Telegram-based mainstreaming of far-right frames. [[noauthor_undated-bm]]'s large Meta-partnered study of 49 deceptive networks around the 2020 US election complicates the picture: reach is real but concentrated, non-network resharers matter more than the networks themselves, and causal effects on beliefs mostly wash out under adjustment — a caution echoed by [[Bergeron-Boutin2026-yh]], which finds concentrated but attitudinally inert exposure to untrustworthy Facebook/Instagram sources, and by [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]], whose SIRVA epidemic model instead finds a measurable, if modest, causal effect of antivaccine tweets on vaccination refusal.

## Sensitive Narratives: War, Health, Elections

The monitoring of specific high-stakes narrative domains — the project's PROMPT-style core — recurs across contexts. On the **Ukraine war**: [[Sadler2025-vu]]'s NATO-blame case study, [[Waight2025-al]]'s biolabs tracing, [[Bosch2024-hj]]'s account of TikTok sound as a novel propaganda vector, [[Graham2025-gp]]'s #IStandWithPutin case on propaganda exploiting "the infrastructure of truth," [[Makeev2026-ma]]'s theorization of dual domestic/foreign propaganda objectives, [[Karlsson2026-hd]]'s finding that mere media narratives *about* election interference can polarize independent of the interference's actual efficacy, and [[Rogers2026-cy]]'s "post-truth spaces" mapping of Moldovan Facebook discourse — together suggest war-adjacent disinformation is simultaneously a content, platform, and meta-discursive problem. On **health**: [[Adam2026-tz]]'s panel/web-tracking study of COVID conspiracy contagion and mitigation, [[Scalco2026-bd]]'s operationalization of "information voids" around vaccine rollouts, [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]]'s finding that UK press coverage of COVID scientists (not the scientists' own polarization) tracks outlet politics, [[Efstratiou2026-ij]]'s mapping of coordinated contrarian amplification of credentialed experts, and [[Lyons2026-ca]]'s tracking-data finding that low-credibility health content, though rare overall, concentrates sharply among older adults — echoing the age-skew long observed in political misinformation. On **elections**: [[Rossini2026-jn]] and [[Rossini2026-mj]] use Brazilian panel data to show belief in electoral fraud claims erodes both tolerance and institutional trust even as mainstream news use buffers it; [[Van_Erkel2026-mk]] identifies a "hostile misinformation effect" — a hostile-media-effect analogue in which partisans perceive their own side as disproportionately targeted; [[Renault2025-uh]] shows Community Notes still flags Republican posts more than Democratic ones, undercutting claims that crowdsourcing eliminates partisan asymmetry; and [[Ventura2026-yc]] / [[Ventura2025-sw]] extend the frame to platform-level shocks (Brazil's X ban, a WhatsApp deactivation experiment), showing durable partisan "sorting ratchets" from state intervention itself.

## Political Elites, Parties, and the Populism-Ideology Puzzle

A distinct strand asks not who consumes misinformation but who *produces and legitimates* it. [[Tornberg2025-ir]]'s 26-country parliamentary Twitter analysis lands on the field's cleanest causal claim: neither populism nor right-wing ideology alone predicts elite misinformation sharing, but their interaction — radical-right populism specifically — does. [[Knupfer2025-vt]]'s "logic of connective faction" traces how the sudden CRT controversy let ideologically extreme Republicans build reciprocal ties to hyper-partisan media, converting engagement into intra-party leverage; [[Tai2026-qk]] examines institutional/ideological "checks" on elected officials' sharing more generally. [[Rodarte2026-dk]] documents Brazilian parliamentarians actively contesting journalistic gatekeeping through derivative reporting, direct-authority claims, and local brokerage during the Manaus crisis, while [[Kalsnes2025-zb]] and [[Anwar2024-34dba628]] show how populist parties' Facebook posts systematically harvest "Angry" reactions that convert into shares — an affective infrastructure for elite-driven virality. [[Copland2025-em]], [[Iris2026-pg]], and [[Gattermann2025-yx]] extend the politics-of-visibility question transnationally: Sky News Australia's dual domestic/international propaganda strategy, disproportionate European news coverage of Radical Right parties relative to their actual electoral strength, and the link between far-right electoral performance and voter disinformation-concern.

## Vernacular Belief, Identity, and the Far-Right Pipeline

Several ethnographically-inflected papers examine belief formation as identity work rather than information deficit. [[Marwick2025-vx]] reframes far-right "redpilling" as gradual socialization rather than sudden conversion, with disinformation functioning as "evidence" that legitimates a self-image of rational, scientific skepticism. [[Marwick2025-ov]] complicates the demographic stereotype of the conspiracy theorist entirely, showing ConspiracyTok's young, non-white, often female creators deploying a "generous epistemology" that is simultaneously standpoint-empowering and disinformation-vulnerable. [[Hollingshead2026-vx]] finds, against expectations from prior Twitter-based immigration research, that popular Canadian TikTok content skews pro-immigration, with opposing camps deploying the platform's affordances symmetrically — suggesting platform-specific vernaculars matter as much as ideology.

## Generative AI and the Coming Synthetic Reality

A newer cluster addresses how large language models and generative media reshape the whole problem space. [[Emilio2026-ik]] offers the most totalizing frame — "synthetic reality" as a four-layer stack (content, identity, interaction, institutions) producing an "epistemic tax" as societies rationally discount all digital evidence. [[Waight2026-ts]] demonstrates a subtler mechanism: state media control seeps into LLM training data and produces measurably more pro-regime outputs in the languages of less-free-media countries — propaganda laundered through apparent algorithmic neutrality. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] finds LLMs are poor fact-checkers absent curated retrieval, with search-enabled models citing credible-but-left-leaning sources; [[Dierickx2026-tw]] proposes "emergent facts" as a needed epistemic category between evidence-based, interpretive, and institutional factuality. On the audience side, [[Wack2026-bt]]'s Kenyan deepfake experiment shows video authenticity judgments are "socially negotiated" through surrounding comments rather than fixed at production, moderated by partisanship; [[Choi2026-bz]] identifies a modality-congruent carryover effect in authentication difficulty; and [[Hameleers2026-mc]] tempers alarm about visual/AI disinformation, finding decontextualized real video — not AI-generated stills — is the more persuasive format, and only for high-salience, high-footage-availability events like the Ukraine war.

## Measurement, Methodology, and the "Overstated Harms" Debate

A self-critical, largely quantitative strand interrogates the field's own evidentiary basis. [[Budak2024-ef]] crystallizes the corrective argument: public discourse overstates prevalence, algorithmic causation, and societal harm relative to the evidence; [[Bakshy2015-rn]], [[Gonzalez-Bailon2024-rq]], and [[Mosleh2024-op]] supply foundational and cross-platform exposure data supporting a "concentrated among a minority" reading, echoed later by [[Bergeron-Boutin2026-yh]] and [[Lyons2026-ca]]. Methodological reflexivity runs throughout: [[Giglietto2022-b30e8b4e]] exposes how Meta's 100-share anonymization threshold itself manufactures spurious cross-country trend similarity; [[Rossi2023-847d5a9f]] and [[Vincent_undated-re]] extend Guess-style newsfeed quality measurement to Europe and to repeated platform monitoring; [[Luhring2025-od]] audits NewsGuard's own stability and US-skew as a scholarly instrument; [[Nenno2025-xa]] and [[Humprecht2025-ml]] press the WEIRD-bias critique, showing news-value patterns in flagged misinformation diverge across WEIRD/non-WEIRD contexts; [[Hartmann2025-px]] performs the same service for echo-chamber research, arguing definitional heterogeneity — not empirical reality — drives much scholarly disagreement; and [[Oswald2025-km]] names the underlying distortion for both citizens and researchers as a "production-consumption gap." [[Spampatti2026-kx]] and [[Frischlich2025-vn]] argue the field needs better theory to match this data, importing lessons from climate psychology and complex-systems thinking respectively, while [[van-Eck2026-xg]], [[Esau2025-tf]], and [[Falkenberg2026-ka]] make parallel arguments about polarization scholarship's own normative and geographic parochialism.

## Platform Power, Industry Capture, and Governance

Running beneath the empirical debates is a growing concern with who controls the evidence base itself. [[Bak-Coleman2026-mk]] and [[Bak-Coleman2025-pm]] document, with tobacco-and-fossil-fuel analogies, how industry ties saturate high-profile social media research — often undisclosed, concentrated among a small elite of scientists, and skewing topics toward user-level rather than platform-level questions. [[Pierri2025-hm]] and [[Giglietto2026-855a54cb]] track the regulatory response, the EU's Digital Services Act Article 40, as a fragile, unevenly implemented attempt to convert platform data access from discretionary "philanthropy" into an enforceable right — situated within a longer arc "from the Wild West to the Walled Garden." [[Hurcombe2025-cs]] shows how Meta's own Newsroom discourse pre-empts this scrutiny, framing problematic content as originating from "outside" actors while excluding hyperpartisan verified accounts and Facebook Groups from blame. [[Cazzamatta2026-lo]] and [[Farkas2026-lr]] turn to the fact-checkers caught between these forces: the former finds Zuckerberg's post-2025 "censorship" framing empirically unsupported by actual removal rates, the latter documents fact-checkers' rhetorical apologia for their own platform-funding dependence. [[Donovan2025-ws]] historicizes the whole arc, arguing "misinformation-at-scale" is not a bug but a structural feature of engagement-driven business models that platforms addressed only opportunistically before retreating after backlash.

## Interventions and Their Limits

Finally, a smaller applied cluster tests remedies. [[van-der-Linden2026-jt]] demonstrates inoculation ("prebunking") against emotional manipulation can scale cheaply as an Instagram ad with persisting effects; [[Lieu2025-nl]] shows climate misinformation's persuasiveness depends more on content category (attacks on solutions) than on logical-fallacy type, informing where debunking effort should concentrate; and [[Xue2025-bp]] finds fact-checkers themselves lean on emotional framing despite the genre's objectivity norms — a reminder that even corrective communication is subject to the narrative and platform logics this whole body of work has spent a decade documenting. [[Rohrbach2026-rc]] closes the loop by asking whether journalism's own strategies for covering conspiracy sometimes *manufacture* the very beliefs they aim to correct — a fittingly recursive final note for a field whose central discovery may be that information disorder is not an aberration from, but a structural feature of, the hybrid media system itself.
