# LLMs in Computational Content Analysis

## From Enthusiasm to Protocol: The Methodological Turn

The papers in this topic trace a visible arc: from early demonstrations that LLMs *can* do content-analytic work, toward increasingly rigorous and skeptical protocols for establishing that they do it *well*. [[Tan2024-vl]] surveys the baseline opportunity — LLMs as flexible annotators and data synthesizers — while [[Balluff2026-if]] supplies the counter-essay, cataloguing the epistemic, environmental, and reproducibility costs of treating LLMs as a default tool rather than a deliberate methodological choice. Between these poles sit a cluster of papers that take the question seriously by building and stress-testing actual pipelines. [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] is paradigmatic: an "LLMs-in-the-loop" pipeline for classifying, embedding, and labeling political Facebook content across two Italian elections, accompanied by a three-phase validation protocol and a frank discussion of why crowdsourced validation (MTurk, Fiverr) is no longer adequate once LLMs outperform low-skilled human raters. [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] extends this logic into qualitative deductive coding, reframing LLM–human divergence not as error but as a diagnostic for ambiguity in theoretical codebooks (e.g., exposing that "morality" framing implicitly presumes explicit normative language, a presumption that may be historically contingent). [[Ober2026-vd]] similarly proposes a human-in-the-loop hybrid of topic modeling and LLM labeling for interview transcripts, explicitly valuing topic modeling's mathematical transparency as a check on LLM opacity. Together these papers converge on a shared methodological stance: LLMs are best understood as collaborators requiring task-specific validation at every pipeline stage, not monolithic replacements for human judgment.

A second validation front concerns domain adaptation and knowledge limits. [[Meher2025-qb]]'s ConflLlama shows that parameter-efficient fine-tuning (QLoRA) can turn a general-purpose LLM into a strong, resource-light conflict-event classifier, substantially beating zero-shot baselines — but only after tailored fine-tuning. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] delivers a sobering parallel finding for fact-checking: reasoning and web search alone barely improve LLM veracity judgments, while curated retrieval-augmented context yields a 233% average F1 gain, with search-enabled models showing a tilt toward left-leaning (if credible) sources. [[Paci2025-ag]] pushes further into the limits of LLM competence, showing that even strong models fall well short of expert human interpretation of implicatures and presuppositions in Italian political speech — a reminder that surface-level classification accuracy does not imply pragmatic understanding. Read together, these three papers argue that validation is not a one-off benchmark exercise but an ongoing negotiation between model capability, task specificity, and the quality of the evidence or context supplied.

## Embedding Choices and What They Encode

A distinct sub-literature compares embedding models and interrogates what semantic space they actually represent. [[Giglietto2024-cbeb3f70]] directly compares OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large against the Italian-specific UmBERTo for clustering political news, finding the LLM-derived embedding consistently more coherent — a finding echoed and operationalized in [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]]'s pipeline. [[Arminio2025-tw]] extends the embedding-comparison question into the visual domain, showing that vision-language models that first produce textual descriptions of images, then embed and cluster those descriptions, capture *connotative* meaning (ideology, symbolism) that CNN-based pipelines systematically miss, at only a small cost to denotative coherence. [[Fan2025-ut]] complicates the naive embed-and-cluster workflow further by showing that pretrained embeddings carry confounding signal from document source and language, and that linear concept erasure (LEACE) can remove this "medium" signal cheaply and without degrading out-of-distribution performance — a methodological corrective directly relevant to any study pooling multi-source or multilingual text, as in [[Waight2025-al]]'s cross-lingual narrative-similarity pipeline, which itself distills documents into claims via LLM summarization before embedding-based comparison, outperforming text reuse, topic modeling, and Relatio baselines on recall and precision alike.

Embeddings are not neutral mirrors of content; they can also encode attributes researchers did not intend to measure. [[Bouchaud2026-lr]] demonstrates this starkly outside the research pipeline altogether, showing that X's recommender system embeds users' ideological position as a near-linear, highly predictive direction, orthogonal to most demographic attributes — a finding that resonates with [[Lee2026-je]]'s demonstration that LLMs can infer political alignment from ostensibly nonpolitical text by exploiting subtle lexical and cultural cues, and with [[Lai2024-to]]'s use of Reddit-sharing behavior to scale the ideology of YouTube videos. Across this cluster, embedding space emerges as a site of both methodological opportunity (better clustering, better scaling) and epistemic risk (unintended profiling, confounding, privacy exposure).

## Human–Machine Disagreement in Annotation

A third strand interrogates where and why LLM annotations diverge from human coders, and what that divergence means for downstream inference. [[Brown2025-jk]] offers the most systematic test: across four contentious annotation datasets and three LLMs, demographic bias in LLM–human agreement is small, dataset-specific rather than model-specific, and overwhelmed in explanatory power by item difficulty (label entropy) — a finding that reframes "LLM bias" debates around task ambiguity rather than model pathology. [[Gomez-Zara2026-as]] and [[Marino2024-2fbc690f]] both treat divergence as informative rather than simply erroneous: in the former, LLM–human mismatches surface latent theoretical assumptions in framing codebooks; in the latter, the variable granularity of LLM-generated cluster narratives (from broad topic to single news story) forces researchers to build multi-level coherence rubrics. [[Paci2025-ag]] adds a cautionary data point from pragmatics, showing that even an LLM-as-judge evaluator rates model outputs more harshly than human experts do, while [[Elfes2026-jb]] reports the opposite reassurance for narrative annotation — LLM–human agreement on actantial roles in YouTube discourse about the Israeli–Palestinian conflict is comparable to human–human agreement, exceeding prior LLM narrative-annotation benchmarks. The throughline is that disagreement is not a uniform signal of failure; its diagnostic value depends on task type, and good validation designs exploit rather than suppress it.

## Scaling Political Content Analysis

A large group of papers puts these methods to work on substantive political-communication questions, each implicitly validating LLM-based classification against a research payoff. [[Bailard2024-pj]] fine-tunes DeBERTa to classify collective-action frames in over 500,000 Proud Boys Telegram messages, linking online framing dynamics to offline violence via Granger causality. [[Larsson2026-ro]] uses GPT-4 zero-shot classification (validated against human coders) to track a decade of sentiment in Norwegian Progress Party Facebook posts, finding negative content reliably drives shares and comments but not likes. [[Achmann-Denkler2026-lx]] and [[Arora2025-tx]] extend content analysis into the visual and multimodal register — GPT-4o outperforming specialized computer-vision models for face recognition and person-counting in German campaign Instagram content, and multimodal framing extraction revealing editorial choices invisible to text-only analysis of U.S. gun-violence coverage. [[Sarmiento2025-as]] and [[Elfes2026-jb]] push framing analysis toward unsupervised and narrative-theoretic territory, the latter introducing "narrative polarisation" as a construct distinct from opinion or interaction-based polarization and showing that video content is far more polarized than the comments beneath it. [[Waight2025-al]] rounds out this cluster methodologically by formalizing "narrative similarity" as a distinct estimand from topical or lexical overlap, with direct substantive payoff in tracing the cross-lingual diffusion of Russian disinformation narratives about Ukrainian "biolabs."

## LLMs as Political Actors, Not Just Analytic Tools

A final conceptual move in this literature treats LLMs not merely as instruments for analyzing political content but as agents that themselves shape political discourse — raising validation questions of a different kind. [[Waight2026-ts]] shows that state media control is "laundered" into LLM training data, producing measurably pro-regime outputs correlated with national media-freedom scores. [[Nguyen2026-vm]] examines how news media frame LLMs themselves, finding mentalistic-agentic coverage is real but neither dominant nor regionally uniform. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]], [[Costello2024-bg]], and [[Kotz2026-lk]] form a persuasion triptych: conversational AI's persuasiveness is driven chiefly by information density and post-training rather than scale or personalization, but the same levers that increase persuasion decrease factual accuracy ([[Hackenburg2025-dj]]); tailored, evidence-dense AI dialogue durably reduces conspiracy belief ([[Costello2024-bg]]); and this persuasive capacity generalizes across climate, vaccination, and inequality domains, working best on skeptics ([[Kotz2026-lk]]). [[Allen2025-ot]] situates one such intervention — content reranking via LLM-detection of antidemocratic-attitude language — within the broader methodological landscape of platform-independent experimentation, necessitated precisely because direct platform collaboration is now rare. [[Miller2026-cp]] closes this thread with an applied governance case: X's Community Notes AI Note Writer API, where AI-generated fact-checking notes are shown to be additive to, not substitutive for, human-rated notes, preserving human authority over what gets amplified.

## Infrastructure, Reproducibility, and the Shrinking Data Commons

Underlying all of the above is a shifting data-access environment that these papers repeatedly cite as both motivation and constraint. [[Freelon2018-ao]] names the "post-API age" inaugurated by Facebook's 2018 API closure; [[Yang2026-tq]], using an LLM-annotation pipeline on 59 journals, documents the empirical consequence — a plateau and decline in social-media-based social science research after 2022 as platform access tightened. [[Peters2026-mo]] and [[Bruns2026-pn]] turn to the EU's Digital Services Act and Meta's clean-room infrastructure respectively, both finding that formal "access" is not equivalent to usable access, with clean rooms in particular disadvantaging qualitative and cross-platform research and widening global resource inequities. [[Zheng2026-bi]] offers an infrastructural remedy, building random-sampling tools (TubeStats/TokStats) to correct the systematic bias of opportunistic platform samples. [[Ulloa2024-jm]] and [[Stagnaro2025-pz]] extend the same quality-control impulse to other measurement layers — showing that the collection environment of web-tracking (in-situ vs. scraped) and the choice of opt-in survey panel each introduce systematic, non-random distortions that mirror the annotation-validity concerns raised elsewhere. [[Bruns2025-fz]] proposes embedding-based "practice mapping" as a way past the visualization limits of conventional network analysis for multimodal platform interactions. Finally, [[Alizadeh2026-es]] closes the loop on reproducibility itself, finding that frontier AI coding agents can reproduce a strikingly high share of published social-science computational findings — while also documenting that confirmatory prompting induces sycophantic specification search, a direct echo of the persuasion-accuracy trade-off identified in [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] and the broader caution urged by [[Balluff2026-if]]. Taken as a whole, this topic's papers argue that the promise of LLM-based content analysis cannot be assessed independently of the data infrastructure, validation discipline, and institutional incentives within which these pipelines are built.
