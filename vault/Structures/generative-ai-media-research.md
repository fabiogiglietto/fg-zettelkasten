# Generative AI in Media & Disinformation

## The arc of inquiry

This cluster traces a single strand of my own current work — coordinated persuasion in gambling promotion, [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] — outward into the wider question of what generative AI is doing to media, persuasion, and truth, and inward into how LLMs can themselves be harnessed as research instruments. The papers filed here move from *empirical mechanism* (how persuasive/deceptive AI content actually works and how much it matters) through *epistemic reframing* (what "fact," "trust," and "truth" even mean once synthetic content is ambient) to *structural and governance* diagnoses (what kind of media ecosystem this leaves us with, and who should be accountable). The new "IA Generativa e Media" course is essentially this note made syllabus.

## Mechanisms: how synthetic persuasion actually works

The empirical core is a set of studies that refuse to treat "AI persuasion" as a monolith and instead isolate its levers. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] is the anchor case: a hybrid LLM/embedding/clustering pipeline applied to 2,300+ images from coordinated Facebook gambling groups shows that generative AI does not invent new persuasion strategies so much as *intensify and recombine* familiar ones (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, FOMO, celebrity endorsement, cultural localization) at industrial scale, with an exponential, dateable jump in volume right after ChatGPT's public launch. This is a domain-specific instance of a pattern the political-persuasion literature makes general: [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] and [[Lin2025-xp]] both find, across huge experimental samples, that conversational AI's persuasive power rests less on model scale or personalization than on *information density* — the sheer volume of fact-checkable claims a chatbot can marshal in dialogue — and that this density-driven persuasiveness comes bundled with a trade-off against factual accuracy, an asymmetry that in [[Lin2025-xp]] even breaks along partisan lines (AI advocating right-leaning candidates is systematically less accurate). Read together with [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]], this suggests a general "engineering" logic: whether the goal is winning an argument or selling a bet, generative AI's advantage is throughput and recombination, not qualitatively novel rhetorical tricks.

Two papers complicate any assumption that synthetic content is automatically more effective than older tactics. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] finds that AI-generated still images do *not* outperform decontextualized real video, and that visual disinformation's edge over text is issue-dependent — contingent on whether ample authentic footage exists to exploit. [[Orlando2025-ul]] shifts from persuasion-of-humans to persuasion-*among*-agents, using generative agent-based modeling to show that networked LLM agents spontaneously reproduce the coordination signatures of real information operations (narrative convergence, synchronized amplification, hashtag adoption) once given mere mutual awareness — a finding that resonates with the "coordinated" architecture Giglietto's gambling networks exhibit, but generated bottom-up rather than observed post-hoc.

## Reception and context: persuasion is negotiated, not fixed

A counter-current insists that the effect of synthetic content is not settled at the point of production. [[Wack2026-bt]], set in Kenya's Ruto–Odinga rivalry, shows that a deepfake's impact is "socially negotiated" through surrounding comments and partisanship — credulous peer framing amplifies harm, skeptical framing partially neutralizes it. [[Hollingshead2026-vx]] similarly finds that TikTok's affordances are used near-identically by pro- and anti-immigration creators, with generative AI surfacing only at the margins as a tool for racist "speculative worldbuilding" — a reminder that platform vernacular, not the technology per se, often determines valence. Together with [[Hameleers2026-mc]]'s issue-contingent findings, these papers argue against technologically deterministic readings of GenAI harm — a corrective explicitly generalized by [[Beacken2026-zb]], whose six-country comparison (Bolivia, Hungary, India, Nigeria, Tunisia, Turkey) shows GenAI propaganda adoption tracking local political-economic structure rather than any uniform trajectory.

## Epistemics: what happens to "fact" and "trust"

A second cluster asks what generative ubiquity does to the very categories fact-checking and journalism trust rely on. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] argues that GenAI outputs are "emergent facts" — plausible, context-dependent, opaque recombinations that fit none of fact-checking's traditional categories (evidence-based, interpretative, rule-based) and require relational rather than binary verification. [[Emilio2026-ik]] scales this up into the "Generative AI Paradox": the risk is not discrete deepfakes but layered "synthetic realities" (content, identity, interaction, institutions) that make rational actors discount *all* digital evidence, raising the cost of truth itself and demanding a shift from artifact-authentication toward institutional "epistemic security." [[Nguyen2026-vm]] shows how news media themselves mediate this epistemic terrain, mapping mentalistic-agentic framings of LLMs in journalism as neither uniformly hype nor uniformly critical, but organizationally variable — journalism as a site where the public imaginary of AI is contested rather than simply transmitted.

Audience-side studies test how these epistemic anxieties cash out in trust. [[Suk2026-ai]] proposes a multilevel (individual/institutional/societal) framework borrowing from mass- and social-media trust research to theorize trust in GenAI as a distinct object; [[Mattis2026-gu]] and [[Sbaraini-Fontes2026-cw]] supply comparable empirical grounding — Dutch audiences penalize *every* task-specific AI disclosure in journalism, most severely for fact-checking, while Italian audiences trust AI-generated search results more than AI-produced news, pointing to search intermediaries as an under-scrutinized locus of algorithmic trust. Read against [[Dierickx2026-tw]] and [[Emilio2026-ik]], these studies suggest that "trust erosion" is not abstract: it is measurably task-, platform-, and disclosure-specific.

## Structural and cultural transformation

[[Tornberg2026-lc]] provides the widest-angle structural claim: generative AI is one of three forces (alongside algorithmic recommendation and retreat into semi-private spaces) dissolving the "social media" paradigm itself, producing "platforms without users" and AI-mediated communication as a new, non-public communicative form — a frame that helps explain why [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]'s gambling networks exploit exactly the organic/paid asymmetry of algorithmic amplification rather than traditional advertising. The cultural register of this shift is taken up by [[Galip2026-ix]] and [[Tang2026-gu]]: the former theorizes "AI slop" and "brainrot" via Ngai's gimmick as forms that work simultaneously "too little" and "too hard," exposing contradictions of cultural production under platform capitalism; the latter grounds this in Chinese short-video creators who treat virality itself as an engineerable output via generative tools and matrix-account systems — a labour-and-authorship analogue to the industrial-scale persuasion machinery documented in the gambling case.

## Governance and the pipeline question

Finally, a policy-facing set asks what should be done. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] maps the deepfake financial-fraud "scam ecosystem" and argues for gatekeeper- rather than victim-centered liability — directly parallel to [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]]'s argument that Meta's asymmetric governance of paid versus organic content, not just content moderation, is the structural enabler of coordinated gambling promotion. [[Goldberg2026-eb]] takes the more constructive tack, surveying LLM-enabled "digital public square" technologies (bridging systems, collective dialogue, AI-assisted moderation) as a second paradigm shift that could counteract the degradation documented elsewhere in this note, provided synthetic participation augments rather than substitutes human deliberation.

Methodologically, [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] also stands as this collection's practical demonstration of the second half of the topic brief: LLMs, embeddings, and clustering used not as an object of study but as a *research pipeline* for classifying tens of thousands of persuasive images — a move that anticipates the "IA Generativa e Media" course's dual mandate to study generative AI's effects on media while teaching generative AI as a tool of media research itself.
