# Problematic Health Information Online

## Coordinated Amplification Across Platforms and Countries

The empirical core of this cluster documents how problematic health content does not simply "go viral" through organic contagion but is often propelled by identifiable, coordinated infrastructures. Working from the Italian Facebook ecosystem, [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] traces the emergence of a covid-skeptic coordinated network as an offshoot of pre-existing political clusters, cataloguing tactics — link laundering through reputable outlets, first-comment link placement, cross-posting into image macros, infiltration of religious groups — used to evade platform enforcement. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends this same Italian dataset by looking at *who* gets amplified within these networks, showing that an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster of alternative influencers achieves outsized, share-driven engagement, and that legacy journalists and right-leaning newspapers remain the central nodes recycling and "remediating" these voices — a hybrid-media dynamic that blurs the line between fringe and mainstream circulation. [[Song2025-yh]] generalizes the coordinated-link-sharing lens cross-nationally, comparing UK and US Facebook vaccine discourse and finding that coordination is ideologically agnostic: dense, high-engagement anti-vaccine networks dominate in the UK (organized around safety concerns) while pro-vaccine coordination is comparatively stronger in the US (organized around individual freedom rhetoric), with credibility profiles diverging sharply by stance rather than by the mere fact of coordination. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] transposes this logic to Twitter/X and scientific-paper circulation, identifying an almost entirely contrarian coordinated retweet network that artificially elevates a narrow set of credentialed dissenting experts above the platform's organic superspreaders, and shows that news coverage of these papers typically *follows* rather than precedes this superspreader activity — with high- and low-trust outlets each aligning with their ideological counterpart among Twitter amplifiers. Together these papers establish coordination — not bots, not isolated virality — as the connective tissue linking platform actors, alternative influencers, and legacy media across three distinct national contexts.

## From Detection to Intervention: Tools for Fact-Checking and Measurement

A second thread turns this descriptive work toward operational tools that could accelerate detection and correction. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] is again pivotal here: beyond mapping the Italian network, it reports a field test of a content-agnostic MINE-FACTS prototype built on the CooRnet coordination signal, which surfaced a substantially higher share of problematic content (≈40%) than routine fact-checking (≈28%) during a live trial with Facta.news — demonstrating that behavioral, coordination-based signals can pre-filter content for human fact-checkers rather than relying solely on claim-matching. [[DeVerna2025-dl]] pushes the automation question further into the LLM era, showing that even reasoning- and search-augmented large language models fact-check poorly on political claims unless given curated retrieval context, after which performance improves dramatically — a cautionary complement to Giglietto et al.'s human-in-the-loop tool, suggesting that scaling verification requires curated evidence infrastructures rather than either purely behavioral heuristics or purely generic AI capability. [[Scalco2026-bd]] contributes a parallel measurement innovation aimed not at flagging false content directly but at anticipating the conditions under which misinformation flourishes: by quantifying "information voids" as anomalous demand-supply imbalances across six European countries' social media and search data, the authors show that voids around vaccine-related events (EMA authorizations, the AstraZeneca suspension) precede drops in high-credibility content share and spikes in unreliable content — effectively offering an early-warning signal that could complement content- and coordination-based detection systems.

## Gatekeepers, Media, and the Politicization of Expertise

A third strand examines how legitimate gatekeeping institutions — news aggregators, newspapers, source-rating systems — themselves shape which health information and which experts gain visibility, sometimes reproducing polarization even without "misinformation" per se. [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] analyzes Google News Italia as an algorithmic gatekeeper for health topics, finding that while medical-scientific and healthcare-system news dominates aggregation, emotionally charged categories like celebrity health stories and healthcare malfunctions drive disproportionate Facebook engagement — illustrating a gap between what gatekeepers surface and what audiences amplify. [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]] shows an analogous but more explicitly political gatekeeping dynamic in the UK press: newspapers systematically over-cite scientists whose COVID-19 precautionary stance matches their own political orientation (Great Barrington vs. John Snow Memorandum signatories), with the resulting bubbles reproduced in academic co-authorship and Twitter-sharing networks — a reminder that polarized "expert selection" by mainstream gatekeepers can segregate legitimate scientific disagreement along partisan lines even absent fringe actors. Underpinning both studies, and much of the broader literature on this list, is the question of how source credibility itself is measured; [[Luhring2025-od]] audits the NewsGuard database used throughout this body of research, showing that continuous trustworthiness scores are reasonably stable since 2022 but that binary trustworthy/untrustworthy cutoffs can radically distort findings depending on which borderline outlets cross the threshold — a methodological caution that bears directly on studies like [[Song2025-yh]] and [[Scalco2026-bd]] that rely on source-level credibility classifications.

## Who Is Exposed, and What Follows: Beliefs, Behavior, and Bodies

The final arc moves from supply-side circulation to downstream consequences — who actually encounters this content, and what harm follows. [[Adam2026-tz]] combines panel survey and web-tracking data in Germany/Switzerland to show that conspiracy belief formation is jointly shaped by media exposure and predispositions: populists selectively engage with conspiracy-supporting alternative media and selectively avoid (or "counterargue" against) mainstream debunking, producing both contagion and, more surprisingly, backfire effects even from mainstream outlets that circulated more conspiracy-supporting than -opposing content overall. [[Lyons2026-ca]] narrows the lens to health content specifically, using linked survey and browsing-history data to show that low-credibility health exposure is rare in aggregate but heavily concentrated among older adults, poor headline discerners, and consumers of dubious political news — and, notably, is driven by habitual navigation between low-credibility sites rather than by search or social media referral, complicating simple platform-blame narratives found elsewhere in this collection. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] closes the causal loop by linking online exposure to offline outcomes: using a compartmental epidemic model fit to geolocated antivaccine tweets and county-level case data, the authors estimate that Twitter antivaccine content causally produced roughly 14,000 vaccine refusals and measurable excess cases and deaths in the US — providing the kind of mechanistic, population-level consequence that the coordination-mapping ([[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], [[Marino2023-9137f448]], [[Song2025-yh]], [[Efstratiou2026-ij]]) and gatekeeping ([[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]], [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]]) studies largely gesture toward but do not themselves quantify. Read together, these three papers suggest a layered account: predispositions and media diets jointly generate belief change ([[Adam2026-tz]]), exposure is concentrated in identifiable vulnerable subpopulations ([[Lyons2026-ca]]), and that exposure translates into measurable, non-trivial public-health cost ([[Bollenbacher2026-vz]]) — closing the causal chain that the coordination, tooling, and gatekeeping literatures in this note trace from the supply side.
