# Health Misinformation & Infodemic Research

## Mapping Coordinated Circulation: From Italy Outward

The empirical core of this cluster is a set of Italian studies that treat coordinated inauthentic behavior as an observable, trackable phenomenon rather than a diffuse suspicion. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] documents the MINE-FACTS project, which used the CooRnet coordinated-link-sharing-behavior (CLSB) methodology to build a content-agnostic tool for Facta.news, Italy's fact-checking outlet, showing that behavioral signals (shares-to-comments ratios, link-laundering, first-comment link placement) can surface problematic content faster and at higher hit-rates than manual fact-checking alone. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends this same underlying dataset of covid-skeptic accounts to trace how an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster of alternative influencers achieves outsized engagement precisely by having legacy journalists and right-leaning newspapers *remediate* their claims — hybridity, in Chadwick's sense, becomes the amplification mechanism rather than an incidental feature. [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] complements this by looking upstream at Google News Italia as a gatekeeper: even before coordination or virality enter the picture, algorithmic aggregation already privileges "emotional" and "popular" health topics (celebrity, malpractice) over technical-scientific ones, setting the raw material that coordinated actors later exploit.

This Italian strand generalizes into comparative territory with [[Song2025-yh]], which applies the same CLSB toolkit to UK and US Facebook vaccine discourse and finds that coordination is not intrinsically malicious — pro-vaccine institutional actors (NHS, CDC) coordinate just as densely as anti-vaccine ones, with the crucial difference lying in source credibility and the national framing of anti-vaccine narratives (safety-focused in the UK, liberty-focused in the US). Read alongside the Italian case studies, this suggests a general finding for the topic as a whole: coordinated amplification is a structural feature of contemporary platform ecosystems, and its normative valence depends entirely on who is coordinating and around what content — precisely the kind of cross-national comparative claim (India, Nigeria, Italy, UK, US) that motivates the broader Gates/MUR-funded infrastructure this cluster sits within.

## Quantifying Causal Impact, Not Just Prevalence

A second thread pushes past description toward causal estimation of real-world harm. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] builds a compartmental SIRVA epidemic model fit to geolocated antivaccine tweets and county-level US outcomes, arriving at a lower-bound causal estimate (~14,000 refusals, ~545 cases, ~8 deaths) directly attributable to Twitter content — a rare attempt to close the loop from platform speech to epidemiological outcome. [[Allen2024-av]] complicates the object of causal attention itself: using large-scale experiments and crowdsourced classification across ~233 million Facebook users, it finds that *unflagged*, technically-true-but-skepticism-inducing content had roughly 46 times the aggregate impact of outright flagged misinformation, challenging fact-checking-centric interventions that target only overtly "false" claims. [[Lyons2026-ca]] reframes the exposure question yet again by combining survey and passive web/YouTube tracking data, showing that low-credibility health content exposure is rare in aggregate but concentrated among older, conspiracist-leaning, cross-domain consumers — and crucially arrives via referral chains between low-credibility sites rather than search or social platforms, meaning platform-level moderation may be targeting the wrong choke point entirely.

Together these three papers triangulate a methodological argument: prevalence, causal effect, and exposure concentration are distinct quantities that can diverge sharply, and infodemic interventions calibrated to one (e.g., flagging false claims) may miss where the actual harm accumulates (unflagged skepticism, concentrated older-adult exposure, cross-site habitual consumption).

## Psychological and Institutional Mechanisms Behind Belief

Underlying the platform-level findings is a psychological substrate addressed most directly by [[Lewandowsky2012-vn]], whose account of the continued influence effect — corrections failing to fully dislodge misinformation, and sometimes backfiring — supplies the theoretical vocabulary that later empirical papers operationalize. [[Adam2026-tz]] gives this a contemporary, data-rich test: combining panel surveys with BERT-classified web tracking in Germany/Switzerland, it shows conspiracy belief formation as a "marriage" of predisposition and exposure, with populist/mistrustful individuals selectively engaging alternative media (reinforcement) and counterarguing against mainstream debunking they do encounter (a real-time demonstration of Lewandowsky's backfire dynamics). [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]] extends the institutional angle to elite discourse itself, showing that UK newspapers' citation patterns of GBD versus JSM signatories track political alignment rather than scientific fringe-ness — a reminder that "misinformation" research must distinguish genuine falsehood from politically selective amplification of legitimate scientific disagreement, a distinction implicit throughout the coordinated-network papers above. [[Xue2025-bp]] closes this thread by turning attention to the correctors themselves, finding that fact-checking posts are far from the dispassionate genre they claim to be, routinely deploying emotional language strategically to drive engagement — raising the question of whether corrective communication inherits the same attention-economy pressures that generate the misinformation it targets.

## Detection Infrastructure and the Supply–Demand Frame

Finally, [[Scalco2026-bd]] supplies a structural, ecosystem-level complement to the actor-centric coordination studies: rather than tracking who circulates content, it quantifies *when* the information environment itself fails, operationalizing "information voids" as anomalous demand-supply imbalances across six European countries' social media and search data. Its finding that voids correlate with reduced high-credibility content and increased misinformation prevalence gives a mechanistic, systems-level explanation for exactly the phenomena the Italian CLSB studies and the German/Swiss tracking study document at the actor and individual level — vacuums in reliable supply are where coordinated and psychologically-primed misinformation circulation takes hold. Read as a whole, this cluster moves from infrastructure for detection ([[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], [[Scalco2026-bd]]), through mapping of coordinated actors and gatekeepers ([[Marino2023-9137f448]], [[Song2025-yh]], [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]]), to causal quantification of downstream harm ([[Bollenbacher2026-vz]], [[Allen2024-av]], [[Lyons2026-ca]]), grounded throughout in a psychological and institutional account of why corrections so often fail ([[Lewandowsky2012-vn]], [[Adam2026-tz]], [[Ghezzi2023-8bebc91f]], [[Xue2025-bp]]).
