# Generative AI & Synthetic Media in Disinformation

## The Shifting Terrain: GenAI as Both Weapon and Target

The papers filed here converge on a single unsettling observation: generative AI has not simply added a new tool to the disinformation toolkit but has begun to reorganize the conditions under which persuasion, trust, and truth-verification operate. [[Emilio2026-ik]] offers the most totalizing framing, arguing that the real danger is not isolated deepfakes but "synthetic realities" — coherent, interactive, personalized informational environments spanning content, identity, interaction, and institutions — culminating in a paradox whereby ubiquitous synthetic media may rationally lead societies to discount *all* digital evidence. [[Dierickx2026-tw]] approaches the same rupture epistemologically, showing that classical fact-checking categories (evidence-based, interpretative, rule-based) cannot accommodate GenAI outputs and proposing "emergent facts" as a new category requiring relational, indicator-based evaluation rather than binary verification. Together these two theoretical papers set the stakes for the more empirical work that follows: if the ground of verification itself is shifting, what exactly do persuasive AI systems do, and how effective are they?

## The Mechanics of AI Persuasion

A cluster of large-scale experimental studies interrogates *how* conversational AI persuades, converging on a strikingly consistent mechanism: information density rather than personalization or rapport. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] systematically decomposes the "levers" of persuasion across 19 LLMs and tens of thousands of conversations, finding that post-training and prompting strategies matter far more than scale, and that the same levers boosting persuasiveness also degrade factual accuracy — a troubling trade-off. [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] escalates the claim, showing AI out-persuades even elite competitive debaters and professional canvassers, with the advantage collapsing only when AI is artificially constrained to human typing speeds, implicating fact-checkable claim throughput as the causal mechanism. [[Lin2025-xp]] corroborates this across four real elections, showing AI persuades mainly through facts and evidence rather than manipulation, though right-leaning advocacy consistently produces less accurate claims — echoing [[Waight2026-ts]]'s finding that state media capture of training data skews LLM outputs toward pro-regime valence in politically unfree language contexts. [[Kotz2026-lk]] extends this logic normatively, showing GPT-5 dialogues shift beliefs and policy support on climate, vaccination, and inequality, with the largest effects among skeptics — suggesting conversational AI's persuasive power could be redirected toward evidence-based public communication rather than manipulation. Against this backdrop, [[Rauchfleisch2026-fa]] offers a governance-relevant counterpoint: disclosing an AI's *identity* does little to blunt persuasion, but disclosing its *persuasive intent* roughly halves it — reframing transparency debates (e.g., EU AI Act Article 50) around intent rather than source labeling.

## Synthetic Visual Persuasion: Gambling, Slop, and Affective Lures

The topic's visual-media strand is anchored by [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]], which documents an exponential, ChatGPT-coincident surge in coordinated Facebook gambling promotion, showing generative AI intensifying rather than replacing established persuasion drivers (aspirational wealth, manufactured trust, FOMO, cultural localization) while exploiting the regulatory gray zone between paid and organic content. This empirical case sits in productive tension with [[Hameleers2026-mc]], whose experimental comparison finds that AI-generated images do *not* universally outperform text or authentic-looking decontextualized video — visual disinformation's power remains issue-bound, suggesting that fears of GenAI's unprecedented persuasive force may be overstated relative to low-tech video recontextualization. [[Choi2026-bz]] complicates the audience side, showing that difficulty authenticating one deepfake modality carries over, in a modality-congruent way, to shape confidence and susceptibility toward subsequent fakes.

A more cultural-theoretical register reframes what "fake" visual content is even for. [[Topinka2026-hb]] coins "slopaganda" to describe AI political content that is obviously fake yet affectively resonant — arguing models are becoming "ideological attunement machines" that intensify existing reactionary formations rather than deceiving anyone. [[Crichton2026-kn]]'s regeneration experiment supports this structurally: commercial image models don't neutralize far-right imagery but sanitize its explicit markers while preserving ideological legibility to in-group audiences — a bias the authors treat as architectural, not incidental. [[Galip2026-ix]] and [[Tang2026-gu]] extend the "slop" vocabulary from politics to platform economics, respectively theorizing slop/brainrot via Ngai's gimmick (working simultaneously "too little" and "too hard") and documenting Chinese creators who industrialize virality itself as an engineered, systematized output of generative pipelines. [[Perez-Curiel2026-ld]] situates these dynamics in electoral campaigning, tracing far-right use of AI-generated multimedia on Instagram and X during the 2024 European elections, while [[Wack2026-bt]]'s Kenyan field experiment shows deepfake impact is not fixed at production but "socially negotiated" through surrounding audience commentary — credulous comments amplify harm, incredulous ones partially restore it, moderated by partisanship.

## Coordination at Scale: Swarms, Agents, and Platform-Specific Detection

A distinct technical strand examines how generative and agentic AI enable coordinated inauthentic behavior beyond individual artifacts. [[Orlando2025-ul]] simulates networked LLM agents running information operations and finds that mere mutual awareness among agents — without explicit deliberation — produces coordination nearly as strong as full collective decision-making, a sobering result for platform governance. [[Schroeder2026-im]] generalizes this into the "malicious AI swarm" concept, arguing that LLM-agent fusion constitutes a qualitative leap beyond earlier botnets (e.g., the IRA), enabling manufactured consensus, training-data poisoning ("LLM grooming"), and erosion of institutional trust — proposing layered governance responses rather than reliance on symmetric "pro-social" counter-swarms. [[Luceri2025-tr]] grounds these concerns in platform specificity, showing that text-centric CIB-detection methods do not transfer cleanly to TikTok's video-first ecosystem, a gap also visible in [[Hollingshead2026-vx]]'s finding that Canadian TikTok immigration discourse skews pro-immigration overall but shows GenAI entering anti-immigration content as a tool for racist "speculative worldbuilding."

## Trust, Disclosure, and the Epistemics of AI-Mediated News

Several papers examine how audiences and institutions negotiate trust once GenAI enters the information chain. [[Suk2026-ai]] proposes a multilevel (individual/institutional/societal) framework building on mass- and social-media trust scholarship to theorize trust in GenAI as a distinct phenomenon. Empirically, [[Gilardi2026-hw]] finds that readers rate AI-generated and human-written news as equivalent in quality pre-disclosure, and that disclosure boosts short-term curiosity-driven engagement without building durable acceptance of AI journalism. [[Mattis2026-gu]] refines this further, showing that task-specific disclosures (rather than generic AI labels) uniformly reduce trust but with a task hierarchy — fact-checking incurs the steepest penalty — moderated by political position and AI literacy, with audiences clustering into five distinct disclosure-preference profiles. [[Kasianenko2026-tn]]'s audit of Google's AI Overviews shows uneven algorithmic guardrails against conspiratorial search framings, debunking established theories like chemtrails more reliably than emergent ones like "15-minute cities," sometimes inadvertently legitimizing the latter.

## Governance, Political Economy, and Structural Variation

A policy-oriented cluster asks who is responsible and how harms vary structurally. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] maps the deepfake financial-fraud "scam ecosystem" and argues regulation must shift liability from victims to gatekeepers — platforms, telecoms, banks — pointing to Singapore's and Taiwan's shared-liability models as templates against the US's Section 230 shield. [[Waight2026-ts]] demonstrates a subtler governance problem: state media control is laundered into LLM training data itself, making model outputs partisan proxies for the media environments states construct. [[Beacken2026-zb]] insists these dynamics are not uniform, showing GenAI propaganda adoption varies substantially across six democratically weakened countries depending on local political-economic structure — a necessary corrective to technologically deterministic narratives running through much of this corpus.

## Reframing the Media Ecosystem Itself

Finally, several papers step back to ask whether existing concepts for "media," "platform," and "AI" even still apply. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] argues the "social media" paradigm is dissolving into algorithmic broadcasting, semi-private micro-communities, and AI-mediated communication — with chatbot use now exceeding active social posting, eroding the shared epistemic infrastructure disinformation research has long assumed. [[Nguyen2026-vm]] shows journalism's own framing of LLMs as agentic is neither uniform nor simply uncritical, complicating assumptions about media-driven AI hype. [[Triedman2025-uy]] offers a concrete case of AI reshaping knowledge infrastructure: Grokipedia is shown to be derivative of Wikipedia yet systematically skewed toward unreliable and blacklisted sources on controversial topics, exemplifying ideologically inflected AI-generated reference content. [[Manovich2026-ih]] closes the loop theoretically, treating generative AI not as a threat vector but as a medium with its own cognitive and aesthetic structure — conservative, encyclopedic, resistant to logical surrealism — a reminder that the persuasive and deceptive capacities catalogued elsewhere in this topic are downstream of deeper medium-specific properties this corpus is only beginning to theorize.
