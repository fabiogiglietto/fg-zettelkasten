# Generative AI & Synthetic Media Manipulation

## The persuasion turn: from static content to conversational agents

The empirical core of this literature is a striking reversal of intuition about what makes generative AI persuasive. [[Hackenburg2025-dj]] and its companion study [[Hackenburg2026-ud]] establish, across tens of thousands of controlled conversations, that AI's persuasive edge over both static messaging and expert humans—including elite debaters and professional canvassers—derives not from personalization or psychological sophistication but from raw *information density*: the sheer volume of fact-checkable claims an AI can produce per exchange. Constraining AI to human throughput erases its advantage almost entirely, which relocates the "threat" of AI persuasion away from microtargeting fears and toward something more mundane and more tractable: bandwidth. [[Lin2025-xp]] corroborates this mechanism across four real elections, finding AI persuades chiefly through evidence and facts rather than manipulation tactics, while also surfacing a partisan asymmetry—AI advocating right-leaning candidates was systematically less accurate. [[Kotz2026-lk]] extends the mechanism to policy domains (climate, vaccination, inequality), showing that evidence-dense AI dialogue is especially effective on skeptics, the audience traditionally hardest to move. Taken together, these four papers converge on an uncomfortable trade-off flagged explicitly by [[Hackenburg2025-dj]]: the very techniques that maximize persuasiveness (information density, post-training optimization) simultaneously degrade factual accuracy. This is the load-bearing empirical finding for the whole topic—AI persuasion is powerful, real, and mechanistically understood, but it is bought at the price of truth.

## Beyond artifacts: visual, coordinated, and swarm-level manipulation

A second cluster shifts from text-based persuasion to the visual and multi-agent dimensions of synthetic manipulation. [[Giglietto2026-9b6a992d]] documents an "exponential" uptick in AI-generated gambling imagery on Facebook coinciding with ChatGPT's release, showing that generative AI doesn't invent new persuasion drivers so much as intensify and recombine existing ones (aspirational wealth, FOMO, manufactured trust) at industrial scale, exploiting Meta's organic/paid regulatory gap. [[Hameleers2026-mc]] complicates the assumption that visual/synthetic disinformation is inherently more potent than text, finding that decontextualized *real* video often outperforms AI-generated stills—suggesting alarm about generative AI specifically may be somewhat displaced from where the actual persuasive risk lies. At the systems level, [[Orlando2025-ul]] and [[Schroeder2026-im]] examine what happens when generation is coupled with coordination: LLM agents in simulated environments spontaneously reproduce hallmark coordinated-inauthentic-behavior signatures (network densification, narrative convergence, synchronized amplification) with only minimal "teammate awareness," and [[Schroeder2026-im]] extrapolates this into a governance-oriented account of "malicious AI swarms" that can manufacture consensus and poison training data ("LLM grooming") at a qualitatively new tempo. Together these pieces argue that the unit of manipulation is shifting from the single deepfake artifact to persistent, adaptive, networked systems.

## Reception, sensemaking, and the fragility of discernment

A parallel strand asks not how synthetic content is produced but how audiences metabolize it. [[Wack2026-bt]]'s Kenyan election study is pivotal here: it shows that a deepfake's political impact is not fixed at production but is *socially negotiated* through surrounding commentary and partisanship—credulous peer reactions amplify harm, skeptical ones neutralize it, especially for co-partisans. [[Choi2026-bz]] offers a complementary individual-level mechanism, the "modality-congruent carryover effect," in which difficulty authenticating one deepfake shapes vulnerability to subsequent same-modality fakes, implicating self-confidence and metacognition rather than raw detection ability. Both push against artifact-centric models of deepfake harm toward relational and contextual accounts—resonant with [[Dierickx2026-tw]]'s theoretical move to redefine factuality itself. Dierickx argues that GenAI outputs are neither evidence-based, interpretative, nor rule-based facts but a new category, "emergent facts," demanding relational rather than binary verification—an epistemological frame that gives conceptual backing to what Wack and Choi observe empirically: truth-assessment of synthetic media is negotiated, contingent, and socially distributed rather than a fixed property of the artifact.

## Infrastructural and geopolitical capture of the information environment

A further set of papers scales up from individual deepfakes to systemic capture of knowledge infrastructures. [[Waight2026-ts]] shows that state media control already seeps into LLM training data, producing measurable pro-regime valence in outputs—an "indirect" propaganda pathway distinct from deliberate model design. [[Triedman2025-uy]]'s forensic analysis of Grokipedia extends this concern to a specific, high-profile case: an AI-generated encyclopedia that is derivative of Wikipedia yet systematically shifts sourcing toward low-quality and blacklisted domains on controversial topics, illustrating how generative rewriting can quietly launder ideological slant into ostensibly neutral reference material. [[Beacken2026-zb]] pushes back against deterministic narratives, showing comparatively that GenAI's use in propaganda across six democratically weakened states is highly contingent on local political-economic structure rather than a uniform technological trajectory—an important corrective note within this cluster. [[Schiffrin_undated-gi]] pivots to the financial-fraud instantiation of the same infrastructural logic, mapping a "scam ecosystem" of platforms, telecoms, and financial intermediaries that enable AI-driven fraud at scale, and arguing—like several papers here—that liability should shift from individual victims to gatekeepers. [[Emilio2026-ik]] offers the most totalizing synthesis of this strand, proposing that the real danger is not discrete deepfakes but layered "synthetic realities" (content, identity, interaction, institutions) that produce a paradox: as synthetic content proliferates, societies may rationally discount *all* digital evidence, raising the cost of truth itself and enabling strategic plausible deniability.

## Reframing the media environment and the medium itself

Finally, several papers step back to theorize what this all means for media studies and trust research. [[Tornberg2026-lc]] argues the "social media" paradigm itself is dissolving under three pressures—algorithmic broadcasting, semi-private retreat, and AI-mediated communication—with generative AI enabling "platforms without users" and severing the historic link between platforms and human-generated content. [[Baym2026-tr]] offers a retrospective political-economy diagnosis, situating generative AI's exploitation of user data and non-consensual deepfakes within a decade-long worsening of platform harms tied to wealth concentration. [[Suk2026-ai]] responds constructively by proposing a multilevel (individual/institutional/societal) framework for trust in generative AI, explicitly building on mass- and social-media trust scholarship to give the empirical findings above a theoretical home. [[Manovich2026-ih]] is the outlier and useful counterweight: bracketing ethics and manipulation entirely, it treats generative AI as an artistic medium with its own cognitive and stylistic affordances—structurally conservative, entangling style and content—reminding the rest of this literature that the same generative mechanisms driving persuasion and deception are also reshaping aesthetic production on their own terms. Read together, [[Hollingshead2026-vx]]'s TikTok immigration study sits at the empirical boundary between these concerns, showing generative AI beginning to enable racist "speculative worldbuilding" within an otherwise platform-mediated, largely human discourse—a reminder that synthetic manipulation is emerging *within*, not replacing, existing platform vernaculars and contestation.
