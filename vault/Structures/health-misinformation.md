# Health Misinformation & Online Gatekeeping

## The architecture of coordinated amplification

A first cluster of papers establishes that problematic health content rarely spreads through diffuse, organic contagion alone — it is engineered. [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] provides the methodological backbone here, showing how Coordinated Link Sharing Behavior (CLSB), detected content-agnostically via the CooRnet package, can expose an Italian covid-skeptic network that emerged as an offshoot of pre-existing political coordination (League, Five Star Movement) and that adapts tactics — link laundering, first-comment link placement, religious-group infiltration — to evade platform enforcement. [[Marino2023-9137f448]] extends this same Italian dataset forward, asking not just *who coordinates* but *whom they amplify*: an "Intellectual Dark Web" cluster of alternative influencers, paradoxically legitimated through remediation by legacy journalists and mainstream outlets, generates outsized share-driven engagement. Together these two papers trace a single Italian case from network detection to influencer-level content analysis, demonstrating that legacy media remain complicit nodes in amplifying alternative narratives rather than simply gatekeeping against them.

[[Song2025-yh]] complicates the coordination story by showing CLSB is "ideologically agnostic": in the UK and US, anti-vaccine *and* pro-vaccine communities both organize coordinated link-sharing, but with sharply different credibility profiles and national framings (UK anti-vax discourse centers on safety, US on individual freedom). This reframes coordination not as inherently nefarious but as a scalable tactic whose normative valence depends on context — a useful corrective to the assumption, implicit in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]], that coordinated behavior is a reliable proxy for malice. [[Efstratiou2026-ij]] pushes the coordination lens onto Twitter and the scientific-preprint ecosystem, finding an almost entirely contrarian coordinated network that amplifies a narrow set of credentialed anti-consensus experts — and crucially shows that news coverage tends to *follow* superspreader activity rather than precede it, positioning Twitter/X as an agenda-setter for both high- and low-trust outlets alike.

## Gatekeepers, old and new

The topic's second thread interrogates what "gatekeeping" even means once algorithmic aggregators and platform affordances have joined journalists in curating the public health agenda. [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] is the clearest articulation of this shift: Google News Italia aggregates mostly technical medical-science content, but it is emotionally charged celebrity and malpractice stories that migrate into high Facebook engagement, revealing a gap between what gatekeepers surface and what audiences amplify. This matters for the coordination papers above, since [[Marino2023-9137f448]] shows the same dynamic — comments/shares ratios distinguishing contested from share-driven content — operating within alternative influence networks rather than mainstream aggregation. Read together, [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]] and [[Marino2023-9137f448]] suggest gatekeeping failures are not confined to fringe actors; the very architecture of engagement-optimized platforms systematically rewards affect and controversy over institutional authority, regardless of who is the original curator.

## Measuring the ecosystem: voids, exposure, and demand

A third thread moves from mapping bad-actor networks to measuring the broader information ecosystem's structural vulnerabilities. [[Scalco2026-bd]] operationalizes "information voids" — moments when public demand for health information outstrips credible supply — and shows these voids, concentrated around vaccine rollout events in six European countries, are empirically linked to reduced high-credibility content and increased misinformation share. This offers a demand-side complement to the supply-side coordination work: voids create the conditions coordinated networks like those in [[Giglietto2022-0e951ac5]] and [[Song2025-yh]] can exploit. [[Lyons2026-ca]] shifts from aggregate ecosystem dynamics to individual-level exposure, using web-tracking to show that low-credibility health content exposure is rare but heavily concentrated among older adults with weaker discernment and more conspiracist worldviews — and crucially, that this exposure flows through cross-domain habitual browsing (other low-credibility sites, dubious political news) rather than through search or social referral, diverging from assumptions embedded in platform-gatekeeping accounts like [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]].

## From belief to behavior: causal stakes and competing estimates

The final and most consequential thread asks whether any of this actually changes vaccination behavior and health outcomes, and here the collection stages a genuine disagreement. [[Adam2026-tz]] models conspiracy belief formation as a "marriage" of media exposure and predisposition, finding both contagion from alternative media and mitigation from mainstream debunking, mediated by selective engagement and counterarguing among populists — a nuanced, bidirectional account that resists simple contagion narratives. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] and [[Allen2024-av]] then offer two methodologically sophisticated but divergent estimates of real-world harm. [[Bollenbacher2026-vz]] uses a causal epidemic model (SIRVA) to attribute roughly 14,000 vaccine refusals and several hundred cases/deaths specifically to antivaccine Twitter content. [[Allen2024-av]], working at Facebook scale, directly challenges the "infodemic" framing underlying much of this topic: fact-check-flagged misinformation had limited reach and thus limited aggregate impact, while unflagged, "vaccine-skeptical" but technically accurate content — often from mainstream outlets — drove 46 times more hesitancy. This finding resonates uncomfortably with [[Marino2023-9137f448]] and [[Ducci2022-10cb5d70]], both of which show mainstream/legacy media as central amplification nodes rather than innocent bystanders, and it suggests that the coordinated-network and gatekeeping literatures surveyed above, by focusing on clearly "problematic" or flagged content, may be training attention on a demonstrably smaller share of the actual harm — leaving the subtler, credibility-laundered content highlighted by [[Allen2024-av]] and [[Lyons2026-ca]] as the frontier the field has yet to fully map, especially outside the US/Europe axis (India, Nigeria) that this topic nominally spans but that remains thin in the current corpus.
